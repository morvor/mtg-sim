# Triggers and durations

Judgment calls about trigger events, delayed triggered abilities and the durations of
effects created by resolving spells and abilities (CR 603, 610, 611). Format and kinds:
`docs/agents/items/interpretation-register.md`; index: `docs/INTERPRETATIONS.md`.

## next-turn-effects-start-with-the-turn: Effects "during [a player]'s next turn" are created as that turn begins
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 611.2a "A continuous effect generated by the resolution of a spell or ability lasts as long as stated by the spell or ability creating it (such as “until end of turn”). ..." / 611.2c "... A continuous effect generated by the resolution of a spell or ability that doesn’t modify the characteristics or change the controller of any objects modifies the rules of the game, so it can affect objects that weren’t affected when that continuous effect began. ..." / 613.11 "Some continuous effects affect game rules rather than objects. ... All other such effects are applied in timestamp order." / Gideon Jura rulings: "Gideon Jura's first ability applies during each combat phase of the affected player's next turn (as opposed to applying during the affected player's next combat phase)." and "Gideon Jura's first ability doesn't lock in what it applies to." / Gideon, Battle-Forged ruling: "If the creature targeted by Gideon's first ability changes controllers before it has the chance to attack Gideon, the ability will apply to it during its new controller's next turn."
- Question: The effect of "During target opponent's next turn, creatures that player controls attack ~ if able" is created as the ability resolves but applies only later. When does it begin, and what timestamp does it have?
- Decision: The engine keeps the instruction waiting, with the resolving ability's targets, choices and controller, and performs it (as an effect lasting "this turn", or "until end of combat" for "during its controller's next combat phase") as that player's next turn (combat phase) begins. "During its controller's next turn" waits for whoever controls the object when a turn begins, so a control change first moves the effect to the new controller's next turn; the effect is dropped if the object leaves the battlefield or the player leaves the game. The compiler accepts only rule-modifying instructions here (restrictions, requirements and skips), whose meaning doesn't depend on their timestamp or on a set of objects locked in as they begin, and a delayed ability for that turn's end step ("At the beginning of that turn's end step, destroy each of the chosen creatures that didn't attack this turn", Oracle en-Vec), created as the turn begins.
- Alternatives: Create the effect at once with a duration that starts later: the same behavior for these effects, with the resolution's timestamp; it would matter only for effects ordered by timestamp, which the compiler doesn't accept here.
- Why: The waiting instruction is simple to apply at the right moment, and the ruling on control changes needs the controller as of the turn anyway.
- Affects: Oracle en-Vec (creatures chosen as the ability resolves), Gideon Jura, Rowan Kenrith, Gideon, Battle-Forged, Taunt, Sphinx's Decree, Azor, the Lawbringer, Wall of Dust, Trench Behemoth, Sizzling Soloist, Empty City Ruse, False Peace, Willie Lumpkin, Postman, The Second Doctor ("that player can't attack you ... during their next turn": creatures the active player controls during that turn, whenever they came under their control).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/next_turn.rs`; `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/durations_players.rs`; `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/durations_4.rs`.
- Tests: `tests/cards/td_next_turn_durations.rs` (`taunt_makes_that_players_creatures_attack_during_their_next_turn_only`).

## end-of-combat-on-your-next-turn-without-combat: "Until end of combat on your next turn" ends with a turn that has no combat
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 500.5a "Effects that last “until end of combat” expire at the end of the combat phase, not at the beginning of the end of combat step." / 611.2a "A continuous effect generated by the resolution of a spell or ability lasts as long as stated by the spell or ability creating it (such as “until end of turn”). ..."
- Question: Brazen Cannonade's "Until end of combat on your next turn, you may play that card" names the end of the combat phase of your next turn. If that turn has no combat phase (all combat phases skipped), when does the permission end?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Oct 21: it ends as that turn ends (in its cleanup step, with "until end of turn" effects). With a combat phase, it ends as the first combat phase of that turn ends.
- Alternatives: Lasting until the end of the next combat phase of yours, whenever it comes: a permission longer than the card promises, through a whole later turn. Ending as the turn's main phase would have been followed by combat: no such moment exists if the phase is skipped.
- Why: The duration is bounded by "your next turn"; a turn without combat has no later point in it than its end.
- Affects: Brazen Cannonade.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/next_turn.rs` (`expire_end_of_combat_on_next_turn`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/td_duration_endpoints.rs` (`brazen_cannonade_permission_ends_with_a_next_turn_that_has_no_combat`).

## delayed-if-not-played-means-still-exiled: "If you haven't cast the card" on a delayed ability checks that it's still exiled
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 400.7 "An object that moves from one zone to another becomes a new object with no memory of, or relation to, its previous existence. ..." / 603.4 "A triggered ability may read “When/Whenever/At [trigger event], if [condition], [effect].” When the trigger event occurs, the ability checks whether the stated condition is true. The ability triggers only if it is; otherwise it does nothing. If the ability triggers, it checks the stated condition again as it resolves. ..." / 603.7c "A delayed triggered ability that refers to a particular object still affects it even if the object changes characteristics. However, if that object is no longer in the zone it’s expected to be in at the time the delayed triggered ability resolves, the ability won’t affect it. ..." / Psychic Theft ruling: "Once it is announced and put on the stack, the card is no longer exiled and the effect of Psychic Theft ends."
- Question: "At the beginning of the next end step, if you haven't cast the card, return it to its owner's hand" (Psychic Theft, Planeswalker's Mischief; "if you haven't played it", Grinning Totem; "any of those cards you didn't play", Three Wishes) asks about an action, not a zone. How is it checked?
- Decision: The condition is read as "if that card is still in exile" (the same object, CR 400.7), checked as the event occurs and as the ability resolves (CR 603.4). Casting or playing the card moves it out of exile; a card that left exile in any other way is a new object the instruction couldn't affect anyway (CR 603.7c).
- Alternatives: Track whether that player cast or played that very card: identical results, since a card cast or played is no longer in exile, and a card no longer in exile can't be returned; the only difference would be the ability triggering (and doing nothing) for a card that left exile without being played.
- Why: The outcome is the same in every case; the zone check needs no history of who played what.
- Affects: Psychic Theft, Planeswalker's Mischief, Grinning Totem, Three Wishes, Elkin Lair ("if the player hasn't played the card").
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/delayed_grammar_2.rs` (`delayed_if_still_exiled`, `delayed_cards_you_didnt_play`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/td_delayed_conditions.rs` (`psychic_theft_cast_before_the_end_step_doesnt_trigger`).

## until-its-controllers-next-step: "Until its controller's next untap step" follows the object's current controller
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 611.2a "A continuous effect generated by the resolution of a spell or ability lasts as long as stated by the spell or ability creating it (such as “until end of turn”). ..." / 500.4 "As a step or phase begins, if there are effects that last until that step or phase, those effects expire."
- Question: "Target land becomes a Swamp until its controller's next untap step." (Orcish Farmer) names the untap step of the land's controller. If control of the land changes before then, whose untap step ends the effect?
- Decision: The effect stops applying to an object as an untap step begins for a player who controls that object at that time (for an effect on several objects, each one separately). Without a control change this is the untap step of the player who controlled it as the effect began.
- Alternatives: Fix the player as the effect begins: a land that changed controllers would stay a Swamp through its new controller's untap step, until the old controller's.
- Why: "its controller" in a duration is read when the duration is checked, as "during its controller's next untap step" is (CR 502.3: the untap step untaps the permanents the active player controls).
- Affects: Orcish Farmer.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/step_durations.rs`; `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/durations_5.rs`.
- Tests: `tests/cards/c4_durations.rs` (`orcish_farmer_swamp_lasts_until_the_lands_controllers_next_untap_step`).

## when-that-permanent-enters-as-it-is-created: "When that permanent enters" after the instruction that put it onto the battlefield triggers at once
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 603.12 "A resolving spell or ability may allow or instruct a player to take an action and create a triggered ability that triggers “when [a player] [does or doesn’t]” take that action or “when [something happens] this way.” These reflexive triggered abilities follow the rules for delayed triggered abilities (see rule 603.7), except that they’re checked immediately after being created and trigger based on whether the trigger event or events occurred earlier during the resolution of the spell or ability that created them." / 603.7a "Delayed triggered abilities are created during the resolution of spells or abilities, ... A delayed triggered ability won’t trigger until it has actually been created, even if its trigger event occurred just beforehand. ..." / 400.7 "An object that moves from one zone to another becomes a new object with no memory of, or relation to, its previous existence. ..." / Jailbreak ruling (2022-04-29): "You don't choose a target from your own graveyard until after the permanent card from your opponent's graveyard has entered the battlefield. If that card doesn't enter the battlefield for some reason, you will not be able to target and return a card from your own graveyard."
- Question: Jailbreak returns a card to the battlefield and then says "When that permanent enters, return up to one target permanent card with equal or lesser mana value ...". Read as a delayed trigger, it would be created after the permanent entered and never trigger (CR 603.7a). How does it work?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 5. By CR 603.12, "When that permanent enters" after the instruction that put it onto the battlefield is a reflexive triggered ability: it is checked as it's created and triggers if the card entered the battlefield during the resolution; it's put on the stack the next time a player would receive priority, choosing its targets then, and "that permanent" is the new object on the battlefield. If the card didn't enter the battlefield (Grafdigger's Cage; a replacement such as Containment Priest's), nothing triggers, and the ability doesn't wait for a later entry.
- Alternatives: (1) A delayed trigger that never triggers (the literal reading of CR 603.7a, created after the permanent entered): rejected, since 603.12's "except" displaces 603.7a's timing for reflexive triggers, and it would empty the card's second sentence against the ruling's first. (2) A delayed trigger that waits for the next time that card enters (603.7b), so a later reanimation after the Cage leaves would let you return your own card turns later: rejected, the ruling's second sentence says you "will not" be able to, not "not yet".
- Why: Conclusion A (reconciled: the ruling applies the CR), confidence high (C at most, never D). 603.12 has said "allow or instruct" and "or “when [something happens] this way.”" since the CR of 2019-01-25 (the 2018-04-27 CR had only "may allow a player to take an action ... “when [a player] [does or doesn’t]” take that action"; checked against archived CR texts), so a spell that instructs the return and creates "when that permanent enters" fits it. 603.12's quoted templates describe the shape of a trigger, not literal text (real reflexive triggers already vary: "When you do", "When you sacrifice a creature this way"), and "that permanent" has no referent but the object the instruction just returned, so its trigger event is the permanent entering as a result of this instruction, "this way" in substance. That last step is an inference (no CR rule says a demonstrative stands in for "this way"); the ruling, published with the card (Streets of New Capenna, 2022-04-29) under a CR that already had the "instruct ... this way" wording, confirms it by applying exactly 603.12's timing. Only Jailbreak has this wording (a scan of the Oracle texts). Decided by the user on Nov 5: accepted as reconciled (A).
- Affects: Jailbreak (and any "[put it onto the battlefield]. When that permanent/creature enters, ..." text).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/triggers_5.rs` (`when_that_permanent_enters`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/c4_effect_triggers.rs` (`jailbreak_returns_a_card_with_equal_or_lesser_mana_value_once_the_permanent_entered`, `jailbreak_returns_nothing_of_yours_if_the_opponents_card_didnt_enter`).

## batch-they-condition-all: "If they [did something]" in a "one or more" trigger means all of them
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 603.2c "An ability triggers only once each time its trigger event occurs. However, it can trigger repeatedly if one event contains multiple occurrences. ..." / 603.4 "A triggered ability may read “When/Whenever/At [trigger event], if [condition], [effect].” ..."
- Question: Twilight Diviner: "Whenever one or more other creatures you control enter, if they entered or were cast from a graveyard, create a token that's a copy of one of them." When several creatures enter at once and only some of them came from a graveyard (or were cast from one), does the condition hold?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 4. "they" is the whole group: the condition holds only if each of the creatures entered from a graveyard or was cast from one (as "if they entered from a graveyard" already reads in the engine). "One of them" is then any of the creatures that entered. Wordings with "one or more of them" ("if one or more of them entered from a graveyard") are the some-of-them reading.
- Alternatives: At least one of them: then "one of them" could still copy a creature that didn't come from a graveyard, which the card's design (copying a reanimated creature) suggests isn't intended either.
- Why: The plural pronoun names the group, and the printed alternative "one or more of them" exists for the weaker reading. Creatures entering at once from different zones are rare.
- Affects: Twilight Diviner.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/history_conditions.rs` (`cast_status`), `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/referents_batches.rs` (`verb_one_of_them`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/c4_batch_referents.rs` (`twilight_diviner_copies_a_creature_that_entered_from_a_graveyard`).

## one-or-more-an-opponent-controls-per-opponent: "One or more creatures an opponent controls deal combat damage to you" triggers once for each opponent
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 603.2c "An ability triggers only once each time its trigger event occurs. However, it can trigger repeatedly if one event contains multiple occurrences." / Stronghold Arena ruling: "Normally, combat damage is dealt all at the same time. In that case, Stronghold Arena's last ability triggers once for each opponent that creatures you control dealt combat damage to, regardless of how many creatures were dealing that damage." (its trigger: "Whenever one or more creatures you control deal combat damage to a player") / 810.10 "Effects that cause players to get poison counters happen to each player individually."
- Question: Norn's Decree: "Whenever one or more creatures an opponent controls deal combat damage to you, that opponent gets a poison counter." When creatures of several opponents deal combat damage to you at once (shared team turns, CR 805.10a: both players of the attacking team attack), does it trigger once, or once for each of those opponents? And who is "that opponent"?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 4. Once for each opponent whose creatures dealt you combat damage, and "that opponent" is the controller of that trigger's creatures. Without shared team turns only the active player's creatures deal combat damage, so it triggers once and that player gets the counter.
- Alternatives: Trigger once per combat damage step, with each of those opponents getting a poison counter as it resolves: the same poison counters, but one trigger instead of several (it differs for abilities that copy, counter or count triggers).
- Why: The singular "an opponent" picks out one opponent per occurrence, as the singular "a player" of Stronghold Arena's trigger does, whose ruling makes it trigger once for each player dealt damage; and "that opponent" names one player.
- Affects: Norn's Decree.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/triggers.rs` (`parse_damage_verb`, `controlled_by_opponent`); `BatchPer::OtherController` in `crates/mtg-engine/src/triggers.rs` and `crates/mtg-engine/src/delayed_batches.rs`.
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_multiplayer.rs` (`norns_decree_triggers_once_for_each_opponent_whose_creatures_dealt_damage`).

## other-than-trigger-object: "Other [type]s" in the intervening "if" of a trigger about "a [type]" on a source without that type means other than the triggering object
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 603.4 "A triggered ability may read “When/Whenever/At [trigger event], if [condition], [effect].” When the trigger event occurs, the ability checks whether the stated condition is true. ... If the ability triggers, it checks the stated condition again as it resolves. ..." / Portcullis ruling: "You check the count of the number of creatures on the battlefield again during resolution."
- Question: Portcullis (an artifact) reads "Whenever a creature enters, if there are two or more other creatures on the battlefield, exile that creature." "Other" usually means other than the object with the ability, which can't be what an artifact counting creatures means. Other than what? And what if Portcullis is itself a creature (March of the Machines)?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 4. In the intervening "if" of an enters, leaves-the-battlefield or dies trigger about "a [type]" (not about the source), "other [type]s" means other than the triggering object when the source's printed type line lacks that type. The reading is fixed when the text is compiled: an animated Portcullis is one of the "other creatures" (it isn't the entering creature), so it counts.
- Alternatives: Read the source's current types: an animated Portcullis would then exclude itself (and, on the usual reading of "other", not the entering creature, so the entering creature would count). Exclude both the source and the triggering object while the source is a creature. Each changes only games where the source has become a creature.
- Why: The words "other creatures" are set against "a creature" (the one that entered); that meaning comes from the printed card and shouldn't flip when an effect changes the source's types.
- Affects: Portcullis; Lifeline ("if another creature is on the battlefield": the dying creature is no longer on the battlefield either way, so the outcome is the same); the same reading for subtypes: Valakut, the Molten Pinnacle and Roiling Canopy ("Whenever a Mountain you control enters, if you control at least five other Mountains", on a land that isn't a Mountain: the Mountains besides the one that entered, as Valakut's ruling says).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/other_than_trigger_object.rs` (`rebind_other`), called from `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/triggers.rs`.
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p039_creature_count.rs` (`portcullis_checks_the_creature_count_again_on_resolution`, `animated_portcullis_counts_itself_among_the_other_creatures`); `tests/rulings/r_p199_lands_and_legends.rs` (`valakut_counts_other_mountains_as_it_resolves`, `valakut_counts_mountains_entering_together`, `roiling_canopy_counts_forests_besides_the_one_that_entered`).


## choose-for-each-player-distinct-objects: Nihiloor's choices "for each opponent" are of different creatures
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: Nihiloor: "When Nihiloor enters, for each opponent, tap up to one untapped creature you control. When you do, gain control of target creature that player controls with power less than or equal to the tapped creature's power ..." / ruling: "As Nihiloor's first triggered ability resolves, you choose up to one creature for each opponent, and tap them all at the same time. Then, a separate triggered ability is put on the stack for each creature you tapped this way."
- Question: Since every choice is made before anything is tapped, may the same untapped creature be chosen for two opponents (and give two reflexive abilities)?
- Decision: No. The choices are made in turn order starting with the next opponent after you (CR 101.4b), and a creature already chosen for one opponent isn't offered for a later one; an answer naming it is invalid and falls back to choosing nothing for that opponent (`turn_order_choices::choose_for_each_player`). Each tapped creature gives one reflexive ability, about the opponent it was chosen for.
- Alternatives: Allow repeats: a creature tapped once would then stand for several opponents, against "up to one creature for each opponent" and "a separate triggered ability ... for each creature you tapped".
- Why: The instruction is to tap a creature for each opponent; one creature can be tapped only once, and the ruling counts the abilities by the creatures tapped.
- Affects: Nihiloor (any "for each opponent, tap up to one ... When you do, ... that player" text).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/turn_order_choices.rs` (`choose_for_each_player`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/players_5.rs` (`when_you_do_for_each_opponent`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/cards5_choices_players.rs` (`nihiloor_chooses_a_creature_for_only_one_opponent`).

## homicide-investigator-triggers-once: Homicide Investigator's "one or more ... die" ability triggers once (and once each turn) when it dies with other nontoken creatures
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: Homicide Investigator (Oracle; printed so in Murders at Karlov Manor, 2024-02-09, its only set): "Whenever one or more nontoken creatures you control die, investigate. This ability triggers only once each turn." / 603.2c "An ability triggers only once each time its trigger event occurs. However, it can trigger repeatedly if one event contains multiple occurrences." / 603.10a "Some zone-change triggers look back in time. These are leaves-the-battlefield abilities, ..." / 108.1 "Use the Oracle card reference when determining a card’s wording. ..." / Homicide Investigator rulings (2024-04-12): "Homicide Investigator's ability triggers when it dies if it's not a token." and "If Homicide Investigator dies at the same time as one or more other nontoken creatures you control, Homicide Investigator's ability triggers for each of them." / Midnight Reaper ("Whenever a nontoken creature you control dies, ...") ruling (2018-10-05): "If Midnight Reaper dies at the same time as one or more other nontoken creatures you control, Midnight Reaper's ability triggers for each of them." / G'raha Tia ruling (2025-06-06): "If G'raha Tia and one or more other creatures and/or artifacts you control die at the same time, its last ability will trigger once (as long as it hasn't already triggered this turn)." / Morbid Opportunist ruling (2024-11-08): "If Morbid Opportunist dies at the same time as one or more other creatures, Morbid Opportunist's ability still triggers."
- Question: The second 2024-04-12 ruling says the ability "triggers for each of them" when the Investigator dies with other nontoken creatures you control; the Oracle text batches the deaths ("one or more") and limits the ability to once each turn. The ruling departs from the card's text, not from the CR (the engine does what 603.2c and the Oracle text say), so this is an interpretation of which source to follow, not a deviation from the CR. Does the Investigator dying with two other nontoken creatures give one Clue or several?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 5. Under the policy `ruling-contradicting-oracle-gives-way` (the card's text wins over a ruling that contradicts it): one trigger per batch of deaths, and at most one each turn, as the Oracle text says; the Investigator dying alongside the others is part of the batch (it looks back, 603.10a). The second ruling gives way to the Oracle text: it is exempt (`docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P005.tsv`), not cited.
- Alternatives: Follow the ruling: one trigger per nontoken creature dying with the Investigator. That contradicts both sentences of the Oracle text; the second sentence alone ("This ability triggers only once each turn") forbids two triggers in one turn however the trigger condition is batched, so no reading of 603.2c or "one or more" rescues it. It would also contradict the G'raha Tia and Morbid Opportunist rulings on the same template and would need a manual override of a compilable block.
- Why: Conclusion D (unresolved under A/B/C: no reading reconciles the sources and no dated change makes either outdated), confidence high; recommendation: the Oracle text. Not A: the ruling names this card and its exact situation, so it isn't misattached data. Not B: the ruling is newer than the card's text, and 603.2c reads the same in the CR effective 2024-04-12 and today (603.2h, "Do this only once each turn", was added in the CR of 2025-11-14 and is a different template). Not C: the CR isn't silent and the card's own sentence is explicit. Evidence: the two 2024-04-12 rulings are word for word Midnight Reaper's Guilds of Ravnica release notes (2018-09-20; Scryfall dates them 2018-10-05) with the name swapped, written for a per-creature trigger, and in the same order; they are the only MKM rulings dated after 2024-02-02 (a post-release Gatherer update that also added rulings to older cards, e.g. Moonshae Pixie). The Oracle text is functionally unchanged since the first preview day: XMage (commit dated 2024-01-16) and Forge (2024-01-20) carry today's text, Gatherer's printed text (a magicthegathering.io capture that predates the 2024-04-12 rulings) is identical but for reminder text, the card has no reprint, and the Outlaws of Thunder Junction Update Bulletin's only Oracle change to older cards was the "next cast" templating (search snippets only; magic.wizards.com is outside this session's allowed hosts). Of the 29 "dies at the same time as one or more other ..." rulings in the 2026-09-24 snapshot, 27 say "for each", and all of them but this one belong to per-object triggers ("a/another creature dies"; e.g. Judith 2019-01-25, Totentanz 2023-09-01, MKM's own Vein Ripper and Merchant of Truth 2024-02-02); the other "one or more ... die" cards with such a ruling say the ability "still triggers" (Morbid Opportunist 2024-11-08, Great Fierce Bee 2026-06-29), and Éomer, Marshal of Rohan (2023-06-16, "one or more ... die ... only once each turn") and G'raha Tia (2025-06-06, "will trigger once") answer the same question the other way. The ruling has never been withdrawn (Gatherer still shows it, per search snippets). Precedent for setting such a ruling aside: Gnoll War Band's ruling is exempt as "Inconsistent with the other rulings on the same question" (`docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P186.tsv`, register `history-counts-players-who-left`), on weaker evidence (a question the CR left open). The question for the user, together with `rimescale-dragon-creatures-only`, was whether a Wizards ruling whose words contradict the card's own Oracle text, with no rules or Oracle change to explain it, may be set aside for the Oracle text (CR 108.1). Decided by the user on Nov 5: yes (register `ruling-contradicting-oracle-gives-way`). The ruling is exempt in `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P005.tsv` (needle "Homicide Investigator's ability triggers for each of them") and logged as a template error in `docs/RULING_DISCREPANCIES.md` for review and reporting to Wizards of the Coast.
- Affects: Homicide Investigator.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/triggers.rs` (`check_batch_triggers`, the `once_per_turn` check).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p005_dies.rs` (`homicide_investigator_investigates_once_for_creatures_dying_together`).

## reflexive-per-counters-put: "Put N counters on [one object]. When you do, ..." triggers once for every N counters put
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 603.12a "Normally, if the trigger event or events occur multiple times during the resolution of the spell or ability that created it, the reflexive triggered ability will trigger once for each of those times. ..." / 614.1a "Effects that use the word “instead” are replacement effects. ..." / Craig Boone, Novac Guard ruling (2024-06-07): "In the rare case where more than two quest counters are placed on Craig Boone because of the effect of its last ability (for example, because of the effect of Doubling Season), the reflexive triggered ability will trigger once for every two quest counters placed on Craig Boone this way." / Etched Slith ruling: "In a case where you put more than one +1/+1 counter on Etched Slith with its last ability (for example, because of the effect of Doubling Season), the reflexive ability will trigger once for each +1/+1 counter you put on it this way."
- Question: A replacement effect (Doubling Season, Hardened Scales) makes "put N counters on [an object]" put more than N. How many times does the reflexive "When you do" after it trigger? The CR counts occurrences of the trigger event but doesn't say whether one instruction putting 2N counters is the event "put N counters" happening twice. And when a replacement makes it put fewer than N but at least one, did you "do" it?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 5. Following the Craig Boone (N = 2) and Etched Slith (N = 1) rulings and applying them to the template: when the instruction put counters on one object, the reflexive ability triggers once for every N counters put, rounded down (Hardened Scales making two counters three is two put once), and once if at least one but fewer than N were put. None put: it doesn't trigger. An instruction putting counters on several objects triggers it once (no card has that form).
- Alternatives: (a) Trigger once per instruction whatever the number put (603.12a read as one event per instruction; the engine's behavior before) — the two rulings rule it out for their cards, and they reason from the template, not the card. (b) For fewer than N put (but some), not trigger at all ("put two counters" didn't happen) — no ruling covers this case (an opponent's Vorinclex, Monstrous Raider halving Craig Boone's two quest counters to one), and this is why the entry was provisional. (c) Count only by the card's own ruling (Craig Boone, Etched Slith) and trigger once for the other cards (Auron, Venerated Guardian; Lockjaw, Slobbering Teleporter; Wishing Well; Red Hulk; Earthbender Ascension; Dimension X Pizzasaur), which have no ruling on it — a card-specific reading of two rulings that agree with each other.
- Why: The rulings give the reasoning (the event is "N counters were put", so kN counters is k of them), the same for every N and every card with the template; Ajani, Nacatl Avenger's ruling counts the same way for tokens created ("once for each Cat Warrior you made this way").
- Affects: Craig Boone, Novac Guard; Etched Slith; and with a counter-adding replacement effect, the other cards named above.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/resolve.rs` (`Effect::AddCounters` sets `Ctx::reflexive_times`; `Effect::Reflexive` uses it up and triggers that many times; `Game::exec` clears it for other instructions); `crates/mtg-engine/src/eval.rs` (`Ctx::reflexive_times`).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p050_timing.rs` (`craig_boone_reflexive_trigger_for_every_two_quest_counters`, `etched_slith_reflexive_trigger_once_for_each_counter_put`, `red_hulk_reflexive_trigger_once_for_every_counter_put`, `reflexive_count_per_n_counters_rounds_down_and_needs_a_counter_put`).

## loses-the-game-trigger-leaves-with-the-loser: A "loses the game" ability of an object that leaves the game with the loser doesn't trigger
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 603.10 "Normally, objects that exist immediately after an event are checked to see if the event matched any trigger conditions, and continuous effects that exist at that time are used to determine what the trigger conditions are and what the objects involved in the event look like. However, some triggered abilities are exceptions to this rule; the game “looks back in time” to determine if those abilities trigger, using the existence of those abilities and the appearance of objects immediately prior to the event. The list of exceptions is as follows:" / 603.10f "Abilities that trigger when a player loses the game look back in time." / 800.4a "When a player leaves the game, all objects (see rule 109) owned by that player leave the game and any effects which give that player control of any objects or players end. Then, if that player controlled any objects on the stack not represented by cards, those objects cease to exist. Then, if there are any objects still controlled by that player, those objects are exiled. This is not a state-based action. It happens as soon as the player leaves the game. If the player who left the game had priority at the time they left, priority passes to the next player in turn order who’s still in the game." / Share the Spoils ruling (2021-07-23): "If you control an opponent's Share the Spoils and that player loses the game, Share the Spoils leaves the game with them and the first ability won't trigger." / Thieving Amalgam ruling (2019-08-23): "If a creature you control but don't own dies at the same time that its owner leaves the game, Thieving Amalgam's last ability triggers. No player loses 2 life, but you gain 2 life." / Grismold, the Dreadsower ruling (2019-08-23): "In a multiplayer game, if a creature token dies at the same time its controller loses the game, Grismold's last ability triggers." / Sanctum of Serra ruling (2009-10-01): "If you planeswalk away from Sanctum of Serra because the player who owns Sanctum of Serra leaves the game, its first ability will still trigger and resolve."
- Question: You control an opponent's Share the Spoils ("When this enchantment enters and whenever an opponent loses the game, exile the top card of each player's library.") and that opponent loses the game. Its ability looks back in time (603.10f): immediately before the loss the enchantment was on the battlefield under your control, so by 603.10 it would trigger, under your control (603.3a), although the enchantment leaves the game with its owner (800.4a). Its ruling says it won't trigger. Does the look-back of 603.10f reach an ability whose own source leaves the game with the loser?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 10: "D1 doesn't seem inconsistent - Thieving Amalgam and Grismold the Dreadsower are permanents YOU own, trigger from something happening to a creature your opponent owns as they leave the game. Whereas with Share the Spoils, the permanent WITH the trigger is the one that is leaving the game. We should take the ruling here." The engine follows the ruling, as narrowly as the user's distinction: a "loses the game" ability (a look-back trigger on the loss, 603.10f) whose source is owned by the losing player, so that the leave-the-game procedure of 800.4a removes it, doesn't trigger on that player's loss. Everything else stays as 603.10f says: the loss is checked before the loser's objects leave, so an object that stays in the game and watches the loser's things keeps triggering (Thieving Amalgam, Grismold, the God-Eternals, "whenever a player loses the game" abilities of permanents the loser merely controlled, whose control effect ends with the loss), and an object the loser owns in the ante zone, which stays (800.4n), or in a game that ends with the loss (104.2a: nothing leaves) isn't concerned. The case is found through the look-back check (`looks_back`), never by card name.
- Alternatives: Follow 603.10f to the letter (the engine's reading until Nov 10, recorded in `docs/RULING_DISCREPANCIES.md` as an inconsistent ruling): the ability triggers under its controller's control and exiles the top card of each library of a player still in the game, cards no one may then play since the enchantment is gone. A reading by analogy with the object's own leaves-the-battlefield abilities (603.10a) and with Sanctum of Serra, whose ruling has a plane that leaves with its owner still trigger: that is the nearest ruling the other way, but it is about a different event (planeswalking away, 603.10g) and predates 603.10f (the CR effective 2016-11-11 split the look-back list out of 603.6d into 603.10 and added "abilities that trigger when a player loses the game").
- Why: The user judged the rulings consistent once the object carrying the trigger is told apart from an object watching another player's things: the rulings that say "triggers" (Thieving Amalgam, Grismold) are all about a permanent you own that sees something happen to a creature its owner takes with them; the one object that goes with the loser, Share the Spoils, has the ruling that it doesn't trigger. The ruling is dated 2021-07-23, well after 603.10f, names the card and matches its release notes, so it is the current word on the card.
- Affects: Share the Spoils (the only card whose ability triggers on an opponent losing the game), and any "loses the game" trigger on a permanent controlled by a player other than its owner when the owner loses; also, were it the losing player's own, a trigger that player would control is never put on the stack anyway (800.4d).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/leaves_with_loser.rs` (`suppressed`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/triggers.rs` (`looks_back`: `TriggerCond::PlayerLoses` on `Event::PlayerLost`; `detect_triggers`, where the check is applied).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p068_players.rs` (`share_the_spoils_borrowed_from_a_player_who_loses_doesnt_trigger`); `tests/cr/r600_look_back.rs` (`loses_the_game_abilities_look_back_unless_their_source_leaves_with_the_loser`).

## ability-triggered-cause-is-the-event: "Whenever [an event] causes a triggered ability to trigger" counts every ability that event made trigger, whatever its wording
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: provisional
- Rules: 603.2 "Whenever a game event or game state matches a triggered ability’s trigger event, that ability automatically triggers. The ability doesn’t do anything at this point." / 603.6a "Enters-the-battlefield abilities trigger when a permanent enters the battlefield. These are written, “When [this object] enters, . . . “ or “Whenever a [type] enters, . . .” ..." / 603.12 "A resolving spell or ability may allow or instruct a player to take an action and create a triggered ability that triggers “when [a player] [does or doesn’t]” take that action or “when [something happens] this way.” ..." / Strict Proctor ruling (2021-04-16): "Triggered abilities use the word “when,” “whenever,” or “at.” They’re often written as “[Trigger condition], [effect].”" / rulings of the same template on other cards: Elesh Norn, Mother of Machines (2023-02-04): "Elesh Norn's second and third ability each affects a permanent's own enters-the-battlefield triggered abilities as well as other triggered abilities that trigger when that permanent enters the battlefield. Such triggered abilities start with "when" or "whenever." ..."; Torpor Orb (2024-04-12): "Torpor Orb stops a creature's own enters-the-battlefield triggered abilities as well as other triggered abilities that would trigger when a creature enters the battlefield."; Hushbringer (2019-10-04): "An ability that triggers when a card is put into a graveyard "from anywhere" may still trigger even if a creature dies. It will trigger if the ability is of a dying creature that triggers only when that creature is put into a graveyard from anywhere, if Hushbringer is the card being put into a graveyard, or if Hushbringer leaves the battlefield at the same time as the dying creature."
- Question: Strict Proctor ("Whenever a permanent entering causes a triggered ability to trigger, counter that ability unless its controller pays {2}"), Aboleth Spawn ("a creature entering under an opponent's control causes a triggered ability of that creature to trigger") and Firebender Ascension ("a creature you control attacking causes ...") name an event, not a wording. The CR doesn't define "causes a triggered ability to trigger". Which abilities count: only those whose trigger condition is that event alone ("When this creature enters"), or every ability the event made trigger, also "When this enters or attacks", "When this enters, if ...", "When this creature enters from a graveyard", and abilities that aren't enters abilities but trigger on the same zone change ("Whenever one or more creature cards leave your graveyard" when a card is put onto the battlefield from a graveyard)? And a reflexive ability created as an enters ability resolves?
- Decision: The event. Every triggered ability that an event of the named kind made trigger counts, whatever the wording of its trigger condition; an ability with several trigger events counts only when the event of that kind made it trigger (Sun Titan's "enters or attacks" ability triggering on its attack isn't caused by a permanent entering). A card put onto the battlefield from a graveyard leaves the graveyard as it enters (one zone change), so Desecrated Tomb's ability triggered by that is caused by a permanent entering. The object the cause names is the permanent that entered (not the card it was in its previous zone), the attacking creature, or a permanent as it last existed on the battlefield. A reflexive triggered ability triggers on the action its resolving ability took (603.12), never on the event that made that ability trigger: Intrepid Adversary's "When you pay this cost one or more times" isn't caused by the Adversary entering. Limitation: reflexive abilities record no event, so Jailbreak's reflexive "When that permanent enters" (`when-that-permanent-enters-as-it-is-created`), which a permanent entering does cause to trigger, isn't counted; Jailbreak is the only card with that wording, and counting it needs the reflexive ability to record what its trigger event was.
- Alternatives: (1) The wording: only an ability whose whole trigger condition is the event, as the engine did before Nov 11 (it compared the kind of the other ability's trigger condition): "enters or attacks", "enters or is put into a graveyard" and "enters, if ..." abilities never counted, against 603.6a and the template's rulings ("other triggered abilities that trigger when that permanent enters"). (2) The event, but only for enters-the-battlefield abilities (603.6a): Strict Proctor wouldn't counter Desecrated Tomb's ability on a reanimated creature. A judge could rule so, reading "leaves your graveyard" as its own trigger event; but Hushbringer's ruling counts a "from anywhere" ability among the abilities a creature dying causes to trigger, which is the event reading, and the engine's other effects of this template (Torpor Orb, Hushbringer, Elesh Norn, Panharmonicon, Teysa Karlov: `kw::torpor`, `Game::additional_triggers`) already judge the event.
- Why: 603.2 makes an ability trigger when an event matches its trigger event; "a permanent entering causes it to trigger" names the event that matched. The template's rulings apply it to "other triggered abilities that trigger when that permanent enters", not to a wording, and Hushbringer's to a "from anywhere" ability. Confidence is high for the enters abilities of any wording and for reflexive abilities; the non-enters abilities on the same zone change are provisional because no ruling of the three cards addresses them.
- Affects: Strict Proctor, Aboleth Spawn, Firebender Ascension (the three cards with this trigger), with any ability they see: "enters or ..." abilities (Ichor Wellspring, Sun Titan), "enters, if ..." (Garruk's Uprising), "enters from a graveyard" (Triarch Praetorian), "leaves your graveyard" on a reanimation (Desecrated Tomb), reflexive abilities of enters abilities (Intrepid Adversary, Tranquil Frillback).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/triggers.rs` (the `TriggerCond::AbilityTriggered` arm of `trigger_matches_ctx`; `tag_triggering_event`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/events.rs` (`TriggeringEvent`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/resolve.rs` (`Effect::Reflexive` clears the parent's event kind).
- Tests: `tests/cr/r603_audit_stack.rs` (`an_ability_triggering_on_enters_abilities_sees_every_wording_of_them`, `an_attack_trigger_of_an_enters_or_attacks_ability_isnt_an_entering_trigger`, `an_enters_from_a_graveyard_ability_is_caused_by_the_permanent_entering`); `tests/cr/r603_audit_reflexive.rs` (`a_reflexive_ability_of_an_enters_ability_isnt_caused_by_the_permanent_entering`).

## reflexive-trigger-per-specified-token: "Create [tokens]. When you do, ..." triggers once for each time the specified tokens were created, and not for tokens created instead
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: provisional
- Rules: 603.12a "Normally, if the trigger event or events occur multiple times during the resolution of the spell or ability that created it, the reflexive triggered ability will trigger once for each of those times. ..." / Ajani, Nacatl Avenger ruling (2024-06-07): "In a case where you create more than one Cat Warrior creature token with Ajani, Nacatl Avenger's second ability (for example, because of the effect of Doubling Season), the reflexive ability will trigger once for each Cat Warrior you made this way. If a replacement effect causes you to create different tokens instead (for example, the effect of Divine Visitation), the creation of those tokens won't cause the reflexive ability to trigger." / Craig Boone, Novac Guard ruling (2024-06-07): "In the rare case where more than two quest counters are placed on Craig Boone because of the effect of its last ability (for example, because of the effect of Doubling Season), the reflexive triggered ability will trigger once for every two quest counters placed on Craig Boone this way."
- Question: After "Create [N tokens]. When you do, ...", how many times does the reflexive ability trigger when a replacement effect changes how many tokens are created, or creates other tokens instead?
- Decision: The tokens "as specified" are the created tokens whose copiable name, card types and subtypes are those the instruction specifies (a replacement that creates other tokens, Divine Visitation's Angels, makes none). If any were created, "When you do" happened; it triggers (number created as specified) / N times, as Craig Boone's ruling counts "two quest counters" put twice. For "create a token" (Ajani) that is once per token, as the ruling says; the provisional part is N of 2 or more ("Create two 1/1 blue Faerie creature tokens. When you do, ..." with Doubling Season: four tokens trigger it twice).
- Alternatives: For N of 2 or more, trigger once however many are created (creating four is one event that includes creating two): a judge could rule this way, since Craig Boone's ruling is about counters.
- Why: Ajani's ruling counts each creation of the specified token as an occurrence of the trigger event (603.12a); Craig Boone's ruling applies the same counting to an instruction with a number, so the same is done for tokens.
- Affects: Ajani, Nacatl Avenger; Faebloom Trick, Princess Snowfall, Eastfarthing Farmer, Forum Filibuster, Generous Plunderer and the other "create ... token(s). When you do" cards, with Doubling Season-like effects or token replacement effects.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/resolve.rs` (`Effect::CreateToken`: `prev_happened` and `reflexive_times`).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p138_misc.rs` (`ajani_reflexive_trigger_once_per_cat_warrior_created`).
