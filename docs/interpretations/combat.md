## combat-tax-of-zero-is-a-cost: A tax to attack or block that comes to {0} is still a cost
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 118.5 "Some costs are represented by {0}, or are reduced to {0}. The action necessary for a player to pay such a cost is the player’s acknowledgment that they are paying it. Even though such a cost requires no resources, it’s not automatically paid." / 508.1d "... If a creature can’t attack unless a player pays a cost, that player is not required to pay that cost, even if attacking with that creature would increase the number of requirements being obeyed. ..." / 508.1h "If any of the chosen creatures require paying costs to attack, or if any optional costs to attack were chosen, the active player determines the total cost to attack. ... Once the total cost is determined, it becomes “locked in.” ..." / 509.1c "... If a creature can’t block unless a player pays a cost, that player is not required to pay that cost, even if blocking with that creature would increase the number of requirements being obeyed. ..." / 509.1d "If any of the chosen creatures require paying costs to block, the defending player determines the total cost to block. ..." / War Tax ruling: "You can choose X=0. This doesn’t have an effect most of the time, but it does mean that creatures can’t attack unless the controller chooses to pay the cost (a cost of zero is not automatically paid, so the controller can choose to not pay)." / War Cadence ruling: "You can choose X=0. This doesn’t have an effect most of the time, but it does mean that creatures can’t block unless the controller chooses to pay the cost (a cost of zero is not automatically paid). You can use this to make your creatures unable to block an attacker."
- Question: A tax to attack or block can come to {0}: a chosen X = 0 (War Tax, War Cadence), a written {0}, or a scaled amount whose multiplier is 0 ("pays {X} for each creature they control that's attacking you, where X is the number of basic land types among lands you control" with no basic land types, "{1} for each +1/+1 counter on it" with no counters, "{1} for each card in your hand" with an empty hand). Is a creature under such a tax one that "can't attack unless a player pays a cost"?
- Decision: Yes, always. Decided by the user, Oct 21: follow 118.5. Every applicable tax to attack or block is a cost, whatever it comes to. A creature under one isn't forced to attack or block by requirements such as "attacks each combat if able" or Lure (508.1d, 509.1c), and a player who attacks or blocks with it acknowledges paying {0} (508.1h, 509.1d): declaring the attack or block is that acknowledgment, and when another player chose the attack or block (Master Warcraft, Odric, Master Tactician) the paying player is asked to pay the {0} and may decline.
- Alternatives: Treat a scaled tax that comes to nothing as no cost (the engine's earlier provisional reading, `combat-tax-scaled-to-nothing`): requirements would force the creature, and no acknowledgment would be asked. Or treat every {0} as no cost: contradicts both rulings.
- Why: 118.5 covers costs "represented by {0}, or ... reduced to {0}" without exception, and the War Tax and War Cadence rulings apply it to combat taxes. No rule or ruling treats a scaled cost that comes to {0} differently from a written one.
- Affects: War Tax, War Cadence (X = 0); Collective Restraint (no basic land types); Myr Prototype and Phyrexian Marauder (no +1/+1 counters); Cowed by Wisdom (its controller's hand empty); any written {0} tax. Taxes "for each of those creatures" (Ghostly Prison, Propaganda, Windborn Muse, Sphere of Safety, Norn's Annex, Elephant Grass, Archangel of Tithes, ...) never come to {0} for an attacker, so they behave as before.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/combat.rs` (`required_attack_cost`, `required_block_cost`, `block_cost_for`: any applicable tax is a cost; `attack_requires_cost`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/attack_choice.rs` (`declaration_chosen_by`: asks to pay a {0} cost too); `crates/mtg-engine/src/block_choice.rs` (`costs_accepted`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/resolve.rs` (X = 0 locks in {0}).
- Tests: `tests/cards/combat2_referents.rs` (`war_tax_with_x_zero_makes_attacking_optional`, `war_cadence_with_x_zero_makes_blocking_optional`); `tests/cr/interp_engine_policies.rs` (`a_scaled_attack_tax_that_comes_to_nothing_is_still_a_cost`); `tests/cr/r118_005_zero_combat_taxes.rs` (`an_attack_tax_of_zero_is_a_cost_however_it_comes_to_zero`, `a_creature_with_a_counter_scaled_tax_and_no_counters_has_a_zero_cost`, `cowed_by_wisdom_with_an_empty_hand_still_taxes_attacks`, `attacking_with_a_creature_taxed_zero_means_acknowledging_paying_zero`, `blocking_with_a_creature_taxed_zero_means_acknowledging_paying_zero`); `tests/cards/conditions_state_unless_states.rs` (`myr_prototype_pays_for_each_counter_on_it`).

## trample-reassignment-counts-only-assigned-damage: After an illegal total assignment, damage is assigned again counting only damage already assigned
- Kind: deviation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 702.19b "... When checking for assigned lethal damage, take into account damage already marked on the creature and damage from other creatures that’s being assigned during the same combat damage step, but not any abilities or effects that might change the amount of damage that’s actually dealt. ..." / 510.1e "Once a player has assigned combat damage from each attacking or blocking creature they control, the total damage assignment (not solely the damage assignment of any individual attacking or blocking creature) is checked to see if it complies with the above rules. If it doesn’t, the combat damage assignment is illegal; the game returns to the moment before that player began to assign combat damage. (See rule 733, “Handling Illegal Actions.”)"
- Question: A trampler may assign damage beyond its blockers counting on damage the attackers assigning after it will assign to them (each attacker's damage is asked for in turn, so the engine offers the lower minimums `lethal_counting_later`). If the total assignment then doesn't assign lethal damage to those blockers, it's illegal and the player assigns again. May the player count on later attackers again the second time?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Oct 28. No. The engine asks for every attacker's damage again, offering only the minimums counting damage already assigned (and marked), which can't produce an illegal total; an answer that doesn't meet them gets the default assignment. The whole assignment is redone (510.1e: the game returns to the moment before the player began to assign), including attackers whose first answers were legal on their own.
- Alternatives: Offer the lower minimums again and repeat until the total is legal: exact, but an agent could loop forever; or ask the player for the whole assignment at once (one decision for all attackers): exact, but a different decision shape.
- Why: Accepted, with no plan to change it: the retry always ends, and the player keeps every legal total that doesn't rely on later attackers; a player who relied on them and was wrong rarely loses anything a careful second assignment would give.
- Affects: two or more attackers with trample (or trample over planeswalkers) whose blockers or attacked planeswalker are shared, e.g. through banding or several attackers on one planeswalker; count not measured.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/combat.rs` (`combat_damage_step`, `later_capacity`, `lethal_assigned`, `assign_trample_damage`); `crates/mtg-api/src/request.rs` (`check_damage`).
- Tests: `tests/keywords/k702_019_trample_counts_damage_assigned_after_it.rs` (`a_total_assignment_without_lethal_damage_is_illegal_and_assigned_again`).

## that-excess-damage-this-turn: "that excess damage" after "was dealt excess damage this turn" is the turn's total
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: provisional
- Rules: 120.10 "Excess damage has been dealt to a creature if the damage dealt to it is greater than lethal damage. ..." / 608.2c "The controller of the spell or ability follows its instructions in the order written. ..."
- Question: Overclocked Electromancer: "Whenever this creature deals combat damage to a creature, if that creature was dealt excess damage this turn, you get X {E}, where X is that excess damage." Is X the excess damage of this combat damage, or all the excess damage the creature was dealt this turn?
- Decision: All the excess damage that creature was dealt this turn (the excess damage the condition asks about), counted as the ability resolves.
- Alternatives: Only the excess of the combat damage event that triggered the ability: the same number unless the creature was also dealt excess damage earlier in the turn (by another source, or in a first-strike damage step).
- Why: "that excess damage" refers back to the phrase "was dealt excess damage this turn", which isn't limited to this event.
- Affects: Overclocked Electromancer.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/referents_values_5.rs` (`atom`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/c4_instead_excess.rs` (`overclocked_electromancer_gets_energy_equal_to_the_excess_damage`).

## put-onto-battlefield-blocking-retriggers-becomes-blocked-by: A creature put onto the battlefield blocking triggers "becomes blocked by a creature" again
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: provisional
- Rules: 509.3d "An ability that reads “Whenever [a creature] becomes blocked by a creature, . . .” triggers once for each creature that blocks the specified creature. ... In addition, it will trigger if a creature is put onto the battlefield blocking that creature. ..." / 509.4 "... A creature put onto the battlefield this way is “blocking” but, for the purposes of trigger events and effects, it never “blocked.”" / Aetherplasm ruling (2005): "The creature you put onto the battlefield may be the Aetherplasm you just returned to your hand. This allows it to dodge abilities like Deathgazer's."
- Question: Deathgazer: "Whenever this creature blocks or becomes blocked by a nonblack creature, destroy that creature at end of combat." Aetherplasm (blue) blocks it, returns itself to hand and is put back onto the battlefield blocking Deathgazer. The old trigger's "that creature" is the old object (CR 400.7), but does the new Aetherplasm trigger Deathgazer again?
- Decision: Yes. The engine follows CR 509.3d: Deathgazer becomes blocked by the new Aetherplasm, its ability triggers again, and the new Aetherplasm is destroyed at end of combat. Aetherplasm dodges only the abilities that already triggered for the old object.
- Alternatives: Follow the ruling literally (the new blocker doesn't trigger "becomes blocked by", so Aetherplasm survives): what the 2005 ruling says, but it contradicts the current text of 509.3d, which names this case. The ruling is listed as superseded in `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P006.tsv` rather than cited, since the engine contradicts its second sentence.
- Why: The CR is newer than the ruling and explicit; 509.4's "never blocked" is about the blocking creature's own "blocks" triggers, which don't trigger (tested with Wall of Junk).
- Affects: Aetherplasm with Deathgazer and other "becomes blocked by a creature" abilities; any effect putting a creature onto the battlefield blocking.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/combat.rs` (`combat_trigger_matches`, `TriggerCond::BlockedByCreature`).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p006_activated_bounce.rs` (`aetherplasm_can_put_itself_back_but_deathgazer_sees_the_new_blocker`).

## your-last-combat: "during your last combat" is during the combats of your last turn
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: provisional
- Rules: 500.1 "A turn consists of five phases, in this order: beginning, precombat main, combat, postcombat main, and ending. ..." / 506.1 "The combat phase has five steps, ..." / Territorial Hellkite ruling: "If Territorial Hellkite didn’t attack during your last combat, most likely because it was tapped or you didn’t control it at that time, then any opponent can be chosen at random."
- Question: "choose an opponent at random that ~ didn't attack during your last combat": which combat is "your last combat" when your last turn had several combat phases, or none?
- Decision: The engine looks at every combat of your latest turn before this one (the history window "during your last turn"): an opponent the creature attacked in any of them is excluded. With no earlier turn of yours, nothing is excluded.
- Alternatives: Only the last combat phase of that turn (exact with extra combats, but the engine keeps the turn's events, not per-combat boundaries of past turns); or the latest combat phase of any of your turns, skipping turns without one.
- Why: Turns with several combat phases or none are rare, and the reading agrees with the card for every turn with exactly one combat phase.
- Affects: Territorial Hellkite.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/history_5.rs` (`choose_opponent_not_attacked`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/c5_history.rs` (`territorial_hellkite_attacks_an_opponent_it_didnt_attack_last_combat`, `territorial_hellkite_taps_if_no_opponent_can_be_chosen`).
