## combat-tax-of-zero-is-a-cost: A tax to attack or block that comes to {0} is still a cost
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 118.5 "Some costs are represented by {0}, or are reduced to {0}. The action necessary for a player to pay such a cost is the player’s acknowledgment that they are paying it. Even though such a cost requires no resources, it’s not automatically paid." / 508.1d "... If a creature can’t attack unless a player pays a cost, that player is not required to pay that cost, even if attacking with that creature would increase the number of requirements being obeyed. ..." / 508.1h "If any of the chosen creatures require paying costs to attack, or if any optional costs to attack were chosen, the active player determines the total cost to attack. ... Once the total cost is determined, it becomes “locked in.” ..." / 509.1c "... If a creature can’t block unless a player pays a cost, that player is not required to pay that cost, even if blocking with that creature would increase the number of requirements being obeyed. ..." / 509.1d "If any of the chosen creatures require paying costs to block, the defending player determines the total cost to block. ..." / War Tax ruling: "You can choose X=0. This doesn’t have an effect most of the time, but it does mean that creatures can’t attack unless the controller chooses to pay the cost (a cost of zero is not automatically paid, so the controller can choose to not pay)." / War Cadence ruling: "You can choose X=0. This doesn’t have an effect most of the time, but it does mean that creatures can’t block unless the controller chooses to pay the cost (a cost of zero is not automatically paid). You can use this to make your creatures unable to block an attacker."
- Question: A tax to attack or block can come to {0}: a chosen X = 0 (War Tax, War Cadence), a written {0}, or a scaled amount whose multiplier is 0 ("pays {X} for each creature they control that's attacking you, where X is the number of basic land types among lands you control" with no basic land types, "{1} for each +1/+1 counter on it" with no counters, "{1} for each card in your hand" with an empty hand). Is a creature under such a tax one that "can't attack unless a player pays a cost"?
- Decision: Yes, always. Decided by the user, Oct 21: follow 118.5. Every applicable tax to attack or block is a cost, whatever it comes to. A creature under one isn't forced to attack or block by requirements such as "attacks each combat if able" or Lure (508.1d, 509.1c), and a player who attacks or blocks with it acknowledges paying {0} (508.1h, 509.1d): declaring the attack or block is that acknowledgment, and when another player chose the attack or block (Master Warcraft, Odric, Master Tactician) the paying player is asked to pay the {0} and may decline.
- Alternatives: Treat a scaled tax that comes to nothing as no cost (the engine's earlier provisional reading, `combat-tax-scaled-to-nothing`): requirements would force the creature, and no acknowledgment would be asked. Or treat every {0} as no cost: contradicts both rulings.
- Why: 118.5 covers costs "represented by {0}, or ... reduced to {0}" without exception, and the War Tax and War Cadence rulings apply it to combat taxes. No rule or ruling treats a scaled cost that comes to {0} differently from a written one.
- Affects: War Tax, War Cadence (X = 0); Collective Restraint (no basic land types); Myr Prototype and Phyrexian Marauder (no +1/+1 counters); Cowed by Wisdom (its controller's hand empty); any written {0} tax. Taxes "for each of those creatures" (Ghostly Prison, Propaganda, Windborn Muse, Sphere of Safety, Norn's Annex, Elephant Grass, Archangel of Tithes, ...) never come to {0} for an attacker, so they behave as before.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/combat.rs` (`required_attack_cost`, `required_block_cost`, `block_cost_for`: any applicable tax is a cost; `attack_requires_cost`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/attack_choice.rs` (`declaration_chosen_by`: asks to pay a {0} cost too); `crates/mtg-engine/src/block_choice.rs` (`costs_accepted`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/resolve.rs` (X = 0 locks in {0}).
- Tests: `tests/cards/combat2_referents.rs` (`war_tax_with_x_zero_makes_attacking_optional`, `war_cadence_with_x_zero_makes_blocking_optional`); `tests/cr/interp_engine_policies.rs` (`a_scaled_attack_tax_that_comes_to_nothing_is_still_a_cost`); `tests/cr/r118_005_zero_combat_taxes.rs` (`an_attack_tax_of_zero_is_a_cost_however_it_comes_to_zero`, `a_creature_with_a_counter_scaled_tax_and_no_counters_has_a_zero_cost`, `cowed_by_wisdom_with_an_empty_hand_still_taxes_attacks`, `attacking_with_a_creature_taxed_zero_means_acknowledging_paying_zero`, `blocking_with_a_creature_taxed_zero_means_acknowledging_paying_zero`); `tests/cards/conditions_state_unless_states.rs` (`myr_prototype_pays_for_each_counter_on_it`); `tests/rulings/r_p054_combat.rs` (`blocking_requirements_skip_tapped_creatures_and_creatures_with_a_blocking_cost`).

## attack-requirements-once-a-cost-is-paid: A declaration that pays costs to attack obeys as many requirements as it could paying only those costs
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 508.1d "The active player checks each creature they control to see whether it’s affected by any requirements (effects that say a creature attacks if able, or that it attacks if some condition is met). If the number of requirements that are being obeyed is fewer than the maximum possible number of requirements that could be obeyed without disobeying any restrictions, the declaration of attackers is illegal. If a creature can’t attack unless a player pays a cost, that player is not required to pay that cost, even if attacking with that creature would increase the number of requirements being obeyed. ..." / Furygale Flocking ruling (and Territorial Hellkite's): "If one of the tokens can't attack the appropriate player, its controller is free to have it attack any other player, planeswalker, or battle or to not attack with it. If there's a cost associated with attacking the appropriate player, its controller is free to have it attack any other player, planeswalker, or battle or not attack with it. However, if there's a cost associated with that token attacking at all, that token has to attack the appropriate player or not attack." / Encore ruling (Impulsivity): "If the effect stops the token from attacking a specific player unless a cost is paid, you don't have to pay that cost unless you want to attack that player." / Goad ruling (Disrupt Decorum): "If there's a cost associated with having a creature attack a player, its controller isn't forced to pay that cost, so it doesn't have to attack that player."
- Question: 508.1d doesn't require paying a cost to obey a requirement, and says nothing of a player who pays costs to attack anyway. Once a creature attacks paying a cost, may it ignore its requirements (attack another player than the one it must attack)? May a requirement obeyed by paying make up for another one that could be obeyed for free? And which costs count as costs "to attack at all"?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 5. A declaration that has creatures attacking for a cost must also obey as many requirements as could be obeyed with each of those creatures free to make its paid attack, or to attack whatever needs no cost beyond its costs to attack at all (the restrictions taxing everything it can attack), and every other creature attacking only for free. So a creature with a cost to attack at all (Brainwash, "can't attack unless its controller pays {3}"; War Tax) may not attack, or attack as its requirements say; it may not attack another player instead. A cost only to attack some players or planeswalkers (Ghostly Prison, Propaganda, Baird) binds nothing else: a creature paying it may attack whatever else it can, paying that one's cost if any, even when every player it can attack has such a tax (with Propaganda on every opponent, a goaded creature may attack the goading player, paying that player's cost). A requirement obeyed by paying (attacking the Ghostly Prison player it must attack) doesn't make up for another creature's requirement that could be obeyed for free. Where the ruling's sentences pull apart (the reason it was provisional): when obeying the requirement needs a cost beyond the cost to attack at all (Brainwash plus Ghostly Prison on the required player), the creature may attack another player paying only the cost to attack at all, since its controller isn't required to pay the other cost; and a single tax on everything the creature can attack (Baird in a two-player game, taxing the player and their planeswalkers) is a cost to attack at all, so paying it the creature attacks the player, not their planeswalker.
- Alternatives: Count only free attacks in the maximum whatever the declaration (the engine's earlier behavior): a creature paying to attack at all could attack anyone, contradicting the ruling. Or treat a creature every attack of which has some cost as having a cost to attack at all, free to attack anything in the count (this item's first version): with Propaganda on every opponent, a goaded creature could attack only a player other than the goading player, paying that player's cost, contradicting the encore and goad rulings (it would have to pay a cost to attack a specific player it doesn't want to attack). Or count every target whose taxes the creature pays anyway (Baird's tax on P1 covers P1's planeswalkers): a token paying Baird to attack P1's planeswalker would have to attack P1, though the ruling leaves a creature with a cost to attack the appropriate player free to attack a planeswalker.
- Why: the ruling says a creature with a cost "associated with that token attacking at all" attacks the appropriate player or not at all: paying that cost is the player's choice, and once it's paid, nothing but another cost stands between the creature and its requirements. A cost to attack a specific player stays the player's choice (508.1d; "you don't have to pay that cost unless you want to attack that player"), whatever other costs the declaration pays.
- Affects: creatures under "attacks [player] if able", "attacks each combat if able" or goad (Furygale Flocking's tokens, Territorial Hellkite, Knight Rampager, encore tokens, goaded creatures) with a tax on attacking (Brainwash, Cowed by Wisdom, War Tax; Ghostly Prison, Propaganda, Baird, Sphere of Safety and the like); declarations that pay to obey one requirement while another creature's could be obeyed for free.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/combat.rs` (`attack_declaration_legal_with`, `best_attack_paying`, `attack_costs`).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p001_combat.rs` (`furygale_tokens_with_a_cost_to_attack_that_player_or_to_attack_at_all`, `a_goaded_creature_paying_one_players_cost_isnt_forced_to_pay_anothers`).

## trample-reassignment-counts-only-assigned-damage: After an illegal total assignment, damage is assigned again counting only damage already assigned
- Kind: deviation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 702.19b "... When checking for assigned lethal damage, take into account damage already marked on the creature and damage from other creatures that’s being assigned during the same combat damage step, but not any abilities or effects that might change the amount of damage that’s actually dealt. ..." / 510.1e "Once a player has assigned combat damage from each attacking or blocking creature they control, the total damage assignment (not solely the damage assignment of any individual attacking or blocking creature) is checked to see if it complies with the above rules. If it doesn’t, the combat damage assignment is illegal; the game returns to the moment before that player began to assign combat damage. (See rule 733, “Handling Illegal Actions.”)"
- Question: A trampler may assign damage beyond its blockers counting on damage the attackers assigning after it will assign to them (each attacker's damage is asked for in turn, so the engine offers the lower minimums `lethal_counting_later`). If the total assignment then doesn't assign lethal damage to those blockers, it's illegal and the player assigns again. May the player count on later attackers again the second time?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Oct 28. No. The engine asks for every attacker's damage again, offering only the minimums counting damage already assigned (and marked), which can't produce an illegal total; an answer that doesn't meet them gets the default assignment. The whole assignment is redone (510.1e: the game returns to the moment before the player began to assign), including attackers whose first answers were legal on their own.
- Alternatives: Offer the lower minimums again and repeat until the total is legal: exact, but an agent could loop forever; or ask the player for the whole assignment at once (one decision for all attackers): exact, but a different decision shape.
- Why: Accepted, with no plan to change it: the retry always ends, and the player keeps every legal total that doesn't rely on later attackers; a player who relied on them and was wrong rarely loses anything a careful second assignment would give.
- Affects: two or more attackers with trample (or trample over planeswalkers) whose blockers or attacked planeswalker are shared, e.g. through banding or several attackers on one planeswalker; count not measured.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/combat.rs` (`combat_damage_step`, `later_capacity`, `lethal_assigned`, `assign_trample_damage`); `crates/mtg-api/src/request.rs` (`check_damage`).
- Tests: `tests/keywords/k702_019_trample_counts_damage_assigned_after_it.rs` (`a_total_assignment_without_lethal_damage_is_illegal_and_assigned_again`).

## that-excess-damage-this-turn: "that excess damage" after "was dealt excess damage this turn" is the turn's total
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 120.10 "Excess damage has been dealt to a creature if the damage dealt to it is greater than lethal damage. ..." / 608.2c "The controller of the spell or ability follows its instructions in the order written. ..."
- Question: Overclocked Electromancer: "Whenever this creature deals combat damage to a creature, if that creature was dealt excess damage this turn, you get X {E}, where X is that excess damage." Is X the excess damage of this combat damage, or all the excess damage the creature was dealt this turn?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 4. All the excess damage that creature was dealt this turn (the excess damage the condition asks about), counted as the ability resolves.
- Alternatives: Only the excess of the combat damage event that triggered the ability: the same number unless the creature was also dealt excess damage earlier in the turn (by another source, or in a first-strike damage step).
- Why: "that excess damage" refers back to the phrase "was dealt excess damage this turn", which isn't limited to this event.
- Affects: Overclocked Electromancer.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/referents_values_5.rs` (`atom`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/c4_instead_excess.rs` (`overclocked_electromancer_gets_energy_equal_to_the_excess_damage`).

## put-onto-battlefield-blocking-retriggers-becomes-blocked-by: A creature put onto the battlefield blocking triggers "becomes blocked by a creature" again
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 509.3d "An ability that reads “Whenever [a creature] becomes blocked by a creature, . . .” triggers once for each creature that blocks the specified creature. ... In addition, it will trigger if a creature is put onto the battlefield blocking that creature. ..." / 509.3c "An ability that reads “Whenever [a creature] becomes blocked, . . .” generally triggers only once each combat for that creature ... but only if the attacking creature was an unblocked creature at that time." / 509.4 "... A creature put onto the battlefield this way is “blocking” but, for the purposes of trigger events and effects, it never “blocked.”" / Aetherplasm ruling (2006-02-01): "The creature you put onto the battlefield may be the Aetherplasm you just returned to your hand. This allows it to dodge abilities like Deathgazer's."
- Question: Deathgazer: "Whenever this creature blocks or becomes blocked by a nonblack creature, destroy that creature at end of combat." Aetherplasm (blue) blocks it, returns itself to hand and is put back onto the battlefield blocking Deathgazer. The old trigger's "that creature" is the old object (CR 400.7), but does the new Aetherplasm trigger Deathgazer again?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 5. Yes (conclusion B: the ruling is outdated). The engine follows CR 509.3d: Deathgazer becomes blocked by the new Aetherplasm, its ability triggers again, and the new Aetherplasm is destroyed at end of combat. Aetherplasm dodges only the abilities that already triggered for the old object, and abilities of the 509.3c form ("becomes blocked"), which don't trigger again for an attacker that is already blocked.
- Alternatives: Follow the ruling literally (the new blocker doesn't trigger "becomes blocked by", so Aetherplasm survives): what the 2006-02-01 ruling says, but it contradicts the current text of 509.3d, which names this case. The ruling's second sentence is listed as outdated in `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P006.tsv` rather than cited. The 2024-01-12 Aetherplasm ruling ("it was never declared as a blocking creature for the purposes of abilities that trigger whenever a creature blocks") doesn't re-endorse the 2006 sentence: it is limited to "blocks" triggers (509.3a/b) and is cited separately.
- Why: Conclusion B (ruling outdated), confidence high, accepted by the user on Nov 5. The ruling was correct when issued: the pre-M10 Comprehensive Rules (the "put into play" era, section 309, Declare Blockers Step) had a single rule for every "becoming blocked" trigger, quoted in several independent copies (an e-reading.life copy of chapter 309, about 2007; an mtg-forum.de thread quoting it as 309.4; MTG Salvation rulings threads such as the Ichorclaw Myr one; seen as search-result extracts only) and now read in the archived full CR texts (https://github.com/pit142857/mtg-cr, commit 8351b94f; the files are named by upload date and give their own effective dates): "Abilities that trigger on a creature becoming blocked trigger only at the first point the creature becomes blocked that combat. They will trigger if a creature becomes blocked by a creature declared as a blocker, by a creature that's put into play as a blocker, or by an effect, but only if the attacking creature hadn't yet been blocked that combat. ..." (309.4, worded so from the CR "current as of May 1, 2006", Dissension, through the CR "current as of May 1, 2009"; the CR "current as of February 1, 2006", in force when the ruling was published, didn't yet mention creatures put into play blocking). Under it Deathgazer triggered once, for the declared Aetherplasm, and the returned Aetherplasm (a new object then too) entered blocking an attacker that was already blocked, so it really dodged. The change, dated by comparing those texts: the M10 CR ("effective as of July 11, 2009", file MagicCompRules_20090708.txt) deleted 309.4 and split the trigger forms into 509.4a-g; its 509.4d gave "Whenever a creature blocks [this creature], . . ." one trigger "for each creature that blocks the named creature ... even if it had already been blocked that combat", so the once-per-combat limit the ruling rests on was gone. The CR effective September 4, 2009 renamed that template "Whenever [this creature] becomes blocked by a creature, . . .", and the CR effective February 1, 2010 (Worldwake) added the sentence 509.3d still has: "In addition, it will trigger if a creature is put onto the battlefield blocking that creature." (renumbered 509.5d by 2017 and 509.3d in the November 2024 update). So the ruling has been outdated since July 11, 2009 and contradicted by an explicit rule since February 1, 2010; the Magic Judges Rules Tips post "'Becomes blocked' vs. 'becomes blocked by a creature'" (February 2011, title seen in search results only) explains the split. 509.4's "never blocked" is about the blocking creature's own "blocks" triggers, which don't trigger (tested with Wall of Junk). Logged in `docs/RULING_DISCREPANCIES.md` (outdated).
- Affects: Aetherplasm with Deathgazer and other "becomes blocked by a creature" abilities; any effect putting a creature onto the battlefield blocking.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/combat.rs` (`combat_trigger_matches`, `TriggerCond::BlockedByCreature`).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p006_activated_bounce.rs` (`aetherplasm_can_put_itself_back_but_deathgazer_sees_the_new_blocker`).

## qualified-landwalk-is-that-landwalk: "snow islandwalk" is an islandwalk ability
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 702.14a "Landwalk is a generic term that appears within an object’s rules text as “[type]walk,” where [type] is usually a land type, but it can also be the card type land plus any combination of land types, card types, and/or supertypes." / 702.14c "... or with both the specified type or supertype and the specified subtype (as in “snow swampwalk”)." / Merfolk Assassin ruling: "Can target a creature with snow islandwalk."
- Question: Does a reference to one landwalk ability ("target creature with islandwalk", "Creatures with islandwalk can be blocked as though they didn't have islandwalk") cover the same landwalk with a qualifier in front ("snow islandwalk")?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 4. Yes: a landwalk ability whose text is the named one with words in front of it ("snow islandwalk" for "islandwalk") is that kind of landwalk, for both "with [type]walk" filters and "blocked as though they didn't have [type]walk" effects. A different land type ("snow swampwalk" for "islandwalk") isn't.
- Alternatives: Match only the exact text: Merfolk Assassin couldn't target a creature with snow islandwalk, contradicting its ruling; or apply the ruling only to filters, so Undertow wouldn't let a snow islandwalker be blocked (no ruling addresses Undertow-style effects directly).
- Why: The Merfolk Assassin ruling treats snow islandwalk as islandwalk; the same words in Undertow and Staff of the Ages-style effects should mean the same thing.
- Affects: Merfolk Assassin, Stormtide Leviathan-style filters; Undertow and other "[type]walk can be blocked as though" effects; creatures with qualified landwalk (no printed card has snow islandwalk; Legions of Lim-Dûl has snow swampwalk).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/kw/landwalk.rs` (`is_kind_of`, `has_landwalk`, `landwalk_ignored`).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p054_misc.rs` (`merfolk_assassin_can_target_a_creature_with_snow_islandwalk`, `undertow_lets_a_snow_islandwalker_be_blocked`).

## entered-blocker-never-blocked: The blocks of a creature put onto the battlefield blocking aren't in the history effects read
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 509.4 "If a creature is put onto the battlefield blocking, ... A creature put onto the battlefield this way is “blocking” but, for the purposes of trigger events and effects, it never “blocked.”" / 509.3d "An ability that reads “Whenever [a creature] becomes blocked by a creature, . . .” ... It will also trigger if an effect causes a creature to block the attacking creature, but only if it wasn’t already blocking that attacking creature at that time. In addition, it will trigger if a creature is put onto the battlefield blocking that creature." / Glyph of Delusion ruling (2004-10-04): "Can be cast at any time after the wall blocked a creature and can affect any creature that the wall was assigned to block or that was blocked by the wall due to being in a band or by being switched into or out of the block by an ability." / Sorrow's Path: "{T}: Choose two target blocking creatures controlled by the same opponent. If each of those creatures could block all creatures that the other is blocking, remove both of them from combat. Each one then blocks all creatures the other was blocking."
- Question: A Wall put onto the battlefield blocking an attacker (Aetherplasm) is blocking it. Is that attacker "a creature that target Wall blocked this turn" (Glyph of Delusion) or one of the "creatures that were blocked by target Wall this turn" (Glyph of Reincarnation)?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 5. No to both: 509.4 says the Wall never "blocked" for effects, and the attacker's "was blocked by" it is the same relation seen from the attacker. Effects that read what a creature blocked, or what was blocked by it, this combat or this turn ("creatures that blocked ~ this turn", "creatures that were blocked by that creature this turn", "blocked or were blocked by") leave out the blocks of a creature put onto the battlefield blocking, until it blocks that attacker otherwise (declared in a later combat, or made to block by an effect, which the Glyph of Delusion ruling counts as "switched into the block by an ability"). What is blocking now, and "had become blocked by only that creature this combat" (the attacker's current blockers), still includes it.
- Alternatives: Read "were blocked by" from the attacker's side as including the creature, as 509.3d's trigger does for "becomes blocked by a creature": Glyph of Reincarnation would destroy the attacker while Glyph of Delusion couldn't target it.
- Why: 509.4 is explicit for effects, and its trigger exception (509.3d) is spelled out for triggers only; Ride Down's "creatures that were blocked by that creature this combat" already leaves such a creature out (CR 509.1a). The user asked whether the Glyph of Delusion ruling's "or by being switched into or out of the block by an ability" covers a creature put onto the battlefield blocking. It doesn't. That phrase is about an effect making a creature already on the battlefield block an attacker, or stop blocking it, as Sorrow's Path does (it removes two blockers from combat, then each blocks what the other was blocking; General Jarkeld does the same from the attackers' side), and 509.3d lists the two cases separately: "if an effect causes a creature to block the attacking creature", then "In addition, ... if a creature is put onto the battlefield blocking that creature". And the ruling predates the cards that put creatures onto the battlefield blocking: it is dated 2004-10-04 (Gatherer's first batch of rulings), before Aetherplasm (Guildpact; its rulings are dated 2006-02-01) and Flash Foliage (Dissension, 2006-05-05), before the CR first mentioned creatures put into play blocking (309.3 and 309.4 of the CR "current as of May 1, 2006"; not in the CR of February 1, 2006) and before 509.4's "never 'blocked'" first appeared (309.5 of the CR "current as of October 1, 2007"; dated from the archived CR texts, https://github.com/pit142857/mtg-cr). The ruling stays cited for its three cases: a creature the Wall was assigned to block, one blocked by it through banding, and one switched into or out of its block by an ability (tested with Sorrow's Path).
- Affects: Glyph of Reincarnation, Glyph of Delusion; Glyph of Doom, Venomous Breath, Gaze of the Gorgon, Joven's Ferrets and the other readers of the turn's or the combat's blocks; Aetherplasm, Flash Foliage, Mirror Match (creatures put onto the battlefield blocking).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/kw/block_history.rs` (`record_block`, `block_relation`; `TurnHistory::entered_blocking`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/kw/blocked_this_turn.rs` (`blocks_this_turn`: the delayed "blocked by that creature this turn" readers, Glyph of Doom and Venomous Breath).
- Tests: `tests/cards/c6_history_permissions.rs` (`a_wall_put_onto_the_battlefield_blocking_never_blocked`, `glyph_of_delusion_targets_creatures_switched_into_or_out_of_the_walls_block`).

## requirement-search-budget: On boards too large to try every declaration, the maximum number of requirements obeyed is searched for within a budget
- Kind: deviation
- Status: provisional
- Rules: 508.1d "... If the number of requirements that are being obeyed is fewer than the maximum possible number of requirements that could be obeyed without disobeying any restrictions, the declaration of attackers is illegal. ..." / 509.1c "... If the number of requirements that are being obeyed is fewer than the maximum possible number of requirements that could be obeyed without disobeying any restrictions, the declaration of blockers is illegal. ..." / 508.1d's example: "A player controls two creatures: one that “attacks if able” and one with no abilities. An effect states “No more than one creature can attack each combat.” The only legal attack is for just the creature that “attacks if able” to attack. ..."
- Question: The maximum is over every possible declaration, and their number grows exponentially with the creatures (each attacker may also choose among players, planeswalkers and battles). How does the engine find it on a large board?
- Decision: Interchangeable creatures (the same controller, characteristics, counters, choices, requirements and restrictions, with nothing attached: tokens, typically) are counted by how many of them make each choice rather than by which ones do, so twenty alike tokens choosing among two players and not attacking are 231 declarations, not 3^20. When there are at most 100,000 declarations counted that way, every one is tried (with pruning). Beyond that, the engine starts from the better of two greedy declarations made only of attacks or blocks that need no cost, if it breaks no restriction: each creature with requirements attacks what obeys most of them (or makes the block obeying most of them); and the same one creature at a time, keeping each choice only if the declaration so far breaks no restriction (so the third creature under "No more than two creatures can attack you each combat" attacks another player, and the second blocker of a creature that "can't be blocked by more than one creature" blocks another attacker). It then searches for a better declaration, visiting at most 200,000 search nodes and cutting off any branch with more attackers or blockers than a "no more than N creatures can attack/block" restriction allows. The best declaration found is the maximum the legality check uses. That finds the true maximum in every case tested (Angler Turtle with Silent Arbiter and 11 to 14 creatures; 18 creatures that must block under Silent Arbiter; twenty goaded creatures, alike or all different, under Crawlspace; seventeen creatures that must block, alike or all different, with an attacker that can't be blocked by more than one creature; twenty goaded Mogg Flunkies under Crawlspace), but on a board of many different creatures under restrictions neither greedy start satisfies (many different creatures that each "can't attack alone", under Crawlspace) the search could stop short of it, and a declaration obeying fewer requirements than the true maximum (but as many as the best found) would then be accepted.
- Alternatives: An exact search with no budget (correct, but a declaration could take minutes on a large board of different creatures); the greedy declaration alone (the engine's behavior before the combat audit: when it broke a restriction, the maximum fell to what not attacking obeys, so with Angler Turtle, Silent Arbiter and 11 creatures not attacking at all was legal); the budgeted search alone, from that greedy start (the audit's first fix: it found only twelve of twenty goaded creatures attacking under Crawlspace, and sixteen of seventeen creatures blocking with Tahngarth, First Mate attacking).
- Why: Accepted for now: a budget bounds each declaration's cost; the grouping makes boards of tokens exact, and the greedy starts and the restriction cutoff make the realistic boards of different creatures exact; the remaining gap needs many different creatures with interacting requirements and restrictions that no greedy start satisfies. Plan: replace the budget with an exact method (a matching formulation) if a real case misses the maximum.
- Affects: large boards under "attacks/blocks each combat if able" effects (Angler Turtle, Grand Melee, goad on many creatures, Lure on several attackers) together with declaration-wide restrictions (Silent Arbiter, Crawlspace, "can't attack alone", "can't attack unless N others do", "can't be blocked by more than one creature").

## marked-deathtouch-damage-isnt-lethal-for-trample: Deathtouch damage already marked counts toward lethal damage only by its amount
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: provisional
- Rules: 702.19b "... Once all those blocking creatures are assigned lethal damage, any excess damage is assigned as its controller chooses among those blocking creatures and the player, planeswalker, or battle the creature is attacking. When checking for assigned lethal damage, take into account damage already marked on the creature and damage from other creatures that’s being assigned during the same combat damage step, but not any abilities or effects that might change the amount of damage that’s actually dealt. ..." / 702.2c "Any nonzero amount of combat damage assigned to a creature by a source with deathtouch is considered to be lethal damage for the purposes of determining if excess damage is being dealt." / 120.6 "... If the total damage marked on a creature is greater than or equal to its toughness, that creature has been dealt lethal damage ..."
- Question: A creature with double strike, deathtouch and trample is blocked by an indestructible creature (a 0/2), assigns it 1 in the first combat damage step, and the blocker survives with 1 damage marked. In the second step, has the blocker already been assigned lethal damage (the marked damage was assigned by a deathtouch source, which 702.2c calls lethal), so that all the damage may go to the player, or does it still need 1 more?
- Decision: It needs 1 more. Damage already marked counts by its amount (120.6: 1 of 2 toughness isn't lethal), and 702.2c makes a nonzero amount lethal only as it is assigned in the current step: the new assignment needs at least 1 to the blocker. Deathtouch damage another attacker assigns to it in the same step still counts as lethal (702.19b's "damage from other creatures that's being assigned during the same combat damage step").
- Alternatives: Count marked damage from a deathtouch source as lethal: the second step could assign everything to the player. 702.2c speaks of damage "assigned", which the first step's damage was, but its purpose clause ("for the purposes of determining if excess damage is being dealt") reads as being about the assignment in progress.
- Why: 702.19b says to take marked damage into account, and 120.6 defines lethal damage by the total marked against toughness; nothing marks damage as "deathtouch damage" for later assignments. No ruling addresses the case. The nearest, Bonds of Mortality's (2016-01-22), treats deathtouch damage once marked as ordinary damage for the state-based action: "However, if a creature with indestructible is dealt damage by a source with deathtouch and then later loses indestructible, that creature won’t be destroyed."
- Affects: a creature with double strike, deathtouch and trample (Crimson Caravaneer, Prophetic Flamespeaker or Bolrac-Clan Basher given deathtouch) blocked by an indestructible creature with toughness 2 or more; also a first-strike deathtouch creature's damage seen by a trampler assigning in the regular step.
