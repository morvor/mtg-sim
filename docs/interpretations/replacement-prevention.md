# Replacement and prevention

Judgment calls about replacement and prevention effects (CR 614–616) and the damage and
draw events they modify. Format and kinds: `docs/agents/items/interpretation-register.md`;
index: `docs/INTERPRETATIONS.md`.

## shared-color-source-locked: "A source of your choice that shares a color with X" compares with X's colors as the effect is created
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 609.7a "... The source is chosen when the effect is created. ..." / 609.7b "Some effects from resolved spells and abilities prevent or replace damage from sources with certain properties, such as a creature or a source of a particular color. When the source would deal damage, the “shield” rechecks the source’s properties. If the properties no longer match, the damage isn’t prevented or replaced. ..."
- Question: Protective Sphere ("a source of your choice that shares a color with the mana spent on this activation cost") and Mourner's Shield ("... that shares a color with the exiled card") name a property relative to something else. When the shield rechecks the source's properties (609.7b), which colors does it compare with: those of the mana or exiled card as the effect was created, or as they are then?
- Decision: The colors as the effect is created. The source must have one of them both when it's chosen and when it would deal damage; the exiled card leaving exile, or the activation's mana being long gone, doesn't change them.
- Alternatives: Recheck against the exiled card as it is when the damage would be dealt (impossible for the mana spent, which no longer exists).
- Why: The mana spent on the activation exists only while the ability resolves, so the effect has to remember its colors; treating the exiled card the same way keeps the two wordings consistent, and the source's own colors are still rechecked as 609.7b requires.
- Affects: Protective Sphere, Mourner's Shield.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/prevention.rs` (`lock_filter`: `SharesColor`, `SHARES_COLOR_WITH_MANA_SPENT`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/c4_mana_provenance.rs` (`protective_sphere_chooses_a_source_sharing_a_color_with_the_mana_spent`).

## per-source-prevention-division: A shared "prevent X of that damage" is divided by its controller only when one source's damage exceeds X
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 615.7 "... If damage would be dealt to the shielded permanent or player by two or more applicable sources at the same time, the player or the controller of the permanent chooses which damage the shield prevents. ..." / 616.1 "If two or more replacement and/or prevention effects are attempting to modify the way an event affects an object or player, the affected object’s controller (or its owner if it has no controller) or the affected player chooses one to apply ..."
- Question: Cover of Winter prevents X of the combat damage each creature would deal to you and/or creatures you control, shared among those recipients; its ruling says "you decide how to divide the prevention effect" when a creature's damage is assigned to several of them (trample). When is the division asked, and what if the answer is invalid?
- Decision: Before the simultaneous damage is replaced, for each creature whose damage to two or more of your recipients totals more than X, Cover of Winter's controller divides X among them (a `Divide` decision, at most each recipient's damage). When the total is at most X, or X is 0, there's nothing to choose (all of it is prevented, or none of it), and nothing is asked. An invalid answer or a default prevents X in the order the engine replaces the damage (APNAP by recipient, then `prevention::order_for_shields`). A part goes with the damage it's on when that damage is redirected before Cover of Winter is applied to it (Pariah applied first to the damage to you: your part is the enchanted creature's for that damage), so the Cover still prevents X of the creature's damage.
- Alternatives: Always prevent in the engine's order without asking (the player loses a real choice); ask only for an order (can't express splitting X across two partially prevented recipients). Leave a part where the damage was proposed when it's redirected first (the engine until November 2026): the part is lost, and the Cover prevents less than X of that creature's damage, against the ruling ("as long as the total amount of that creature's damage that would be prevented is X").
- Why: Follows the ruling; the default only fills in for a missing or invalid answer. Audit (decision-audit-1, Nov 20): an apostrophe in the 616.1 quote matched to the CR text ("object’s").
- Affects: Cover of Winter.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/replacement.rs` (`divide_per_source_prevention`, `PreventPerSource`, `move_division_parts`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/actions.rs` (`deal_damage_batch`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/c4_replacements.rs` (`cover_of_winter_prevents_x_of_each_creatures_combat_damage`, `cover_of_winter_controller_divides_x_among_one_creatures_recipients`); `tests/cr/r615_per_source_division.rs` (nothing asked with one recipient, X = 0 or no more than X; a trampler's two blockers and you; each creature's damage on its own; an answer that isn't a division of X); `tests/cr/r615_divided_prevention_parts.rs` (two Covers' own parts; a part going with damage redirected before the Cover is applied).

## words-target-on-activation: A next-draw replacement's "any target" is chosen as the ability is activated; if it's gone, the draw is still replaced and nothing is dealt damage
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 115.1c "An activated ability is targeted if it identifies something it will affect by using the phrase “target [something],” ... The target(s) are chosen as the ability is activated; see rule 602.2b." / 608.2b "If the spell or ability specifies targets, it checks whether the targets are still legal. ... If all its targets ... are now illegal, the spell or ability doesn't resolve." / 614.11 "Some effects replace card draws." / Mirrorwood Treefolk ruling: "If the target creature is not on the battlefield (or is not a creature) at the time the damage would be redirected, then the damage goes on this card." / Words of War ruling: "If multiple Words have been used prior to drawing a card, then you can choose which one to apply (and use up) each time you draw a card."
- Question: Words of War ("{1}: The next time you would draw a card this turn, this enchantment deals 2 damage to any target instead.") names a target inside a replacement effect that applies later. No rule or ruling says whether the target is chosen as the ability is activated or as the draw is replaced, nor what happens when the target is no longer there when the draw is replaced.
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 4. The target is the activated ability's (115.1c): chosen as it's activated, checked as it resolves (608.2b: if it's illegal then, the ability doesn't resolve and no shield is created), and locked into the shield. When the draw is replaced, the shield applies and is used up whatever became of the target; if the target object has left its zone (a new object, 400.7) or the player has left the game, the 2 damage is dealt to nothing. Its legality isn't checked again (the replacement effect isn't a spell or ability that targets), so hexproof or protection gained afterwards doesn't stop the damage (damage prevention by protection still applies, 702.16e).
- Alternatives: (a) The target is chosen as the draw is replaced (the replacement effect would choose targets like a triggered ability; nothing in 614 or 616 provides for that). (b) The replacement doesn't apply when its target is gone, and the draw happens (the shield would wait for a later draw or expire); this would make the shield's applicability depend on something 614.1a's "instead" doesn't mention.
- Why: 115.1c is explicit that "target" in an activated ability is chosen on activation, and the same wording on Glarecaster, General's Regalia and Mirrorwood Treefolk (targeted next-time redirections) works that way, with Mirrorwood's ruling: the effect still applies and the missing target simply gets nothing. The draw's replacement is the event the shield watches for; the damage is only what happens instead.
- Affects: Words of War; the other Words (Words of Wind, Waste, Wilding, Worship) have no target.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/replacements_next_draw.rs` (`next_draw_instead`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/prevention.rs` (`lock_def`: `ReplacementAction::Instead` → `lock_targets`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/c5_mana_replacements.rs` (`words_of_war_replaces_the_next_draw_with_damage_to_the_target_chosen_on_activation`, `words_of_war_target_gone_still_replaces_the_draw_without_damage`).

## runesword-damage-this-turn: Runesword's effects cover every creature its target has dealt damage to this turn, from the moment the damage is dealt
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 701.19c "... Effects that say that a permanent can't be regenerated don't preclude such abilities from being activated or such spells from being cast; rather, they cause regeneration shields to not be applied." / 704.5g "If a creature has toughness greater than 0, it has damage marked on it, and the total damage marked on it is greater than or equal to its toughness, that creature has been dealt lethal damage and is destroyed. ..." / 614.1a "Effects that use the word “instead” are replacement effects. ..."
- Question: Runesword's ability says "If the creature deals damage to a creature this turn, the creature dealt damage can't be regenerated this turn. If a creature dealt damage by the targeted creature would die this turn, exile that creature instead." Is the first sentence a trigger (which would resolve only after state-based actions destroyed, and regenerated, the creature dealt lethal damage), and does damage the target dealt earlier in the turn, before the ability resolved, count?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 4. Both sentences are continuous effects created as the ability resolves, lasting until end of turn, about the creatures the targeted creature has dealt damage to this turn (the turn's damage history, as Torch the Tower's "a permanent dealt damage by ~"). A creature it deals damage to can't be regenerated from that moment, so a regeneration shield doesn't save it from lethal damage, and if it would die this turn it's exiled instead. Damage it dealt earlier in the turn counts too. The targeted creature is locked in as the effect begins; damage dealt by other creatures doesn't count.
- Alternatives: (a) Read the first sentence as "Whenever that creature deals damage to a creature this turn, that creature can't be regenerated this turn" (a delayed trigger): the ban would come too late for lethal damage, and the sentence would do nothing in Runesword's usual case. (b) Count only damage dealt after the ability resolves: "this turn" and "dealt damage by" don't say so; with an attacking target, damage before the ability resolves is rare (first strike).
- Why: The Oracle wording is a conditional statement, not a "whenever" trigger, and only a continuous effect gives the sentence its evident purpose. "Dealt damage by the targeted creature ... this turn" reads as the turn's damage history, like every other "dealt damage by ~ this turn" in the engine.
- Affects: Runesword.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/prevention_6.rs`; `crates/mtg-engine/src/prevention.rs` (`lock_damage_history`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/kw/damage_history_by.rs`.
- Tests: `tests/cards/c5_mana_replacements.rs` (`runesword_creatures_dealt_damage_by_the_target_cant_regenerate_and_are_exiled`, `runesword_doesnt_affect_creatures_damaged_by_other_creatures`).

## enter-sacrifice-instead-partial: "Sacrifice two untapped lands instead. If you do, ..." with fewer than two sacrifices those it can, and ~ doesn't enter
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 609.3 "If an effect attempts to do something impossible, it does only as much as possible." / 614.12 "Some replacement effects modify how a permanent enters the battlefield. ..."
- Question: Lotus Vale and Scorched Ruins say "If ~ would enter, sacrifice two untapped lands instead. If you do, put ~ onto the battlefield. If you don't, put it into its owner's graveyard." The sacrifice is an instruction, not an offer (Mox Diamond's "you may discard a land card instead" is one), so with two untapped lands they're sacrificed. With only one untapped land, is it sacrificed, and does ~ enter?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 4. The one land is sacrificed (as much as possible, 609.3), and "if you do" is true only if two were sacrificed, so ~ goes to its owner's graveyard. With no untapped land nothing is sacrificed.
- Alternatives: Treat the instruction like a cost that can't be paid in part (nothing sacrificed with one land); treat it as optional like Mox Diamond (the previous reading, which let a player keep both lands and put ~ into the graveyard).
- Why: The card's text gives no choice and no cost; 609.3 is the general rule for an instruction that can't be done in full, and Lake of the Dead's and Heart of Yavimaya's rulings ("You have to sacrifice a swamp before this card is put onto the battlefield") settle that the sacrifice isn't optional; Lotus Vale's and Scorched Ruins' rulings ("If you don't sacrifice the lands, Lotus Vale never enters") say only that it doesn't enter, not what happens to the one land.
- Affects: Lotus Vale, Scorched Ruins (the one-land versions, Lake of the Dead and the others, have no partial case).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/replacement_bodies.rs` (`enter_only_if_sacrificed`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/roundtrip_pairs_4.rs` (`lotus_vale_with_one_untapped_land_sacrifices_it_and_never_enters`, `lake_of_the_dead_sacrifices_a_swamp_without_asking`); `tests/cards/replacement_bodies_misc.rs` (`lotus_vale_enters_only_if_two_untapped_lands_are_sacrificed`).

## next-time-damage-batch: A one-use "the next time ... would deal damage / be dealt damage" effect applies to all the damage dealt at the same time
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 615.8 "Some prevention effects generated by the resolution of a spell or ability refer to the next time a specific source would deal damage. These effects prevent the next instance of damage from that source, regardless of how much damage that is. Once an instance of damage from that source has been prevented, any subsequent instances of damage that would be dealt by that source are dealt normally." / 615.12 "... Existing damage prevention shields won’t be reduced by damage that can’t be prevented." / 510.2 "... all combat damage that's dealt during the combat damage step is dealt simultaneously." / Glarecaster ruling: "If both you and this card would be dealt damage at the same time, or if either you or this card would be dealt damage from multiple sources at the same time, the redirection will apply to both chunks of damage."
- Question: Damage dealt at the same time is several damage events to the engine (one per source and recipient). A limited-use replacement or prevention effect ("the next time damage would be dealt to this creature and/or you this turn", "the next time target creature would deal damage this turn") is used up by the first of them it applies to. Does it then apply to the others dealt at the same time?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 5. Yes. While damage dealt at the same time is being replaced, a limited-use effect used up by one of its events still applies to the others (as one entering-the-battlefield batch already did for "the next time one or more creatures enter"). So Glarecaster redirects both chunks, and Awe Strike prevents both the damage a trampler deals to its blocker and the damage it deals to the player. Effects that prevent a number of damage ("the next 3 damage") are unaffected: they're shields with an amount, not one-use effects. Damage of the batch that can't be prevented doesn't use up a prevention effect (615.12), so when part of the batch can't be prevented, the effect prevents the rest and is used up by it.
- Alternatives: Use the effect up on the first event only, so the others (in APNAP and shield order) are dealt normally; this contradicts the Glarecaster ruling and 615.8's "instance of damage ... regardless of how much damage that is".
- Why: The Glarecaster ruling settles redirection to two recipients from two sources; 615.8 treats one "instance" of a source's damage as a whole, however it's split among recipients. No ruling found treats simultaneous chunks separately. It was provisional because the ruling covers redirection, and the extension to every one-use damage effect is ours.
- Affects: Glarecaster, Awe Strike, and every one-use damage replacement or prevention effect (`AddReplacement { uses: Some(1) }` on a damage event).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/actions.rs` (`deal_damage_batch`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/replacement.rs` (`used_by_this_entry`, `apply_replacement`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/zones.rs` (`damage_batch_uses`).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p002_replacement.rs` (`glarecaster_redirects_every_chunk_dealt_at_the_same_time`, `a_next_time_prevention_covers_all_of_a_source_s_simultaneous_damage`, `a_next_time_prevention_is_used_up_by_a_batch_part_of_which_can_t_be_prevented`).

## shield-divided-among-recipients: A damage shield applying to simultaneous damage to several of its recipients is divided by its controller
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 615.7 "... These work like shields. Each 1 damage that would be dealt to the “shielded” permanent or player is prevented. Preventing 1 damage reduces the remaining shield by 1. If damage would be dealt to the shielded permanent or player by two or more applicable sources at the same time, the player or the controller of the permanent chooses which damage the shield prevents. ..." / 614.9 (redirection effects) / Harm's Way ruling: "You choose which 2 damage is redirected. If you like, you can choose to redirect 1 damage that would be dealt by the chosen source to each of two different recipients." / Refraction Trap ruling: "you choose which of that damage to prevent. ... You don’t decide until the point at which the source would deal its damage."
- Question: 615.7 says who chooses when several sources damage one shielded recipient. A shield can also cover several recipients at once ("the next 2 damage that a source of your choice would deal to you and/or permanents you control"). When damage would be dealt to two or more of them at the same time, more than is left of the shield, who chooses which of it the shield applies to, and how?
- Decision: As the damage would be dealt, the shield's controller divides what's left of it among those recipients (a `Divide` decision over the distinct recipients, each at most the damage it would be dealt). Damage the shield can't apply to (it can't be prevented, or can't be redirected) takes no part, and when fewer than two recipients or no more damage than the shield is left, nothing is asked. A recipient dealt damage by several sources gets its part applied in the order its controller put that damage in (`order_for_shields`, CR 615.7). A part goes with the damage it's on when that damage is redirected before the shield is applied to it (Pariah's "All damage that would be dealt to you is dealt to enchanted creature instead." applied first: the part P0 put on the damage to P0 is the enchanted creature's for that damage, added to its own part, or its only one, even a part of 0); a shield already applied to the damage keeps its part where it was. An invalid answer or a default applies the shield in the order the engine replaces the damage (APNAP by recipient, then `order_for_shields`).
- Alternatives: Ask each recipient's controller in turn (the rulings say "you" choose); never ask, applying the shield in the engine's order (loses the choice the rulings describe). Leave a part where the damage was proposed when the damage is redirected first (the engine until November 2026): the part is lost, and the redirected damage takes the new recipient's part, or is prevented with no limit if that recipient has none, so applying the redirection first changes what the shield prevents, though the player divided it.
- Why: Follows the rulings. The cards with such shields cover only their controller and that player's permanents, so the shield's controller is also every recipient's controller.
- Affects: Harm's Way, Refraction Trap, Divine Deflection, Shining Shoal, and every shield with an amount (`ReplacementInstance::remaining`) on damage to several recipients.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/prevention.rs` (`divide_shields`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/replacement.rs` (`apply_replacement`: `PreventAmount`/`PreventAndThen`/`RedirectNext` with `per_source_division`; `move_division_parts`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/actions.rs` (`deal_damage_batch`).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p043_shields.rs` (`harms_way_redirects_just_2_of_simultaneous_damage_as_you_choose`, `refraction_trap_you_choose_which_simultaneous_damage_it_prevents_as_it_is_dealt`, `a_divided_shield_is_divided_among_recipients_then_ordered_by_source`); `tests/cr/r615_divided_prevention_parts.rs` (a recipient's part given once with more damage redirected to it; a part going with damage redirected before the shield is applied, and a part of 0).

## entering-counters-one-event: The counters of one kind a permanent enters with are put on it as one event, however many "enters with" effects contributed
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 122.6 "Some spells and abilities refer to counters being put on an object. This refers to putting counters on that object while it’s on the battlefield and also to an object that’s given counters as it enters the battlefield." / 614.1c "Effects that read “[This permanent] enters with . . . ,” “As [this permanent] enters . . . ,” or “[This permanent] enters as . . . “ are replacement effects." / 614.5 "A replacement effect doesn’t invoke itself repeatedly; it gets only one opportunity to affect an event or any modified events that may replace that event." / Hardened Scales ruling (2023-09-01): "If a creature you control would enter the battlefield with a number of +1/+1 counters on it, it enters with that many plus one instead." / Spark Double ruling (2019-05-03): "If it copies a creature, Spark Double enters with a +1/+1 counter on it, plus any counters that will be put on it from abilities it copied and other abilities of other objects." / Winding Constrictor ruling (2017-02-09): "If you would get counters of multiple kinds at the same time, Winding Constrictor increases the number of each of those kinds of counters by one. The same is true if counters of multiple kinds would be placed on an artifact or creature you control."
- Question: A permanent can be given counters by several "enters with" effects at once: Spike Feeder's own two +1/+1 counters and Master Biomancer's additional ones, or a Spark Double copying Spike Feeder (the copied ability's two, the copy exception's one). Is that one event of putting counters on it, which "if one or more +1/+1 counters would be put on a creature you control, that many plus one" (Hardened Scales) modifies once, or one event per "enters with" effect, each getting one more?
- Decision: One event for each kind of counter (and each player putting them, CR 122.6a): the permanent enters once and is given all its counters of that kind as it does. Hardened Scales makes Spark Double copying Spike Feeder enter with 4 counters (not 5), and Spike Feeder under Master Biomancer (power 2) enter with 5 (not 6).
- Alternatives: One event per "enters with" effect, each modified separately (the engine's previous behavior): Hardened Scales would add one counter for each effect that contributed, which no ruling describes.
- Why: Settled by the rules: the "enters with" effects are replacement effects modifying the one event of the permanent entering (614.1c), its counters are put on it in that event (122.6), and Hardened Scales gets one opportunity to modify it (614.5): "it enters with that many plus one", as its ruling says, "that many" being the number the "enters with" effects gave it. Kinds are separate (Winding Constrictor's ruling). Decided in the review of engine-audit-1/replacement (it was provisional only because no ruling names two "enters with" effects and Hardened Scales together). How the effects that modify the number are ordered with the "enters with" effects is `entering-counter-modifiers-after-enters-with`.
- Affects: Hardened Scales, Winding Constrictor, Corpsejack Menace, Vorinclex, and every permanent given counters of one kind by two or more "enters with" effects (copied ones, copy exceptions, Master Biomancer, Bramblewood Paragon, riot, ...).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/actions.rs` (`perform_move`: the counters it enters with, merged by kind and putter before replacement).
- Tests: `tests/cr/r614_audit_counters.rs` (`hardened_scales_adds_one_counter_to_spark_doubles_whole_entry`, `hardened_scales_adds_one_counter_to_a_feeder_entering_under_master_biomancer`).

## entering-counter-modifiers-after-enters-with: Effects that modify how many counters are put apply to a permanent's entry after all its "enters with" effects
- Kind: deviation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 616.1e "Any of the applicable replacement and/or prevention effects may be chosen." / 616.1f "Once the chosen effect has been applied, this process is repeated (taking into account only replacement or prevention effects that would now be applicable) until there are no more left to apply." / 616.2 "A replacement or prevention effect can become applicable to an event as the result of another replacement or prevention effect that modifies the event." / Hardened Scales ruling (2023-09-01): "If two or more effects attempt to modify how many counters would be put on a creature you control, you choose the order to apply those effects, no matter who controls the sources of those effects."
- Question: Spike Feeder ("enters with two +1/+1 counters") enters under its controller's Master Biomancer ("enters with a number of additional +1/+1 counters ... equal to this creature's power") while an opponent controls Vorinclex, Monstrous Raider ("If an opponent would put one or more counters on a permanent or player, they put half that many ..."). Once Spike Feeder's effect has given it two counters, Vorinclex's effect applies too (616.2), and the controller may apply it before Master Biomancer's (616.1e): 2 halved is 1, plus 2 is 3, where applying Master Biomancer's first gives (2 + 2) / 2 = 2. Can the controller interleave them?
- Decision: (to be fixed later; the user, Nov 12) Not in the engine: the "enters with" effects are applied as the entry is replaced, and the effects that modify how many counters are put (Hardened Scales, Corpsejack Menace, Doubling Season, Vorinclex) to the counters of each kind it then enters with, in an order the controller chooses among themselves (`entering-counters-one-event`): Spike Feeder enters with 2 counters, not the 3 the controller could choose. Counters of one kind that different players put (tribute's opponent, CR 122.6a) are separate events, so Hardened Scales adds one to each.
- Alternatives: Offer the counter-modifying effects among the entry's replacement effects once it has counters (exact, but the counters an entry is given would be replaced in two places, before and after it enters, and every "enters with" counter path, loyalty and lore included, would need the same treatment).
- Why: The order matters only when an effect reduces the number (an opponent's Vorinclex) or a player wants fewer counters; with effects that add or double, applying every "enters with" effect first gives the most counters, as a player would choose. Recorded for the user to accept or ask for the exact behavior: the user accepted it for now, to be fixed later (the user, Nov 12). Audit (decision-audit-1, Nov 20): the Question's Master Biomancer quote matched to its Oracle text ("equal to this creature's power", not "Master Biomancer's power").
- Affects: Vorinclex, Monstrous Raider (an opponent's) with Master Biomancer, Bramblewood Paragon-style "enters with an additional counter" effects, copy exceptions (Spark Double) and tribute.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/actions.rs` (`perform_move`: the counters it enters with, replaced after the move's replacement effects).
- Tests: `tests/cr/r614_audit_counters.rs` (`an_opponents_vorinclex_halves_all_the_counters_a_feeder_enters_with_under_master_biomancer`).

## entering-would-be-controller-chooses: For a permanent entering the battlefield, the player it would enter under the control of chooses among replacement effects
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 616.1 "If two or more replacement and/or prevention effects are attempting to modify the way an event affects an object or player, the affected object’s controller (or its owner if it has no controller) or the affected player chooses one to apply, following the steps listed below. ..." / 614.12 "... To determine which replacement effects apply and how they apply, check the characteristics of the permanent as it would exist on the battlefield, taking into account replacement effects that have already modified how it enters the battlefield (see rule 616.1), ..." / Gather Specimens ruling (2008-10-01): "If two or more players have each cast Gather Specimens during the same turn and a creature would enter, the creature's would-be controller (the controller of the creature spell, for example) chooses one of the applicable Gather Specimens to apply. Then the new would-be controller of the creature repeats this process among the remaining Gather Specimens, and so on, until there are no more possible Gather Specimens effects to apply." / Crafty Cutpurse ruling (2018-01-19): "If two or more players each have resolved Crafty Cutpurse’s triggered ability and a token would be created, the token’s would-be controller chooses one of the applicable Crafty Cutpurse effects to apply. Then the new would-be controller of the token repeats this process among the remaining Crafty Cutpurse effects, and so on, until there are no more possible such effects to apply. Each effect can be applied to the token only once this way." / Land Equilibrium ruling (2009-10-01): "If a land that would enter has its own "If [this land] would enter" replacement effect, the player who will control that land chooses which replacement effect to apply."
- Question: Who chooses among the replacement effects that would modify how a permanent enters: the controller of the spell (or the owner of the card, which has no controller in a graveyard, hand, library or exile), or the player it would enter under the control of?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 12 (as recommended): "I agree with all recommendations". The player it would enter under the control of, as the effects applied so far have it (`Game::entering_controller`): a creature spell an opponent's Gather Specimens takes has the remaining choices (another Gather Specimens, that player's copy effects) made by the new would-be controller; a card put onto the battlefield "under your control" from an opponent's graveyard has them made by the player putting it there. Simultaneous entries are ordered for these choices in APNAP order by the same player; a player's own permanents entering together, in the order that player chooses where it can matter (`simultaneous-entries-ordered-by-controller`).
- Alternatives: The spell's controller or the card's owner throughout (the engine's previous behavior): contradicts the Gather Specimens ruling for spells; for a card with no controller, the parenthetical of 616.1 read literally gives its owner the choice.
- Why: The rulings for Gather Specimens and Crafty Cutpurse have the would-be controller choose, and the new one after a control change; Land Equilibrium's has "the player who will control that land" choose; and 614.12 judges the entering permanent as it would exist on the battlefield, under that player's control. No ruling addresses a card from a zone where it has no controller, and 616.1's "or its owner if it has no controller" can be read to apply; the user decided on Nov 12 that the would-be controller chooses there too (Reanimate on an opponent's card), as in the Gather Specimens ruling.
- Affects: Gather Specimens, Crafty Cutpurse's entering creatures, Xantcha, and reanimation or theft of an opponent's card ("put onto the battlefield under your control") with two or more replacement effects that need ordering (Essence of the Wild and Infinite Reflection, Master Biomancer and Spike Feeder's own counters).

## simultaneous-damage-counters-one-event: The counters infect and wither damage dealt at the same time causes are put on each recipient as one event
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 120.3d "Damage dealt to a creature by a source with wither and/or infect causes that source’s controller to put that many -1/-1 counters on that creature." / 120.3b "Damage dealt to a player by a source with infect causes that source’s controller to give the player that many poison counters." / 120.4c "Third, damage that’s been dealt is processed into its results, as modified by replacement effects that interact with those results (such as life loss or counters)." / Vizier of Remedies ruling (2017-04-18): "If multiple creatures with wither and/or infect deal damage to a creature at once, Vizier of Remedies causes only one counter fewer to be put on that creature. - Question: Damage from several sources at once (combat damage) causes the -1/-1 or poison counters of each source to be put. Is that one counter-putting event for each recipient, or one per source?
- Decision: The engine gathers the counters that damage dealt at the same time causes and puts them, after all of that damage is dealt, as one event for each recipient and kind of counter (the first source standing for them as the event's source); counters different players put are separate events (`damage-counters-put-by-different-players-are-separate-events`). What a replacement effect does right after that event (Melira, the Living Cure's "and you can't get additional poison counters this turn") is done before the next one. Toxic's poison counters (CR 120.3g, 702.164c) are put afterwards, as events of their own (`infect-and-toxic-poison-counters-are-separate-events`).
- Alternatives: One event per source (what the engine did before): Vizier of Remedies and other "one or more counters" replacement effects would apply once per source, against the ruling.
- Why: The Vizier ruling; CR 120.4c processes the damage that has been dealt into its results together.
- Affects: Vizier of Remedies, Melira, the Living Cure, Vorinclex, Monstrous Raider, Hapatra and other effects on putting -1/-1 or poison counters, with wither and infect damage.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/actions.rs` (`Game::put_batched_damage_counters`, `Game::put_damage_counters`).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p086_counters.rs` (`vizier_of_remedies_one_fewer_counter_for_simultaneous_damage`, `melira_the_living_cure_infect_and_toxic_damage_give_one_poison_counter`).

- Status: decided
- Rules: 120.3d "Damage dealt to a creature by a source with wither and/or infect causes that source’s controller to put that many -1/-1 counters on that creature." / Vizier of Remedies ruling (2017-04-18): "If multiple creatures with wither and/or infect deal damage to a creature at once, Vizier of Remedies causes only one counter fewer to be put on that creature. - Why: CR 120.3d names the source's controller as the one putting the counters; the ruling's example situation has one such player.
- Affects: Vizier of Remedies, Vorinclex, Monstrous Raider, Melira, the Living Cure and other effects on putting -1/-1 or poison counters, with wither and infect damage from sources controlled by different players at once (multiplayer).

## simultaneous-entries-ordered-by-controller: The replacement effects of a player's permanents entering at the same time are applied in the order that player chooses (asked only when the order can matter)
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 616.1 "If two or more replacement and/or prevention effects are attempting to modify the way an event affects an object or player, the affected object’s controller (or its owner if it has no controller) or the affected player chooses one to apply, following the steps listed below. If two or more players have to make these choices at the same time, choices are made in APNAP order (see rule 101.4)." / 616.1e "Any of the applicable replacement and/or prevention effects may be chosen." / 616.1f "Once the chosen effect has been applied, this process is repeated (taking into account only replacement or prevention effects that would now be applicable) until there are no more left to apply." / 616.1g's example "A player is instructed to create a token that’s a copy of Voice of All, which has the ability “As this creature enters, choose a color.” Doubling Season has an ability that reads “If an effect would create one or more tokens under your control, it creates twice that many of those tokens instead.” Because entering the battlefield is an event contained within the event of creating a token, the effect of Doubling Season must be applied first, and then the effects of the two Voice of All tokens may be applied in either order." (in every archived CR from the one dated 2017-04-28 on, then under 616.1f) / 614.12 "Some replacement effects modify how a permanent enters the battlefield. ... To determine which replacement effects apply and how they apply, check the characteristics of the permanent as it would exist on the battlefield, taking into account replacement effects that have already modified how it enters the battlefield (see rule 616.1), ..." / 614.12b "If multiple replacement effects that require choices from a player would modify how multiple permanents enter the battlefield simultaneously, that player may not make choices for those effects that would cause the combined costs of those effects to not be payable." / Thief of Blood ruling (2015-11-04): "If more than one Thief of Blood enters the battlefield at the same time, you can apply the replacement effect of any of them. That one will enter with additional +1/+1 counters due to its own ability and the others will not." / Lakeside Shack ruling (2024-09-20): "If one of these lands is entering at the same time as a "shock land" ..., use the life totals of all players before choices are made for that shock land's replacement effect to determine whether or not the land enters tapped. ..." / Ixidron ruling (2006-09-25): "If Ixidron and another creature are entering at the same time, the other creature enters face up." / Rhythm of the Wild ruling (2019-05-03): "If Rhythm of the Wild leaves the battlefield at the same time that a nontoken creature enters the battlefield (most likely because that creature has a replacement effect, such as that of Rescuer Sphinx), that creature still gets a +1/+1 counter or haste."
- Question: Several permanents enter the battlefield at the same time under one player's control, each with replacement effects of its own that modify how it enters ("As this creature enters, remove all counters from all permanents. This creature enters with a +1/+1 counter on it for each counter removed this way.", "This creature enters with a +1/+1 counter on it for each creature card in your graveyard.", "You may have this creature enter as a copy of any creature on the battlefield."). In which order are those effects applied, when applying one changes what another does? (616.1's words are about the effects modifying how an event affects an object.)
- Decision: In the order the player chooses: before the effects of each of those permanents are applied, the player picks which of the remaining ones comes next (a `ChooseReplacement` question naming each permanent and its effects; 616.1f's "this process is repeated"). The question is asked only when the order can matter: two or more of the player's entering permanents have effects that read the game or change it, and at least one of those changes the game as it's applied (removes counters, exiles, sacrifices or discards objects, returns a permanent to hand, turns permanents face down, including what's done instead of entering: Lotus Vale's "If this land would enter, sacrifice two untapped lands instead.", Mox Diamond's "you may discard a land card instead"; any effect not known to change nothing counts as changing it, riot's and tribute's choices among them, so some questions can't change anything). An effect another permanent generates ("Each other non-Human creature you control enters with an additional +1/+1 counter on it.", Grumgully, the Generous) counts as reading the game: applying another permanent's effects first can change that permanent (Ixidron turning it face down, after which the effect no longer applies, 616.1f: "taking into account only replacement or prevention effects that would now be applicable"). Which effects apply to each permanent is determined as the event is proposed, before any of them is applied (614.12): one whose source another entering permanent's effect made leave the battlefield still applies, as Rhythm of the Wild's ruling says of "a nontoken creature" entering as Rhythm of the Wild leaves (a creature entering with the Rescuer Sphinx that returns it keeps riot), so for those effects the order doesn't matter; which effects a permanent keeps when its own effects make their source leave is `entry-effects-of-a-leaving-source`. Choices, conditions, revealing a card, "enters tapped", a fixed number of counters, and life or energy payments (paid only once every choice is made, 614.12b and the Lakeside Shack ruling; `entry_costs.rs`) change nothing as they're applied, so permanents with only those are applied in the order of the moves without a question. Default (no answer or an invalid one): the next one in the order of the moves, the engine's earlier fixed order. Each permanent's own effects are applied together, with that player's 616.1 choices among them as before. Different players' permanents: in APNAP order, each player choosing for the permanents entering under their control (`entering-would-be-controller-chooses`), as before.
- Alternatives: Keep the order of the moves (the engine's earlier behavior): the player loses the choice the 616.1g example and Thief of Blood's ruling give them (Ixidron and Clone, Thief of Blood and Sin, Sutured Ghoul and Golgari Grave-Troll entering together come out one way only). Ask whenever two or more permanents with such effects enter, also two shock lands or two Voice of All: the same games, with questions whose answer can't change anything. Choose effect by effect across the permanents (interleaving one permanent's own effects with another's): would differ only if another permanent's effect could come between two of one permanent's own with a different result. Determine each permanent's effects when its turn in the order comes (the engine before this item's review): a Rescuer Sphinx applied first and returning Rhythm of the Wild took riot from a creature entering with it, against that ruling's words.
- Why: 616.1 has the controller choose the replacement effects one at a time (616.1e–f), and its own example applies two simultaneously entering tokens' "as enters" effects "in either order"; Thief of Blood's ruling says the same for several Thieves ("you can apply the replacement effect of any of them"). The sources agree, so the order across a player's entering permanents is that player's (decided by the 616.1g example and the ruling). The Lakeside Shack ruling is why payments don't make the order matter: whatever the order, life paid for a shock land isn't paid before the other lands' conditions are checked. Rhythm of the Wild's ruling is why effects whose source leaves the battlefield meanwhile don't either: they apply as they were determined when the event was proposed. Asking only where the order can matter, and the default, are the engine's policy for a choice the rules give the player. Audit (decision-audit-1, Nov 20), open for the user (results in `docs/agents/items/decision-audit-1.md`): no ruling covers a source that stays on the battlefield but loses its abilities while the entering permanents' effects are applied (Ixidron turning Grumgully, the Generous face down), which this entry treats unlike a source that leaves (Rhythm of the Wild's ruling); and the engine adds back all the effects determined as the event was proposed once any permanent then on the battlefield has left it (`Game::replace_rec`), so when Ixidron, Rescuer Sphinx and Grizzly Bears enter together and Ixidron turns Grumgully face down, the Bears still get Grumgully's counter if the Sphinx returns another permanent, and none without the Sphinx (seen in a scratch test; no test pins it).
- Affects: Thief of Blood (with another Thief or Sin, Unending Cataclysm), Sutured Ghoul, Living Lore, Mimeoplasm, Revered One and the other "as this enters, exile ... from your graveyard" creatures with Golgari Grave-Troll and other "for each creature card in your graveyard" entries, Ixidron with Clone and the other "enter as a copy" creatures, Rescuer Sphinx with Crowd-Control Warden, devour (`kw/devour.rs`); the "If this land would enter, sacrifice ... instead" lands (Lotus Vale, Scorched Ruins, Heart of Yavimaya, Soldevi Excavations, Balduvian Trading Post, Kjeldoran Outpost, Lake of the Dead) and Mox Diamond; effects other permanents generate (Grumgully, the Generous, Master Biomancer, Rhythm of the Wild's riot) with Rescuer Sphinx or Ixidron.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/entry_replacement_order.rs` (`EntryOrder`, `effect_reach`, `action_reach`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/actions.rs` (`move_objects`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/replacement.rs` (`entry_snapshot`, `replace_entering`, `EntrySnapshot`).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/entering_together_order.rs` (`ixidron_and_clone_entering_together_in_the_order_their_controller_chooses`, `thieves_of_blood_entering_together_the_chosen_one_gets_the_counters`, `thief_of_blood_and_sin_entering_together_in_the_chosen_order`, `sutured_ghoul_and_golgari_grave_troll_entering_together_in_the_chosen_order`, `no_order_question_when_no_entering_permanents_effect_changes_the_game`, `each_players_entering_permanents_have_their_effects_applied_in_apnap_order`, `token_copies_doubling_season_doubles_have_their_effects_applied_in_the_chosen_order`, `mox_diamond_and_golgari_grave_troll_entering_together_in_the_chosen_order`, `rhythm_of_the_wild_returned_as_another_creature_enters_still_gives_it_riot`, `an_effect_another_permanent_generates_is_ordered_too`, `cards_from_both_graveyards_entering_under_your_control_are_ordered_by_you`).

## entry-effects-of-a-leaving-source: The effects modifying how a permanent enters that applied just before their source left the battlefield still apply
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: provisional
- Rules: 614.12 "Some replacement effects modify how a permanent enters the battlefield. (See rules 614.1c–d.) Such effects may come from the permanent itself if they affect only that permanent (as opposed to a general subset of permanents that includes it). They may also come from other sources. To determine which replacement effects apply and how they apply, check the characteristics of the permanent as it would exist on the battlefield, taking into account replacement effects that have already modified how it enters the battlefield (see rule 616.1), continuous effects from the permanent’s own static abilities that would apply to it once it’s on the battlefield, and continuous effects that already exist and would apply to the permanent." / 616.1f "Once the chosen effect has been applied, this process is repeated (taking into account only replacement or prevention effects that would now be applicable) until there are no more left to apply." / 616.2 "A replacement or prevention effect can become applicable to an event as the result of another replacement or prevention effect that modifies the event." / 611.3b "The effect applies at all times that the permanent generating it is on the battlefield or the object generating it is in the appropriate zone." / 613.1 "The values of an object’s characteristics are determined by starting with the actual object. ... Then all applicable continuous effects are applied in a series of layers in the following order:" / Rhythm of the Wild ruling (2019-05-03): "If Rhythm of the Wild leaves the battlefield at the same time that a nontoken creature enters the battlefield (most likely because that creature has a replacement effect, such as that of Rescuer Sphinx), that creature still gets a +1/+1 counter or haste." / Rescuer Sphinx rulings (2019-05-03): "You choose whether to return a nonland permanent and which one to return for Rescuer Sphinx’s ability while it’s entering the battlefield. No player may take actions between the time you choose which permanent to return and the time Rescuer Sphinx is on the battlefield with a +1/+1 counter." and "If the returned permanent had an ability that would otherwise have triggered when Rescuer Sphinx enters the battlefield (such as that of Kronch Wrangler), that ability won’t trigger." / Clone ruling (2025-10-02): "Any enters abilities of the copied creature will trigger when Clone enters. Any "as [this creature] enters" or "[this creature] enters with" abilities of the chosen creature will also work." / Grumgully, the Generous ruling (2019-10-04): "If a non-Human creature enters the battlefield as a copy of a Human creature, it won't get a +1/+1 counter. Similarly, if a Human enters as a non-Human creature, it will get a +1/+1 counter." / Metallic Mimic ruling (2017-02-09): "Even though Metallic Mimic is a Shapeshifter, other Shapeshifter creatures you control won't get a +1/+1 counter unless you chose Shapeshifter as Metallic Mimic entered the battlefield. If you do, that creature must be entering the battlefield as a Shapeshifter to get a +1/+1 counter. It won't get a +1/+1 counter if it enters the battlefield as a copy of a non-Shapeshifter creature." / Master Biomancer ruling (2013-01-24): "To determine how many additional +1/+1 counters a creature enters with, use Master Biomancer's power as that creature is entering."
- Question: While a permanent's entry is being modified, one of the effects applied (an "as this enters, return a nonland permanent you control" choice, Rescuer Sphinx; devour's sacrifice) makes a permanent that generated another of the effects leave the battlefield. Rhythm of the Wild's ruling says such an effect still applies, but which of that permanent's effects: those determined as the event was proposed, or those that applied to the event just before the permanent left, with the effects applied so far? They differ when an effect applied before it left changed what applies: a copy, control, back-face or face-down effect (Clone entering as a copy of Rescuer Sphinx), or one that changed what the permanent would be (Master Biomancer's "as a Mutant", after which Metallic Mimic naming Mutant applies, 616.2).
- Decision: Those that applied just before it left. The engine determines the effects that apply at each step of the entry's replacement until a permanent leaves (`EntrySnapshot`), from the permanent as it would exist with the effects applied so far (614.12), and once one has left it adds back those not yet applied, as long as the permanent still enters the same way (no copy, control, back-face or face-down effect applied since). A Clone entering as a copy of Rescuer Sphinx that returns Grumgully, the Generous gets Grumgully's counter as Rescuer Sphinx itself does (2 counters); Rescuer Sphinx entering under Master Biomancer and Metallic Mimic naming Mutant, with Biomancer's effect applied first and then the Sphinx's returning the Mimic, gets the Mimic's counter too (4 counters).
- Alternatives: The effects determined as the event was proposed, determined again only once a copy, control, back-face or face-down effect changes how it enters: the same for the Clone, but the Sphinx gets 3 counters, though Metallic Mimic's effect applied to the event when the Mimic left (616.2). A permanent leaving as part of the entry treated as still on the battlefield until the entry is done, its effects applying even if they'd become applicable only later (the Sphinx's effect applied first, returning the Mimic, then Biomancer's: still the Mimic's counter): more than the ruling says, against 611.3b, and the engine would need the departed permanent's abilities. The engine before November 2026 kept the effects determined as the event was proposed even after a copy effect changed what applies, so the Clone entering as a Sphinx lost Grumgully's counter.
- Why: 614.12 determines the applicable effects "taking into account replacement effects that have already modified how it enters the battlefield", and 616.2 makes an effect applicable through another; Rhythm of the Wild's ruling keeps an effect whose source leaves "at the same time" as the creature enters, whenever that effect became applicable, and Rescuer Sphinx's rulings put the return inside the entry (no player acts in between; the returned permanent's triggered abilities don't see the Sphinx enter, so it's no longer on the battlefield, 611.3b, for any effect that would only become applicable after it left). Clone's, Grumgully's and Metallic Mimic's rulings show that what applies is decided after the copy effect. Provisional: no ruling covers an effect that became applicable through another (616.2) and whose source then leaves. Not covered: an effect whose source left before a copy, control, back-face or face-down effect of the same entry was applied, which only another permanent entering at the same time can cause (Clone and Rescuer Sphinx entering together under Rhythm of the Wild, the Sphinx's effects applied first returning Rhythm: once the Clone's copy effect is applied, riot is no longer among its effects); and the effects added back include those whose source is still on the battlefield but no longer generates them (the Ixidron case of `simultaneous-entries-ordered-by-controller`).
- Affects: Rescuer Sphinx and devour creatures with the effects of permanents they return or sacrifice (Rhythm of the Wild's riot, Grumgully, the Generous, Metallic Mimic, Master Biomancer); Clone and the other "enter as a copy" creatures copying them.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/replacement.rs` (`EntrySnapshot`, `Game::replace_rec`).

## leaving-the-stack-exile-among-other-replacements: A spell's "exile this card instead of putting it anywhere else" (flashback and the like) is chosen among the other effects replacing its move to a graveyard
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 702.34a "Flashback appears on some instants and sorceries. It represents two static abilities: one that functions while the card is in a player’s graveyard and another that functions while the card is on the stack. “Flashback [cost]” means “You may cast this card from your graveyard if the resulting spell is an instant or sorcery spell by paying [cost] rather than paying its mana cost” and “If the flashback cost was paid, exile this card instead of putting it anywhere else any time it would leave the stack.” ..." / 702.127a "... “If this spell was cast from a graveyard, exile it instead of putting it anywhere else any time it would leave the stack.”" (aftermath; likewise 702.133a jump-start, "If this spell was cast using its jump-start ability, exile this card instead of putting it anywhere else any time it would leave the stack.", and 702.180a harmonize, "If the harmonize cost was paid, exile this card instead of putting it anywhere else any time it would leave the stack.") / 614.15 "Some replacement effects are not continuous effects. Rather, they are an effect of a resolving spell or ability that replace part or all of that spell or ability’s own effect(s). Such effects are called self-replacement effects. ... When applying replacement effects to an event, self-replacement effects are applied before other replacement effects." / 616.1 "If two or more replacement and/or prevention effects are attempting to modify the way an event affects an object or player, the affected object’s controller (or its owner if it has no controller) or the affected player chooses one to apply, following the steps listed below. ..." / 616.1e "Any of the applicable replacement and/or prevention effects may be chosen." / 701.6a "To counter a spell or ability means to cancel it, removing it from the stack. It doesn’t resolve and none of its effects occur. A countered spell is put into its owner’s graveyard." / 608.2n "As the final part of an instant or sorcery spell’s resolution, the spell is put into its owner’s graveyard. ..." / Feather, the Redeemed rulings (2021-03-19): "If another replacement effect instructs you to exile an instant or sorcery spell, such as that of Dreadhorde Arcanist or the flashback keyword, you may choose to apply Feather's replacement effect first. If you do, Feather's delayed triggered ability will return that card to your hand." and "If an instant or sorcery spell's own instructions tell you to exile it or put it anywhere else, it won't try to be put into your graveyard or exiled with Feather's effect, so you won't return it to your hand." / Memory Lapse ruling (2016-06-08): "Memory Lapse has a self-replacement effect that replaces the spell going to the graveyard before any other effect can replace that event. If the spell was cast using flashback, however, flashback will change the spell's destination from its owner's library to exile." / Think Twice ruling (2024-11-08): "A spell cast using flashback will always be exiled afterward, whether it resolves, is countered, or leaves the stack in some other way."
- Question: A spell cast with flashback (or aftermath, jump-start, harmonize) is countered or resolves while other effects would replace its being put into its owner's graveyard (Rest in Peace, Leyline of the Void, Wheel of Sun and Moon, Dauthi Voidwalker's "instead exile it with a void counter on it", Valgavoth, Terror Eater's "exile it instead", after which Valgavoth's controller may play it). Does the keyword's exile apply first, as part of the spell, or does the spell's controller choose among it and the others?
- Decision: The spell's controller chooses (616.1). The spell is proposed to its owner's graveyard, countered (`Game::counter_or_fizzle`) or resolving (`keyword_impls::choose_resolved_spell_destination`), and the keyword's effect (its static ability, functioning on the stack) is one of the replacement effects of that move, applied after any self-replacement effect of the effect countering it (616.1a: Memory Lapse, Delay). The choice is asked as any 616.1 choice is, also when every order exiles the card (Rest in Peace, Leyline of the Void), and the keyword's effect is listed first (a spell leaving the stack lists its own effects first, `default-first-listed-choice`), so a default answer exiles the card with the keyword's effect, as the engine did before, with no void counter. Applied first, Dauthi Voidwalker's effect exiles the card with a void counter (and flashback's no longer applies to a card already going to exile); Valgavoth's exiles it with Valgavoth. Feather, the Redeemed's effect, applied to the resolving spell outside the replacement pipeline, is offered against the keyword's first, as before; not chosen, the move goes on to the graveyard's effects. A spell's own instruction ("Exile this spell.", paradigm) isn't a replacement effect: the spell goes there and no effect replacing a move to a graveyard applies (Feather's second ruling).
- Alternatives: Apply the keyword's exile directly, with no choice (the engine before November 2026): no question where every choice exiles the card, but the spell's controller can never choose Dauthi Voidwalker's or Valgavoth's effect, though the rules let them.
- Why: 702.34a (and 702.127a, 702.133a, 702.180a) make the exile a static ability of the card, and 614.15 limits self-replacement effects to "an effect of a resolving spell or ability that replace part or all of that spell or ability’s own effect(s)": putting a countered or resolved spell into a graveyard is a rule (701.6a, 608.2n), not the spell's effect. So 616.1 applies, as Feather's ruling says of flashback ("you may choose to apply Feather's replacement effect first"), and Memory Lapse's ruling puts flashback after a self-replacement effect, not with it. No ruling names Dauthi Voidwalker or Valgavoth with flashback; applying their effect first is only ever worse for the spell's controller, but it's a choice the rules give them. Asking when every order exiles the card is the engine's usual 616.1 behavior (it asks among effects whatever they do).
- Affects: flashback, aftermath, jump-start and harmonize spells, and spells granted flashback (Snapcaster Mage), with Rest in Peace, Leyline of the Void, Wheel of Sun and Moon, Dauthi Voidwalker, Valgavoth, Terror Eater and other "would be put into a graveyard" replacements; Feather, the Redeemed.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/stack.rs` (`counter_or_fizzle`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/keyword_impls.rs` (`choose_resolved_spell_destination`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/replacement.rs` (`replace_rec`: a spell's own effects listed first); `crates/mtg-engine/src/kw/mod.rs` (`anywhere_else_destination`, `own_instruction_destination`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/kw/flashback.rs`, `aftermath.rs`, `jump_start.rs`, `harmonize.rs` (the derived replacement abilities).
- Tests: `tests/cr/r616_001_spells_leaving_the_stack.rs` (`a_countered_flashback_spell_under_rest_in_peace_is_exiled_by_the_effect_its_controller_chooses`, `a_flashback_spell_leaving_the_stack_under_dauthi_voidwalker_gets_a_void_counter_only_if_chosen`, `by_default_a_spell_leaving_the_stack_is_exiled_by_its_own_keyword_with_no_void_counter`: flashback, jump-start, harmonize and aftermath); `tests/rulings/r_s14_rebound.rs` (`flashback_exiles_the_card_whatever_else_would_apply`, resolving under Rest in Peace); `tests/rulings/r_p141_returns.rs` (`feather_can_apply_before_flashback`); `tests/rulings/r_p158_misc.rs` (`memory_lapse_puts_the_spell_on_top_unless_flashback_exiles_it`).

## face-down-entry-checked-as-the-face-down-card: "[Cards] in [zones] can't enter the battlefield" checks a card put onto the battlefield face down as the face-down card the effect makes it
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: provisional
- Rules: 708.3 "Objects that are put onto the battlefield face down are turned face down before they enter the battlefield, so the permanent’s enters-the-battlefield abilities won’t trigger (if triggered) or have any effect (if static)." / 708.2 "Face-down spells and face-down permanents have no characteristics other than those listed by the ability or rules that allowed the spell or permanent to be face down. ..." / 708.2a "... A permanent that enters the battlefield face down also has these characteristics unless otherwise specified by the effect that put it onto the battlefield face down or allowed it to be cast face down. ..." / 701.40a "To manifest a card, turn it face down. It becomes a 2/2 face-down creature card with no text, no name, no subtypes, and no mana cost. Put that card onto the battlefield face down. ..." / 614.17d "Some “can’t” effects modify how a permanent enters the battlefield or whether it can enter the battlefield. ... To determine which “can’t” effects apply, check the characteristics of the permanent as it would exist on the battlefield, ..." / Yedora, Grave Gardener ruling (2021-04-16): "The face-down card has no name or colors. Its only type is land, its only subtype is Forest, and its only ability is "{T}: Add {G}."" / Grafdigger's Cage rulings (2019-07-12): "Look at the card as it exists in your graveyard to determine whether it can enter the battlefield. For example, Sculpting Steel can be put onto the battlefield as a copy of a creature, but Phyrexian Metamorph can't be put onto the battlefield, even if it would copy a noncreature artifact." and "Manifesting a card from a graveyard or library is an impossible action while Grafdigger's Cage is on the battlefield." / Weathered Runestone ruling (2021-02-05): "Look at the card as it exists in your graveyard or library to determine whether it can enter the battlefield. If it’s a double-faced card, use its front-face characteristics only." / Kunoros, Hound of Athreos ruling (2020-01-24): "Look at the card as it exists in your graveyard to determine whether it can enter the battlefield. ..." / Soulless Jailer ruling (2023-02-04): "Putting a permanent card onto the battlefield from a graveyard is an impossible action while Soulless Jailer is on the battlefield."
- Question: Yedora, Grave Gardener returns a creature card from its owner's graveyard "to the battlefield face down under its owner's control. It's a Forest land." Is the card checked against "Creature cards in graveyards and libraries can't enter the battlefield" (Grafdigger's Cage, Kunoros) and "Nonland permanent cards in graveyards and libraries can't enter the battlefield" (Weathered Runestone) as the face-up creature card in the graveyard, or as the face-down Forest land card it is turned into before it enters?
- Decision: As the face-down card: turned face down before it enters (708.3), it has only the characteristics the effect lists (708.2, 708.2a), so Yedora's face-down card is a Forest land card, which Grafdigger's Cage and Weathered Runestone don't stop and Soulless Jailer ("Permanent cards in graveyards can't enter the battlefield.") does. A card put onto the battlefield face down with no characteristics listed (manifest, cloak) is checked as a 2/2 face-down creature card, which Grafdigger's Cage stops, as its ruling says of manifesting (`Game::cant_enter`, with the hypothetical face-down entry).
- Alternatives: Check the face-up card in the graveyard (the rulings' "Look at the card as it exists in your graveyard"): Yedora's return is stopped by Grafdigger's Cage, Kunoros and Weathered Runestone; manifesting would still be stopped by the Cage (701.40a makes the card a creature card first), so only the effects that list other characteristics change.
- Why: 708.3 turns the card face down before it enters, and 701.40a shows the face-down card is what's checked: manifesting is impossible under Grafdigger's Cage only because the manifested card is "a 2/2 face-down creature card" by then (a manifested land card, face up a noncreature card, would otherwise enter). The rulings' "as it exists in your graveyard" separates the card in the graveyard from what it would become on the battlefield (Sculpting Steel, Phyrexian Metamorph), which a face-down card isn't. Provisional: no ruling covers a face-down entry whose effect lists characteristics (Yedora's), and a judge could read "as it exists in your graveyard" as the face-up card.
- Affects: Yedora, Grave Gardener with Grafdigger's Cage, Kunoros, Hound of Athreos, Weathered Runestone and Soulless Jailer; other effects returning a card face down with listed characteristics.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/replacement.rs` (`Game::cant_enter`, the face-down branch; `with_hypothetical_entry`).
- Tests: `tests/cr/r614_017d_face_down_entry_restrictions.rs` (`yedoras_face_down_forest_is_checked_as_a_forest_land_card`); `tests/rulings/r_p188_graveyard.rs` (`grafdiggers_cage_makes_manifesting_from_a_library_impossible`).

## parts-of-one-modified-event-unordered: The parts of one modified event, and what a replacement effect left for after it, happen in one step whose order isn't pinned
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 614.6 "If an event is replaced, it never happens. A modified event occurs instead, which may in turn trigger abilities. ..." / 603.3 "Once an ability has triggered, its controller puts it on the stack as an object that’s not a card the next time a player would receive priority. ..." / 603.3b "If multiple abilities have triggered since the last time a player received priority, the abilities are placed on the stack in a two-part process. First, each player, in APNAP order, puts each triggered ability they control with a trigger condition that isn’t another ability triggering on the stack in any order they choose. ..."
- Question: A modified event can have several parts. Eye for an Eye's "instead that source deals that much damage to you and Eye for an Eye deals that much damage to that source's controller", once Undead Alchemist's "instead that player mills that many cards" has replaced the damage to you, is [you mill that many cards, and Eye for an Eye deals that much damage to that source's controller]. In which order do the parts happen, and does the engine have to keep one?
- Decision: In one step, in an order that isn't part of the rules and isn't pinned: no state-based action is checked and no triggered ability is put on the stack between the parts, or between them and the instructions a replacement effect left for after them (`rider-order`). The engine deals Eye for an Eye's damage after the milled cards move, or before they move when a replacement effect watches the mill (Bruvac the Grandiloquent's "If an opponent would mill one or more cards, they mill twice that many cards instead."); either way the triggered abilities they cause are listed the same, the mill's first, for their controller to order (603.3b), and only the order of the events in the event feed differs. docs/MUTATION.md counts a mutant that changes only that order as equivalent (replacement.rs `2885:9`, `2889:13`, the fast path of `replace_action`).
- Alternatives: Fix an order for the parts (as the text lists them, or the riders always after the keyword action) and pin the event feed: no rule asks for one, and nothing in the game can see it.
- Why: 614.6 has the modified event occur instead, as one event; triggered abilities wait until a player would receive priority (603.3), and their controller orders those that triggered together (603.3b), so the order of the parts can't change what happens.
- Affects: Eye for an Eye with Undead Alchemist or Szadek, Lord of Secrets (their "instead ... mills that many cards"); other replacement effects that leave instructions for after an event that another effect turns into a keyword action.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/replacement.rs` (`replace_action`, `statics_or_instances_have_action`: when an effect watches the keyword action, the instructions left so far run before it); `crates/mtg-engine/src/actions.rs` (`run_post_replacement_effects`, also run by the mill's moves).
- Tests: `tests/cr/r614_006_parts_of_one_modified_event.rs` (`a_riders_damage_and_the_mill_that_replaced_its_event_trigger_together_whatever_their_order`).
