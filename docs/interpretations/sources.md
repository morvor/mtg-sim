# Sources: Oracle text, rulings and the Comprehensive Rules

Judgment calls about which source the engine follows when the Comprehensive Rules, a card's
Oracle text and Wizards' rulings (Scryfall's `data/rulings.jsonl.gz`) seem to disagree
(CR 101.1, 108.1). Each ruling set aside under these entries is listed in
`docs/rulings-exemptions/` and logged, with its evidence, in `docs/RULING_DISCREPANCIES.md`
for human review and reporting to Wizards of the Coast. Format and kinds:
`docs/agents/items/interpretation-register.md`; index: `docs/INTERPRETATIONS.md`.

## ruling-contradicting-oracle-gives-way: A ruling whose words contradict the card's own Oracle text gives way to the Oracle text
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 108.1 "Use the Oracle card reference when determining a card’s wording. A card’s Oracle text can be found using the Gatherer card database at Gatherer.Wizards.com." / 101.1 "Whenever a card’s text directly contradicts these rules, the card takes precedence. ..." / The user's decision of Nov 5: "It looks like some rulings are applied as a template, and are therefore occasionally incorrect. This is the case for Homicide Investigator and the similar cases you mentioned. I agree with all of your recommendations. We should still keep a detailed account of all of the instances though, so they can be checked by a human, and reported to Wizards of the Coast if erroneous."
- Question: A Wizards ruling's words contradict the card's own Oracle text, and no dated rules change or Oracle change explains the difference (no reading reconciles them, and the ruling isn't older than a change that would make it outdated). Such rulings are often a ruling written for a similar card, applied to this one with the name swapped. Which source does the engine follow, and what happens to the ruling's citation?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 5. The Oracle text (108.1). The part of the ruling that contradicts it gives way: it is listed in `docs/rulings-exemptions/` with a reason naming this policy, the date of the decision and the ruling's likely origin, and the ruling is logged in `docs/RULING_DISCREPANCIES.md` (kind "template error"). The parts of the ruling that agree with the Oracle text stay cited by tests. This policy doesn't cover a ruling older than a dated rules or Oracle change (an outdated ruling: exempt as outdated, with the change named and dated, and logged too), a ruling the data attaches to the wrong card (tested on the card it describes and cited under the card it's attached to; never exempt), or a ruling that contradicts other rulings on a question the CR leaves open (decided case by case, as `history-counts-players-who-left` set Gnoll War Band's aside). A contradiction with no clear explanation still goes to the coordinator as a conflict case before anything is exempted.
- Alternatives: (a) Follow the ruling: each case would need a hand-written override of compilable Oracle text, against the card's own words (Homicide Investigator's "This ability triggers only once each turn"; Rimescale Dragon's "Creatures") and, for a copied ruling, against the rulings of the card it was copied from. (b) Leave such rulings uncited and unexempt (the practice from Nov 4 until this decision): the engine was the same, but the cases stayed OPEN in the coverage report with no record of why.
- Why: 108.1 makes the Oracle text the card's wording, and a ruling is an explanation of that wording, not a source of its own; where the two can't both be true and nothing dated explains it, the ruling is the one in error. The known cases fit the user's diagnosis: Homicide Investigator's 2024-04-12 ruling is Midnight Reaper's ruling (2018-10-05) with the name swapped; Rimescale Dragon's (2006-07-15) is Rimefeather Owl's same-day ruling with the name swapped; Citanul Druid's (2004-10-04) is the multiplayer note that the twelve other cards with such a note whose abilities apply only to opponents word with "opponents" (ten of them in the same 2004-10-04 batch, e.g. Malignant Growth's "In multiplayer games it affects all opponents."), with "players" instead; Combat Celebrant's (2017-04-18) restates "untap all other creatures you control" as "untaps all of your creatures". Work items ruling-conflicts-2 (cases 2 and 4, conclusion D) and register-decisions-nov5 have the evidence; the log has each case in full.
- Affects: Homicide Investigator (`homicide-investigator-triggers-once`), Rimescale Dragon (`rimescale-dragon-creatures-only`), Combat Celebrant, Citanul Druid; any later case a rulings batch finds.
- Code: none of its own: the engine compiles the Oracle text. The records: `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P005.tsv`, `rulings-P056.tsv`, `rulings-P096.tsv`, `rulings-P178.tsv`; `docs/RULING_DISCREPANCIES.md`, kept complete by `crates/mtg-data/src/discrepancies.rs` (`check`, run by `cargo test -p mtg-data` and `mtg-tools rulings-coverage --check`).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p005_dies.rs` (`homicide_investigator_investigates_once_for_creatures_dying_together`); `tests/rulings/r_p096_lockdown.rs` (`rimescale_dragon_freezes_creatures_with_ice_counters_from_any_source_but_not_other_permanents`); `tests/rulings/ruling_contradicting_oracle.rs` (`combat_celebrant_untaps_all_other_creatures_but_not_itself`, `citanul_druid_grows_for_each_opponents_artifact_spells_but_not_its_controllers`).
