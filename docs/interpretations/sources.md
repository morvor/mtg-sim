# Sources: Oracle text, rulings and the Comprehensive Rules

Judgment calls about which source the engine follows when the Comprehensive Rules, a card's
Oracle text and Wizards' rulings (Scryfall's `data/rulings.jsonl.gz`) seem to disagree
(CR 101.1, 108.1). Each ruling set aside under these entries is listed in
`docs/rulings-exemptions/` and logged, with its evidence, in `docs/RULING_DISCREPANCIES.md`
for human review and reporting to Wizards of the Coast. Format and kinds:
`docs/agents/items/interpretation-register.md`; index: `docs/INTERPRETATIONS.md`.

## ruling-contradicting-oracle-gives-way: A ruling whose words contradict the card's own Oracle text gives way to the Oracle text
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 108.1 "Use the Oracle card reference when determining a card’s wording. A card’s Oracle text can be found using the Gatherer card database at Gatherer.Wizards.com." / 101.1 "Whenever a card’s text directly contradicts these rules, the card takes precedence. ..." / The user's decision of Nov 5: "It looks like some rulings are applied as a template, and are therefore occasionally incorrect. This is the case for Homicide Investigator and the similar cases you mentioned. I agree with all of your recommendations. We should still keep a detailed account of all of the instances though, so they can be checked by a human, and reported to Wizards of the Coast if erroneous."
- Question: A Wizards ruling's words contradict the card's own Oracle text, and no dated rules change or Oracle change explains the difference (no reading reconciles them, and the ruling isn't older than a change that would make it outdated). Such rulings are often a ruling written for a similar card, applied to this one with the name swapped. Which source does the engine follow, and what happens to the ruling's citation?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 5. The Oracle text (108.1). The part of the ruling that contradicts it gives way: it is listed in `docs/rulings-exemptions/` with a reason naming this policy, the date of the decision and the ruling's likely origin, and the ruling is logged in `docs/RULING_DISCREPANCIES.md` (kind "template error", or "copying error" when the ruling as Wizards first published it, in the set's release notes, agrees with the card and the data's text doesn't; the user decided that kind on Nov 5, with this policy covering it). The parts of the ruling that agree with the Oracle text stay cited by tests. This policy doesn't cover a ruling older than a dated rules or Oracle change (an outdated ruling: exempt as outdated, with the change named and dated, and logged too), a ruling the data attaches to the wrong card (tested on the card it describes and cited under the card it's attached to; never exempt), or a ruling that contradicts other rulings on a question the CR leaves open (decided case by case, as `history-counts-players-who-left` set Gnoll War Band's aside). A contradiction with no clear explanation still goes to the coordinator as a conflict case before anything is exempted.
- Alternatives: (a) Follow the ruling: each case would need a hand-written override of compilable Oracle text, against the card's own words (Homicide Investigator's "This ability triggers only once each turn"; Rimescale Dragon's "Creatures") and, for a copied ruling, against the rulings of the card it was copied from. (b) Leave such rulings uncited and unexempt (the practice from Nov 4 until this decision): the engine was the same, but the cases stayed OPEN in the coverage report with no record of why.
- Why: 108.1 makes the Oracle text the card's wording, and a ruling is an explanation of that wording, not a source of its own; where the two can't both be true and nothing dated explains it, the ruling is the one in error. The known cases fit the user's diagnosis: Homicide Investigator's 2024-04-12 ruling is Midnight Reaper's ruling (2018-10-05) with the name swapped; Rimescale Dragon's (2006-07-15) is Rimefeather Owl's same-day ruling with the name swapped; Citanul Druid's (2004-10-04) is the multiplayer note that reads "it affects all opponents" (or "all opposing players") on the thirteen other cards with one whose abilities apply only to opponents (e.g. Malignant Growth's "In multiplayer games it affects all opponents.", the same day), with "players" instead; Combat Celebrant's (2017-04-18) restates "untap all other creatures you control" as "untaps all of your creatures". Work items ruling-conflicts-2 (cases 2 and 4, conclusion D) and register-decisions-nov5 have the evidence; the log has each case in full.
- Affects: Homicide Investigator (`homicide-investigator-triggers-once`), Rimescale Dragon (`rimescale-dragon-creatures-only`), Combat Celebrant, Citanul Druid; Run Out of Town and Sanguine Spy (work item ruling-conflicts-3, cases 1 and 5: each ruling is the card's own note in the Streets of New Capenna Release Notes, 2022-04-20, with a word changed, "controller" for "owner" and "cards you control" for "cards in your graveyard"; logged with the kind "copying error", for rulings whose first publication agrees with the card, decided by the user Nov 5 as falling under this policy); Sakashima's Protege (rulings-P067: the Clone template's "You may choose only a creature" for a card that copies "any permanent"); Taeko, the Patient Avalanche, Slinza, the Spiked Stampede, Syndicate Heavy and Sorin of House Markov (work item rulings-residue-reflexive-refund and its review: a hybrid mana symbol written as two symbols, "{U}{B}" for "{U/B}", "{1}{R}{G}" for "{1}{R/G}", "{W}{B}" for extort's "{W/B}"; the same template on Hurska Sweet-Tooth, and the extort ruling on 14 other cards, keep the hybrid symbol whole); any later case a rulings batch finds.
- Code: none of its own: the engine compiles the Oracle text. The records: `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P005.tsv`, `rulings-P056.tsv`, `rulings-P093.tsv`, `rulings-P096.tsv`, `rulings-P140.tsv`, `rulings-P178.tsv`, `rulings-P067.tsv`, `rulings-P130.tsv`, `rulings-P214.tsv`; `docs/RULING_DISCREPANCIES.md`, kept complete by `crates/mtg-data/src/discrepancies.rs` (`check`, run by `cargo test -p mtg-data` and `mtg-tools rulings-coverage --check`).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p005_dies.rs` (`homicide_investigator_investigates_once_for_creatures_dying_together`); `tests/rulings/r_p096_lockdown.rs` (`rimescale_dragon_freezes_creatures_with_ice_counters_from_any_source_but_not_other_permanents`); `tests/rulings/ruling_contradicting_oracle.rs` (`combat_celebrant_untaps_all_other_creatures_but_not_itself`, `citanul_druid_grows_for_each_opponents_artifact_spells_but_not_its_controllers`, `sanguine_spy_counts_mana_values_among_cards_in_your_graveyard`); `tests/rulings/r_p140_tuck.rs` (`run_out_of_town_follows_its_oracle_text`, `run_out_of_town_puts_a_melded_permanent_all_on_top_or_all_on_the_bottom`); `tests/rulings/r_p067_misc.rs` (`sakashimas_protege_cant_copy_a_permanent_entering_with_it`); `tests/rulings/r_residue_reflexive_refund.rs` (`taeko_targets_an_attacking_creature_after_paying`, `slinza_pays_its_hybrid_cost_with_two_mana`); `tests/rulings/r_s07_extort.rs` (`extort_is_paid_at_most_once_per_trigger_as_it_resolves`).

## subgame-random-player-chooses-first: In a subgame, a randomly selected player chooses who takes the first turn
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 729.2 "As the subgame starts, an entirely new set of game zones is created. Each player takes all the cards in their main-game library, moves them to their subgame library, and shuffles them. No other cards in a main-game zone are moved to their corresponding subgame zone, except as specified in rules 729.2a–c. Randomly determine which player goes first. The subgame proceeds like a normal game, following all other rules in rule 103, “Starting the Game.”" / 103.1 "At the start of a game, the players determine which one of them will choose who takes the first turn. In the first game of a match (including a single-game match), the players may use any mutually agreeable method (flipping a coin, rolling dice, etc.) to do so. In a match of several games, the loser of the previous game chooses who takes the first turn. If the previous game was a draw, the player who made the choice in that game makes the choice in this game. The player chosen to take the first turn is the starting player. The game’s default turn order begins with the starting player and proceeds clockwise." / 103.1a "In a game using the shared team turns option, there is a starting team rather than a starting player." / Shahrazad's ruling (2004-10-04): "You randomly chose which player chooses to go first or draw first." / The user's decision of Nov 12: "as for who goes first, the randomly selected player should always get the option to choose".
- Question: 729.2 says to "randomly determine which player goes first" in a subgame, and Shahrazad's ruling says a randomly chosen player chooses who goes first. Does a random draw decide the starting player outright, or does it select the player who makes 103.1's choice?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 12. A player selected at random among the players in the subgame (taken in APNAP order of the main game, CR 101.4) chooses who takes the subgame's first turn, as the chooser does at the start of any game (103.1): the same question (`Choose who takes the first turn`), offering the players in the subgame (with shared team turns, a representative of each team, 103.1a), the chooser first and the others in turn order from them. A default or invalid answer lets the chooser go first, as in the main game (`default-first-listed-choice`). The subgame records the chooser (`StartState::chooser`). The selected player always chooses: a configured starting player or chooser of the main game (a match's loser, 103.1) doesn't carry over, since the subgame isn't a game of the match. That they also choose in a subgame of an Archenemy, Team vs. Team or Emperor game, rather than the variant's rule deciding who goes first, is a further reading of "always", recorded on its own and provisional: `subgame-variants-dont-decide-who-goes-first` (players-multiplayer).
- Alternatives: (a) A random draw decides the starting player and no one chooses (the engine's behaviour until Nov 12, reading 729.2's "Randomly determine which player goes first" literally): the ruling stayed uncited as a conflict case (rulings-P200). (b) In a subgame of a variant game, the variant's rule decides who goes first (103.1b, 904.6, 808.4, 809.4): see `subgame-variants-dont-decide-who-goes-first`.
- Why: 729.2 goes on to say the subgame proceeds "following all other rules in rule 103", and 103.1 has the players determine one of them who chooses who goes first; the 2004-10-04 ruling says the random determination selects that player. The two readings of 729.2 differ only in whether the selected player may let another player go first, which the ruling and the user's decision settle. Work item ruling-conflicts-8 (raised by rulings-P200).
- Affects: Shahrazad (the only card that creates a subgame, 729.1).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/subgame.rs` (`begin`, `choose_first`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/start.rs` (`ask_who_takes_the_first_turn`, shared with the main game's `choose_starting_player`).
- Tests: `tests/cr/r729_subgames.rs` (`a_randomly_selected_player_chooses_who_goes_first_in_the_subgame`, `an_invalid_or_default_answer_lets_the_subgame_chooser_go_first`, `only_players_in_the_subgame_choose_or_are_offered_the_first_turn`, `the_subgame_chooser_is_offered_every_player_in_turn_order_from_them`, `with_shared_team_turns_the_subgame_chooser_chooses_the_starting_team`, `shahrazads_subgame_starts_with_the_chosen_player`, and with `subgame-variants-dont-decide-who-goes-first`: `an_archenemy_subgame_starts_with_the_player_the_chooser_chose`, `a_team_vs_team_subgame_starts_with_the_player_the_chooser_chose`).

## pre-battle-rulings-give-way-to-the-cr: A ruling that predates battles and leaves them out, or contradicts their rules, gives way to the CR
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 110.4 "There are six permanent types: artifact, battle, creature, enchantment, land, and planeswalker. Instant and sorcery cards can’t enter the battlefield and thus can’t be permanents. Some kindred cards can enter the battlefield and some can’t, depending on their other card types. See section 3, “Card Types.”" / 310.9d "If a battle’s protector is a different player than its controller, all rules and effects that refer to the “defending player” relative to a battle that is being attacked refer to that battle’s protector rather than its controller. See rule 508.5." / 508.5 "If an ability of an attacking creature refers to a defending player, or a spell or ability refers to both an attacking creature and a defending player, then unless otherwise specified, the defending player it’s referring to is the player that creature is attacking, the controller of the planeswalker that creature is attacking, or the protector of the battle that creature is attacking. If that creature is no longer attacking, the defending player it’s referring to is the player that creature was attacking before it was removed from combat, the controller of the planeswalker that creature was attacking before it was removed from combat, or the protector of the battle that creature was attacking before it was removed from combat." / also 110.4a, 110.4b, 506.2, 508.1b, 508.1d, 508.4, 508.7, 509.1a, 702.22d, 702.49c and 802.2a, each naming battles beside players and planeswalkers / The user's decision of Nov 19: "Agreed on the decisions around battles".
- Question: Many Wizards rulings were written before battles existed (the battle card type came with March of the Machine, released 2023-04-21, and the Comprehensive Rules effective 2023-04-14) and still read as they did: they list the permanent types, the card types of permanent cards or permanent spells, who the defending player is, or what a creature may attack or block, without battles, in the words of the rules of their time. One older ruling was edited for battles in a way the battle rules contradict (Hellkite Whelp's "that permanent's controller is the defending player"). Does the ruling or the CR decide the battle case, and what happens to the ruling's citation?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 19. The CR decides the battle case, and the engine follows it: battle is a permanent type (110.4), and a permanent card or spell may be a battle card or spell (110.4a, 110.4b); the defending player of a creature attacking a battle is its protector (310.9d, 508.5); a creature may attack a battle (declared, 508.1b; to obey a requirement, 508.1d; entering attacking, 508.4; reselected, 508.7; in a band, 702.22d; with ninjutsu, 702.49c); and a battle's protector may block creatures attacking it (509.1a, 310.9c). A ruling covered is one that, read as written, gives a wrong or incomplete answer for battles to the question it answers: a definition or complete list that leaves battles out, a choice or set of things to attack or block that leaves them out, or a statement about battles that their rules contradict. The ruling's other parts stay cited by tests; the part that leaves battles out is exempt as outdated in `docs/rulings-exemptions/pre-battle-rulings.tsv` (the reason naming this policy and the CR rule), and the ruling is logged as outdated in `docs/RULING_DISCREPANCIES.md` for reporting to Wizards (grouped entries). A ruling covered that no test cites yet is cited by a test here when its card is supported, so the exemption doesn't hide the rest of it; one whose every card is deferred (docs/DEFERRED.md) is logged but not exempt. The policy applies too to the few such rulings dated after battles came (Liliana, Dreadhorde General's 2024-01-12 list of permanent types, Brought Back's 2025-10-02 "A permanent card is ..."), which repeat the older words.
- Alternatives: (a) Follow the rulings: a creature couldn't attack a battle in the situations they describe, battles wouldn't count as a permanent type for Revival Experiment and the others, and Hellkite Whelp's defending player would be a Siege's controller, the attacking player when they attack their own Siege. That contradicts the CR's battle rules, and the newer rulings that state them (below). (b) Leave the rulings cited with no record (the practice until Nov 19, with the Revival Experiment and Emeria Shepherd groups and Hellkite Whelp noted in the log for review): the engine was the same, but nothing recorded that the rulings and the CR disagree.
- Why: Each ruling repeats the rules text of its time. The archived Comprehensive Rules (github.com/pit142857/mtg-cr, commit 8351b94f) show the change: the CR effective 2023-02-03 had 110.4 "There are five permanent types: artifact, creature, enchantment, land, and planeswalker. ...", 110.4a "... Specifically, it means an artifact, creature, enchantment, land, or planeswalker card.", 508.1b "... announces which player or planeswalker each of the chosen creatures is attacking.", 508.4 "... chooses which defending player or which planeswalker a defending player controls it’s attacking ...", 508.5 "... the player that creature is attacking, or the controller of the planeswalker that creature is attacking. ...", 508.7 "Some cards allow a player to reselect which player or planeswalker a creature is attacking.", 509.1a "... that’s attacking that player or a planeswalker they control.", 702.22d "All creatures in an attacking band must attack the same player or planeswalker." and 702.49c "... It will be attacking the same player or planeswalker as the creature that was returned to its owner’s hand."; the CR effective 2023-04-14 added battles to every one of them, in today's words. Wizards revised some rulings for battles without changing their dates (Bond of Flourishing, 2019-05-03: "A permanent card is an artifact, battle, creature, enchantment, land, or planeswalker card."; Brimaz, King of Oreskos, 2014-02-01: "... you declare which player, planeswalker, or battle the token is attacking ..."; Mogg Flunkies, 2018-03-16), and newer rulings of the same templates name battles (the myriad template on Auton Soldier, 2023-10-13, "or the protector of the battle"; Kazuul, Tyrant of the Cliffs, 2023-07-28: "You're the defending player if a creature is attacking you, a planeswalker you control, or a battle you protect."; Furygale Flocking, 2026-03-20, "any other player, planeswalker, or battle"; Korvold, Gleeful Glutton, 2023-09-01: "The permanent types are artifact, battle, creature, enchantment, land, and planeswalker. ..."; Heart-Piercer Bow, 2023-07-28 (11 more cards say the same that day, "that battle's protector is the defending player"): "If a creature is attacking a battle, the protector of that battle is the defending player."), so the rulings left unrevised are outdated, not a different reading. Hellkite Whelp's (dated 2018-10-05, revised later) names battles but makes the battle's controller the defending player, which 310.9d contradicts for every Siege (a Siege's protector is always an opponent of its controller, 310.12a).
