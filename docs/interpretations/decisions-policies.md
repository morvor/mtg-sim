# Decisions and policies

How the engine fills in choices that the rules leave to a player when the player's agent gives
no answer (`Answer::Default`) or an invalid one, and what agents can see and do. One entry per
kind of decision. Every `game.ask` caller validates the answer and falls back to these
defaults; an answer of the wrong shape counts as `Default`. Format and kinds:
`docs/agents/items/interpretation-register.md`; index: `docs/INTERPRETATIONS.md`.

## default-priority-pass: With priority, a default or invalid answer passes; repeated illegal actions pass too
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 117.3d "If a player has priority and chooses not to take any actions, that player passes. ..." / 733.1 "If a player takes an illegal action or starts to take an action but can’t legally complete it, the entire action is reversed and any payments already made are canceled. ..." / 733.2 "When reversing illegal spells and abilities, the player who had priority retains it and may take another action or pass. ..."
- Question: What does a player with priority do when their agent gives no action, or keeps proposing actions that turn out to be illegal?
- Decision: `Answer::Default` or an answer that isn't an action passes priority. An action that turns out to be illegal is reversed and the player keeps priority (733.1, 733.2); after three illegal attempts in a row, the player passes. During a shortcut, a choice the player may not make is replaced by the first one they may.
- Alternatives: Re-ask indefinitely after illegal actions (733.2 read strictly): an agent that always proposes the same illegal action would stall the game.
- Why: Passing is the choice "not to take any actions" of 117.3d; the attempt limit keeps games finite.
- Affects: every priority decision.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/shortcuts.rs` (`priority_decision`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/turn.rs` (`take_action`, `MAX_ILLEGAL_ATTEMPTS`).
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_decision_defaults.rs` (`a_default_priority_answer_passes`, `an_invalid_answer_is_treated_as_the_default`); `tests/cr/r733_002_repeated_illegal_actions.rs` (`a_player_who_keeps_attempting_illegal_actions_passes_priority`).

## default-mulligan-keep: A default mulligan decision keeps; the default bottom cards are the first ones offered
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 103.5 "... A player who is dissatisfied with their initial hand may take a mulligan. ... then puts a number of those cards equal to the number of times that player has taken a mulligan on the bottom of their library in any order. Once a player chooses not to take a mulligan, the remaining cards become that player’s opening hand, ..."
- Question: Keep or mulligan, and which cards go to the bottom, when the agent doesn't say?
- Decision: Keep. When cards must go to the bottom, the first `n` cards of the hand as offered (hand order) go, in that order.
- Alternatives: Mulligan by a heuristic (no lands, all lands); choose the bottom cards by a heuristic. Both are strategy, which belongs in agents.
- Why: Keeping is the choice that ends the procedure; the engine makes no strategic choices.
- Affects: every game's opening hands.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/mulligan.rs` (the mulligan loop, `PutOnBottom`).
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_decision_defaults.rs` (`default_mulligan_answers_keep_and_bottom_the_first_cards`).

## default-yes-no: A default yes/no answer does what "you may" offers but doesn't pay optional costs
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 603.5 "Some triggered abilities’ effects are optional (they contain “may,” as in “At the beginning of your upkeep, you may draw a card”). ... The choice is made when the ability resolves. ..." / 118.12 "... “[A player] may [do something]. If [that player] [does, doesn’t, or can’t], [effect].” The action [do something] is a cost, paid when the spell or ability resolves. ..."
- Question: What does a player choose for a "you may" or "unless [a player] pays" question when the agent doesn't say?
- Decision: Each call site sets the default, following one rule: yes for doing what an optional instruction or ability offers at no further cost ("you may draw", "you may search", optional replacement effects, casting a card for free, revealing a miracle); no for paying an optional cost or accepting a drawback ("unless that player pays {1}", "pay N life?", exert, tribute, intentional draws, choosing new targets for a copy).
- Alternatives: Always no: every "may" left undone. Always yes: agents would pay every tax and life cost.
- Why: The defaults stand in for an uninterested player: take free value, don't spend resources.
- Affects: every yes/no decision (about 50 call sites).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/game.rs` (`ask_yes_no`, its `default` argument at each call site); `crates/mtg-engine/src/resolve.rs` (`Effect::May`: yes; `Effect::PayOptional`: no).
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_decision_defaults.rs` (`default_yes_no_does_what_you_may_but_doesnt_pay_optional_costs`).

## default-first-listed-choice: Default choices take the first listed options: objects, options, targets, modes, casting method, replacement effect
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 601.2b "If the spell is modal, the player announces the mode choice (see rule 700.2). ... If the spell has alternative or additional costs that will be paid as it’s being cast ..., the player announces their intentions to pay any or all of those costs ..." / 601.2c "The player announces their choice of an appropriate object or player for each target the spell requires. ..." / 700.2a "The controller of a modal spell or activated ability chooses the mode(s) as part of casting that spell or activating that ability. If one of the modes would be illegal (due to an inability to choose legal targets, for example), that mode can’t be chosen. ..." / 616.1 "If two or more replacement and/or prevention effects are attempting to modify the way an event affects an object or player, the affected object’s controller (or its owner if it has no controller) or the affected player chooses one to apply, ..."
- Question: Which objects, option, targets, modes, casting method or replacement effect does a player choose when the agent doesn't say?
- Decision: The first legal ones in the order the engine lists them: `ChooseEntities` takes the first `min` candidates; `ChooseOption` takes option 0; `ChooseTargets` takes the first `min` candidates (at least one when targets are required); `ChooseModes` takes the first available modes up to the minimum (at least one, skipping modes that can't be combined; when the same mode may be chosen more than once, the available modes again, in order, until the minimum is reached: Eldrazi Confluence with no nonland permanent to target takes its token mode three times. This only fills a gap: before, such a default came out short of the minimum and the cast failed, although the player could legally cast the spell); `ChooseCastingMethod` takes the first way to cast that could be begun; `ChooseReplacement` applies the first listed effect. A few choices have defaults of their own, documented where they're made: costs paid with permanents or cards (`default-cost-helpers`); friend or foe (the controller and their teammates are friends, `kw/friend_or_foe.rs`); a party (a largest one, `game_terms.rs`); "search for any number of cards with that name" (all of them, `kw/search_same_name.rs`); a coin flip one may keep or reflip (keep a winning flip, `dice.rs`); "you may choose not to untap" while a "remains tapped" effect applies (stay tapped, `untap_choice.rs`); the starting player's chooser, in a game or a subgame (their own seat or team, `start.rs`, `subgame-random-player-chooses-first`).
- Alternatives: Random legal choices (games would vary with the seed rather than with the agent), or heuristics (strategy, which belongs in agents).
- Why: Deterministic, always legal, and with "the minimum" the least commitment the rules allow.
- Affects: every such decision.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/game.rs` (`ask_entities`, `ask_option`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/stack.rs` (modes, targets); `crates/mtg-engine/src/casting.rs` (`ChooseCastingMethod`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/replacement.rs` (`ChooseReplacement`).
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_decision_defaults.rs` (`default_choices_take_the_first_listed_candidates`, `default_targets_are_the_first_legal_ones`, `default_modes_are_the_first_available_ones`, `the_default_way_to_cast_is_the_first_that_can_be_begun`, `the_default_replacement_effect_is_the_first_listed`); `tests/cr/r700_002a_modal_castability.rs` (`default_modes_repeat_a_mode_that_may_be_chosen_more_than_once`, `fiery_confluence_default_modes_repeat_the_available_ones`); `tests/rulings/r_p154_commander.rs` (`stinging_study_default_choice_is_the_first_commander_offered`: an object chosen for a value, `Value::OfChosen`).

## default-cost-helpers: Costs paid with permanents or cards (convoke, delve, improvise, crew, ...) use them only as needed by default
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 702.51a "Convoke is a static ability that functions while the spell with convoke is on the stack. “Convoke” means “For each colored mana in this spell’s total cost, you may tap an untapped creature of that color you control rather than pay that mana. For each generic mana in this spell’s total cost, you may tap an untapped creature you control rather than pay that mana.”" / 702.66a "Delve is a static ability that functions while the spell with delve is on the stack. “Delve” means “For each generic mana in this spell’s total cost, you may exile a card from your graveyard rather than pay that mana.”" / 702.126a "Improvise is a static ability that functions while the spell with improvise is on the stack. “Improvise” means “For each generic mana in this spell’s total cost, you may tap an untapped artifact you control rather than pay that mana.”"
- Question: Which creatures, artifacts or graveyard cards does a player use for convoke, improvise, delve, crew (702.122a: "Tap any number of other untapped creatures you control with total power N or greater"), emerge, offering, harmonize, waterbending, assist or Heirloom Epic's "you may tap an untapped creature you control rather than pay that mana" when the agent doesn't say? (The audit decision-audit-1, Nov 20, corrected the quotation of crew, which read 'tap any number of creatures with total power N or greater'.)
- Decision: As little as possible: none if the mana can be paid otherwise; else the fewest that make the rest payable (convoke, improvise and Heirloom Epic prefer creatures and artifacts without mana abilities; delve takes the cards at the bottom of the graveyard; crew taps the most powerful creatures until the total is reached; emerge, offering and harmonize use the permanent that reduces the cost most; assist asks a teammate who could pay, who pays as much as they can, and an opponent pays nothing).
- Alternatives: Use the alternative resources first (keep mana open): strategy.
- Why: The least commitment that pays the cost, like `automatic-mana-payment`.
- Affects: spells and abilities with those keywords.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/kw/convoke.rs`, `kw/delve.rs`, `kw/improvise.rs`, `kw/crew.rs` (`pay_total_power`), `kw/emerge.rs`, `kw/offering.rs`, `kw/harmonize.rs`, `kw/assist.rs`, `kwa/bending.rs`, `cards/heirloom_epic.rs` (each `default_choice` or equivalent).
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_decision_defaults.rs` (`default_convoke_taps_creatures_only_for_what_mana_cant_pay`); `tests/cards/m_heirloom_epic.rs` (`by_default_only_the_creatures_the_mana_cant_pay_for_are_tapped`).

## default-optional-costs-declined: Optional additional costs aren't paid by default
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 601.2b (see `default-first-listed-choice`) / 702.33a "Kicker is a static ability that functions while the spell with kicker is on the stack. “Kicker [cost]” means “You may pay an additional [cost] as you cast this spell.” ..."
- Question: Does a player pay kicker, buyback, multikicker, gift or other optional additional costs when the agent doesn't say?
- Decision: No (multikicker: zero times).
- Alternatives: Pay whenever affordable: spends resources the player may want for something else.
- Why: Same rule as `default-yes-no`: don't spend resources by default.
- Affects: every optional additional cost.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/casting.rs` (`OptionalCost`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/kw/offered_costs.rs`, `crates/mtg-engine/src/kw/gift.rs`, `crates/mtg-engine/src/splice.rs`, `crates/mtg-engine/src/cost_choices.rs`.
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_decision_defaults.rs` (`default_optional_costs_are_declined_and_the_first_castable_way_is_used`).

## default-numbers: A default X for a spell is the greatest affordable; other numbers default to the least allowed
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 107.3 "Many objects use the letter X as a placeholder for a number that needs to be determined. Some objects have abilities that define the value of X; the rest let their controller choose the value of X." / 601.2b "... If the spell has a variable cost that will be paid as it’s being cast (such as an {X} in its mana cost; see rule 107.3), the player announces the value of that variable. ..."
- Question: Which number does a player choose when the agent doesn't say?
- Decision: X in a spell's cost: the greatest legal value the spell could be cast with: its total cost, the parts that use X included ("discard X cards", "sacrifice X Mountains", "remove X counters") and the cost reductions applied (those the additional costs announced before X bring too: March of Burgeoning Life's "{2} less to cast for each card exiled this way"), could be paid, and it would have enough legal targets and modes for that X ("tap X target creatures"). The ChooseX decision offers values up to that one (the check is optimistic: it rules out only values that can't work). Every other number (X of an activated ability, "choose a number", join forces, morph and other X costs): the least allowed, usually 0 (suspend's X: the least payable value of at least 1).
- Alternatives: Always the least (an X spell cast for 0 usually does nothing); always the greatest (abilities would spend everything).
- Why: Casting an X spell signals intent to use it; for the other numbers, spend least.
- Affects: every X and number decision.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/casting.rs` (`ChooseX` for spells and for abilities); `crates/mtg-engine/src/default_x.rs`; `crates/mtg-engine/src/game.rs` (`ask_number`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/kw/` (join_forces, morph_face_up, optional_cost_x, spell_cost_grammar, suspend); `crates/mtg-engine/src/mana_abilities.rs`.
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_decision_defaults.rs` (`a_default_x_is_the_greatest_affordable_and_a_default_number_the_least`); `tests/cr/r601_2b_default_x_costs_and_targets.rs`; `tests/rulings/r_p167_colors.rs` (`march_of_burgeoning_life_default_x_counts_the_reduction`).

## default-order-as-listed: Default orderings keep the listed order (triggers: the first listed goes on the stack first)
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 603.3b "If multiple abilities have triggered since the last time a player received priority, the abilities are placed on the stack in a two-part process. First, each player, in APNAP order, puts each triggered ability they control with a trigger condition that isn’t another ability triggering on the stack in any order they choose. ..." / 101.4 "If multiple players would make choices and/or take actions at the same time, the active player (the player whose turn it is) makes any choices required, then the next player in turn order ... makes any choices required, followed by the remaining nonactive players in turn order. ..."
- Question: In which order does a player put simultaneous triggered abilities on the stack (or order cards, copies and the like) when the agent doesn't say?
- Decision: The order offered (the identity permutation). For triggered abilities that's the order they were detected in, the first one going on the stack first, so it resolves last. Players still order in APNAP order (101.4), which the engine follows as a rule.
- Alternatives: The reverse order (the first detected resolves first); a heuristic.
- Why: Deterministic and neutral.
- Affects: every ordering decision.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/game.rs` (`ask_order`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/triggers.rs` (stacking triggers).
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_decision_defaults.rs` (`a_default_order_keeps_the_listed_order`).

## simultaneous-entries-listed-by-owner-then-creation: A player orders their objects entering the battlefield together from a list by owner in APNAP order, then by creation
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 613.7m "If two or more objects would receive a timestamp simultaneously, such as by entering a zone simultaneously or becoming attached simultaneously, their relative timestamps are determined in APNAP order (see rule 101.4). Objects controlled by the active player (or owned by the active player, if they have no controller) have an earlier relative timestamp in the order of that player’s choice, followed by each other player in turn order." / 101.4 "If multiple players would make choices and/or take actions at the same time, the active player (the player whose turn it is) makes any choices required, then the next player in turn order ... makes any choices required, followed by the remaining nonactive players in turn order. ..."
- Question: The rules leave the relative timestamps of a player's own objects entering the battlefield together to that player. In which order does the engine list them for the choice, which is also their order when the agent doesn't answer (`default-order-as-listed`)?
- Decision: Each player's objects are listed by owner in APNAP order (the active player's cards first), then each owner's in the order the objects were created (`Game::sort_objects_apnap`), whatever order the moves were proposed in. The vanguard and conspiracy cards that receive timestamps together at the beginning of the game (613.7i, 613.7j) get them in the same order: by owner in APNAP order (613.7m: their controllers are their owners), each player's in creation order (until small-fixes-6, in the command zone's order, which put the vanguards in seat order).
- Alternatives: The order the moves were proposed in (the engine until small-fixes-6): often an order of object ids, which follow the seats the players sit in, so the same game with the players seated differently listed the objects differently (seat symmetry property, game 405 of `--properties --seed 1`); or by name.
- Why: Deterministic and independent of seats: APNAP order depends only on turn order (101.4), and an owner's objects are created in the same order wherever the owner sits. The same order the engine uses before choosing among objects at random (`sort_objects_apnap`).
- Affects: every "Order objects entering the battlefield" decision (mass reanimation, flickers of several permanents, tokens and cards entering together); vanguards and conspiracies at the beginning of the game.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/actions.rs` (`order_simultaneous_entries`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/apnap.rs` (`sort_objects_apnap`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/variants.rs` (`begin_game`).
- Tests: `tests/cr/r613_7m_entry_order_listing.rs` (`objects_entering_together_are_listed_the_same_wherever_the_players_sit`, `one_owners_objects_entering_together_are_listed_in_creation_order`); `tests/cr/r609_613_variant_timestamps.rs` (`vanguards_get_their_timestamps_in_apnap_order`).

## default-combat-declarations: A default or illegal declaration of attackers or blockers is replaced by the least legal one
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 508.1d "The active player checks each creature they control to see whether it’s affected by any requirements ... If the number of requirements that are being obeyed is fewer than the maximum possible number of requirements that could be obeyed without disobeying any restrictions, the declaration of attackers is illegal. ..." / 509.1a "The defending player chooses which creatures they control, if any, will block. ..."
- Question: What does the engine declare when the agent gives no declaration, or an illegal one?
- Decision: The declaration that obeys the most requirements without any costs and without anything else: only creatures that must attack (or block) do. An illegal declaration (or one whose costs can't be paid) is undone and replaced by that one. When another player chooses the blocks (Odric, Master Tactician; Master Warcraft) and the blocking player declines the costs, new blocks are proposed up to three times.
- Alternatives: Re-ask until legal (can stall); declare nothing even when requirements exist (illegal).
- Why: The least commitment that is legal.
- Affects: every combat.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/combat.rs` (declaring attackers and blockers, `best_blocks`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/attack_choice.rs`; `crates/mtg-engine/src/block_choice.rs`.
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_decision_defaults.rs` (`a_default_attack_declaration_obeys_requirements_and_nothing_more`, `a_default_block_declaration_blocks_with_nothing`).

## default-damage-assignment: Default combat damage is lethal to each blocker in order, then the rest to what it's attacking (with trample)
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 510.1c "... If two or more creatures are blocking it, it assigns its combat damage to those creatures divided as its controller chooses among them." / 702.19b "The controller of an attacking creature with trample first assigns damage to the creature(s) blocking it. Once all those blocking creatures are assigned lethal damage, any excess damage is assigned as its controller chooses among those blocking creatures and the player, planeswalker, or battle the creature is attacking. ..."
- Question: How does a creature's controller divide its combat damage when the agent doesn't say?
- Decision: Lethal damage to each blocker in the order listed, then the rest to the player, planeswalker or battle it's attacking if it has trample (with trample over planeswalkers: the planeswalker first, then its controller), otherwise the rest to the last blocker. When the defending player assigns the damage (banding, 702.22j), all of it stays on the blockers.
- Alternatives: All damage to the first blocker; spread evenly.
- Why: Kills the most blockers and pushes the most damage through, which is what dividing damage is usually for.
- Affects: every combat with several blockers or trample.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/combat.rs` (`TrampleShape::default_assignment`, `AssignCombatDamage`).
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_decision_defaults.rs` (`default_combat_damage_assignment_is_lethal_in_order_then_the_rest_through`).

## default-scry-surveil: Default scry and surveil keep every card on top in its order
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 701.22a "To “scry N” means to look at the top N cards of your library, then put any number of them on the bottom of your library in any order and the rest on top of your library in any order." / 701.25a "To “surveil N” means to look at the top N cards of your library, then put any number of them into your graveyard and the rest on top of your library in any order."
- Question: Where do the looked-at cards go when the agent doesn't say?
- Decision: All of them back on top, in the order they were.
- Alternatives: All to the bottom (or the graveyard): a strategic choice.
- Why: Changes nothing: the neutral choice.
- Affects: scry, surveil, fateseal and the like.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/scry_rules.rs`; `crates/mtg-engine/src/kwa/fateseal_clash.rs`.
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_decision_defaults.rs` (`default_scry_keeps_every_card_on_top`).

## default-division: A default division splits evenly, the remainder to the first recipients
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 601.2d "If the spell requires the player to divide or distribute an effect (such as damage or counters) among one or more targets, the player announces the division. Each of these targets must receive at least one of whatever is being divided."
- Question: How is damage (or counters) divided among targets when the agent doesn't say?
- Decision: As evenly as possible, the remainder one each to the first targets.
- Alternatives: All but one each to the first target.
- Why: Always legal (there are never more targets than the amount) and neutral.
- Affects: spells and abilities that divide (Forked Bolt, Arc Lightning, ...).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/stack.rs` (`choose_divisions`).
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_decision_defaults.rs` (`a_default_division_splits_evenly_with_the_remainder_first`).

## default-card-name: A default or invalid card name names nothing
- Kind: deviation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 201.4 "If an effect instructs a player to choose a card name, the player must choose the name of a card in the Oracle card reference. (See rule 108.1.) A player may not choose the name of a token unless it’s also the name of a card." / 607.5a "... If an ability refers to an undefined choice, that part of the ability won’t do anything."
- Question: 201.4 requires a real card name. What is chosen when the agent gives none, or a name that isn't a card's (or doesn't fit the effect's restriction)?
- Decision: Nothing is named: the choice is left undefined and what refers to it does nothing (like 607.5a's undefined choice).
- Alternatives: Name some real card by a fixed rule (the first card of an opponent's deck, or of the Oracle card reference): satisfies 201.4 but is an arbitrary strategic choice.
- Why: Accepted: naming nothing never helps the player who failed to name a card, and the engine makes no strategic choices.
- Affects: "choose a card name" cards such as Pithing Needle, and hidden agenda, only when the agent gives no valid name.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/choices.rs` (`ChoiceKind::CardName`, `valid_card_name`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/kw/hidden_agenda.rs`.
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_decision_defaults.rs` (`a_default_card_name_names_nothing`); `tests/rulings/r_p033_misc.rs` (`council_of_the_absolute_names_one_half_of_a_split_card`: both halves of a split card named at once).

## test-harness-defaults: TestGame's scripted agents pass, keep, and attack and block with nothing
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 117.3d (see `default-priority-pass`)
- Question: What do tests' agents do when a test queues no answer for a decision?
- Decision: `testing::ScriptedAgent` uses queued answers first (matched by kind of decision, so unrelated prompts don't consume them); with nothing queued it passes priority, keeps its hand, declares no attackers and no blockers, and answers everything else with `Answer::Default` (the engine defaults in this file).
- Alternatives: Fail the test on an unscripted decision: stricter, but most tests rely on the defaults for incidental decisions.
- Why: Tests should say only what matters to them; a test that relies on a default for the behavior it checks should cite that default's entry.
- Affects: every TestGame test. "No attackers" is an explicit declaration, so with attack requirements the engine replaces it (`default-combat-declarations`).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/testing.rs` (`ScriptedAgent::decide`).
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_decision_defaults.rs` (`the_test_harness_answers_unscripted_decisions_with_fixed_defaults`).

## external-agent-invalid-answers: External agents get three more tries after an invalid answer, then the engine's default
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: none directly (the agent interface); its parallel in the rules is 733.2 "When reversing illegal spells and abilities, the player who had priority retains it and may take another action or pass. ..."
- Question: What happens when an agent outside the engine answers a decision invalidly, or stops answering?
- Decision: `ProtocolAgent` replies to an invalid answer with an error and asks again, up to `max_retries` times (default 3); then the decision gets `Answer::Default`. After the transport closes, every later decision gets `Answer::Default` without asking. With `auto_pass` on (off by default), priority is passed without asking when passing, conceding and mana abilities are the only options.
- Alternatives: Fail the game; or concede for the agent.
- Why: The game always finishes with defined behavior, and agents get feedback to correct themselves.
- Affects: games with external agents (mtg-api).
- Code: `crates/mtg-api/src/agent.rs` (`ProtocolAgent::decide`, `ProtocolOptions`).
- Tests: `crates/mtg-api/tests/protocol.rs` (`invalid_answers_get_an_error_and_are_asked_again`, `too_many_invalid_answers_fall_back_to_the_default`).

## hidden-information: In-process agents see everything; mtg-api views follow the hidden-information rules
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 400.2 "... Hidden zones are zones in which not all players can be expected to see the cards’ faces. Library and hand are hidden zones, even if all the cards in one such zone happen to be revealed." / 401.2 "Each library must be kept in a single face-down pile. Players can’t look at or change the order of cards in a library." / 402.3 "... A player can’t look at the cards in another player’s hand but may count those cards at any time." / 106.4a "If any mana remains in a player’s mana pool after mana is spent to pay a cost, that player announces what mana is still there." / 106.4b "If a player passes priority (see rule 117) while there is mana in their mana pool, that player announces what mana is there." / 712.6 "Players who are allowed to look at a double-faced card may look at both sides of that card."
- Question: The rules say what each player may see; announcements (106.4) and handling the cards are left to the players. What does the engine give agents?
- Decision: The in-process `Agent` trait receives the whole `Game`, hidden information included: agents that shouldn't cheat must restrict themselves. mtg-api's observations, requests and event feed show each player only what the rules let them see: other players' hands and libraries only as counts, face-down objects' identities only to those allowed to look, and a controlled player's view shared with their controller (723). Mana pools are public, standing in for 106.4's announcements. A player allowed to look at a double-faced card sees both faces.
- Alternatives: Filter the in-process interface too: safer, but the engine's own helpers and tests read the full state through it.
- Why: Hidden information is enforced at the boundary where untrusted agents live (mtg-api); in-process agents are part of the program.
- Affects: all agents.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/decision.rs` (`Agent`); `crates/mtg-api/src/view.rs` (`observe`); `crates/mtg-api/src/events.rs`; `crates/mtg-api/src/describe.rs`.
- Tests: `crates/mtg-api/tests/hidden_info.rs` (`opponents_hand_and_libraries_are_hidden`, `face_down_permanents_are_seen_only_by_their_controller`, `requests_and_events_dont_leak_hidden_cards`, `every_players_mana_pool_is_public`).

## seeded-randomness: Random results come from a pseudorandom generator seeded per game
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 701.24a "To shuffle a library or a face-down pile of cards, randomize the cards within it so that no player knows their order." / 705.1 "Some cards refer to flipping a coin. A coin used in a flip must be a two-sided object with easily distinguished sides and equal likelihood that either side lands face up. ..."
- Question: Shuffles, coin flips, die rolls and random choices must be random. How does the engine randomize?
- Decision: Every game has one ChaCha8 generator seeded from `GameConfig::seed`; all random results (shuffles, flips, rolls, "at random" choices, the starting player) come from it, so a game replays exactly from its seed and its agents' answers. No player (agent) can predict results without reading the generator's state, which in-process agents could do (see `hidden-information`). With `GameConfig::player_rng_keys` (set by the seat symmetry property), what a player randomizes themselves comes from a stream of their own, keyed by their deck (`Game::rng_for`): their library's shuffles, random cards from their hand and, since small-fixes-6, the shuffles of their own supplementary decks before the game (Attraction, scheme and planar decks, CR 103.3a), which are also shuffled and renumbered in APNAP order rather than seat order.
- Alternatives: An unseeded (system) random source: no replays or reproducible fuzz failures.
- Why: Reproducibility: mtg-sim prints a repro command with the seed for every failure.
- Affects: every random event.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/game.rs` (`Game::rng`, `Game::rng_for`, `shuffle_library`, `GameConfig::seed`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/dice.rs`; `crates/mtg-engine/src/attraction_cards.rs` (`shuffle_attraction_decks`), `crates/mtg-engine/src/variants.rs` (`shuffle_scheme_decks`), `crates/mtg-engine/src/planechase.rs` (`shuffle_planar_decks`).
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_decision_defaults.rs` (`random_results_come_from_the_games_seed`); `tests/properties/seat_symmetry.rs` (`supplementary_decks_are_shuffled_and_renumbered_whatever_the_seats`, `a_players_supplementary_decks_are_shuffled_with_their_own_randomness`).

## ability-ids-depend-on-the-process: Ability ids in requests come from a process-wide counter; answers name options by their place
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: none directly (the agent interface). Abilities have identities because of rules such as 602.5c "If an object acquires an activated ability with a restriction on its use from another object, that restriction applies only to that ability as acquired from that object. It doesn’t apply to other, identically worded abilities." and 607.1 "An object may have two abilities printed on it such that one of them causes actions to be taken or objects or players to be affected and the other one directly refers to those actions, objects, or players. If so, these two abilities are linked: ..."
- Question: The rules don't number abilities; the engine does, to tell them apart (activation limits, linked abilities, removing one ability). Must the numbers be the same every time a game is played?
- Decision: No. Ability ids come from one counter the whole process shares (`ability::next_ability_uid`) and are handed out as abilities are first needed: compiled card abilities (cached for every game), abilities effects give, delayed triggers, tokens' abilities. So the ids in agent-protocol requests (`action.ability`) differ between processes, and even between two plays of one game in one process, unless everything before them happened the same way. Answers refer to options by `index`, which doesn't depend on the ids, so a game replays from its answers; replays and the property checks compare ids up to a consistent renaming. The links of abilities effects give, which are also numbers, are numbered per game (`ability_grants::FreshLinks`).
- Alternatives: Allocate ability ids per game: the same requests in every process, but the caches of compiled and derived abilities, shared by all games, would have to be reworked to renumber per game.
- Why: Agents never need to send an id back, so stable ids would buy only easier comparison of requests across processes, which renaming already gives.
- Affects: games with external agents (mtg-api requests and transcripts), replays, the determinism and snapshot properties.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/ability.rs` (`next_ability_uid`); `crates/mtg-api/src/replay.rs` (`AbilityRenaming`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/properties/agent.rs` (`rename_uids`).
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_ability_ids.rs` (`the_same_game_numbers_a_gained_ability_differently_but_lists_it_in_the_same_place`).

## unbounded-number-ceiling: "Choose a number" with no upper limit goes up to 1,000,000
- Kind: deviation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 107.1 "The only numbers the Magic game uses are integers." / 107.1a "You can’t choose a fractional number, deal fractional damage, gain fractional life, and so on. ..." / 107.1b "Most of the time, the Magic game uses only positive numbers and zero. You can’t choose a negative number, deal negative damage, gain negative life, and so on. ..." / Menacing Ogre ruling (2004-10-04): "You have to choose zero or a positive number. It must be an integer number." (The audit decision-audit-1, Nov 20, corrected the Menacing Ogre quote, whose second sentence read 'You can’t choose a negative number.': those words are 107.1b's.)
- Question: "Choose a number" (Void, Menacing Ogre), "a number 0 or greater" (Wheel of Misfortune), "a number greater than 0" (Mindblaze), "hides at least one item" (Goblin Game) set no upper limit. What range does the number decision offer?
- Decision: From the least number the text allows to `choices::MAX_CHOSEN_NUMBER` (1,000,000), shown to agents as the decision's `max` (mtg-api `number` spec). An answer outside the range is invalid and the least number is chosen (`default-numbers`). The same ceiling bounds life bids (`bidding.rs`) and "any number of counters" moved.
- Alternatives: No maximum at all (the protocol can express it: a `number` spec without `max`), which needs every arithmetic path that a chosen number reaches (life totals, damage, counters, all 32-bit) to saturate; the former ceiling of 1000, which a player could want to exceed (losing more life than 1000 is never needed, but numbers are also compared and matched against mana values and card counts).
- Why: A million is beyond every life total, library, card count or mana value of a real game (outside the un-cards not supported), so no choice a player could want is excluded, while the engine's 32-bit game arithmetic stays exact.
- Affects: Void, Menacing Ogre, Wheel of Misfortune, Mindblaze, Goblin Game, "As ~ enters, choose a number", bidding (Illicit Auction, Pain's Reward, ...), "move any number of counters".
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/choices.rs` (`MAX_CHOSEN_NUMBER`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/choice_grammar_secret_numbers.rs`, `choice_grammar_multi.rs`, `etb_choices.rs`, `r122_move_counters.rs`; `crates/mtg-engine/src/bidding.rs`.
- Tests: `tests/cards/choices_number_ceiling.rs`.

## deck-order-ids-before-shuffle: Until the decks are shuffled, cards' ids follow the order the decks were given in
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 103.3 "After the starting player has been determined and any additional steps performed, each player shuffles their deck so that the cards are in a random order. Each player may then shuffle or cut their opponents’ decks. The players’ decks become their libraries." (The audit decision-audit-1, Nov 20, restored the rule's opening words, which the quote had left out without an ellipsis.) / 400.2 "... Library and hand are hidden zones, even if all the cards in one such zone happen to be revealed." / 401.2 "Each library must be kept in a single face-down pile. Players can’t look at or change the order of cards in a library."
- Question: Object ids are visible to agents (an object in a hidden zone is shown with its id). Cards are made into objects from the deck lists as the game is set up, so their ids follow the lists' order. Does that tell anyone which hidden card is which?
- Decision: Not once the decks are shuffled. Library objects keep their ids but aren't shown; cards drawn, moved or put anywhere become new objects with new ids (CR 400.7); the cards of face-down decks kept in the command zone (Attraction, planar and scheme decks) become new objects in their shuffled order (`zones::renumber_shuffled_deck`); conspiracies put into the command zone become new objects in random order. Before the decks are shuffled (the decisions of the game's setup, such as choosing who goes first or which conspiracies to put into the command zone), the ids of a deck's face-down cards in the command zone follow the order the deck was given in, which the shuffle then makes meaningless.
- Alternatives: Make every card a new object, in random order, before any decision is asked; or shuffle the deck lists before making their objects (which would change every seeded game).
- Why: Before the shuffle, the order of a deck is the player's own arrangement and says nothing about the game's hidden information once play begins; the hidden information property compares what players see from the opening hands on.
- Affects: decisions during the game's setup (choosing the starting player, conspiracies, companions) in games with Attraction, planar or scheme decks.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/game.rs` (`Game::new`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/zones.rs` (`renumber_shuffled_deck`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/start.rs` (`conspiracies_to_command_zone`); `crates/mtg-api/src/hidden.rs` (`check_start`).
- Tests: `crates/mtg-api/tests/hidden_information.rs` (`object_ids_dont_tell_hidden_cards_apart`, `the_ids_of_face_down_decks_in_the_command_zone_dont_tell_their_cards`).

## controller-picks-the-choosing-opponent: When "an opponent" makes a choice, the controller of the spell or ability picks which opponent (by default the next in turn order)
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 601.7 "Some spells specify that one of their controller’s opponents does something the controller would normally do while it’s being cast, such as choose a mode or choose targets. In these cases, the opponent does so when the spell’s controller normally would do so." / 601.7a "If there is more than one opponent who could make such a choice, the spell’s controller decides which of those opponents will make the choice." / 602.3a "If there is more than one opponent who could make such a choice, the ability’s controller decides which of those opponents will make the choice." / 801.5a "If a player is asked to choose an object or player, they must choose one within their range of influence." / 801.5c "If an effect requires a choice and there’s no player who can make that choice within its controller’s range of influence, the closest appropriate player to its controller’s left makes that choice." / 801.5b's example: "Alex, who has a range of influence of 2, is seated to the left of Rob, and Carissa, who has a range of influence of 1, is seated to the right of Rob. Alex casts a spell that reads, “An opponent chooses one — You draw two cards; or each creature you control gets +2/+2 until end of turn,” and chooses Carissa to make that choice. ..." / rulings for choices made as the spell or ability resolves, in the same direction: Steam Augury (2013-09-15) "Steam Augury doesn't target any opponent. In a multiplayer game, you choose the opponent as the spell resolves."; Sphinx of Uthuun and Unesh, Criosphinx Sovereign "In multiplayer games, you choose an opponent to separate the cards when the ability resolves. This doesn't target that opponent. ..."; Boneyard Parley (the same words); Sphinx of Clear Skies (2022-09-09) "The player who controls the triggered ability chooses which opponent separates the cards into two piles. This choice is made after revealing those cards."; Curator of Destinies (2024-11-08), Intrude on the Mind (2024-02-02) and Split the Spoils (2022-06-10) "You decide which opponent chooses the pile while resolving ..."; Abstract Performance (2026-03-20) "You choose which opponent chooses the pile while resolving Abstract Performance."; Akroan Horse "... In a multiplayer game, you choose the opponent as the ability resolves."; Baleful Mastery, Devastating Mastery, Fervent Mastery, Ingenious Mastery (2021-04-16) "In a multiplayer game, you choose which opponent takes the prescribed action as the spell resolves."; Plargg and Nassari (2023-05-12) "In a multiplayer game, you choose an opponent to choose a nonland card exiled with Plargg and Nassari."; Deliver Unto Evil (2019-05-03) and Choose Your Demise (2017-06-13) "You choose one opponent to make the final choice ..."; Wishclaw Talisman, Rohgahh of Kher Keep, Rogue Skycaptain, Rainbow Vale (the controller chooses the opponent who gains control) / 702.144a "... If you copy the spell, choose an opponent. That player copies the spell and may choose new targets for that copy."
- Question: When the text has "an opponent" make a choice or do something ("An opponent chooses one of them", "An opponent separates those cards into two piles", "An opponent chooses one of those piles", "an opponent gains control of it") and there are several opponents, who decides which opponent? 601.7a and 602.3a answer it for choices made while a spell is cast or an ability activated; no rule speaks to choices made as the spell or ability resolves, and before this entry the engine picked the first opponent in APNAP order for some wordings (Burning-Rune Demon's "An opponent chooses one of them") while asking the controller for others (Turtles Forever's "An opponent chooses two of them").
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 19: "when "an opponent chooses" the controller should always get to pick the opponent unless the text, a ruling, or the CR specifically says otherwise." The controller of the spell or ability picks the opponent, whenever the choice is made (while casting or activating, 601.7a and 602.3a, or as it resolves), among the opponents within their range of influence (801.5a; the closest to their left if none is, 801.5c). One shared helper asks them (`choosing_opponent::pick`): the opponents are offered in turn order starting with the next one after the controller, who is the default when the agent gives no answer (`default-first-listed-choice`). It covers every wording that has "an opponent" choose or act: modes and targets chosen by an opponent ("An opponent chooses one —", "target creature of an opponent's choice"), choices among cards ("An opponent chooses one of them", "chooses two of those cards", "chooses from among them ..."), piles ("An opponent separates ...", "An opponent chooses one of those piles", "the pile of an opponent's choice"), "[object] of an opponent's choice" chosen as the effect happens (`opponents-choice-untargeted`), "an opponent [does something]" ("gains control of it", "draws a card", "guesses ..."), and the opponent the controller chooses for tribute (702.104a "choose an opponent. That player may ..."), demonstrate (702.144a "choose an opponent. That player copies the spell and may choose new targets for that copy.") and Memories Returning ("Then choose an opponent. They put one on the bottom of your library."). A plain "choose an opponent" (the controller's own choice by its text, `ChoiceKind::Opponent`) goes through the same helper, so its default is the next opponent in turn order too. With no opponent within the controller's range of influence, the closest one to their left makes the choice (801.5c), for a choice among cards (Burning-Rune Demon) as for piles and modes. Exceptions kept, where the text or a rule says otherwise: an opponent chosen at random, by the text (Knight Rampager: "choose an opponent at random" and "target opponent chosen at random"; Raving Dead, Ruhan of the Fomori and Indoraptor, the Perfect Hybrid: "choose an opponent at random"; Explosion of Riches: "target opponent chosen at random from among your opponents"; Maddening Hex: "attach this Aura to another one of your opponents chosen at random"); "target opponent" (the controller already picks it as a target, 601.2c, as for Gifts Ungiven's "Target opponent chooses two of those cards"); "the opponent to your left" (Barroom Brawl: "Target creature you control fights target creature the opponent to your left controls. Then that player may copy this spell and may choose new targets for the copy.") and the tempting offers' order, by their ruling (Tempt with Discovery, Tempt with Glory, Tempt with Immortality, Tempt with Reflections and Tempt with Vengeance, 2013-10-17: "Your opponents decide in turn order whether or not they accept the offer, starting with the opponent on your left."); "each opponent" and "any opponent may" (every opponent acts or is offered the action, not one picked: Desecration Demon's "any opponent may sacrifice a creature of their choice"); and "that player"/"that opponent" naming a particular one (Fatespinner's "At the beginning of each opponent's upkeep, that player chooses ..."). "[permanent] enters under the control of an opponent of your choice" is the controller's own choice by its text and keeps its own path (`replacement.rs`, `EnterUnderControl`). Not covered, since the opponent makes no choice: the controller's own choice of an opponent for gift (702.174a "you may choose an opponent") and fateseal (701.29a "look at the top N cards of an opponent’s library"), which keep the first opponent in APNAP order as their default.
- Alternatives: (A) The first opponent in APNAP order chooses (the engine's earlier default for some wordings): no source, and the rulings above say the controller chooses. (B) Each opponent in turn order (more choices than the card makes). (C) The default opponent the first in APNAP order rather than the next in turn order: the default only matters for agents that give no answer; the next opponent in turn order doesn't depend on whose turn it is.
- Why: The user's policy; it extends 601.7a and 602.3a to choices made as the spell or ability resolves, as every ruling found on such a choice does, and no ruling, text or rule found says otherwise for "an opponent" in a multiplayer game.
- Affects: Burning-Rune Demon (its chooser was the first opponent in APNAP order: changed); every card with "an opponent [chooses, separates, guesses, gains control, ...]" or "of an opponent's choice": Turtles Forever, Murmurs from Beyond, Realms Uncharted, Fact or Fiction, Sphinx of Uthuun, Steam Augury, Curator of Destinies, Akroan Horse, the Masteries, Fatal Lore, Volcanic Offering, Demonic Hordes, The Toymaker's Trap and the rest (their default opponent is now the next in turn order rather than the first in APNAP order); every plain "choose an opponent" (the same default); tribute; demonstrate; Memories Returning; with the limited range of influence option, Burning-Rune Demon with no opponent in range (its controller chose for them: now the closest opponent to the left, 801.5c).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/choosing_opponent.rs` (`pick`); its callers: `crates/mtg-engine/src/stack.rs` (`Game::deciding_opponent`: modes and targets chosen by an opponent while casting or activating, and `kw::choice_grammar::DECIDING_OPPONENT`, "An opponent chooses one of them"); `crates/mtg-engine/src/choices.rs` (`ChoiceKind::Opponent`: "an opponent [does something]", "of an opponent's choice"); `crates/mtg-engine/src/piles.rs` (`one_player`: piles); `crates/mtg-engine/src/dig_steps.rs` (`take`: Burning-Rune Demon); `crates/mtg-engine/src/kw/tribute.rs`; `crates/mtg-engine/src/kw/demonstrate.rs`; `crates/mtg-engine/src/cards/memories_returning.rs`.
- Tests: `tests/rulings/choosing_opponent.rs` (`burning_rune_demon_controller_picks_the_second_opponent`, `by_default_the_next_opponent_in_turn_order_chooses`, `sphinx_of_uthuun_controller_picks_the_opponent_who_separates`, `steam_augury_controller_picks_the_opponent_who_chooses_a_pile`, `akroan_horse_controller_picks_the_opponent_who_gains_control`, `murmurs_from_beyond_controller_picks_the_opponent_who_chooses_a_card`, `fatal_lore_controller_picks_the_opponent_who_chooses_the_mode`); `tests/cr/r801_range_of_influence.rs` (`with_no_opponent_in_range_the_closest_one_to_the_left_chooses_a_card`).

## player-destroys-chosen-permanents: "[Player] destroys N lands they control": chosen by that player among those that can be destroyed
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 701.8a "To destroy a permanent, move it from the battlefield to its owner’s graveyard." / 702.12b "A permanent with indestructible can’t be destroyed. ..." / Burning of Xinye's rulings: "That player chooses which of their lands to destroy. ## chosen-permanents-to-destroy-can-be-destroyed: A permanent a player chooses to destroy as the effect is performed must be one that can be destroyed
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 608.2d "If an effect of a spell or ability offers any choices other than choices already made as part of casting the spell, activating the ability, or otherwise putting the spell or ability on the stack, the player announces these while applying the effect. The player can’t choose an option that’s illegal or impossible, ..." / 702.12b "A permanent with indestructible can’t be destroyed. ..." / Drop of Honey's and Porphyry Nodes's ruling: "If one of the creatures with the least power has indestructible, you must destroy one without indestructible. If all creatures with the least power have indestructible, you don't destroy any creature."
- Question: "Destroy the creature with the least power. [...] If two or more creatures are tied for least power, you choose one of them." With a tie between an indestructible creature and another, may the controller choose the indestructible one (destroying nothing)?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 20 (as recommended): "Accept". Whenever an instruction destroys permanents a player chooses as it's performed (`Effect::Destroy` of a `Sel::Choose`), only permanents without indestructible may be chosen; if none qualifies, nothing is destroyed. The selection itself ("the creature with the least power") is still made among all the permanents it names, so an indestructible creature with less power than every other creature means nothing is destroyed. Extends `player-destroys-chosen-permanents` (Burning of Xinye's "Neither of you can choose to destroy a land with indestructible", a compile-time filter there) to every such choice, at run time (`impossible_options::destroyable_choice`).
- Alternatives: (A) Let the player choose an indestructible permanent, which then survives (destroying it is attempted, not impossible): contradicts the rulings. (B) Exclude indestructible permanents before finding the least power: contradicts the second sentence of the ruling.
- Why: The rulings, generalized to the same kind of choice on any card. Provisional: 608.2d forbids only illegal or impossible options, and a judge could hold that choosing a permanent that wouldn't be destroyed isn't one outside these cards' rulings (Celestial Judgment's ruling lets a player choose freely, but there the chosen creatures are the ones spared). The user accepted the reading (Nov 20).
- Affects: Drop of Honey, Porphyry Nodes, Light of Judgment ("Destroy up to one Equipment attached to that creature"), and any "destroy [an object chosen as it's performed]".
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/impossible_options.rs` (`destroyable_choice`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/resolve.rs` (`Effect::Destroy`).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_residue_misc_1_sacrifice.rs` (`drop_of_honey_and_porphyry_nodes_skip_indestructible_least_power`).

## mill-until-stops-when-nothing-moves: A "mill until" process goes on past milled cards that come back to the library, and ends when the library is empty, a mill moves nothing, or only cards it milled are left
- Kind: deviation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 701.17a "For a player to mill a number of cards, that player puts that many cards from the top of their library into their graveyard." / 104.4b "If a game that’s not using the limited range of influence option (including a two-player game) somehow enters a “loop” of mandatory actions, repeating a sequence of events with no way to stop, the game is a draw. Loops that contain an optional action don’t result in a draw." / Helm of Obedience's ruling (2008-10-01): "If an effect like that of Leyline of the Void prevents cards from being put into your opponent’s graveyard, the process described in the first sentence of Helm of Obedience’s effect will never stop. Your opponent’s entire library will be exiled, even if X is 1." / Arcbond's ruling (2014-11-24): "If two Arcbonds have resolved targeting different creatures, and damage is dealt to one of them, the delayed triggered ability will cause that creature to deal damage to each other creature and player. This will cause the delayed triggered ability from the other Arcbond to trigger. The second creature will then deal damage to each other creature and player. This will continue until one or both of the creatures die or the game ends. If this doesn’t happen (perhaps because both creatures have indestructible and damage dealt to the players is being prevented), and no player breaks the loop, the game will be a draw." / Faceless Butcher's ruling (2022-12-08): "If Faceless Butcher exiles a second Faceless Butcher, and then a third Faceless Butcher comes along and exiles the first one, the second one will return to the battlefield. If there are no other creatures on the battlefield, the second will exile the third one, returning the first one, and so on. If no one can stop the loop, the game will be a draw."
- Question: Helm of Obedience: "Target opponent mills a card, then repeats this process until a creature card or X cards have been put into their graveyard this way, whichever comes first." The process counts only cards put into the graveyard. If milled cards go elsewhere (exiled by Leyline of the Void, shuffled back into the library by Darksteel Colossus's "If Darksteel Colossus would be put into a graveyard from anywhere, reveal Darksteel Colossus and shuffle it into its owner's library instead.", put on the bottom by Wheel of Sun and Moon's "If a card would be put into enchanted player's graveyard from anywhere, instead that card is revealed and put on the bottom of that player's library."), when does it stop?
- Decision: One card at a time; only cards put into that graveyard count toward X and only a creature card put there stops it. A milled card that comes back into the library doesn't end the process: it goes on with the next card (and mills a returned card again if it comes back on top). It ends when the library is empty (Leyline of the Void: the whole library is exiled, the ruling), when a mill moved no card, or when every card left in the library is one the process already milled (with a safety cap of 64 mills per card the library had). Known deviation, pending the loops work (C4, postponed by the user; finish-line-plan §5 "Revisit later", Loops): when only cards the process milled are left (Wheel of Sun and Moon on the milling player, or a library of only Darksteel Colossus with X above 0), the process would repeat forever, a loop of mandatory actions that 104.4b makes a draw (as the Arcbond and Faceless Butcher rulings apply it); the engine ends the process instead (against the Wheel, after every card of the library has gone to the bottom once) and the game goes on.
- Alternatives: Count every milled card toward X (contradicts the ruling). End the process when a mill leaves the library no smaller (the engine until small-fixes-3, Nov 20: a Darksteel Colossus milled first ended it with no card in the graveyard, where the card's text goes on with the next card). End the game in a draw when the process can't end (104.4b): needs the loops work.
- Why: The card's text and the ruling for the counting and the empty library. A card that comes back is just another card of the library, and "repeats this process" mills the top card whatever it is. The loop end is the deviation: recognizing it as a draw belongs to the loops work the user postponed (the entry `growing-loops-run-to-the-limit` has the other loop the engine doesn't recognize). History: decision-audit-1 (Nov 20) found both the Colossus bug and the Wheel deviation (results in `docs/agents/items/decision-audit-1.md`); small-fixes-3 fixed the first and registered the second.
- Affects: Helm of Obedience, with Leyline of the Void-style exile replacements, with cards that shuffle themselves back (Darksteel Colossus, Blightsteel Colossus, Progenitus, Nexus of Fate, Legacy Weapon), and with Wheel of Sun and Moon or Yawgmoth's Testament ("If a card would be put into your graveyard or exile from anywhere this turn, put it on the bottom of your library instead.") on the milling player.

- Tests: `tests/cards/paper_loops.rs` (`helm_of_obedience_mills_the_whole_library_against_leyline_of_the_void`, `helm_of_obedience_stops_after_x_cards`, `helm_of_obedience_goes_on_past_a_card_shuffled_back_into_the_library`, `helm_of_obedience_against_wheel_of_sun_and_moon_ends_after_one_pass_a_known_deviation`).

## toymakers-trap-every-number-chosen: With every number already chosen, "choose a number ... that hasn't been chosen" chooses nothing, so "If you do" fails
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 608.2c "The controller of the spell or ability follows its instructions in the order written. ..." / The Toymaker's Trap's ruling: "If The Toymaker's Trap leaves the battlefield and later returns to the battlefield, it has no memory of which numbers were chosen for its effect before. If [a player] [does, doesn’t, or can’t], [effect].” Or “[A player] may [do something]. If [that player] [does, doesn’t, or can’t], [effect].” The action [do something] is a cost, paid when the spell or ability resolves. - Affects: The Toymaker's Trap (the only card with a number choice "that hasn't been chosen").
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/choices.rs` (`ChoiceKind::NumberNotChosen`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/modal_history.rs` (`unchosen_numbers`, `record_number`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/choices_6.rs`.

## permission-choice-always-asked: When two or more permissions allow playing a card the same way, the card's own among them, the player is asked which one they're using, by default the card's own
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 601.2 "To cast a spell is to take it from where it is (usually the hand), put it on the stack, and pay its costs, so that it will eventually resolve and have its effect. ..." / 601.3 "A player can begin to cast a spell only if a rule or effect allows that player to cast it and no rule or effect prohibits that player from casting it." / 305.1 "A player who has priority may play a land card from their hand during a main phase of their turn when the stack is empty. ..." / 702.187c "“Mayhem” without a cost means “You may play this card from your graveyard if you discarded it this turn.”" / 601.3c "If an effect allows a player to cast a spell as though it had flash only if an alternative or additional cost is paid, that player may begin to cast that spell as though it had flash." / Muldrotha, the Gravetide ruling (2020-11-10): "If multiple effects allow you to play a card from your graveyard, you must announce which permission you're using as you begin to play the card." / Kess, Dissident Mage ruling (2019-06-14): "If you cast a card from your graveyard using another permission (such as that of flashback), Kess's effect doesn't apply. You can cast another instant or sorcery card from your graveyard."
- Question: When a card's own permission (its own ability: mayhem, aftermath, an adventurer card on an adventure; or the rules' permission to cast from the hand) and an effect's permission (Muldrotha, Crucible of Worlds, Kess, Omniscience) both let the player play it the same way, which is used, and is the player asked?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 20, changing the engine's earlier policy (registered as `own-permission-first`, provisional: the card's own permission, without asking); the user: "We should always ask. There could be a permanent on the field that punishes flashback costs specifically or something of the sort, making the cards own permission a worse option." Whenever two or more permissions allow playing a card the same way, the card's own among them, the player announces which one they're using (Muldrotha's ruling): a land a keyword's rule lets the player play from outside their hand (mayhem without a cost, Oscorp Industries; an adventurer land on an adventure) with an effect's permission to play it too (Muldrotha's land) is played after a "Choose the permission you're using" question (its own permission, then each effect's); a way of casting a card that its own permission allows outside its owner's hand (Ribbons by aftermath, a creature from an adventure in exile) is offered with each effect's permission that allows it too, and casting it that way asks which. Default (no answer or an invalid one): the card's own permission if its costs could be paid, otherwise the first permission whose costs could be paid. An effect's permission chosen is recorded on the card ("cast this way", `GameObject::played_with`) and, if it can be used only once each turn, used up (Muldrotha's land or creature spell, Kess's instant or sorcery); the card's own uses nothing up. Ways that differ were already separate options, so their choice is the player's as before: a flashback, escape or other alternative cost comes with the card's own permission (flashback's cost isn't paid with Kess's permission), and retrace's additional cost outside the hand with retrace's, so those ways aren't offered with an effect's permission; casting a flashback card from a graveyard with Kess out offers flashback and Kess's way for its mana cost. Kept from the earlier policy: in the player's own hand, where the rules let them cast the card (601.3), an effect's permission is offered with it only if it changes how the card is cast (flash, a cost it requires, a cost reduction, a cost increase per card, the face, how mana may be spent); the engine models Omniscience's "You may cast spells from your hand without paying their mana costs" as a permission for the hand whose alternative cost is offered as a way of its own, and that permission allows nothing the rules don't (`a_card_cast_from_hand_for_its_mana_cost_with_omniscience_out_asks_no_permission`).
- Alternatives: The card's own permission without asking (the engine's policy until Nov 20, `own-permission-first`): the other answer only used up a once-each-turn permission or recorded the effect's permission on the card, which nothing in the pool rewarded for a card with a permission of its own; the user: a permanent could punish the card's own permission ("punishes flashback costs specifically"), making it the worse choice. Prefer the effect's permission: uses up permissions for nothing. Ask in the hand too (an effect's permission for the hand that changes nothing): a question whose other answer is the same cast.
- Why: Muldrotha's ruling says the player announces the permission whenever several allow playing the card, and the user decided the engine asks every time (Nov 20); the default keeps what the engine did before, which uses nothing up. In the hand the rules' permission isn't one of several an effect gives, and the effect's permission there stands for its alternative cost, offered separately (the engine's model; a judgment call extending the decision, kept from the earlier policy).
- Affects: Oscorp Industries with Muldrotha, Crucible of Worlds, Ramunap Excavator and the like; aftermath halves with Kess or Yawgmoth's Will; adventurer cards on an adventure with an effect's permission to cast from exile; flashback, escape and mayhem cards with Yawgmoth's Will, Kess, Muldrotha or Karador (different ways, as before); every card cast from the hand while Omniscience is out (not asked).

## cast-if-able-mana-abilities-default: A spell cast "if able" whose mana the pool doesn't cover is paid for with mana abilities by default
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 601.2g "If the total cost includes a mana payment, the player then has a chance to activate mana abilities (see rule 605, “Mana Abilities”). Mana abilities must be activated before costs are paid." / 608.2g / 733.1 "If a player takes an illegal action or starts to take an action but can’t legally complete it, the entire action is reversed and any payments already made are canceled. ..." / 733.2 "... The player may redo the reversed action in a legal way or take any other action allowed by the rules." / Wild Evocation rulings (2010-08-15): "If the mandatory additional cost includes a mana payment, the situation is more complex. If the player has enough unspent mana to pay the cost, that player must do so. If the player can't possibly pay the cost, the card remains uncast in their hand. However, if the player has the means to produce enough mana to pay the cost, then they have a choice: The player may cast the card, produce mana, and pay the cost. Or the player may choose to activate no mana abilities, thus making the card impossible to cast because the additional mana can't be paid." and "If a player reveals a nonland card, that player must cast that card if it's possible to do so, even if they don't want to. ..."
- Question: A spell an effect makes a player cast "if able" during its resolution (Wild Evocation's "casts it without paying its mana cost if able") has a total cost with a mana payment (an additional cost such as Lash of the Balrog's "sacrifice a creature or pay {4}" with no creature to sacrifice, a kicker cost the player announced, a cost increase) that the mana in their pool doesn't cover. The ruling lets them decline to activate mana abilities, so the card stays uncast. What does a player who gives no answer choose, and what does declining do when the card could be cast without that mana?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 19. The engine asks the player (a yes/no decision, "Activate mana abilities to pay for this spell?") only in that situation: not when the pool's mana pays the cost (it must be spent), nor when no mana ability could pay it (the card stays uncast). A default answer activates them and casts the spell. Declining reverses the casting (733.1), and the player casts the card again, as they must if able, with no mana ability (nor special action that adds mana) available: only costs the pool's mana pays can be chosen, so a player who announced a kicker cost and then won't produce the mana for it casts the spell unkicked (733.2); if a mandatory cost needs more mana than the pool has, the card stays uncast.
- Alternatives: Default to declining, like other optional payments (`default-yes-no`: "no for paying an optional cost"): an uninterested player would keep their lands untapped and the card in hand. Declining could also leave the card uncast whatever made the cost need mana abilities: a player could then dodge the mandatory cast by announcing a kicker cost and refusing to produce the mana for it.
- Why: The effect's instruction is mandatory and the player is able to follow it; declining is a choice the ruling allows, not what the card asks for, and the engine's other question about a mandatory "if able" cast, CR 118.8c's "Cast this spell?" (`cost_rules::may_decline_cast_if_able`), also casts by default. This also keeps the engine's earlier behavior (it always paid). Declining leaves the card uncast only when the ruling says it does: the mandatory costs need the mana. It was provisional because `default-yes-no` points the other way for optional payments. Accepted by the user on Nov 19 with the coordinator's explanation: the player is asked; the default is only the fallback for an agent's `Answer::Default` or an invalid answer, which every decision has.
- Affects: Wild Evocation and other mandatory "cast it if able" effects, with spells whose total cost includes mana despite being cast without paying their mana cost (additional mana costs, kicker and other optional costs, cost increases such as Thalia, Guardian of Thraben).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/cost_rules.rs` (`agrees_to_activate_mana_abilities`, `DECLINED_MANA_ABILITIES`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/casting.rs` (the spell's payment); `crates/mtg-engine/src/resolve.rs` (`Effect::CastCard`, which sets `special.casting_if_able` and casts again with `special.pool_only`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/mana_abilities.rs` (`sources_inner`: no mana sources for that player).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p078_group_hug.rs` (`wild_evocation_pays_a_mandatory_additional_cost_if_able`, `wild_evocation_declining_mana_abilities_for_a_kicker_still_casts_the_spell`).

- Decision: No, for an instruction that puts counters on an object it has a player choose in the same instruction (`Effect::AddCounters` of a `Sel::Choose`, in `Game::exec` and in the simultaneous planner for "each player": Agitator Ant, Orzhov Advokist): the candidates are the objects that can have counters of that kind (`counter_rules::counters_prevented` doesn't stop them). An object that can have them but gets none because of a replacement effect (two "that many minus one" effects) can still be chosen, and then didn't have counters put on it "this way". A choice the compiled text makes first and stores (Eye of Doom's "each player chooses a nonland permanent and puts a doom counter on it", Promise of Loyalty's "Each player puts a vow counter on a creature they control and sacrifices the rest") isn't narrowed, nor is a keyword action's own choice (bolster's, `kwa/bolster_adapt.rs`): there a player may still choose an object that can't have the counters (with Melira's Keepers as their only creature, a Promise of Loyalty player keeps it, with no vow counter), a gap in this decision's reach (review of rulings-residue-misc-2, Nov 20).
- Alternatives: Offer every object matching the filter, as before (choosing the Keepers puts nothing on it, and Agitator Ant goaded only what got counters, so nothing but the offer itself changed); restrict only Agitator Ant's wording (the AST doesn't distinguish it from other "put counters on a creature" choices).
- Why: 608.2d forbids choosing an impossible option, and Agitator Ant's ruling applies it to a creature that can't have the counters, as the Solemnity, Suncleanser, Blightbeetle and Blossombind rulings do for any "may put counters" instruction; the same reading as `impossible-choice-options-not-offered` for a choice between instructions. For an optional instruction those rulings decide it; for a mandatory one ("put a +1/+1 counter on a creature you control") whether the object is an "option" in 608.2d's sense is a reading by analogy, which is why it's provisional.
- Affects: Agitator Ant, Orzhov Advokist and the other instructions that put counters on an object they have a player choose, with Melira's Keepers, Solemnity, Suncleanser's and similar "can't have counters" effects.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/kw/counter_choice.rs` (`chosen_for_counters`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/resolve.rs` (`Effect::AddCounters`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/simultaneous.rs` (the "each player may put counters" plan).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_residue_misc_2c_replacement.rs` (`agitator_ant_goads_only_creatures_that_got_the_counters`).

## impossible-choice-options-not-offered: A choice between instructions offers only those that can be carried out, while any can
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 608.2d "If an effect of a spell or ability offers any choices other than choices already made as part of casting the spell, activating the ability, or otherwise putting the spell or ability on the stack, the player announces these while applying the effect. The player can’t choose an option that’s illegal or impossible, with the exception that having a library with no cards in it doesn’t make drawing a card an impossible action (see rule 121.3). ..." / 609.3 "If an effect attempts to do something impossible, it does only as much as possible." / 701.55b "While facing a villainous choice, a player may choose an option that is illegal or impossible. In that case, they perform as much of the action as is possible. This is an exception to rule 608.2d." / Eumidian Wastewaker ruling (2025-07-25): "When Eumidian Wastewaker’s first ability resolves, you choose to discard a card or sacrifice a permanent, and then defending player chooses to discard a card or sacrifice a permanent. If one of those players can’t discard a card (such as by having no cards in hand), that player must sacrifice a permanent." / Feed the Machine ruling (2010-06-15): "The targeted player may choose “self” even if they can’t perform the resulting action." / Brain Gorgers ruling (2013-07-01), on "any player may sacrifice a creature of their choice. If a player does, counter Brain Gorgers.": "A player can't choose to sacrifice a creature unless they actually control a creature that can be sacrificed. For example, if the action were "discard three cards," a player with two or fewer cards in hand couldn't take the option to counter the spell."
- Question: When an effect has a player choose between instructions as it resolves ("discard a card or sacrifice a permanent"), which options may the player choose? What is an "impossible" option: one none of whose instructions can be carried out, or one any of whose can't? And if none can be carried out?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 20 (as recommended): "Sure, this works." The options offered are those that aren't impossible. Only an option whose words are the instruction itself ("discard a card", "sacrifice a permanent") can be impossible; a word whose consequences the text gives afterward (Feed the Machine's "self or others. If that player chooses self, ...") can always be chosen, as its ruling says. Such an option is impossible only when every instruction in it (besides the engine's bookkeeping) is known to be impossible now: a discard with no card in the player's hand that could be discarded, a sacrifice with no permanent the player controls that matches and may be sacrificed. An option partly possible is offered, and does as much as it can (609.3). If every option is impossible, all are offered. Other instructions are treated as possible (the engine knows the two above, the ones in the "discard or sacrifice" choices of today's cards). A villainous choice is asked by its own code and offers both options (701.55b).
- Alternatives: Offer every option and let an impossible one do nothing: what the engine did before; a player with no hand would escape Eumidian Wastewaker's "discard or sacrifice" for free, against its ruling. Treat an option as impossible when any of its instructions is: a player could then avoid an option that would do most of what it says. Treat an option as impossible when it can't be carried out in full ("discard two cards" with one card in hand), as Brain Gorgers' ruling has it for an action a player may take to counter a spell: such an option would then not be offered while another can be done.
- Why: 608.2d forbids the impossible choice outright; the ruling applies it to the case in hand. An option one of whose instructions can be done isn't "impossible", so 609.3 governs it. Provisional: 608.2d doesn't say whether an option that can be carried out only in part is "impossible", and Brain Gorgers' ruling reads its own optional action the other way (an action that can't be performed in full can't be chosen); today's "discard or sacrifice" choices ask for one card or one permanent, where the two readings agree. The user accepted the reading (Nov 20).
- Affects: Eumidian Wastewaker and the other resolving "choose one" between instructions (`Effect::ChooseOne`).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/impossible_options.rs` (`choosable`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/resolve.rs` and `crates/mtg-engine/src/simultaneous.rs` (`Effect::ChooseOne`).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_residue_misc_1_players.rs` (`eumidian_wastewaker_you_choose_first_and_a_player_who_cant_discard_sacrifices`); `tests/rulings/r_s19_scheme.rs` (`the_targeted_player_may_choose_self_even_if_it_does_nothing`).
