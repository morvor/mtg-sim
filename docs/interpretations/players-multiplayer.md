## conspiracy-draft-needs-three-players: A Conspiracy Draft game (like every multiplayer variant) needs more than two players
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 905.3 "A Conspiracy Draft game is a multiplayer game. The default multiplayer setup is the Free-for-All variant with the attack multiple players option and without the limited range of influence option. See rule 806, “Free-for-All Variant.”" / 100.1b "A multiplayer game is a game that begins with more than two players. See section 8, “Multiplayer Rules.”" / 806.1 "In Free-for-All multiplayer games, a group of players compete as individuals against each other."
- Question: Is a Conspiracy Draft game between exactly two players valid? 905.3 calls the game multiplayer and 100.1b defines multiplayer as more than two players, but no rule forbids a two-player Conspiracy Draft game outright.
- Decision: Invalid. `GameConfig::validate` rejects a Conspiracy Draft game (a Free-for-All configuration with `limited`) for two players with `SetupError::NotMultiplayer`, as it does every multiplayer variant (Free-for-All, Grand Melee, Two-Headed Giant, Emperor, Team vs. Team, Alternating Teams).
- Alternatives: Read 905.3 as describing the usual setup and allow two players: the game would then be an ordinary two-player game with conspiracies (CR 315, 905.4–905.6 still apply), with no attack-multiple-players option to speak of.
- Why: 905.3 states that the game is a multiplayer game and 100.1b leaves no room for a two-player multiplayer game; the default setup it names (Free-for-All, 806) is a multiplayer variant. The judge audit (`docs/agents/reports/judge-audit-1.md`, cr-04) agreed with medium confidence. Decided by the user, Oct 21: keep rejecting two-player Conspiracy Draft games, consistent with 905.3 and 100.1b (two-player Conspiracy play is common in practice, but the CR calls the game multiplayer).
- Affects: game setup only (Conspiracy Draft games with two players); no card behaves differently.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/multiplayer/setup.rs` (`GameConfig::validate`, `NotMultiplayer`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/casual.rs` (`GameConfig::conspiracy_draft_game`).
- Tests: `tests/cr/r905_conspiracy_draft.rs` (`a_conspiracy_draft_game_is_multiplayer_free_for_all`).

## attacking-player-with-shared-team-turns: "The attacking player" is one specific attacking player, fixed by a blocking creature (CR 805.10c)
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 506.2 "During the combat phase, the active player is the attacking player; creatures that player controls may attack. ..." / 805.10a "... During the combat phase, the active team is the attacking team and each player on the active team is an attacking player. ..." / 805.10c "Any rule, object, or effect that refers to an “attacking player” refers to one specific attacking player, not to all attacking players. If an ability of a blocking creature refers to an attacking player, or a spell or ability refers to both a blocking creature and an attacking player, then unless otherwise specified, the attacking player it’s referring to is the player who controls the attacking creature that blocking creature is blocking. If a spell or ability could apply to multiple blocking creatures, the appropriate attacking player is individually determined for each of those blocking creatures. If there are multiple attacking players that could be chosen, the controller of the spell or ability chooses one."
- Question: With shared team turns (Two-Headed Giant, 805.10), which attacking player does card text "the attacking player" mean? Two cases are open: a blocking creature that blocks creatures of both attacking players, and Invasion Plans ("The attacking player chooses how each creature blocks each combat"), whose blockers aren't blocking anything yet when the choice is made.
- Decision: Decided by the user, Oct 21: implement 805.10c fully. "The attacking player" compiles to its own player reference (`PlayerRef::AttackingPlayer`). Without shared team turns it is the active player (506.2), unchanged. With them: for an ability of a blocking creature, or one that refers to a blocking creature (the creature whose blocking or damage triggered it), it is the controller of the attacking creature that blocking creature is blocking, as it last existed if it has left the battlefield; each blocking creature determines its own (each Souls of the Faultless drains the controller of what it blocked; Goblin Goon's "can't block unless you control more creatures than attacking player" compares with the controller of each attacker it would block). When several attacking players could be meant (no blocking creature is involved, as for Contested Game Ball or Norn's Decree, or the blocker blocks creatures of both attacking players), the controller of the spell or ability chooses one as its effect is applied. Invasion Plans: its controller chooses which attacking player declares the blocks, since which creature a blocker blocks is the choice being made.
- Alternatives: Resolve "the attacking player" like "the active player" (805.9): the ability's controller chooses one of the active team's players every time (the engine's earlier provisional behavior, recorded as `attacking-player-is-an-active-player`). For Invasion Plans, let each attacking player choose the blocks of the creatures attacking them: there is a single combined block (805.10d), so the engine doesn't split it.
- Why: 805.10c says so directly. Invasion Plans' choice comes before any creature is blocking, so its blocker rule can't fix a player and the rule's last sentence applies.
- Affects: in games with shared team turns only: Souls of the Faultless, Contested Game Ball, Norn's Decree, Invasion Plans, Karazikar, the Eye Tyrant (whose trigger, an opponent attacking another opponent, can't happen between two teams), Goblin Goon, Mogg Toady, Monstrous Hound, and "that attacking player" of attack triggers (Curse of Chaos, Curse of Inertia, Curse of Shallow Graves, Jolene, the Plunder Queen, Breena, the Demagogue, Combat Calligrapher, Ellie, Brick Master), and the "that player" of "whenever a player/an opponent attacks you" triggers, which is the attacking player (Emberwilde Captain, Archnemesis, Struggle for Project Purity's Enclave mode). Goblin Goon, Mogg Toady and Monstrous Hound already compared with each attacker's controller. Compiling every card before and after the change differs in fourteen cards only (Souls of the Faultless, Contested Game Ball, Norn's Decree, Karazikar and the ten attack-trigger cards above; each `ActivePlayer` became `AttackingPlayer`), and without shared team turns both evaluate to the active player.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/teams.rs` (`attacking_player_candidates`, `choose_attacking_player`, `choose_one_attacking_player`, `ATTACKING_PLAYER_VAR`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/eval.rs` (`PlayerRef::AttackingPlayer`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/block_choice.rs` (`block_decider`: Invasion Plans); `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/phrases.rs`, `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/player_subjects.rs`, `choice_grammar.rs`, `triggers_attacks.rs` (compiling "the attacking player"); `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/statics.rs` (Goblin Goon family).
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_multiplayer.rs` (`the_attacking_player_without_a_blocker_is_the_one_its_controller_chooses`, `a_blocking_creatures_attacking_player_is_the_controller_of_what_it_blocks`, `a_blocker_blocking_both_attacking_players_creatures_lets_its_controller_choose`, `each_blocking_creature_determines_its_own_attacking_player`, `norns_decree_draws_for_the_attacking_player_its_controller_chooses`, `invasion_plans_controller_chooses_which_attacking_player_declares_blocks`, `without_shared_team_turns_the_attacking_player_is_the_active_player`); `tests/cr/r805_shared_team_turns.rs` (`the_attacking_player_for_a_blocker_is_the_controller_of_what_it_blocks`).

## range-combat-trigger-events: The trigger event of "attacks", "blocks" and "blocks or becomes blocked" is the creature alone (CR 801.7)
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 801.7 "A triggered ability doesn’t trigger unless its trigger event happens entirely within the range of influence of its source’s controller." and its example: "Whenever enchanted creature becomes blocked" triggers "because the entire event (Runeclaw Bear becomes blocked) happens within Rob’s range of influence", while "Whenever enchanted creature becomes blocked by a creature" doesn't, "because that event includes the blocking creature, which is out of Rob’s range."
- Question: The example settles "becomes blocked" versus "becomes blocked by a creature". Which players and creatures are part of the event of the other combat triggers that don't name them: "whenever [a creature] attacks" (the player, planeswalker or battle it attacks; the other attacking creatures), "whenever [a creature] blocks" (the creature it blocks) and "whenever [a creature] blocks or becomes blocked" (the creature it blocks or is blocked by)?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Oct 21: as in the example, only what the trigger condition names. The event of "[a creature] attacks", "blocks", "becomes blocked" and "blocks or becomes blocked" is that creature attacking, blocking or becoming blocked, and it happens within a player's range of influence if the creature is within it. Conditions that name the other party ("attacks you", "attacks a player", "blocks a creature", "becomes blocked by a creature") still include it.
- Alternatives: Treat "attacks" as including what the creature attacks and "blocks" as including the creature it blocks (the engine's earlier behavior), so that a third player's ability about another player's creature doesn't trigger when that creature fights someone out of the third player's range.
- Why: The example's reasoning is that the event consists of what the trigger condition describes; "blocks" and "attacks" are worded like "becomes blocked", without the other party.
- Affects: games with the limited range of influence option only (CR 801; Emperor, Grand Melee): abilities of a player's permanents about another player's creature (Auras, "whenever a creature attacks/blocks") when that creature attacks or blocks with or against a player out of the first player's range.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/multiplayer/range.rs` (`trigger_in_range`).
- Tests: `tests/cr/r801_combat_trigger_events.rs` (`attacks_and_becomes_blocked_ignore_the_defending_player_and_blockers_out_of_range`, `blocks_ignores_the_attacker_out_of_range_but_blocks_a_creature_doesnt`); `tests/cr/r801_examples.rs` (`becomes_blocked_triggers_but_becomes_blocked_by_a_creature_out_of_range_doesnt`).

## venture-limit-looks-at-whole-turn: "Can't venture into the dungeon more than once each turn" counts ventures earlier in the turn
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: Keen-Eared Sentry: "Each opponent can't venture into the dungeon more than once each turn." / 101.2 "When a rule or effect allows or directs something to happen, and another effect states that it can’t happen, the “can’t” effect takes precedence." / Rule of Law ruling: "Rule of Law looks at the entire turn to see if a player has cast a spell, even if Rule of Law wasn’t on the battlefield when that spell was cast."
- Question: Does an opponent's venture made this turn before Keen-Eared Sentry came under its controller's control count toward the limit? And does taking the initiative (whose triggered ability ventures into Undercity, CR 725.2) count as a venture?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Oct 28. Every venture this turn counts (a venture marker moved into a room, CR 309.4c), including those before the Sentry arrived and those of the initiative; once an opponent has ventured this turn, any further venture by them does nothing (the initiative is still taken).
- Alternatives: Count only the ventures made while the Sentry was on the battlefield: would let an opponent venture again in the turn the Sentry arrives, unlike the rulings for the analogous "can't cast more than one spell each turn" (Rule of Law, Deafening Silence, Arcane Laboratory).
- Why: The "each turn" limit is worded like Rule of Law's, whose rulings look at the whole turn; venturing into Undercity is venturing into the dungeon (CR 701.49d).
- Affects: Keen-Eared Sentry.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/cards/keen_eared_sentry.rs` (`venture_prohibited`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/dungeons.rs` (`venture_into`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/m_keen_eared_sentry.rs` (`an_opponent_ventures_only_once_each_turn`, `ventures_before_it_entered_count_and_without_it_there_is_no_limit`).

## history-counts-players-who-left: "For each opponent who [did something] this turn" counts opponents who have since left the game
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 800.4i "If an effect requires information about a specific player, the effect uses the current information about that player if they are still in the game; otherwise, the effect uses the last known information about that player before they left the game. If an effect requires information from the game about actions players have taken, the effect can find actions that were taken by a player who has left the game. ..." / 102.1 "A player is one of the people in the game. ..." / 702.125b "Players who have left the game are not counted when determining how many opponents you have."
- Question: An effect counts the players for whom something happened this turn ("for each of your opponents who lost life this turn", "for each opponent who was dealt damage this turn"). Is a player who has since left the game counted? The CR doesn't decide it: 800.4i's second sentence covers only actions taken by a player who left (being dealt damage or losing life isn't one), its first sentence (last known information about a specific player) supports counting without compelling it, and 102.1 and 702.125b support a strict present-tense reading of "opponent". The rulings disagree: about 15 cards' rulings from 2007 to 2024 count them (Spinerock Knoll 2007-10-01; Notorious Throng 2008-04-01; Sygg, River Cutthroat 2008-05-01; Cryptborn Horror 2012-10-01; Thought Sponge 2019-08-23; Chandra's Incinerator 2020-06-23; Belbe, Corrupted Observer, Rakdos, Lord of Riots, Tymna the Weaver and Ludevic, Necro-Alchemist 2020-11-10; Dunerider Outlaw 2021-03-19; Teysa, Opulent Oligarch 2024-02-02; Moonshae Pixie 2024-04-12; Kaito, Bane of Nightmares 2024-09-20); Gnoll War Band's (2022-06-10) says "Opponents who have left the game will not be counted when determining how many opponents have been dealt damage this turn."
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 5. Counted; Gnoll War Band's ruling is set aside as inconsistent with the other rulings. `Value::EventsThisTurn` and `Value::EventsDuring` with `Tally::Players` count every player the matching events were about, in the game or not. Counts of players now ("for each opponent you have") are unaffected: they only see players in the game. Likewise a value taken over players of what each did this turn (`Value::OverPlayers` of a `Value::EventsThisTurn` or `Value::EventsDuring` tally, or of a threshold on one: "the greatest number of cards an opponent has drawn this turn", "for each opponent who drew two or more cards this turn") considers every player, in the game or not; other values over players ("the highest life total among all players") only see players in the game. A count of players selected by what they did or what happened to them this turn (`Value::CountPlayers` with a `PlayerFilter::History` tally or `PlayerFilter::DealtDamageThisTurn`: Ludevic, Necro-Alchemist's "if a player other than you lost life this turn", Antagonism's "unless one of their opponents was dealt damage this turn", bloodthirst's "if an opponent was dealt damage this turn") also considers every player, in the game or not; whether a player who left is an opponent is judged as they left (team membership doesn't change).
- Alternatives: Leave them out, as Gnoll War Band's ruling says (the engine's earlier behavior), reading "opponent" strictly as a player still in the game (102.1, 702.125b). That contradicts about 15 cards' rulings. No wording sets Gnoll War Band apart: combat vs any damage doesn't (Notorious Throng and Dunerider Outlaw count any damage and count players who left); "left" vs "lost" the game doesn't (Thought Sponge, Dunerider Outlaw, Cryptborn Horror, Notorious Throng, Sygg and Spinerock Knoll say "left"); being a cost reduction doesn't (Chandra's Incinerator, Rakdos); a count of players vs a sum doesn't (Belbe, Teysa, Kaito, Moonshae Pixie, Tymna count players). Moonshae Pixie is from Gnoll War Band's own set (CLB, 2022-06-10) with the same structure ("the number of opponents who were dealt combat damage this turn"), and its ruling counting players who left was added 2024-04-12, after Gnoll's. Every other ruling in the data that leaves departed players out is about a present state (encore and "for each opponent" token counts, Voice of Many, "the number of opponents you have", Apostle of Invasion's poison counters, up to 2025-11-17). Gnoll War Band's ruling is exempt as inconsistent with the rest (`docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P186.tsv`); if the user prefers it, it would apply to every such count, not to Gnoll War Band alone.
- Why: Conclusion C (the CR is ambiguous, the rulings settle it), confidence medium-high, with one outlier. Wizards answered the strict reading of "opponent" with a fixed formula used since 2007: "a player who is currently your opponent, or a player who was your opponent at the time they left the game" (Spinerock Knoll 2007-10-01, Sygg, River Cutthroat 2008-05-01; Notorious Throng 2008-04-01: "players who were your opponents at the time they left the game"). The rulings before and after Gnoll War Band's agree with each other, including the newest (Moonshae Pixie 2024-04-12, Kaito 2024-09-20), so Gnoll's is an isolated inconsistent ruling, not one outdated by a rules change: no dated change was found. 800.4i's first sentence (last known information about a player who left) entered the CR of 2007-10-01 (as 600.4e: "If an effect requires information about a specific player, the effect uses the current information about that player if he or she is still in the game; otherwise, the effect uses the last known information about that player before he or she left the game."), the date of Spinerock Knoll's Lorwyn ruling; its second sentence (actions taken by a player who left) entered the CR effective 2019-08-23 (as 800.4h); both dated from the archived CR texts (https://github.com/pit142857/mtg-cr), both before Gnoll War Band's ruling, and no later change explains it. Decided by the user on Nov 5 as recommended: keep counting them, with Gnoll War Band's ruling set aside as inconsistent (exempt in `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P186.tsv`, logged in `docs/RULING_DISCREPANCIES.md`).
- Affects: Belbe, Corrupted Observer; Gnoll War Band; Teysa, Opulent Oligarch; Moonshae Pixie; Sygg, River Cutthroat; Tymna the Weaver; Kaito, Bane of Nightmares; Thought Sponge; Smuggler's Share; Ludevic, Necro-Alchemist; Antagonism; bloodthirst; and every other "for each opponent who ... this turn" count in a multiplayer game.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/kw/value_results.rs` (`events_this_turn`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/history_query.rs` (`events_during`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/aggregates.rs` (`over_players`, `count_players`).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p186_phyrexians.rs` (`belbe_counts_an_opponent_who_lost_life_then_the_game`, `history_counts_include_opponents_who_left`); `tests/rulings/r_p186_left_game.rs` (`moonshae_pixie_counts_an_opponent_dealt_combat_damage_who_then_left`, `sygg_counts_an_opponent_who_lost_3_life_then_left`, `tymna_counts_an_opponent_dealt_combat_damage_who_then_lost`, `kaito_counts_an_opponent_who_lost_life_then_the_game`, `ludevic_counts_a_player_who_lost_life_then_the_game`, `bloodthirst_counts_an_opponent_dealt_damage_who_then_left`); `tests/cards/value_results_history.rs` (`gnoll_war_band_counts_opponents_who_were_dealt_damage`); `tests/rulings/r_p118_misc.rs` (`thought_sponge_counts_the_greatest_total_drawn_by_one_opponent`, `smugglers_share_counts_an_opponent_who_drew_two_cards_then_left`).

## arbiter-of-knollridge-uses-controllers-range: Arbiter of Knollridge's ability uses its controller's range of influence, not each player's own
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 801.10 "Spells and abilities can’t affect objects or players outside their controller’s range of influence. The parts of the effect that attempt to affect an out-of-range object or player will do nothing. The rest of the effect will work normally." / 801.11 "If a spell or ability requires information from the game, it gets only information from within its controller’s range of influence. It doesn’t see objects or events outside its controller’s range of influence." / Arbiter of Knollridge ruling (2007-10-01): "In other multiplayer formats, each player's life total is set to the highest life total within their range of influence. These changes happen at the same time. For example, if each player has a range of influence of one, and the players in the game are Alice (4 life), Barry (8 life), Carrie (11 life), and Doug (7 life), in that order, then Alice's life total will become 8 and Barry and Doug's life totals will each become 11." / Imperial Mask ruling (2007-05-01): "Only teammates within the range of influence of Imperial Mask's controller will get a token. ..." / Benediction of Moons ruling (2006-02-01): "... In a Free-for-All multiplayer game, players who have been eliminated or who are outside your range of influence don't count toward the total."
- Question: Arbiter of Knollridge: "When this creature enters, each player's life total becomes the highest life total among all players." With a limited range of influence, whose range decides which players are affected and which life totals are compared: the ability's controller's (801.10, 801.11), or each affected player's own, as the card's ruling and its example have it?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 5. The controller's range, as 801.10 and 801.11 say: the ability affects only the players within its controller's range and sets each to the highest life total among the players within that range. Arbiter's ruling, which uses each affected player's own range, is set aside as inconsistent with 801.10, 801.11 and the other range-of-influence rulings (Imperial Mask's and Benediction of Moons'), as Gnoll War Band's was (exempt in `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P014.tsv`, logged in `docs/RULING_DISCREPANCIES.md`). With Alice controlling Arbiter in the ruling's example, Alice, Barry and Doug become 8; Carrie, out of range, keeps 11.
- Alternatives: Follow the ruling: each player's total becomes the highest within that player's own range (Alice 8, Barry and Doug 11). No controller's range gives that result under 801.10 (only Alice has Alice, Barry and Doug in range, and Carrie, whose 11 they get, is outside it), so it would need a card-specific exception to 801.10 and 801.11.
- Why: Work item ruling-conflicts-3, case 7 (conclusion D). 801.10 and 801.11 were already in the CR current as of 2007-10-01, the ruling's date, word for word as 601.10 and 601.11 (archived CR texts), and 601.11's Coat of Arms example then narrowed an affected player's view further, to "players within range of both Rob and the controller of Coat of Arms" (changed to the controller's whole range in the CR effective 2011-05-01), which gives Barry 8 and Doug 7, not 11; so no rules change explains the ruling. The other rulings on range of influence use the controller's range: Imperial Mask (2007-05-01) and Benediction of Moons (2006-02-01: "players who have been eliminated or who are outside your range of influence don't count toward the total"). Read alone, the ruling's first sentence could mean the controller's range ("their" for the Arbiter's controller), but its example rules that out. Its other two rulings (life-gain triggers; Two-Headed Giant teams) are cited. The user's decision of Nov 5 (evening) accepted the recommendation of work item ruling-conflicts-3 ("For the user", item 1).
- Affects: Arbiter of Knollridge, in games with the limited range of influence option (CR 801; Emperor, Grand Melee).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/eval.rs` (`eval_players`, `player_filter_matches`: no player outside the controller's range, by `crates/mtg-engine/src/multiplayer/range.rs` `sees_player`), used by `crates/mtg-engine/src/resolve.rs` (`Effect::SetLife`) for the players affected and by `crates/mtg-engine/src/aggregates.rs` (`over_players`) for the highest life total.
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p014_catch_up.rs` (`arbiter_of_knollridge_with_range_of_influence_uses_its_controllers_range`).

## greatest-card-unique-player: "The player who exiled the card with the greatest mana value" is one player; with a tie, no player is
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 202.3 "The mana value of an object is a number equal to the total amount of mana in its mana cost, regardless of color. ..." / Psychic Battle: "The player who reveals the card with the greatest mana value may change the target or targets. If two or more cards are tied for greatest, the target or targets remain unchanged." / Timesifter's rulings: "Each time there's a tie for the highest cost, only the players involved in the tie continue." and "If a player fails to exile a card, they can't take the extra turn."
- Question: When each player exiles (reveals) a card and "the player who exiled the card with the greatest mana value" does something, who is that when two or more cards tie, and is a player with no card ever that player?
- Decision: Only a single player whose card has the greatest mana value among the cards compared; with two or more tied, nobody (Timesifter's own tie rule then repeats the process among the tied players; Psychic Battle's leaves the targets). A player who exiled no card is never that player. "Each player who exiled the card with the greatest mana value" is every player whose card ties for it.
- Alternatives: (A) On a tie, every tied player (as "each player who" says).
- Why: "The player" names one player; both cards with this wording state a tie rule that gives the instruction to no one directly.
- Affects: Timesifter; Psychic Battle (hand-written, uses the same comparison).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/per_player_results_6.rs` (`greatest_mana_value_comparison`, `tied_players_repeat`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/cards6_per_player_results.rs` (`timesifter_tied_players_repeat_until_the_tie_is_broken`, `timesifter_tied_until_no_card_is_left_gives_no_extra_turn`).

## player-named-twice-draws-twice: A player named twice in "you and that player each draw" is given one instruction to draw twice that many
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 121.2 "Cards may only be drawn one at a time. If a player is instructed to draw multiple cards, that player performs that many individual card draws." / 121.2a "An instruction to draw multiple cards can be modified by replacement effects that refer to the number of cards drawn. This modification occurs before considering any of the individual card draws. See rule 616.1g." / 119.9 "Some triggered abilities are written, “Whenever [a player] gains life, . . . .” Such abilities are treated as though they are written, “Whenever a source causes [a player] to gain life, . . . .” If a player gains 0 life, no life gain event has occurred, and these abilities won’t trigger." / Diviner Spirit ruling (2013-10-17): "If Diviner Spirit deals combat damage to its controller (perhaps because damage was redirected), that player will draw twice that number of cards." / Angel of Destiny ruling (2020-09-25): "If a creature you control somehow deals combat damage to you (most likely because its combat damage was redirected), you gain twice that much life as a single life-gaining event." / Alms Collector ruling (2017-08-25): "To determine whether a player is instructed to draw multiple once or instructed multiple times to draw one card, count how many times the word “draw” is used."
- Question: When "you" and "that player" of "you and that player each draw that many cards" are the same player, that player draws twice that many cards (Diviner Spirit ruling). Is that one instruction to draw twice that many cards, or two instructions to draw that many? The rulings give only the total. It matters to replacement effects that refer to the number of cards an instruction draws (121.2a), such as Alms Collector's "If an opponent would draw two or more cards, instead you and that player each draw a card."
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 7 (work item user-questions-nov7, Part B): "I feel like the Diviner Spirit ruling does not contradict the Angel of Destiny ruling at all. They both seem to imply a single instance, and that's what I would go with." One instruction: the player named both as "you" and as "that player" is instructed once to draw twice that many cards, and a replacement effect that refers to the number of cards drawn applies once to the combined draw (with an opponent's Alms Collector, Diviner Spirit's 2 combat damage redirected to its controller has that player and Alms Collector's controller each draw one card). The same goes for gaining life (one life-gaining event of twice that much, Angel of Destiny's ruling) and any other "you and that player each [do N]" whose referents coincide. A player counts twice only in a group of players each named by "you" or by a reference to one player ("you and that player"); "each player" and other groups name each player once.
- Alternatives: Two instructions (this entry's provisional decision until Nov 7): the player is instructed to draw that many cards as "you" and again as "that player", and a replacement effect that refers to the number of cards drawn applies to each (with Alms Collector, that player and Alms Collector's controller would each draw two cards); draws never happen at the same time (121.2), so nothing would merge them as Angel of Destiny's ruling merges two gains of life.
- Why: The two rulings read alike: Diviner Spirit's "that player will draw twice that number of cards" and Angel of Destiny's "you gain twice that much life as a single life-gaining event" each describe a single instance of twice the amount. The sentence uses the word "draw" once, and Alms Collector's ruling counts instructions by the word ("count how many times the word “draw” is used"), so a player it names twice gets one instruction, for the total.
- Affects: Diviner Spirit, Xyris, the Writhing Storm and Sergeant John Benton ("you and that player each draw that many cards") when their combat damage is dealt to their controller, together with a replacement effect that counts the cards drawn (Alms Collector); Angel of Destiny ("you and that player each gain that much life").
