# Zones and objects

Judgment calls about zones and what's in them (CR 400–408). Format and kinds:
`docs/agents/items/interpretation-register.md`; index: `docs/INTERPRETATIONS.md`. What the
engine doesn't support at all (Contraptions, host/augment, sticker sheets, ...) is a scope
decision listed in `docs/DEFERRED.md`, not here.

## outside-the-game-is-the-sideboard: Cards a player brings in "from outside the game" come from their sideboard
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 400.11 "An object is outside the game if it isn’t in any of the game’s zones. Outside the game is not a zone." / 400.11a "Cards in a player’s sideboard are outside the game. See rule 100.4." / 400.11b "Some effects bring cards into a game from outside the game. ..." / Eyetwitch rulings (shared by the Lesson cards): "If you bring a card into the game from outside the game in a Constructed tournament (or a Constructed game played under tournament conditions), you may select only a card from your sideboard. In casual Constructed games, the card comes from your collection." and "If you bring a card into the game from outside the game in a Sealed Deck or Booster Draft event, you may select only a card that is in the card pool that you built your deck from."
- Question: Which cards are outside the game for "a card you own from outside the game" (learn, wishes, Coax from the Blind Eternities)? Under the rulings it depends on the setting: the sideboard in tournaments, the card pool in Limited, the whole collection in casual games.
- Decision: A player's cards outside the game are the cards set up as their sideboard (`Game::add_to_sideboard`, in `Zone::Outside`); the engine has no collection.
- Alternatives: A configurable collection or card pool per player for casual or Limited games: closer to the casual ruling, but the "whole collection" would be an arbitrary list of cards.
- Why: The sideboard is the tournament reading and the only well-defined set of cards; deck setups that want a Limited pool put it in the sideboard.
- Affects: learn (Lessons), wishes (Burning Wish, Glittering Wish, ...), Coax from the Blind Eternities, Research // Development, The Raven's Warning, Eyetwitch.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/start.rs` (`Game::add_to_sideboard`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/object.rs` (`Zone::Outside`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/game_rules_outside.rs` (`coax_takes_an_eldrazi_from_outside_the_game_or_face_up_exile`).

## looked-at-hand-card-stays-seen: A card looked at in another player's hand stays visible to the looker while it remains there
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 402.3 "A player may arrange their hand in any convenient fashion and look at it at any time. A player can’t look at the cards in another player’s hand but may count those cards at any time." / 701.20e "Some effects instruct a player to look at one or more cards. Looking at a card follows the same rules as revealing a card, except that the card is shown only to the specified player." / 400.7 "An object that moves from one zone to another becomes a new object with no memory of, or relation to, its previous existence. ..."
- Question: Urza's Bauble: "Look at a card at random in target player's hand." By 701.20e/701.20a the card is shown only for as long as the effect needs it, but the player remembers what they saw. Should the looker's view keep showing it?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 4. The ability's controller looks at one card chosen at random (only they see it, it isn't revealed) and may see that card for as long as it remains in that hand; once it changes zones it's a new object and the permission ends. In mtg-api observations the card shows in the looker's view.
- Alternatives: (A) Only a log line, as `Effect::LookAtHand` does: the looker learns the card but no view shows it. (B) Visible until the end of the resolution: the player "forgets" it, which a real player wouldn't.
- Why: The information was legitimately obtained and the card is the same object until it moves (400.7); keeping it visible models a player who remembers the card (a presentation choice: no game rule reads it). Only the looking player gains the permission; other players' views are unchanged.
- Affects: Urza's Bauble.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/kw/look_at_random.rs`; `crates/mtg-engine/src/zones.rs` (`allow_look`, `may_look`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/facedown.rs` (`can_look_at`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/paper_random_hand.rs` (`urzas_bauble_looks_privately_at_a_random_card`).

## the-token-another-ability-created: "The token" in another ability is the one token the card's token-making ability created
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 607.1d "Abilities printed on two objects can be linked if one object is a token, emblem, or nontoken permanent and the second object was the source of the ability that either created the token or emblem or put that nontoken permanent onto the battlefield. ..." / Dance of Many's rulings: "Each Dance of Many is associated only with its token creature. If one Dance leaves the battlefield, only the corresponding token is affected, not all tokens from all instances of Dance of Many." and "If Dance of Many leaves the battlefield before its first ability has resolved, ... That token won't have any connection to a Dance of Many permanent".
- Question: Dance of Many's "exile the token" and "When the token leaves the battlefield" are in abilities other than the one that creates it. Which token do they mean?
- Decision: Only on a card with exactly one instruction that creates exactly one token ("create a token ..."), "the token" in its other abilities is the token that permanent's ability created (`Game::link_to_creator`): "exile the token" acts on it while it's on the battlefield, and "when the token leaves the battlefield" triggers for it, judged by its creator record as it left (look-back, CR 603.10a). A token created by the ability after the source left has no creator and isn't "the token".
- Alternatives: Any token with the same name, or every token the card's abilities ever created: both contradict the rulings.
- Why: The rulings; 607.1d's link between a token and the source of the ability that created it.
- Affects: Dance of Many.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/paper_referents.rs` (`act_on_the_token`, `the_token_leaves`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/kw/created_by_source.rs`.
- Tests: `tests/cards/paper_cost_token_referents.rs` (`dance_of_many_exiles_its_token_when_it_leaves`, `dance_of_many_is_sacrificed_when_its_token_leaves`).

## alteration-host-of-two-types: "Another permanent of that type" for a host with both named types
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 701.3a "To attach an Aura, Equipment, or Fortification to an object or player means to take it from where it currently is and put it onto that object or player. ..."
- Question: Enchantment Alteration moves "target Aura attached to a creature or land to another permanent of that type". For an Aura on a land creature (Dryad Arbor), "that type" could be either.
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 4. A host that's a creature is treated as a creature (the first type named): the Aura moves to another creature.
- Alternatives: Let the caster choose either type, or allow any permanent sharing either type.
- Why: The text reads "that type" as one type; a host of both types is rare and the first named type is the simplest reading.
- Affects: Enchantment Alteration.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/attach_moves.rs` (`to_another_permanent_of_that_type`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/c4_attach_moves.rs` (`enchantment_alteration_moves_an_aura_to_a_permanent_of_its_hosts_type`, `enchantment_alteration_treats_a_land_creature_host_as_a_creature`).

## ice-cauldron-last-exiled: Ice Cauldron's "last card exiled with ~" is the last card its first ability exiled with a permission to cast it
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: provisional
- Rules: Ice Cauldron: "{X}, {T}: You may exile a nonland card from your hand. You may cast that card for as long as it remains exiled. ... / {T}, Remove a charge counter from this artifact: Add this artifact's last noted type and amount of mana. Spend this mana only to cast the last card exiled with this artifact." / 607.2a "If an object has an activated or triggered ability printed on it that instructs a player to exile one or more cards and an ability printed on it that refers either to “the exiled cards” or to cards “exiled with [this object],” these abilities are linked. ..." / Ice Cauldron rulings (2004-10-04, the Gatherer-import date: at least that old): "If multiple cards are exiled by the Cauldron, the mana can only be used for the card that was exiled by the Cauldron when the most recent charge counter was put there." and "Produces mana of the last type used to put a counter on itself, not via any other charge counters."
- Question: Which card is "the last card exiled with" the Cauldron, and is it still that card once it's cast from exile?
- Decision: The card its first ability most recently exiled (and gave the permission to cast); the mana can be spent only to cast a spell that was that card in exile as it was cast (the spell is a new object, CR 400.7, whose previous object was the exiled card). If an activation exiled nothing, or a charge counter came from another source (Coretapper, Power Conduit), the card exiled before stays the last one.
- Alternatives: Track only the card exiled by the activation that put the most recent charge counter (a literal reading of the ruling: "the card that was exiled ... when the most recent charge counter was put there"), so that a charge without an exile, or a counter from another source, allows no spell at all: a result the ruling never states. If this reconciliation were rejected the case would be D (unresolved), with the Oracle text as the provisional decision; not B, since the gap cases existed before the 2019 change.
- Why: Conclusion A (reconciled), confidence medium-high. The ruling answers which of several exiled cards the mana may pay for, in the normal case where each charge exiled a card; there "the card exiled when the most recent charge counter was put there" and "the last card exiled with this artifact" are the same card. The same 2004 rulings set uses "counter" for the Cauldron's own activation ("not via any other charge counters"), so the ruling means "the card exiled by the activation that most recently charged it". The printed card said "This mana is usable only to cast the spell on top of Ice Cauldron" (printed text quoted in Ask Wizards, 2007-12-03), that is "the last card put there". A charge could exile nothing before 2019 too (no nonland card in hand under the mandatory "exile a nonland card from your hand"; charge counters put by other effects such as Coretapper, 2003, or Power Conduit, 2004, which the July 2008 Update Bulletin, 2008-07-09, names: "They didn't foresee a day in which Power Conduit, Clockspinning, and Fate Transfer would exist"), and voluntarily since the Core Set 2020 Oracle change (Eli Shiffrin, "Core Set 2020 Oracle Changes", 2019-07-03, labelled functional: "You may exile a nonland card from your hand"). That change, made with the no-exile case squarely in view, kept "Spend this mana only to cast the last card exiled with Ice Cauldron" (sources read from the ormos mirror of the Wizards article archive; magic.wizards.com is outside this session's allowed network hosts). No later errata, ruling or CR rule bears on it. Recommendation: accept A (status decided once the user accepts).
- Affects: Ice Cauldron.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/cards/ice_cauldron.rs` (`on_event`, `custom_filter`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/m_ice_cauldron.rs` (`only_the_last_exiled_card_can_be_cast_with_the_mana`, `a_charge_that_exiles_nothing_keeps_the_earlier_card_as_the_last`, `a_charge_counter_from_another_source_releases_mana_for_the_last_exiled_card`).

## cyclopean-tomb-land-choice: Cyclopean Tomb's controller chooses which of its lands loses its mire counters, among those with mire counters
- Kind: policy
- Status: provisional
- Rules: Cyclopean Tomb (Oracle since the September 2008 Update Bulletin, 2008-09-24): "... at the beginning of each of your upkeeps for the rest of the game, remove all mire counters from a land that a mire counter was put onto with this artifact but that a mire counter has not been removed from with this artifact." / 608.2d "... The player can’t choose an option that’s illegal or impossible ..." / 603.7e "If an activated or triggered ability creates a delayed triggered ability, the source of that delayed triggered ability is the same as the source of that other ability. The controller of that delayed triggered ability is the player who controlled that other ability as it resolved." / 122.1 "... Counters with the same name or description are interchangeable." / ruling (2008-08-01): "As it resolves, you must remove a mire counter from a land that had a mire counter put on it by that instance of Cyclopean Tomb, but it doesn't matter where the mire counter you remove came from. ..."
- Question: Several lands qualify: which one? And does a qualifying land that no longer has mire counters (they were removed some other way) use up an upkeep?
- Decision: The Tomb's controller chooses among the qualifying lands that still have mire counters (all qualifying lands if none has any). A land stops qualifying once mire counters have been removed from it this way; removing none (it had none) doesn't count as removing one "with ~".
- Alternatives: Choose among every qualifying land, including those with no mire counters, which lets the controller waste each upkeep's removal ("all" of zero counters is vacuous, so 608.2d doesn't strictly forbid it) and keep a land with the Tomb's counters a Swamp forever. Remove one counter per upkeep, as the ruling's "you must remove a mire counter" says: that is the July 2008 wording, since replaced (exempt as outdated, `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P197.tsv`).
- Why: Part of the ruling is outdated (conclusion B for its quantity clause, confidence high); the land choice itself is a policy. History: the July 2008 Update Bulletin (Mark L. Gottlieb, 2008-07-09) gave the card "remove a mire counter from a land that a mire counter was put onto with Cyclopean Tomb" (because "this card ignored the rule that says counters are indistinguishable"); the ruling (2008-08-01, a week after Gilder Bairn's release, 2008-07-25) paraphrases that wording; the September 2008 Update Bulletin (Mark L. Gottlieb, 2008-09-24) replaced it with today's "remove all mire counters ... but that a mire counter has not been removed from with Cyclopean Tomb", because a first Tomb's eternal trigger could otherwise remove the counter a second Tomb later put on the same land ("Bad times"; "the upshot is that the card basically does what it says it does"). Later changes were templating only. Sources: both bulletins read from the ormos mirror of the Wizards article archive (Wayback captures 2017-07-08 and 2017-06-29); magic.wizards.com is outside this session's allowed network hosts. The rest of the ruling still holds (each upkeep for the rest of the game; that instance's lands, the delayed trigger's source being the Tomb, 603.7e; the counters' source doesn't matter, 122.1). The policy: "remove all mire counters from a land" isn't targeted, so the delayed trigger's controller chooses on resolution (603.7e, 608.2d); preferring a land that still has counters follows the September bulletin's aim that the card "does what it says" (lands revert over time) and treats a land the removal can't act on as an impossible option (608.2d); the ruling's "must" supports it only historically. Recommendation: accept B for the quantity clause and keep the policy (status decided once the user accepts).
- Affects: Cyclopean Tomb.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/cards/cyclopean_tomb.rs` (`custom_effect`, `REMOVE`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/m_cyclopean_tomb.rs` (`after_it_dies_each_upkeep_one_of_its_lands_loses_its_mire_counters`, `all_mire_counters_go_whatever_their_source_and_the_land_then_no_longer_qualifies`).

## library-in-any-order-arranged-by-instructing-player: Cards an effect puts into another player's library "in any order" are arranged by the player the text instructs
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 401.4 "If an effect puts two or more cards in a specific position in a library at the same time, the owner of those cards may arrange them in any order. That library’s owner doesn’t reveal the order in which the cards go into the library." / 101.1 "Whenever a card’s text directly contradicts these rules, the card takes precedence. ..." / Misinformation ruling: "You don’t need to show the opponent what order you put the cards on top of their library." / Sealed Fate ruling: "The controller of this spell decides the order of the cards on the library."
- Question: When a spell's controller puts cards owned by another player into that player's library "in any order" (Misinformation, Drafna's Restoration, Lodestone Bauble, Agonizing Memories), who arranges them: the cards' owner (401.4) or the player the text instructs to put them?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 4. The player the text instructs arranges them: "in any order" is recorded on the destination (`Destination::arranged_by`, the instructed player) and carried by each move (`EtbInfo::arranged_by`) to `zones::order_simultaneous`, which asks that player when every card put into that position at the same time was put there by such an instruction. Without "in any order" (Hallowed Burial), or for cards a replacement effect sends there, the owner arranges them (401.4). Cards rearranged within a library by a dig ("look at the top N cards of target opponent's library ... put the rest back in any order") are arranged by the player the text instructs too (`dig_steps::rearrange`).
- Alternatives: The owner arranges them whatever the text says (401.4 alone; the engine's earlier provisional reading).
- Why: The text instructs its controller to put the cards "in any order", which overrides 401.4 (101.1), as Misinformation's ruling says; the other cards with that wording have no ruling of their own and are read the same way.
- Affects: Misinformation, Drafna's Restoration, Lodestone Bauble, Agonizing Memories (about 4 cards).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/zones.rs` (`order_simultaneous`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/destinations.rs` (`prepare_destination`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/hand_graveyard_grammar.rs` (`destination_of`, " in any order"); `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/control_exile_library.rs` (`p_put_into_library`).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p147_restock.rs` (`misinformation_you_arrange_the_cards_on_their_library`, `drafnas_restoration_its_caster_arranges_the_cards_on_their_library`, `agonizing_memories_its_caster_arranges_the_cards_on_their_library`, `hallowed_burial_without_in_any_order_the_owner_arranges`); `tests/cards/m_lodestone_bauble.rs` (`its_controller_arranges_the_cards_on_the_players_library`).

## mdfc-back-face-up-enters-transformed: A modal double-faced card cast or played with its back face up "enters transformed"
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: provisional
- Rules: 701.27g "Some spells and abilities refer to a “transformed permanent.” This phrase refers to a double-faced permanent on the battlefield with its back face up. A permanent with its front face up is never considered a transformed permanent, even if it had its back face up previously. Similarly, an object represented by more than one card, such as a melded or merged permanent, is never considered a transformed permanent, even if it has components that are back face up. ..." / 712.2 "Nonmodal double-faced cards ... allow the card to be cast or enter the battlefield “transformed” or “converted” (with its back face up)." / 712.11b "A player casting a modal double-faced card or a copy of a modal double-faced card as a spell chooses which face they are casting before putting it onto the stack." / 712.12 "A player playing a modal double-faced card or a copy of a modal double-faced card as a land chooses one of its faces that’s a land before putting it onto the battlefield. It enters the battlefield with that face up. ..." / 712.13 "By default, a resolving double-faced spell that becomes a permanent is put onto the battlefield with the same face up that was face up on the stack." / 712.14a "If a spell or ability puts a double-faced card onto the battlefield “transformed” or “converted,” it enters the battlefield with its back face up. ..." / 702.145b "... “If it is night and this permanent is represented by a double-faced card, it enters transformed,” ..." / Corruption of Towashi ruling (2023-04-14): "Only a transforming double-faced permanent can enter the battlefield "transformed," and only if it enters with its back face up. Notably, melded permanents can't enter transformed, and modal double-faced permanents can't enter transformed, even if they enter with their back faces up." / Spider-Man ruling (Eddie Brock and four others, 2025-09-19): "A modal double-faced card can be transformed or be put onto the battlefield transformed. This is a change from previous rules. ... Similarly, if an effect attempts to put a modal double-faced card onto the battlefield transformed, it will enter transformed if its back face has a permanent type. If it doesn't, it will simply stay in its current zone."
- Question: The CR uses "enters transformed" only as an outcome (702.145b, 712.13a, 712.2) and never defines it. Corruption of Towashi: "Whenever a permanent you control transforms or a permanent you control enters transformed, you may draw a card." Does a modal double-faced card cast as its back face (712.11b, 712.13) or played as its back-face land (712.12; a Pathway, Hagra Broodpit) enter transformed? A modal double-faced card put onto the battlefield transformed does (the 2025-09-19 ruling), and is a transformed permanent once on the battlefield either way (701.27g, which needs no interpretation).
- Decision: Yes. "Enters transformed" means "is a transformed permanent (701.27g) as it enters": a double-faced permanent entering with its back face up, whatever put that face up (an instruction to put it onto the battlefield transformed, casting it transformed or as its back face, playing its back face). Melded and merged permanents, face-down permanents and a card that isn't double-faced don't enter transformed.
- Alternatives: (i) Only a permanent put onto the battlefield or cast by an instruction or permission that says "transformed": fails for daybound, whose card "enters transformed" at night with no such instruction (702.145b; Curse of Leeches ruling, a spell cast at night "enters the battlefield transformed"). (ii) Only a permanent entering with a different face up than it had on the stack or in its previous zone (712.13's default): would exclude nonmodal double-faced cards cast transformed (disturb, a defeated Siege), which the Towashi ruling ("only if it enters with its back face up") and the nonmodal-DFC rulings of 2025-06-06 and 2025-10-02 ("unless a spell or ability instructs you to put it onto the battlefield transformed or allows you to cast it transformed, in which case it enters with its back face up") count. (iii) Keep the Towashi ruling's exclusion for modal double-faced cards cast or played back face up: no rule supports a distinction by kind since the update, which removed exactly that kind restriction.
- Why: The Towashi ruling's own test has two parts: an eligible kind of permanent, and back face up as it enters. Its exclusion of modal double-faced permanents was a kind exclusion under the CR of its day (701.28g then: "a permanent on the battlefield with its back face up that’s also a transforming double-faced card or a transforming token"; unchanged through the CR effective 2025-07-25). The Comprehensive Rules update effective 2025-09-19 (Marvel's Spider-Man) removed it: 701.27g now covers any double-faced permanent, and 712.14a any double-faced card. Dated from the archived full CR texts of 2023-04-14, 2025-07-25 and 2025-09-19 (https://github.com/pit142857/mtg-cr) and the Spider-Man rulings of 2025-09-19 ("This is a change from previous rules"); see work item ruling-conflicts-1, case 12 (conclusion B, outdated). Provisional because it reverses the Towashi ruling's explicit "even if they enter with their back faces up" by extrapolation, with no post-update ruling on it, and it reaches far: every modal double-faced land played as its back face triggers Corruption of Towashi.
- Affects: Corruption of Towashi (the one card that says "enters transformed"); its exempted ruling (docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P225.tsv). Every modal double-faced card cast or played with its back face up.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/a701_transform.rs` (`enters_transformed`: `EntersBattlefield` with the `TRANSFORMED` filter); `crates/mtg-engine/src/transform_rules.rs` (`is_transformed`).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p226_corruption_of_towashi.rs` (`corruption_of_towashi_draws_when_a_modal_double_faced_card_is_cast_or_played_back_face_up`).

## attach-as-it-transforms-to-a-legal-player: Curse of Leeches is attached as it transforms to a player its controller chooses among the players it can enchant
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: Curse of Leeches: "As this permanent transforms into Curse of Leeches, attach it to a player." / 712.20 "If a double-faced card would have an “As [this permanent] transforms . . .” ability after it transforms or converts, that ability is applied while that permanent is transforming or converting, not afterward." / 701.3a "... An Aura, Equipment, or Fortification can’t be attached to an object or player it couldn’t enchant, equip, or fortify, respectively." / 701.3b "If an effect tries to attach an Aura ... to an object or player it can’t be attached to, the Aura ... doesn’t move." / 303.4f "If an Aura is entering the battlefield ... and the effect putting it onto the battlefield doesn’t specify the object or player the Aura will enchant, that player chooses what it will enchant as the Aura enters the battlefield. The player must choose a legal object or player according to the Aura’s enchant ability and any other applicable effects." / ruling: "Attaching Curse of Leeches as it transforms doesn't target a player, so it can be attached to a player with hexproof this way."
- Question: Who chooses "a player", and may the chooser pick a player it can't be attached to (one with protection from black), leaving it unattached so it's put into the graveyard (CR 704.5m)?
- Decision: Its controller chooses, among the players in range of influence it can legally enchant (hexproof doesn't matter: no targeting). With no such player it stays unattached, and the state-based action puts it into its owner's graveyard.
- Alternatives: Any player may be chosen, and choosing one it can't enchant leaves it unattached (701.3b), so the controller could choose to lose it.
- Why: 303.4f, the rule for an Aura attached without an Aura spell's target, requires a legal choice; transforming into an Aura in place is the same situation, and "attach it to a player" reads as an instruction to attach it, not an option to fail.
- Affects: Curse of Leeches // Leeching Lurker (the only card).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/cards/curse_of_leeches.rs` (`custom_effect`, `ATTACH`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/m_curse_of_leeches.rs` (`the_choice_is_among_players_it_can_enchant`).

## self-shuffle-gone-library-still-shuffled: "Its owner shuffles it into their library" shuffles that library even when it has left
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: provisional
- Rules: 701.24c "If an effect would cause a player to shuffle one or more specific objects into a library, that library is shuffled even if none of those objects are in the zone they’re expected to be in or an effect causes all of those objects to be moved to another zone or remain in their current zone." (with the Guile example) / Blitz Hellion's ruling (2009-05-01): "If it’s not on the battlefield by the time this ability resolves, nothing happens. Blitz Hellion stays wherever it is, and its owner doesn’t shuffle their library."
- Question: Blitz Hellion ("At the beginning of the end step, this creature's owner shuffles it into their library.") has left the battlefield when its ability resolves. It stays where it is; is its owner's library shuffled?
- Decision: Yes. The engine follows CR 701.24c, which has an example of the same shape (Guile exiled in response to "shuffle it into its owner's library"): the object stays where it is and the library is shuffled. Shuffle triggers trigger.
- Alternatives: Follow the 2009 ruling: no shuffle when the object isn't where the ability expects it. That was the rule until 2015 and isn't any more; the ruling's last clause is exempt as outdated (`docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P180.tsv`).
- Why: Conclusion B (ruling outdated), confidence high. The Blitz Hellion ruling (2009-05-01) applied the rule of its time (then CR 701.15c, later 701.16c: "If an effect would cause a player to shuffle one or more specific objects into a library, but none of those objects are in the zone they're expected to be in, that library is not shuffled.", quoted from a third-party copy, jmagic's ShuffleTriggersTest.java). The Magic Origins Comprehensive Rules update reversed it: Wizards' "Magic Origins Update Bulletin—Comprehensive Rules Changes" (Matt Tabak, 2015-07-16; http://magic.wizards.com/en/articles/archive/feature/magic-origins-update-bulletin-comprehensive-rules-changes-2015-07-16, read from the Wayback-derived ormos mirror, capture 2015-07-19; in force with Magic Origins, 2015-07-17) says of 701.16c: "Under old rules, you wouldn't shuffle your library, but that's changing. Now, if the effect says to shuffle something into your library, you shuffle even if no cards are being added to your library." No later change went the other way; the current 701.24c only adds "or remain in their current zone" and the examples. ## face-down-instant-or-sorcery-stays-face-down: A face-down permanent represented by an instant or sorcery card stays face down when an effect would turn it face up
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 110.4 "There are six permanent types: artifact, battle, creature, enchantment, land, and planeswalker. Instant and sorcery cards can’t enter the battlefield and thus can’t be permanents. ..." / 708.8 "As a face-down permanent is turned face up, its copiable values revert to its normal copiable values. ..." / 701.40g "If a manifested permanent that’s represented by an instant or sorcery card would turn face up, its controller reveals it and leaves it face down. If such a permanent would turn face up, its controller reveals it and leaves it face down. ..." Tezzeret, Cruel Machinist's ruling: "If such an effect tries to turn an instant or sorcery card face up, reveal that card and it stays face down. In that case, it remains a 5/5 artifact creature." Magar of the Magic Strings's ruling: "If something tries to turn a face-down instant or sorcery card on the battlefield face up, reveal that card to show all players it’s an instant or sorcery card. - Question: Tezzeret's −7 ("Put any number of cards from your hand onto the battlefield face down. They're 5/5 artifact creatures.") and Magar put instant and sorcery cards onto the battlefield face down without manifesting or cloaking them. The CR says what happens when a manifested or cloaked permanent, or a face-down merged permanent, represented by an instant or sorcery card would turn face up (701.40g, 701.58g, 730.2g), but nothing about any other face-down permanent; 708.8 read alone would turn it face up as an instant or sorcery permanent, which 110.4 says can't exist.
- Decision: Every face-down permanent represented by an instant or sorcery card is treated as 701.40g and 701.58g describe: when an effect would turn it face up, its controller reveals it and it stays face down with the characteristics it has face down (Tezzeret's stays a 5/5 artifact creature), and "turned face up" abilities don't trigger.
- Alternatives: Turn it face up (708.8 alone): an instant or sorcery permanent on the battlefield, contrary to 110.4 and to both cards' rulings.
- Why: 110.4 rules out instant and sorcery permanents, the CR's three specific rules give this answer for every face-down permanent they cover, and the rulings of both cards that put such cards onto the battlefield face down otherwise say the same. The CR is silent here rather than contradicted by the rulings.
- Affects: Tezzeret, Cruel Machinist (−7) and Magar of the Magic Strings, with any effect that turns a face-down permanent face up (such as Break Open); manifested, cloaked and merged permanents follow their own rules, which say the same.

- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p003_face_down.rs` (`turning_a_tezzeret_card_face_up_ends_the_5_5_effect_but_an_instant_stays_face_down`).
