# Stack, casting and costs

Judgment calls about casting spells, activating abilities and paying costs (CR 117, 118,
601–602, 605, 733). Format and kinds: `docs/agents/items/interpretation-register.md`;
index: `docs/INTERPRETATIONS.md`.

## automatic-mana-payment: The engine chooses which mana abilities pay a cost
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 601.2g "If the total cost includes a mana payment, the player then has a chance to activate mana abilities (see rule 605, “Mana Abilities”). Mana abilities must be activated before costs are paid." / 601.2h "The player pays the total cost. ..." / 605.3a "A player may activate an activated mana ability whenever they have priority, whenever they are casting a spell or activating an ability that requires a mana payment, or whenever a rule or effect asks for a mana payment, ..."
- Question: Which mana abilities a player activates to pay a cost is the player's choice; there is no decision for it in the agent interface.
- Decision: The engine plans the payment: mana already in the pool first, then a backtracking search over the player's mana sources, preferring cheaper sources (lands before creatures, tapping before sacrificing, e.g. a land before a Treasure; abilities that deal damage to the player, like a pain land's colored mana, after those that don't) and, among equals, those that make fewer kinds of mana (a Mountain before a Volcanic Island for {R}). The damage the planned mana abilities deal counts against the life left to pay Phyrexian mana with (601.2g–h: they're activated before the cost is paid; 119.4). An agent that wants a particular payment activates mana abilities with priority first (mtg-api lists them) and the pool is spent before anything else.
- Alternatives: Ask the player for each mana ability (a `ChoosePayment` decision): exact, but far more decisions per game and an interface every agent would have to handle.
- Why: The choice rarely matters and the planner keeps the most flexible sources untapped; agents keep full control through priority-time mana abilities.
- Affects: every mana payment.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/mana_abilities.rs` (`mana_sources_with` ranking, `plan_payment`, `pay_mana`).
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_engine_policies.rs` (`mana_is_paid_from_the_cheapest_least_flexible_sources`).

## castability-is-optimistic: The engine's list of castable spells assumes favorable choices
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 601.2f "The player determines the total cost of the spell. ... Some effects may increase or reduce the cost to pay, ..." / 733.1 "If a player takes an illegal action or starts to take an action but can’t legally complete it, the entire action is reversed and any payments already made are canceled. ..."
- Question: Whether a spell can be cast can depend on choices made while casting it (a reduction "if it targets ..."). What should the list of legal actions offered with priority contain?
- Decision: `Game::legal_actions` is optimistic: a cost change that depends on the spell's targets or other choices is assumed to apply if it's a reduction and not to apply if it's an increase, so a spell is listed if some choices might make it affordable. Trying it with choices that don't is reversed (733.1) and the player keeps priority. mtg-api's `legal` module tries each listed action on a copy of the game, so external agents get only actions that can really be taken.
- Alternatives: Search every combination of targets and choices before listing a spell: exact but expensive on every priority check.
- Why: An action listed but impossible is harmless (it is reversed as the CR prescribes); an action wrongly left out would be a missing legal play.
- Affects: spells and abilities with target- or choice-dependent cost changes (Roadside Blowout, Depower, ...).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/spell_costs.rs` (`own_change_applies`, `assume_targets`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/activation_costs.rs`; `crates/mtg-api/src/legal.rs`.
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_engine_policies.rs` (`a_spell_whose_reduction_depends_on_its_targets_is_listed_as_castable`).

## rider-order: Instructions replacement effects leave for after their events run in the order the effects were applied
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 614.6 "If an event is replaced, it never happens. A modified event occurs instead, which may in turn trigger abilities. ..." / 616.1f "Once the chosen effect has been applied, this process is repeated (taking into account only replacement or prevention effects that would now be applicable) until there are no more left to apply." / 701.19a "... “Regenerate [permanent]” means “The next time [permanent] would be destroyed this turn, instead remove all damage marked on it and its controller taps it. ...”" / 603.12 "A resolving spell or ability may allow or instruct a player to take an action and create a triggered ability that triggers “when [a player] [does or doesn’t]” take that action ..."
- Question: When one event (Day of Judgment destroying several regenerating creatures, a board wipe with Head of the Hunt out) leaves several riders at once ("When it regenerates this way, ...", "... exile it instead. When you do, ...", "and also ..."), in which order do they happen? The rules are silent; the ones that are triggered abilities are then ordered by their controller (603.3b), but the order they're offered in decides the default and the order of non-triggered riders.
- Decision: In the order the replacement effects were applied: for simultaneous events, the order the engine replaces them in (objects in battlefield order); for one event, in the order the effects were chosen (616.1f), an effect's own nested riders innermost first. Riders queued while a rider runs happen before the remaining ones.
- Alternatives: The reverse order (what a stack of pending riders gave); grouping by controller or source.
- Why: It follows the order the modified events themselves happened in, and with the default trigger order (`default-order-as-listed`) it keeps the earliest rider at the bottom of the stack like any triggers detected in that order.
- Affects: regeneration riders (Matopi Golem, Skeleton Scavengers, Soldevi Sentry), "When you do" replacement riders (Head of the Hunt, Nemata, Valentin), "and also" replacements, prevention follow-ups queued after damage.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/actions.rs` (`run_post_replacement_effects`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/replacement.rs` (queuing `ReplacementAction::Then` riders and regeneration riders).
- Tests: `tests/cards/rider_order.rs`.

## illegal-action-reversal-restores-libraries: Reversing an illegal action keeps the library actions 733.1 names, and undoes the rest
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 733.1 "If a player takes an illegal action or starts to take an action but can’t legally complete it, the entire action is reversed and any payments already made are canceled. No abilities trigger and no effects apply as a result of an undone action. If the action was casting a spell, the spell returns to the zone it came from. Each player may also reverse any legal mana abilities that player activated while making the illegal play, unless mana from those abilities or from any triggered mana abilities they caused to trigger was spent on another mana ability that wasn’t reversed. Players may not reverse actions that moved cards to a library, moved cards from a library to any zone other than the stack, caused a library to be shuffled, or caused cards from a library to be revealed." / 601.2h "The player pays the total cost. First, they pay all costs that don’t involve random elements or moving objects from the library to a public zone, in any order. Then they pay all remaining costs in any order. Partial payments are not allowed. Unpayable costs can’t be paid." / 605.1a "An activated ability is a mana ability if it meets all of the following criteria: ... and its cost and effect don’t move any card to or from a library. ..." / 605.1b "A triggered ability is a mana ability if it meets all of the following criteria: it doesn’t require a target (see rule 115.6), it triggers from the activation or resolution of an activated mana ability (see rule 605.1a) or from mana being added to a player’s mana pool, and it could add mana to a player’s mana pool when it resolves." / 401.5 "... If the top card of the player’s library changes while a spell is being cast, the new top card won’t be revealed and can’t be looked at until the spell becomes cast (see rule 601.2i). ..."
- Question: An action that turns out to be illegal is reversed, but 733.1 says the library actions taken while attempting it stay. How does the engine keep them, and what goes with them: the card a kept move moved (which had left the battlefield, say), the mana of a mana ability whose payment became a library action, the rest of an event a replacement effect made a library action, a shuffle's randomness, a revealed card?
- Decision: The engine keeps them, as 733.1 says. Decided by the user, Oct 21 (item `reversal-library-exceptions`); implemented Oct 28. Before that the engine restored the whole game, libraries included (a deviation): the user had first accepted that while no fully supported card was known to reach the difference, and chose to follow 733.1 exactly when the scan test below found 16 that do (22 since). How: the engine restores the copy of the game taken before the action began (`Game::roll_back`), then performs again on it, in the order they happened, the library actions of the attempt (`reversal::keep_library_actions`): (1) each zone change to a library, or from a library to any zone other than the stack: the card is followed back through the attempt's zone changes to the object it is in the restored game, which leaves wherever it is there and becomes a new object in the zone the kept move put it in (CR 400.7). So a Darksteel Colossus sacrificed for mana and shuffled into its library leaves the battlefield for the library (an Aura on it goes to the graveyard as state-based actions are next checked, a token put into a library ceases to exist); a card a payment put into a graveyard and Lich's Mirror then shuffled into a library goes there from where it is in the restored game; a card drawn and then discarded for the cost stays in the hand. A draw is a draw (it counts, `Event::Drew`, and "as you draw it" abilities apply), a mill a mill. (2) Each library a kept action touched gets the order the attempt left it in; a card whose leaving the library was undone (cast from it: it goes back, 733.1's "other than the stack") keeps its place counted from the top. The random generators keep the state the kept shuffles left them in, so the same game still gives the same shuffles. (3) Each library card that was revealed is revealed again, so it stays known: in the restored game's events, and for agents in the event feed, whose positions never go back on a reversal (`EventFeed::skip_to`). Everything else is undone: the payments and the cast or activation, the mana abilities activated for them and their mana (`reversal-undoes-mana-abilities`: the mana is gone even when a part of the mana ability's payment, such as the sacrificed Colossus, stays in the library), life gained, lost or paid, damage, counters, a loss of the game, the other zone changes ("your life total becomes 20" after Lich's Mirror's shuffle and draws is undone; the shuffle and the draws aren't). Abilities trigger on the kept actions as zone changes, draws, mills and shuffles, but not on the payment they were part of (`reversal-kept-library-actions-trigger`). A cost's library actions are still paid after everything that can make the action illegal (601.2h), so only replacement effects, rules applied in the middle of a payment, and triggered mana abilities (605.1b) make them while an action can still turn out illegal. The cards that reach the exceptions, by making something that happens in the middle of a payment a library action when the action then turns out illegal (22): a card a payment moves goes to a library instead (Blightsteel Colossus, Darksteel Colossus, Legacy Weapon and Progenitus, sacrificed or discarded; Nexus of Fate, discarded; Gravebane Zombie, Nissa's Chosen and Pulmonic Sliver, sacrificed; Wheel of Sun and Moon, the enchanted player's cards); damage dealt for mana (Shivan Reef's to its controller; the damage Overabundance's triggered mana ability deals to the player who tapped a land) becomes a mill or an exile from a library (Angel of Suffering, Crumbling Sanctuary, Bone Mask's shield; The Mindskinner only for Overabundance's damage to an opponent of its controller); life a mana ability gains (Pristine Talisman) becomes a draw (Lich, Nefarious Lich); life paid (a cost's "Pay N life", Mana Confluence's mana ability, Phyrexian mana) becomes exiling that many cards from the top of the payer's library (Ashiok, Wicked Manipulator, a rule implemented in code, `kw::pay_life_instead`); through what other replacement effects and rules make of a payment: damage dealt to Swans of Bryn Argoll (a player's damage that Pariah, Pariah's Shield, With Great Power . . . or Ancient Adamantoise deals to a permanent instead) becomes a draw for the source's controller (Swans of Bryn Argoll); a loss (Nefarious Lich's for damage its controller's graveyard can't pay for, or Enduring Angel's) becomes shuffling a hand, a graveyard and permanents into a library and drawing seven (Lich's Mirror), or drawing seven (Stunning Reversal); a creature an "until" exile returns in the middle of a payment (when Banisher Priest, say, is sacrificed for mana) is shuffled into its owner's library instead (Don't Blink); and a discard an effect makes in the middle of a payment made outside casting a spell and activating an ability (a cost paid as an ability resolves, an attack or block cost, a special action's cost) goes on top of a library instead (Library of Leng, Nephalia Academy): Heightened Awareness's "As ~ enters, discard your hand" (or Indominus Rex, Alpha's discard) for a card an "until" exile returns there; whether such a payment can return one before something in it fails is more than the scan follows, so these two are counted. (A cost's discard is never caused by an effect, wherever it's paid, as Library of Leng's ruling has it: `discard_rules::DISCARDED_BY_EFFECT`. Nephalia Academy would also apply to a cost's discard paid as an opponent's ability resolves, and no supported payment has one that something can fail after.) For example, casting Fling with Ashnod's Altar, Darksteel Colossus and a Mountain sacrifices the Colossus to the Altar for mana, which shuffles it into its owner's library; then no creature is left to sacrifice for Fling, and the engine reverses the shuffle with the cast, which 733.1 says stays. A test checks all this on every run, `tests/cr/r733_001_reversal_reachability.rs`: it scans the compiled abilities of every fully supported card (31,456 on Oct 21: playable cards legal in some format, sticker sheets aside, Arena-only cards and cards whose listed printing is an MTGO one included) and fails, naming the card and the ability, on any way to reach the difference not listed here, and on one listed here it no longer finds. What it checks: (a) A cost's library actions are paid where nothing can fail afterwards. The engine pays the mana first, checks that each other part can be paid, and pays the parts that move cards from a library to a public zone after the others (601.2h); nothing can fail after an activation cost or a cost paid as an ability resolves is paid, and the library parts are paid after every part that can fail (the parts the test says can't fail once checked are paid in a game with what they need gone). One of several costs, and a cost an effect has a player pay, are checked as payments of their own; an activation cost is checked again, in a game, with the parts each activation cost modifier adds after it (Drought, Brutal Suppression, Carth the Lion, Thran Portal) and with each alternative cost offered for it (the {0} equip, cycling and power-up costs of Forge Anew, Bruenor Battlehammer, Kíli the Resourceful, Gavi, Nest Warden, New Perspectives and Advancing the Spirit; Heart of Kiran's loyalty counter). The library-touching costs are all of those kinds: exiling the top cards of a library (Arc-Slogger, Royal Herbalist, Seasoned Tactician, Whirling Catapult, Thought Lash), milling (Rot Farm Skeleton, Sinister Concoction; and Millikin, Deranged Assistant, The Warring Triad, whose mill costs make their abilities not mana abilities, 605.1a), putting cards on a library (Penance, Leashling, Hidden Retreat from hand; Battlefield Scrounger from graveyard; Timestream Navigator itself), and costs paid as an ability resolves (Gurzigost, Anurid Scavenger, Deep Spawn, Tainted Specter; Decoy Gambit and Research // Development, which an opponent pays by having you draw a card; the cumulative upkeep of Thought Lash and Psychic Vortex). No spell's cost (a cost written in a rule implemented in code, such as casting a card from a graveyard by discarding, included), keyword cost, attack or block cost or special action's cost touches a library: a spell's cost is followed by the check that the spell still has the keywords that paid part of it, so one would be reachable. No cost has two parts that take cards from a library either: the engine checks each part against the whole library, not jointly, so with one card left "exile the top card of your library, mill a card" would be paid in part. (b) No mana ability (activated, triggered, or a delayed triggered mana ability an effect creates) and no special action that can be taken during a mana payment moves, shuffles or reveals library cards (the mana abilities of Rosheen, Roaring Prophet; Sacellum Godspeaker; and Metalworker reveal cards from a hand); the automatic payment uses only mana abilities whose costs are tapping, sacrificing, life and counters. Triggers on spending the mana or on casting the next spell (Gilanra, Caller of Wirewood draws; Lapis Orb of Dragonkind scries 2; Brass Infiniscope's delayed trigger draws) and Shaun & Rebecca's reflexive mill are ordinary triggered abilities, which 733.1 says don't trigger from an undone action; abilities that add mana and draw a card (Chromatic Sphere, the Eggs such as Skycloud Egg) or mill (Millikin, Deranged Assistant, Charmed Pendant) aren't mana abilities (605.1a), so they can't be activated while an action is attempted. (c) A cost part that searches or shuffles a library, or reveals library cards or moves them to a public zone, is paid after every other part (601.2h); no supported cost searches, shuffles or reveals a library. (d) No ability can be activated from a library (which would reveal the card, 602.2a); a keyword's cost is checked where the abilities it stands for pay it; a card cast from the top of a library goes to the stack, which may be reversed, and the next card isn't revealed while it's cast (401.5). (e) Which events can happen in the middle of a payment is computed over all the fully supported cards together: those every payment can cause (a permanent sacrificed, a card discarded, damage dealt to a player by a land tapped for mana, life paid, gained or lost, counters, tokens without abilities, mana), and those something supported causes there: a cost or a mana ability; a replacement effect that applies there, and what its action does (a redirection deals a player's damage to a permanent instead; Nefarious Lich and Enduring Angel make a player lose the game, and Enduring Angel transforms; Kill-Suit Cultist's shield destroys a creature dealt damage); the prevention and regeneration shields an effect creates, which are replacement effects too; and a rule the engine applies there: each mana ability's activation ends with `flush_events`, which begins with `until::check_untils`, so a card exiled "until [a permanent] leaves the battlefield" (Banisher Priest, Banishing Light and the other supported cards that exile until then) returns as soon as that permanent is sacrificed for mana (onto the battlefield; a card exiled from a library would go back into it, a library action, and no supported card does that). The events this adds today: a card or a token with abilities put onto the battlefield, a permanent destroyed, a permanent transformed, damage dealt to a permanent, a player losing the game, a card discarded because of an effect (`KNOWN_EVENTS` in the test, which fails when they change). Each replacement effect that does a library action and applies to an event that can happen there is listed above; what follows a library action isn't followed further (that library action is listed); a permanent that enters there wasn't cast, so "if it was kicked" doesn't hold for it (Monstrous War-Leech, Urborg Lhurgoyf). An event the test doesn't follow (a card cast, the game restarted, an unclassified keyword action) that a payment, a mana ability or such a replacement effect causes fails it and can't be listed. Every rule implemented in code that a supported card turns on by name, and every custom effect performed there, is classified in the test (`custom_rule`, `custom_effect_library`; the keywords' own rules in code were read by hand). Nothing else acts in the middle of a payment: triggered abilities wait (and are dropped with an undone action), and state-based actions aren't checked. Cards legal in no format are in no card pool (`docs/DEFERRED.md`) and outside the scan: Panglacial Shinobi (library ninjutsu: activated from a library, which reveals the card, 602.2a, with a cost that shuffles a creature into a library) and The Secret Lair aren't supported; Under-Construction Skyscraper is fully supported, and its LEVEL 8+ mana ability scries, which would reach the difference (the test checks that the scan reports it), but no card pool (random decks, the properties, the API's) deals it.
- Alternatives: Restore the whole game, libraries included (what the engine did before Oct 28: simple and exact for everything else, but a deviation from 733.1). Keep only the final state of the libraries rather than performing the actions again (no events, so nothing could trigger on them and agents wouldn't be told). Leave the kept card where the attempt put it but keep its other moves too (a card drawn and then discarded for the cost would stay in the graveyard, although the discard is undone).
- Why: 733.1 lists the library actions players may not reverse; performing exactly those again on the restored game keeps them and undoes everything else 733.1 reverses, with the restore staying as exact as before. Following the card rather than the object is what "moved cards to a library" means: the card is in the library, whatever else the attempt did with it. A reversal with no library action is the exact restore it always was (the rollback property checks both, `docs/PROPERTIES.md`). The scan test keeps the list of the cards that reach the exceptions exact, so that each way is known and tested in a game.
- Affects: the 22 cards above (`KNOWN_REACHABLE` in the scan test), in a game where one applies in the middle of a payment of an action that then turns out illegal (Swans of Bryn Argoll with a redirection, Lich's Mirror or Stunning Reversal with Nefarious Lich or Enduring Angel, Don't Blink with an "until" exile, Library of Leng and Nephalia Academy with a card that discards as it enters returned by one); custom cards with a triggered mana ability that draws or reveals library cards.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/reversal.rs` (`keep_library_actions`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/game.rs` (`roll_back`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/event_feed.rs` (`skip_to`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/casting.rs` (`cast_with_option`, `activate_ability`, `pay_cost`, `pay_total_cost`, `pay_total_cost_ordered`, `cost_part_pays_last`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/until.rs` (`check_untils`, which acts in the middle of a payment).
- Tests: `tests/keywords/k702_048_offering.rs` (`generic_mana_reduces_only_generic_mana_and_not_below_zero`: Blightsteel Colossus sacrificed for offering stays in its library when the cast is reversed); `tests/cr/r121_drawing.rs` (`a_card_drawn_while_a_spell_is_cast_stays_face_down_until_it_becomes_cast`); `tests/cr/r733_001_kept_library_actions.rs` (`a_draw_a_replacement_effect_makes_of_life_gained_for_mana_stays`, `a_shuffle_stays_with_the_order_it_gave_the_library`, `a_kept_move_triggers_as_a_zone_change_but_not_as_the_sacrifice_it_was_paid_as`, `a_library_card_revealed_while_attempting_an_action_stays_known`, `a_reversal_without_library_actions_restores_the_game_exactly`, `the_rollback_property_accepts_a_reversal_that_kept_library_actions`); `tests/cr/r733_001_library_exceptions.rs` (`library_costs_are_paid_after_every_other_cost`, `a_reversed_action_keeps_a_draw_made_while_attempting_it`); `tests/cr/r733_001_reversal_reachability.rs` (`every_fully_supported_card_is_scanned_for_ways_to_reach_the_exceptions`, `the_scan_reports_each_way_to_reach_the_exceptions`, `the_scan_reports_nested_payments_rules_in_code_and_cost_modifiers`, `the_scan_follows_what_replacement_effects_and_rules_cause_in_the_middle_of_a_payment`, `the_cost_parts_the_scan_says_cant_fail_dont_fail_in_a_game`, `a_reversed_cast_keeps_a_card_shuffled_into_a_library_by_a_replacement_effect`, `a_reversed_cast_keeps_a_mill_a_replacement_effect_made_of_a_lands_damage`, `a_reversed_cast_keeps_cards_exiled_instead_of_paying_life_for_mana`, `a_reversed_cast_keeps_a_draw_a_redirected_lands_damage_causes`, `a_reversed_cast_keeps_what_lichs_mirror_does_with_a_loss_in_the_middle_of_a_payment`, `a_reversed_cast_keeps_a_creature_shuffled_into_a_library_as_an_exile_ends`, `a_card_cast_from_the_top_of_a_library_goes_back_when_the_cast_is_reversed`); the rollback property (`properties::rollback_identity`).

## reversal-kept-library-actions-trigger: Abilities trigger on the library actions a reversal keeps, but not on the payment they were part of
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 733.1 "If a player takes an illegal action or starts to take an action but can’t legally complete it, the entire action is reversed and any payments already made are canceled. No abilities trigger and no effects apply as a result of an undone action. ... Players may not reverse actions that moved cards to a library, moved cards from a library to any zone other than the stack, caused a library to be shuffled, or caused cards from a library to be revealed." / 603.2 "Whenever a game event or game state matches a triggered ability’s trigger event, that ability automatically triggers. ..."
- Question: The library actions an illegal action took while it was attempted stay (`illegal-action-reversal-restores-libraries`). Do abilities trigger on them ("whenever you draw a card", "when ~ leaves the battlefield", "whenever one or more cards are put into exile from your library")? And on the payment a replacement effect made one of them (a creature sacrificed for mana that Darksteel Colossus's ability shuffles into the library instead: "whenever you sacrifice a permanent")?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 4. The kept actions are actions that happened, not undone ones, so abilities trigger on them as on any other: as zone changes (a permanent leaving the battlefield for a library: Nefarious Lich's "When Nefarious Lich leaves the battlefield, you lose the game" after Lich's Mirror shuffled it away in the middle of a payment), draws, mills, shuffles and reveals. The payment, the cast or the activation they were part of is undone, so nothing triggers on it: a kept move is performed again without the cause that was the payment (a sacrifice, a discard, a destruction, a cost; `reversal::kept_cause`), so "whenever you sacrifice" and "whenever you discard" don't trigger, and neither does anything on the parts of the event the replacement didn't keep (damage, life). What triggered while the action was attempted is dropped with it, and triggers again from the kept actions, in the restored game, once they're performed.
- Alternatives: Nothing triggers on the kept actions either, since they happened as part of an action that is reversed (733.1's "no abilities trigger ... as a result of an undone action" read as covering the whole attempt). Or the kept move keeps its cause, so a sacrifice the replacement effect made a library move still triggers "whenever you sacrifice".
- Why: 733.1 says players may not reverse those actions, and the sentence about triggers is about undone actions; an action that stands triggers abilities like any other (603.2). The sacrifice or discard is the payment, which 733.1 cancels; only the move to or from the library stands. A reasonable judge could read the whole attempt as undone for triggers, so the entry is provisional.
- Affects: the cards that reach 733.1's exceptions (`KNOWN_REACHABLE` in `tests/cr/r733_001_reversal_reachability.rs`) with an ability that triggers on what they do: Nefarious Lich shuffled away by Lich's Mirror (its controller loses the game), draws with "whenever you draw" abilities, Ashiok's exiles with abilities that watch cards leaving a library.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/reversal.rs` (`keep_library_actions`, `kept_cause`).
- Tests: `tests/cr/r733_001_kept_library_actions.rs` (`a_kept_move_triggers_as_a_zone_change_but_not_as_the_sacrifice_it_was_paid_as`, `a_draw_a_replacement_effect_makes_of_life_gained_for_mana_stays`); `tests/cr/r733_001_reversal_reachability.rs` (`a_reversed_cast_keeps_what_lichs_mirror_does_with_a_loss_in_the_middle_of_a_payment`).

## reversal-undoes-mana-abilities: Reversing an illegal action also reverses the mana abilities activated while attempting it
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 733.1 "... Each player may also reverse any legal mana abilities that player activated while making the illegal play, unless mana from those abilities or from any triggered mana abilities they caused to trigger was spent on another mana ability that wasn’t reversed. ..."
- Question: Reversing a mana ability activated while attempting an illegal action is the player's choice. Does the engine reverse them?
- Decision: Yes, always: the whole game is restored to the moment before the action was proposed (`Game::roll_back`), so the mana abilities the engine activated to pay the cost (601.2g, `automatic-mana-payment`) are reversed with it: their sources untap, sacrificed sources return, and the mana they made is gone. A part of their payment that a replacement effect made a library action stays, as 733.1 says (a Darksteel Colossus sacrificed for mana is shuffled into its library), but the mana ability is reversed all the same and its mana is gone (`illegal-action-reversal-restores-libraries`). Mana abilities a player activates with priority before proposing the action aren't part of it and stay.
- Alternatives: Ask the player which mana abilities to keep (the mana stays in the pool, and the costs stay paid).
- Why: The engine, not the player, chooses the mana abilities activated during a payment, so undoing its choices leaves the player where they decided to be; a player who wants the mana anyway can activate the abilities with priority first.

## x-in-a-cost-equal-to-a-cards-mana-cost-is-0: A granted ability's cost "equal to its mana cost" has X as 0 when the card isn't a spell
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 107.3h "If an effect instructs a player to pay an object’s mana cost that includes {X}, the value of X is treated as 0 unless the object is a spell on the stack. - Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 5. X is 0: the cost is the card's mana cost with X as 0, and no X is announced; tokens copying the card have X 0 too. Araumi's ruling is in the Commander Legends Release Notes as the data has it (Eli Shiffrin, 2020-11-06; ormos mirror, Wayback capture 2020-11-07), so it isn't a copying error, and no rules change since explains it. Likely origin (an inference): the ruling on cards that give flashback "equal to its mana cost" (Past in Flames, Snapcaster Mage: "If you cast an instant or sorcery with {X} in its mana cost this way, you still choose the value of X as part of casting the spell and pay that cost."), right there because the card becomes a spell on the stack (107.3h's exception), applied to encore, whose card is never cast. ## trigger-x-before-targets: A triggered ability whose targets depend on an X it pays as it resolves has X chosen as it's put on the stack
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 107.3f "Sometimes X appears in the text of a spell or ability but not in a mana cost, alternative cost, additional cost, or activation cost. If the value of X isn’t defined, the controller of the spell or ability chooses the value of X at the appropriate time (either as it’s put on the stack or as it resolves)." / 603.3d "The remainder of the process for putting a triggered ability on the stack is identical to the process for casting a spell listed in rules 601.2c–d. ..." / 601.2b, 601.2c (X is announced before targets are chosen)
- Question: Relentless Dead: "When this creature dies, you may pay {X}. If you do, return another target Zombie creature card with mana value X from your graveyard to the battlefield." The {X} is paid as the ability resolves, but the target must be chosen as it's put on the stack. When is X chosen?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 4. As the ability is put on the stack, before its targets (the "appropriate time" of 107.3f, since the targets depend on it); the {X} paid as it resolves is that value. Implemented for triggered abilities whose target filters mention X and whose effect pays a cost with {X} ("you may pay {X}. If you do, ..."). Before, X was undefined (0) while targets were chosen, so the ability was removed from the stack for lack of a target whenever no card had mana value 0.
- Alternatives: X chosen as it resolves, with the target chosen freely on the stack and the card returned only if its mana value equals the X paid; equivalent in outcome for a player who pays the target's mana value, but it lets the target be chosen without regard to X.
- Why: 107.3f leaves the time open, and 601.2b–c (applied to triggered abilities by 603.3d) has X announced before targets whenever targets depend on it; the target can then be checked against a known X on resolution.
- Affects: Relentless Dead and any other "you may pay {X}. If you do, ... target ... with mana value X" trigger.

## division-after-cost-amount: An amount to divide that a cost sets is divided right after the costs are paid
- Kind: deviation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 601.2d "If the spell requires the player to divide or distribute an effect (such as damage or counters) among one or more targets, the player announces the division. Each of these targets must receive at least one of whatever is being divided." / 601.2h "The player pays the total cost. ..." (602.2b applies both to activated abilities) / 601.2 "... If a player is unable to comply with the requirements of a step listed below while performing that step, the casting of the spell is illegal; the game returns to the moment before the casting of that spell was proposed ..."
- Question: Captain America, First Avenger: "{3}, Unattach an Equipment from Captain America: He deals damage equal to that Equipment's mana value divided as you choose among one, two, or three targets." The division is announced (601.2d) before the costs are paid (601.2h), but the amount is the mana value of the Equipment chosen to pay the cost. When is it divided?
- Decision: The targets are chosen as usual; an amount that refers to what a cost pays for (the unattached Equipment, the sacrificed permanent) and isn't known yet is divided as soon as the costs are paid, before the spell or ability becomes cast or activated, by the same player and with the same rules (each target at least one; the default is an even split). If the amount turns out smaller than the number of targets (an Equipment with mana value 1 unattached for two targets, or one with mana value 0), no division gives each target at least one, so the division couldn't have been announced with that payment: casting or activating it is illegal and is reversed (601.2, 733.1), and the player may try again with fewer targets or another Equipment.
- Alternatives: Have the player announce which object will pay the cost before the division (equivalent for a player who plans the payment, but the engine asks costs in 601.2h order); divide with the amount undefined (0), which makes the ability do nothing; let a default division give the extra targets nothing (breaks 601.2d's at-least-one requirement).
- Why: A player announcing the division already knows which Equipment they'll unattach; dividing right after paying gives the same outcomes without reordering the payment, and it's the only reading in which the ability does what it says. A combination of targets and payment that no legal announcement allows is an illegal action, as when the division itself can't be announced.
- Affects: divided damage or counters whose amount is set by an activation's or a spell's cost (Captain America, First Avenger; no other card divides such an amount today).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/stack.rs` (`divide_after_costs`, `amount_awaits_costs`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/casting.rs` (both payment paths).
- Tests: `tests/cards/captain_america_first_avenger.rs` (`the_unattached_equipments_mana_value_is_divided_among_the_targets`, `an_amount_smaller_than_the_number_of_targets_cant_be_divided`, `an_equipment_with_mana_value_0_leaves_nothing_to_divide`).

## mana-ability-cost-trigger-uses-the-stack: A trigger on an event of paying a mana ability's cost isn't a triggered mana ability
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 605.1b "A triggered ability is a mana ability if it meets all of the following criteria: it doesn’t require a target (see rule 115.6), it triggers from the activation or resolution of an activated mana ability (see rule 605.1a) or from mana being added to a player’s mana pool, and it could add mana to a player’s mana pool when it resolves." / 605.4a "A triggered mana ability doesn’t go on the stack, so it can’t be targeted, countered, or otherwise responded to. Rather, it resolves immediately after the mana ability that triggered it, without waiting for priority." / 605.5a "... The same is true for a triggered ability that could produce mana but triggers from an event other than activating a mana ability, ..." / 106.12 "To “tap [a permanent] for mana” is to activate a mana ability of that permanent that includes the {T} symbol in its activation cost. ..." / 602.2 "To activate an ability is to put it onto the stack and pay its costs, ..." / Cathodion's ruling (2004-10-04): "The ability is never a mana ability." / Myr Moonvessel's ruling (2011-01-25): "The triggered ability isn't a mana ability. It uses the stack and can be responded to." / Bone Miser's ruling (2025-02-07): "This creature’s second ability isn’t a mana ability. It uses the stack and can be responded to. ..."
- Question: Paying the costs is part of activating an ability (602.2). When an ability without a target that could add mana triggers on an event of paying an activated mana ability's cost (Rose, Cutthroat Raider's "Whenever you sacrifice a Junk, add {R}." when the Junk is sacrificed to Krark-Clan Ironworks' "Sacrifice an artifact: Add {C}{C}."; Cathodion's "When this creature dies, add {C}{C}{C}." when it's sacrificed to Ashnod's Altar), does it "trigger from the activation" of that mana ability?
- Decision: No. A triggered ability is a mana ability only if its trigger event is the activation or resolution of an activated mana ability ("is tapped for mana" (106.12), "whenever a land's ability causes you to add mana") or mana being added. One that triggers on a sacrifice, a death, a discard or any other event uses the stack even when that event happened while a mana ability's cost was paid: it's put on the stack the next time a player would receive priority (above the spell being cast, if any), and its mana can't pay for that spell.
- Alternatives: Count any trigger on an event that happens while a mana ability is activated (its cost) or resolves as triggering from it: Rose's, Cathodion's, Myr Moonvessel's, Su-Chi's, Crime Novelist's and Bone Miser's abilities would then resolve at once in those cases, and their mana could pay for the spell being cast.
- Why: Cathodion's ruling says its dies ability "is never a mana ability", although sacrificing it to Ashnod's Altar or Krark-Clan Ironworks was its best-known use, and Bone Miser's ruling says without exception that its land-discard ability isn't one (Lion's Eye Diamond's cost discards); 605.4a has a triggered mana ability resolve "after the mana ability that triggered it": the mana ability itself is the trigger event.
- Affects: Rose, Cutthroat Raider; Cathodion, Myr Moonvessel, Su-Chi (dying); Crime Novelist (sacrificing an artifact); Bone Miser, Waste Not, Mishra, Excavation Prodigy (discarding).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/ability.rs` (`is_triggered_mana_ability`: only triggers on a mana ability or on mana being added); `crates/mtg-engine/src/triggers.rs` (a found trigger resolves at once only if `is_mana_ability`).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p110_mana_misc.rs` (`rose_triggers_for_any_junk_sacrifice`, `mana_triggers_on_a_mana_abilitys_cost_use_the_stack`).

## dependent-target-checked-against-an-illegal-target: A target whose requirement names another target is checked against that target even when it's illegal

- Status: decided

- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 7 (work item user-questions-nov7, Part A), as recommended (work item ruling-conflicts-4, case 1, conclusion D): the engine keeps checking the dependent target against the illegal one. Fiery Annihilation's Equipment still attached to a creature that gained hexproof or protection from red is exiled; Searing Blaze still deals damage to the creature when the player gained hexproof; Yosei still taps the permanents; Run Away Together still compares the other creature with the illegal one's controller. Fiery Annihilation's ruling, which reads the other way, is set aside as inconsistent (exempt in `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P047.tsv`; logged in docs/RULING_DISCREPANCIES.md).
- Alternatives: (R2) The last sentence of 608.2b reaches a requirement that needs the illegal target, so the dependent target isn't affected: Fiery Annihilation's ruling; it would make Searing Blaze's (2010-03-01) and Suffer the Past's (2010-06-15) rulings outdated (older than the change below) and Yosei's (2017-11-17), Run Away Together's and the "share a creature type" rulings (Return from Extinction 2019-06-14, Raise the Draugr 2021-02-05, Unbury 2025-11-17, Secret Tunnel 2025-10-02) inconsistent, reverse `anchor-target-chosen-first`'s resolution clause, and keep Gaea's Blessing and Witness the Future from resolving at all when their player is illegal (every target would be). (R1′) This entry within one instance of "target" (Run Away Together, the share-a-type cards) but R2 across instances (Fiery Annihilation, Searing Blaze, Yosei): keeps Fiery Annihilation's ruling, makes Searing Blaze's and Suffer the Past's outdated and Yosei's inconsistent, and needs a distinction the CR doesn't draw (601.2c and 608.2b use "instance" only for the same object chosen twice and for whether every target is illegal). Treating player anchors (Searing Blaze, Suffer the Past, Yosei) and object anchors (Fiery Annihilation) differently would reconcile every ruling found, but no rule draws that line either (115.9b treats them alike).
- Why: The information sentence is about "part of the effect" (609.1: something that happens), while checking the targets is 608.2b's separate first step, which takes last known information where it needs it ("If the source of an ability has left the zone it was in, its last known information is used during this process."). Until the CR effective 2015-03-27, 608.2b said "The effect may still determine information about illegal targets, though, and other parts of the effect for which those targets are not illegal may still affect them."; the CR effective 2015-07-17 (Magic Origins) replaced it with today's sentence (Magic Origins Update Bulletin, Matt Tabak, 2015-07-16: "And if the spell needs information from the target, it can't do anything that relies on that information."), and the CR effective 2016-07-22 removed "or a target that's become illegal" from 608.2g, now 608.2h (Eldritch Moon Update Bulletin, 2016-07-28: "The problem is effects can't get information from an illegal target."); archived CR texts at https://github.com/pit142857/mtg-cr, bulletins from the ormos mirror. Wizards' rulings after those changes still check the dependent target against the illegal one: Yosei (Iconic Masters Release Notes, 2017-09-11) on the same two-instance structure as Fiery Annihilation, and Run Away Together (first in the Throne of Eldraine Release Notes, 2019-09-20; republished 2025-11-17, after Fiery Annihilation's ruling), which states the principle ("can still determine its controller only to check whether the other creature is a legal target") for a target still on the battlefield. 115.9b (since the CR effective 2009-07-11, as 113.7a), the CR's nearest rule, uses an illegal target's current information for another spell's check (Keep Safe ruling, 2020-04-17), for objects and players alike. Counted by structure, the post-2015 rulings on the exact case (a second "target" described by the first, the first illegal but still there) are one each way: Yosei (2017-11-17) and Fiery Annihilation (2024-11-08); so this entry rests on the CR's text and on not drawing distinctions it doesn't make, not on a lopsided count. Fiery Annihilation's ruling is the newest on that structure and a judge could read 608.2b's sentence as it does; the user decided (Nov 7) to set it aside. Fiery Annihilation's ruling adds anything to its sibling ("If the target Equipment is no longer attached to the target creature when Fiery Annihilation resolves, it won't be exiled.") only in this case: when the creature leaves or stops being a creature, the Equipment becomes unattached first (301.5c, 704.5n) and is illegal under either reading. The left-the-zone half (last known information, as Run Away Together's ruling says) has rulings behind it but no CR text; 115.9b ignores a target that has left, for its own purpose. The user asked (Nov 7) whether attachment differs from control (Fiery Annihilation is the only one of these cards whose dependent target is described by attachment rather than by a controller); the investigation (work item user-questions-nov7, Part A) found nothing in the CR that makes the kind of relationship matter. 608.2b's last sentences ("If part of the effect requires information about an illegal target, it fails to determine any such information. Any part of the effect that requires that information won’t happen.") separate no kinds of information. 109.3 lists control and attachment together as information that isn't a characteristic: "Any other information about an object isn’t a characteristic. For example, characteristics don’t include whether a permanent is tapped, a spell’s target, an object’s owner or controller, what an Aura enchants, and so on."; attachment is the Equipment's state (301.5a "The creature an Equipment is attached to is called the “equipped creature.” The Equipment is attached to, or “equips,” that creature."; 701.3a, 701.3d) as control is the permanent's (110.2), and 301.5d keeps them apart ("An Equipment’s controller is separate from the equipped creature’s controller"). 109.3 also classes "a spell’s target" as information about the spell, so knowing which creature or player is the anchor isn't information about the illegal target. "Target Equipment attached to that creature" needs only the illegal creature's identity, as Yosei's "permanents that player controls" needs only the player's; Run Away Together needs more (the illegal creature's own controller), and its ruling still allows that "only to check whether the other creature is a legal target": an exception stated by its purpose, which covers Fiery Annihilation's check too. The CR's rules specific to attachment (301.5c, 303.4c, 701.3d, 702.16c, 702.16d, 704.5m, 704.5n) all end the attachment before resolution when they apply, where both readings agree; none applies when the creature stays on the battlefield as an illegal target (hexproof, shroud, or protection from a quality the Equipment lacks). 800.4i ("If an effect requires information about a specific player, the effect uses the current information about that player if they are still in the game; ...") parallels 608.2h for objects, and 115.9b treats players and objects alike. Among the rulings on the exact shape (a second target described by the first, the first illegal but still there, after 2015), Yosei's (2017-11-17) and Down for Repairs' (2022-10-07, "Destroy up to one target Attraction that player controls": "If only one of those targets is legal as the spell resolves, you’ll still do as much as you can to the remaining legal target."; read the other way, an illegal opponent would always take the Attraction with it, so only the opponent could remain legal, which the ruling doesn't say) go one way and Fiery Annihilation's (2024-11-08) the other; Fiery Annihilation is the only card whose second target is described by attachment to the first (Oracle search), so its ruling can't show that attachment is what matters. A combined line (an object anchor in a different instance of "target" isn't seen) fits every post-2015 ruling but has no CR text either. Possible origin (an inference): the wording echoes single-target templates (Blastfire Bolt 2014-07-18, Turn to Slag, Fires of Mount Doom, Light of Judgment 2025-06-06) where the whole spell doesn't resolve.
- Affects: Fiery Annihilation (an illegal creature still on the battlefield with its Equipment); Searing Blaze; Suffer the Past; Yosei, the Morning Star; Run Away Together; Return from Extinction, Raise the Draugr, Unbury, Secret Tunnel ("share a creature type"); Glyph of Delusion (`anchor-target-chosen-first`); other spells and abilities with a target described by another target. An illegal player who would act doesn't, under either reading (Memory's Journey: "If the player is an illegal target ..., the spell will have no effect, even if the cards are still legal targets."; Witness the Future, Gaea's Blessing, Loaming Shaman); but the rest of such a spell (Gaea's Blessing's draw, Witness the Future's look at the top four) still happens only because the cards, "from their graveyard", stay legal targets: were they illegal too, every target would be, and the spell wouldn't resolve.

## one-target-per-card-type: "Up to one target card of each card type" is one set of targets, each card standing for a different card type it has
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 205.2a "The card types are artifact, battle, conspiracy, creature, dungeon, enchantment, instant, kindred, land, phenomenon, plane, planeswalker, scheme, sorcery, and vanguard. ..." / 115.3 "The same target can’t be chosen multiple times for any one instance of the word “target” on a spell or ability. If the spell or ability uses the word “target” in multiple places, the same object or player can be chosen once for each instance of the word “target” (as long as it fits the targeting criteria). ..." / 601.2c "... If the spell has a variable number of targets, the player announces how many targets they will choose before they announce those targets. ..." / Decimate ruling: "However, if a permanent has multiple card types, it may be chosen as the target for more than one instance of the word "target." For example, you could cast Decimate targeting an artifact creature, an enchantment, and a land."
- Question: Uldaros Theorix exiles "up to one target nonland card of each card type from your graveyard". Is that one instance of "target" with a requirement on the targets together, or one instance per card type (so an artifact creature card could be the target for artifact and for creature at once, as with Decimate's separate "target" words)? And may a card with two card types stand for either?
- Decision: One instance of "target": up to as many targets as there are card types, chosen so that each can stand for a different card type it has (an artifact creature card is the artifact card or the creature card, and another creature card can then be the creature card). A choice that doesn't have that relationship is fitted to the largest group of the chosen cards, in the order chosen, that does (as for other related targets, `target_groups.rs`).
- Alternatives: One instance of "target" per card type: the same artifact creature card could be chosen for both of its types. It's exiled and copied once either way, so nothing in the game differs but how many times it's a target.
- Why: The text has the word "target" once, and 115.3 lets the same card be chosen only once for it; Atraxa, Grand Unifier's ruling for the same "card of each card type" wording ("an artifact creature card could be put into your hand as the artifact card you choose or as the creature card you choose") is the reading of a card with several types. Grime Gorger's untargeted "up to one card of each card type" is chosen the same way.
- Affects: Uldaros Theorix.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/phrases.rs` (`parse_target`, `target_group_suffix`: `TargetGroup::OnePerCardType`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/target_groups.rs` (`one_per_card_type`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/c6_copies_casting.rs` (`uldaros_theorix_targets_one_card_of_each_card_type`).

## capped-casts-one-at-a-time: Spells cast from among copies up to a count or total mana value are chosen one at a time, each against what's left
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 608.2g "... If an effect specifically instructs or allows a player to cast a spell during resolution, they do so by following the steps in rules 601.2a–i, except no player receives priority after it’s cast. That spell becomes the topmost object on the stack, and the currently resolving spell or ability continues to resolve, which may include casting other spells this way. ..." / 707.12a "An effect that creates multiple copies and says a player “may cast” those objects allows that player to choose individually, for each of those objects, whether or not to cast it." / 601.3e "Some rules and effects state that an alternative set of characteristics or a subset of characteristics are considered to determine if a card or copy of a card is legal to cast. ..." / 733.1 "If a player takes an illegal action or starts to take an action but can’t legally complete it, the entire action is reversed and any payments already made are canceled. ..." / 733.2 "... The player may redo the reversed action in a legal way or take any other action allowed by the rules."
- Question: "You may cast any number of spells with total mana value 6 or less from among the copies" (Uldaros Theorix) and "You may cast up to three of the copies" (Baron Helmut Zemo) cap the casts. The spells are cast one after another (608.2g); the CR doesn't say how the player states which copies they'll cast. The cards have no rulings on it.
- Decision: The player chooses one copy to cast, it's cast, then they choose the next among the copies left that fit what's left of the count and the total (or none, which ends the casting), until the cap is reached. A total is of the mana values of the spells as they're cast (a split card's half, X as 0). A copy whose casting can't be completed (no legal target) is reversed (733.1): it isn't one of the count, and that way of casting it is offered again once another spell has been cast (which may give it a target, 733.2); until then trying it again would only be reversed again.
- Alternatives: Choose every copy to cast up front, in casting order, as one choice (as `cast-order-as-chosen` does for uncapped choices): the same sets and orders are possible, but the choice of a later spell couldn't depend on what an earlier one did as it was cast.
- Why: It's the same mechanism as Invoke Calamity and Rod of Absorption, whose rulings have the spells cast one after the other and judged as spells; choosing each after the previous one is cast gives the player every set and order the cap allows.
- Affects: Uldaros Theorix, Baron Helmut Zemo (and, with the same mechanism from other zones, Invoke Calamity and Rod of Absorption).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/kw/cast_up_to_total_mana_value.rs` (`copies_effect_name`, `custom_effect`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/oracle/patterns/copies_7.rs`.
- Tests: `tests/cards/c6_copies_casting.rs` (`uldaros_theorix_casts_copies_with_total_mana_value_six_or_less`, `baron_helmut_zemo_casts_up_to_three_copies_of_the_cards_its_boast_exiled`, `baron_helmut_zemo_a_copy_that_cant_be_cast_isnt_one_of_the_three`, `uldaros_theorix_a_copy_tried_too_early_may_be_cast_after_another`, `uldaros_theorix_counts_the_mana_value_of_the_half_of_a_split_card_cast`).

## total-mana-value-choice-fitted: Cards chosen at once to cast "with total mana value N or less" are fitted to the total by the spells they can be cast as, in the order listed
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 601.3e "Some rules and effects state that an alternative set of characteristics or a subset of characteristics are considered to determine if a card or copy of a card is legal to cast. These alternative characteristics replace the object’s characteristics for this determination. Continuous effects that would apply to that object once it has those characteristics are also considered." / 608.2g "... If an effect specifically instructs or allows a player to cast a spell during resolution, they do so by following the steps in rules 601.2a–i, except no player receives priority after it’s cast. That spell becomes the topmost object on the stack, and the currently resolving spell or ability continues to resolve, which may include casting other spells this way. ..." / 712.11c "Only the face that will be face up on the stack is evaluated to determine if it can be cast. Only that face is considered to be put onto the stack." / Primeval Spawn rulings (2022-09-09): "Primeval Spawn’s last ability checks the mana values and types of the spells on the stack, not the mana values and types of the cards in exile. Notably, this means that you may cast the back face of a modal double-faced card or either face of a split card as long as the spells you are casting together have a total mana value of 10 or less." and "The spells are cast one after the other during the resolution of Primeval Spawn’s last ability. The one you cast last will be the first one to resolve."
- Question: Primeval Spawn ("You may cast any number of spells with total mana value 10 or less from among them without paying their mana costs.") is compiled as one choice of the cards to cast, listed in casting order (`Effect::CastCard`, as in `cast-order-as-chosen`), not as one spell at a time (`capped-casts-one-at-a-time`, Uldaros Theorix, Invoke Calamity, Rod of Absorption). The total is of the spells the cards are cast as (a modal double-faced card's back face, a split card's half), which the choice of cards alone doesn't settle. What happens to an answer whose cards can't fit the total, and which faces is each chosen card offered?
- Decision: The answer is fitted in the order it lists the cards: a card is kept if the cheapest spell it could be cast as (X is 0) fits with the cheapest spells of the cards kept before it; the others aren't cast. As each kept card is cast in the order chosen, it's offered only the faces or halves that keep the total within the limit, counting the spells already cast this way and the cheapest spells of the kept cards still to be cast, so each of them can still be cast.
- Alternatives: Cast the spells one at a time, each chosen against what's left (`capped-casts-one-at-a-time`): the same sets, orders and faces are possible, but Primeval Spawn's "from among them" (the cards the previous instruction exiled) isn't one of that mechanism's sources. Cast nothing when the answer doesn't fit: legal, but other choices with a requirement on the chosen objects together fit an invalid answer to the largest group of it, in the order chosen, that meets the requirement (`target_groups::fit_together`).
- Why: The answer lists every card the player will cast, in casting order, so every legal set of spells, in every order and with every combination of faces, can be expressed; an answer that can't fit is fitted the way other choices with a requirement on the chosen objects together are. Uncontroversial (how the agent interface expresses the choice and the fallback for an invalid answer, not a reading of the rules), so not provisional.
- Affects: Primeval Spawn (any "cast ... spells with total mana value N or less from among [cards]" compiled to `Effect::CastCard` with a choice).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/spell_choice.rs` (`choose_cards_to_cast`, `total_mana_value_limit`, `faces_within_total`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/resolve.rs` (`Effect::CastCard`).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p069_misc.rs` (`primeval_spawn_uses_the_mana_values_of_the_spells_cast`, `primeval_spawn_fits_an_answer_over_the_total_in_the_order_listed`).

## target-player-reference-rechecked: A target restriction naming "that player" (another target's controller) is checked against that target's controller as the spell resolves
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 608.2b "If the spell or ability specifies targets, it checks whether the targets are still legal. ... Other changes to the game state may cause a target to no longer be legal; for example, its characteristics may have changed ..." / Mutiny ruling: "If either or both targets are illegal when Mutiny resolves, no creature will deal or be dealt damage."
- Question: Mutiny ("Target creature an opponent controls deals damage equal to its power to another target creature that player controls") is cast in a multiplayer game; before it resolves, a third player (also an opponent) gains control of the first target. The first target is still a creature an opponent controls. Is the second target, still controlled by the original player, a creature "that player" controls?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 5. No. "That player" is the first target's controller as the targets are checked on resolution, so the second target is illegal and, per the ruling, no damage is dealt.
- Alternatives: "That player" stays the player who controlled the first target as the spell was cast, so both targets are legal and the first deals its damage to the second.
- Why: 608.2b checks each target against the current game state, and the restriction's referent is part of that state; nothing in the rules locks the referent in when targets are chosen. No ruling addresses the case. It was provisional because the reading of the referent is ours.
- Affects: Mutiny and other spells whose second target is restricted by the first target's controller ("another target creature that player controls").
- Code: target legality on resolution (`crates/mtg-engine/src/stack.rs`).

## hybrid-half-for-a-reduction: A cost reduction that can reduce a hybrid symbol of the cost reduces it, as though the player had announced the half it reduces
- Kind: deviation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 601.2b "... If a cost that will be paid as the spell is being cast includes hybrid mana symbols, the player announces the nonhybrid equivalent cost they intend to pay. ..." / 601.2f "... The total cost is the mana cost or alternative cost (as determined in rule 601.2b), plus all additional costs and cost increases, and minus all cost reductions. ..." / 118.13a "If the mana cost of a spell or the activation cost of an activated ability contains a mana symbol that can be paid in multiple ways, the choice of how to pay for that symbol is made as its controller proposes that spell or ability (see rule 601.2b)." / Ragemonger ruling (2014-02-01): "If a spell has hybrid mana symbols in its mana cost, you choose which half you will be paying before determining the total cost. If you choose to pay such a cost with {B} or {R}, Ragemonger can reduce that part of the cost." / Morophon, the Boundless ruling (2019-06-14): "... For example, if a spell of the chosen type costs {2}{W/U}{W/U}, you may choose for the cost to be {2}{W}{U} and then reduce it to {2}." / Spectral Procession ruling (2008-05-01): "If an effect reduces the cost to cast a spell by an amount of generic mana, it applies to a monocolored hybrid spell only if you've chosen a method of paying for it that includes generic mana."
- Question: The halves of a cost's hybrid symbols are announced before the total cost is determined, so whether a reduction reduces a hybrid symbol depends on the half announced. The engine asks how hybrid symbols will be paid only once the total cost is known (`choose_payment_ways_for`, after the reductions). Which half does a reduction see?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 5. The half the reduction can reduce, chosen for the player: a reduction by colored mana ("cost {B}{R} less", Ragemonger, Edgewalker, Morophon) that finds no plain symbol of its color in the cost reduces a hybrid symbol with that color ({R/W}, {2/R}, ...), as though that half had been announced; a reduction by an amount of generic mana ("cost {1} less") that the cost's generic mana can't absorb reduces a monocolored hybrid symbol as though its generic half had been announced ({2/W} reduced by {1} becomes {1}). The player announces the halves of the other hybrid symbols as they pay. The player can't announce the other half of a symbol a reduction would reduce, to keep the reduction from applying.
- Alternatives: Ask for the nonhybrid equivalent of each hybrid symbol before the total cost is determined (601.2b), as the rulings describe: exact, but one more decision for each hybrid symbol of every spell, and the later payment would have to follow the announcement.
- Why: Accepted as a simplification for now: the half the reduction reduces is never the worse choice for what has to be paid (the symbol goes away, or a {2/W} leaves {1}, which any mana pays). The other half only matters to effects that look at the mana spent (adamant, converge, "if only white mana was spent"), where paying with that color could be wanted; there the engine takes a legal choice away. Plan: announce hybrid halves before the total cost (601.2b) when such a case comes up.
- Affects: colored reductions applied to costs with hybrid symbols (Ragemonger with Boros Reckoner, Edgewalker with Moonrise Cleric, Morophon with Portal Manipulator) and generic reductions applied to monocolored hybrid costs (Goblin Electromancer with Spectral Procession).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/cost_rules.rs` (`reduce_one_colored`, `reduce_generic_and_hybrid`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/activation_costs.rs` (`CostChanges::apply`).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p033_total_cost.rs` (`ragemonger_reduces_black_and_red_mana_of_minotaur_spells`); `tests/rulings/r_p184_clerics_dinosaurs.rs` (`edgewalker_reduces_a_hybrid_symbol_paid_with_white_or_black`); `tests/rulings/r_p183_costs.rs` (`morophon_reduces_both_hybrid_halves_once_they_are_chosen`); `tests/rulings/r_s22_costs.rs` (`spectral_procession_a_generic_reduction_applies_only_to_generic_halves`).

## target-restriction-source-attachment-as-announced: A targeting restriction naming the source's equipped or enchanted creature is checked against what the source was attached to as the ability was put onto the stack
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 113.7a "... any activated or triggered ability that references information about the source for use while announcing an activated ability or putting a triggered ability on the stack checks that information when the ability is put onto the stack. Otherwise, it will check that information when it resolves. In both instances, if the source is no longer in the zone it’s expected to be in at that time, its last known information is used." / 608.2b "If the spell or ability specifies targets, it checks whether the targets are still legal. A target that’s no longer in the zone it was in when it was targeted is illegal. Other changes to the game state may cause a target to no longer be legal; for example, its characteristics may have changed or an effect may have changed the text of the spell. If the source of an ability has left the zone it was in, its last known information is used during this process. ..." / Plasma Caster rulings (2024-03-08): "If Plasma Caster is no longer on the battlefield or no longer attached to the same creature when its third ability resolves, the ability will still resolve as normal." and "If the target creature is no longer on the battlefield or no longer blocking the equipped creature when Plasma Caster's third ability tries to resolve, it won't resolve and none of its effects happen. You won't flip a coin."
- Question: Plasma Caster's "Pay {E}{E}: Choose target creature that's blocking equipped creature. Flip a coin. ..." is activated, and in response the Equipment is attached to another creature (or leaves the battlefield). As the ability resolves, 608.2b rechecks the target: is "equipped creature" the creature the Equipment was attached to when the target was chosen, or the one it's attached to now?
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 10 (as recommended). The creature it was attached to when the ability was put onto the stack. Which creature is equipped is information about the source used while announcing the ability (choosing its target), which 113.7a says is checked then; the rest of the restriction ("blocking") is rechecked against the current game state, so a target that has left the battlefield, or no longer blocks that creature, is illegal (the second ruling). The effect itself ("this Equipment deals 1 damage to it") reads the source as it is when it resolves, or its last known information. In general: while a target's legality is checked, a reference to what the source is attached to (`Sel::AttachedTo`) reads the attachment recorded as the ability was put onto the stack; nothing else about the source is fixed this way, and outside target checks the current attachment is read.
- Alternatives: Read "equipped creature" as whatever the Equipment is attached to as the targets are rechecked (608.2b's "other changes to the game state"): the ability would fail to resolve once the Equipment is attached to another creature (while it would still resolve, by last known information, after the Equipment left the battlefield). A judge could read 608.2b that way; the first ruling says the ability resolves.
- Why: The first ruling is explicit that the ability resolves when the Equipment is attached to another creature, and 113.7a's "information about the source for use while announcing" covers the attachment the target restriction depends on. Provisional because the generalization to every attachment reference in a targeting restriction is ours, and because 608.2b's recheck could be read with the current attachment.
- Affects: Plasma Caster, the only card whose targeting restriction names the equipped or enchanted creature; a copy of such an ability keeps the recorded attachment (CR 707.10).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/object.rs` (`StackInfo::source_attached_to`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/stack.rs` (`create_stack_ability`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/eval.rs` (`Sel::AttachedTo` under `Ctx::target_legality`).
- Tests: `tests/rulings/r_p081_blocked_state.rs` (`plasma_caster_resolves_after_leaving_or_coming_unattached`).

For example, if a spell of the chosen type costs {4}{R}{W}{W}, it will cost {4}{W} after applying Morophon's effect." / Vault Skirge ruling (2011-06-01): "A card with Phyrexian mana symbols in its mana cost is each color that appears in that mana cost, regardless of how that cost may have been paid."
- Question: Morophon naming Imp makes Imp spells cost {W}{U}{B}{R}{G} less, colored mana only. Vault Skirge costs {1}{B/P}. Is the {B/P} the cost's black mana component, which the {B} of the reduction reduces to nothing (118.7c), leaving {1}? Or does a cost with a Phyrexian symbol not "require" black mana (118.7b, since 2 life may pay it), so that the reduction finds nothing to reduce (and, for a reduction that isn't colored-only, reduces generic mana instead)? The rules on cost reductions (118.7a–g) name hybrid, Phyrexian and snow symbols only in the reduction, never in the cost being reduced, and no ruling addresses a Phyrexian symbol met by a reduction of its color.
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 11 (as recommended). The reduction reduces the Phyrexian symbol: it is a colored mana symbol of that color (107.4f), so it is part of the cost's mana component of that color (118.7c), as a hybrid symbol with that color is once the player announces its colored half (`hybrid-half-for-a-reduction`). The player is taken to have announced paying the symbol with mana (601.2b) rather than with 2 life, as paying nothing for it is never worse for them than paying 2 life or one mana. Vault Skirge costs {1} under Morophon naming Imp, and no life is paid. When the cost has several Phyrexian symbols of that color and the player announced 2 life for some of them, the reduction removes the symbols announced to be paid with mana first, and the announcements of life payments carry over to the symbols left (Thundering Tanadon {4}{G/P}{G/P} under Morophon naming Beast, one symbol announced {G} and the other 2 life: {4} and 2 life). A reduction that isn't colored-only still reduces generic mana by the excess when the cost has no symbol of its color at all (118.7b–c).
- Alternatives: A Phyrexian symbol isn't a mana component of its color because 2 life can pay it: the reduction leaves it alone (and a reduction that isn't colored-only reduces generic mana by that amount instead, 118.7b). This is what the engine did before: a colored reduction reduced plain and hybrid symbols of its color but never a Phyrexian one.
- Why: 107.4f settles that the symbol is a colored mana symbol of that color, and 118.7c reduces "its mana component of that color"; reading "require" in 118.7b as excluding a symbol that mana of that color pays would also exclude hybrid symbols, which the rulings on Ragemonger, Edgewalker and Morophon reduce once the colored half is announced. A reasonable judge could read 118.7b the other way, since 2 life pays the symbol without any mana, so the entry is provisional.
- Affects: colored cost reductions ("Cleric spells you cast cost {W}{B} less", Edgewalker; "Minotaur spells you cast cost {B}{R} less", Ragemonger; Morophon's chosen type) applied to spells with Phyrexian mana symbols of those colors (Vault Skirge, Porcelain Legionnaire, Phyrexian Metamorph, Dismember as a Cleric's granted cost, ...).

## keyword-cost-reduction-on-an-alternative-cost-after-increases: "[Keyword] costs cost {N} less" on an alternative cost comes off after the cost increases
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 601.2f "... The total cost is the mana cost or alternative cost (as determined in rule 601.2b), plus all additional costs and cost increases, and minus all cost reductions. If multiple cost reductions apply, the player may apply them in any order. If the mana component of the total cost is reduced to nothing by cost reduction effects, it is considered to be {0}. Casting a spell using its flashback ability follows the rules for paying alternative costs in rules 601.2b and 601.2f–h." / Warbringer ruling (2015-02-25): "Warbringer’s first ability can’t affect the colored mana requirement of a dash cost. If a card’s dash cost includes one generic mana, that cost will be reduced by {1}." / Devil's Play ruling (2021-03-19): "To determine the total cost of a spell, start with the mana cost or alternative cost (such as a flashback cost) you're paying, add any cost increases, then apply any cost reductions. ..." / Trinisphere ruling (2020-08-07): "... Similarly, the cost reduction from its second ability applies only to generic mana in the unlock costs of Rooms you control. (In other words, it's impossible for these abilities to combine to make anything cost {2} less.)"
- Question: Catalyst Stone ("Flashback costs you pay cost {2} less") with Thalia, Guardian of Thraben ("Noncreature spells cost {1} more to cast") on Deep Analysis's flashback cost {1}{U}, Pay 3 life. Is the reduction taken off the total cost, after the increase (601.2f: "minus all cost reductions"), making it {1}{U} + {1} - {2} = {U}? Or does it reduce the flashback cost itself, one component of the total, so that the second {1} of the reduction finds no generic mana there and is lost, and the total is {U} + {1} = {1}{U}? The CR knows only reductions of "a cost" (118.7a) subtracted from the total (601.2f); the rulings describe these effects as changes to the keyword's cost.
- Decision: Decided by the user, Nov 11: "for thalia + flashback reduction, '... The total cost is the mana cost or alternative cost (as determined in rule 601.2b), plus all additional costs and cost increases, and minus all cost reductions. ...' implies that, since the increased cost is still a flashback cost, it is reduced, and the flashback in the described situation would only be {U}". When the keyword's cost is an alternative cost (the keyword's own rule says "rather than [paying] its mana cost": flashback, dash, blitz, escape, evoke, ...), the cost increases that apply to the spell are applied to that alternative cost (118.9d), so the effect's reduction comes off the total cost after all of them, as the total cost's other reductions do (601.2f): only generic mana (118.7a), never below {0} (601.2f), never the colored mana, and before the effects that directly affect the total cost (Trinisphere's floor). Catalyst Stone and Thalia make Deep Analysis's flashback {1}{U} + {1} - {2} = {U}, Pay 3 life, and Trinisphere then {2}{U}; Warbringer ("Dash costs you pay cost {2} less") and Sphere of Resistance make Mardu Scout's dash {1}{R} + {1} - {2} = {R}; Warbringer with Lodestone Golem and Sphere of Resistance makes it {1}{R} + {2} - {2} = {1}{R}; a reduction larger than all the generic mana leaves {R}. An X in the alternative cost is part of it, with the value announced before the total cost is determined (107.3a, 601.2f), so the reduction can reduce it: Devil's Play's flashback {X}{R}{R}{R} for X = 3 under Lodestone Golem and Catalyst Stone is {3}{R}{R}{R} + {1} - {2} = {2}{R}{R}{R}, and X is still 3. The increases of the keyword's costs ("Flashback costs your opponents pay cost {2} more") are added to that cost too; the order doesn't matter for them. Two neighboring calls are separate entries, decided by the user on Nov 12 for now: the additional costs paid with the alternative cost (`alternative-keyword-cost-reduction-reduces-additional-costs`) and a reduction of an additional keyword cost such as buyback (`keyword-cost-reduction-on-an-additional-cost-within-it`). A morph cost paid to turn a permanent face up (a special action, 702.37e) isn't a spell's cost; the effects on morph costs (Exiled Doomsayer) change that cost alone.
- Alternatives: Reduce the keyword cost itself on an alternative cost too (the engine's reading before this decision, entry `keyword-cost-reduction-bounded-by-the-keyword-cost`): the reduction is applied to the keyword cost as the way of casting the spell is built, before the increases, and whatever exceeds that cost's generic mana is lost; Catalyst Stone and Thalia would make Deep Analysis's flashback {U} + {1} = {1}{U}, Warbringer and Sphere of Resistance Mardu Scout's dash {R} + {1} = {1}{R}. Rejected by the user. (Built before X was announced, that reduction also missed an X in the keyword cost: Devil's Play's flashback for X = 3 stayed {3}{R}{R}{R} under Catalyst Stone.)
- Why: 118.9d makes the increases part of the alternative cost being paid, so the cost the effect names after them is still the flashback (or dash) cost, and 601.2f subtracts the reductions after every increase; this is the user's reading. The Warbringer ruling ("If a card’s dash cost includes one generic mana, that cost will be reduced by {1}") and the Inquisitive Glimmer ruling ("applies only to generic mana in the unlock costs", "it's impossible for these abilities to combine to make anything cost {2} less") stay true: with no increase the dash cost has only that generic mana, and the reduction still reduces only generic mana and never adds to another reduction.
- Affects: Catalyst Stone (flashback), Warbringer (dash) and Henzie "Toolbox" Torre (blitz) with a cost increase (Thalia, Sphere of Resistance, Lodestone Golem, an opponent's Catalyst Stone, ...) on a spell whose alternative cost has less generic mana than the reduction, and Catalyst Stone on a flashback cost with X (Devil's Play). The engine applies the effects on a keyword's costs the same way to the alternative costs of 21 keywords (flashback, dash, blitz, escape, evoke, emerge, harmonize, mayhem, warp, sneak, web-slinging, cleave, awaken, surge, bestow, disturb, prowl, overload, spectacle, freerunning, More Than Meets the Eye), though only flashback, dash and blitz have such effects among today's cards (and only theirs are compiled). Not the reductions of a spell's whole cost ("Spells cost {1} less", affinity), which the total cost subtracts after every increase anyway, nor the keyword costs of activated abilities (equip, cycling, ninjutsu, boast) or the unlock costs of Rooms.
