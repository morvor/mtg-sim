# Stack, casting and costs

Judgment calls about casting spells, activating abilities and paying costs (CR 117, 118,
601–602, 605, 733). Format and kinds: `docs/agents/items/interpretation-register.md`;
index: `docs/INTERPRETATIONS.md`.

## automatic-mana-payment: The engine chooses which mana abilities pay a cost
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 601.2g "If the total cost includes a mana payment, the player then has a chance to activate mana abilities (see rule 605, “Mana Abilities”). Mana abilities must be activated before costs are paid." / 601.2h "The player pays the total cost. ..." / 605.3a "A player may activate an activated mana ability whenever they have priority, whenever they are casting a spell or activating an ability that requires a mana payment, or whenever a rule or effect asks for a mana payment, ..."
- Question: Which mana abilities a player activates to pay a cost is the player's choice; there is no decision for it in the agent interface.
- Decision: The engine plans the payment: mana already in the pool first, then a backtracking search over the player's mana sources, preferring cheaper sources (lands before creatures, tapping before sacrificing, e.g. a land before a Treasure; abilities that deal damage to the player, like a pain land's colored mana, after those that don't) and, among equals, those that make fewer kinds of mana (a Mountain before a Volcanic Island for {R}). The damage the planned mana abilities deal counts against the life left to pay Phyrexian mana with (601.2g–h: they're activated before the cost is paid; 119.4). An agent that wants a particular payment activates mana abilities with priority first (mtg-api lists them) and the pool is spent before anything else.
- Alternatives: Ask the player for each mana ability (a `ChoosePayment` decision): exact, but far more decisions per game and an interface every agent would have to handle.
- Why: The choice rarely matters and the planner keeps the most flexible sources untapped; agents keep full control through priority-time mana abilities.
- Affects: every mana payment.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/mana_abilities.rs` (`mana_sources_with` ranking, `plan_payment`, `pay_mana`).
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_engine_policies.rs` (`mana_is_paid_from_the_cheapest_least_flexible_sources`).

## castability-is-optimistic: The engine's list of castable spells assumes favorable choices
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 601.2f "The player determines the total cost of the spell. ... Some effects may increase or reduce the cost to pay, ..." / 733.1 "If a player takes an illegal action or starts to take an action but can’t legally complete it, the entire action is reversed and any payments already made are canceled. ..."
- Question: Whether a spell can be cast can depend on choices made while casting it (a reduction "if it targets ..."). What should the list of legal actions offered with priority contain?
- Decision: `Game::legal_actions` is optimistic: a cost change that depends on the spell's targets or other choices is assumed to apply if it's a reduction and not to apply if it's an increase, so a spell is listed if some choices might make it affordable. Trying it with choices that don't is reversed (733.1) and the player keeps priority. mtg-api's `legal` module tries each listed action on a copy of the game, so external agents get only actions that can really be taken.
- Alternatives: Search every combination of targets and choices before listing a spell: exact but expensive on every priority check.
- Why: An action listed but impossible is harmless (it is reversed as the CR prescribes); an action wrongly left out would be a missing legal play.
- Affects: spells and abilities with target- or choice-dependent cost changes (Roadside Blowout, Depower, ...).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/spell_costs.rs` (`own_change_applies`, `assume_targets`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/activation_costs.rs`; `crates/mtg-api/src/legal.rs`.
- Tests: `tests/cr/interp_engine_policies.rs` (`a_spell_whose_reduction_depends_on_its_targets_is_listed_as_castable`).

## rider-order: Instructions replacement effects leave for after their events run in the order the effects were applied
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 614.6 "If an event is replaced, it never happens. A modified event occurs instead, which may in turn trigger abilities. ..." / 616.1f "Once the chosen effect has been applied, this process is repeated (taking into account only replacement or prevention effects that would now be applicable) until there are no more left to apply." / 701.19a "... “Regenerate [permanent]” means “The next time [permanent] would be destroyed this turn, instead remove all damage marked on it and its controller taps it. ...”" / 603.12 "A resolving spell or ability may allow or instruct a player to take an action and create a triggered ability that triggers “when [a player] [does or doesn’t]” take that action ..."
- Question: When one event (Day of Judgment destroying several regenerating creatures, a board wipe with Head of the Hunt out) leaves several riders at once ("When it regenerates this way, ...", "... exile it instead. When you do, ...", "and also ..."), in which order do they happen? The rules are silent; the ones that are triggered abilities are then ordered by their controller (603.3b), but the order they're offered in decides the default and the order of non-triggered riders.
- Decision: In the order the replacement effects were applied: for simultaneous events, the order the engine replaces them in (objects in battlefield order); for one event, in the order the effects were chosen (616.1f), an effect's own nested riders innermost first. Riders queued while a rider runs happen before the remaining ones.
- Alternatives: The reverse order (what a stack of pending riders gave); grouping by controller or source.
- Why: It follows the order the modified events themselves happened in, and with the default trigger order (`default-order-as-listed`) it keeps the earliest rider at the bottom of the stack like any triggers detected in that order.
- Affects: regeneration riders (Matopi Golem, Skeleton Scavengers, Soldevi Sentry), "When you do" replacement riders (Head of the Hunt, Nemata, Valentin), "and also" replacements, prevention follow-ups queued after damage.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/actions.rs` (`run_post_replacement_effects`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/replacement.rs` (queuing `ReplacementAction::Then` riders and regeneration riders).
- Tests: `tests/cards/rider_order.rs`.

## illegal-action-reversal-restores-libraries: Reversing an illegal action restores libraries too, although 733.1 says library actions stay
- Kind: deviation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 733.1 "If a player takes an illegal action or starts to take an action but can’t legally complete it, the entire action is reversed and any payments already made are canceled. No abilities trigger and no effects apply as a result of an undone action. If the action was casting a spell, the spell returns to the zone it came from. Each player may also reverse any legal mana abilities that player activated while making the illegal play, unless mana from those abilities or from any triggered mana abilities they caused to trigger was spent on another mana ability that wasn’t reversed. Players may not reverse actions that moved cards to a library, moved cards from a library to any zone other than the stack, caused a library to be shuffled, or caused cards from a library to be revealed." / 601.2h "The player pays the total cost. First, they pay all costs that don’t involve random elements or moving objects from the library to a public zone, in any order. Then they pay all remaining costs in any order. Partial payments are not allowed. Unpayable costs can’t be paid." / 605.1a "An activated ability is a mana ability if it meets all of the following criteria: ... and its cost and effect don’t move any card to or from a library. ..." / 605.1b "A triggered ability is a mana ability if it meets all of the following criteria: it doesn’t require a target (see rule 115.6), it triggers from the activation or resolution of an activated mana ability (see rule 605.1a) or from mana being added to a player’s mana pool, and it could add mana to a player’s mana pool when it resolves." / 401.5 "... If the top card of the player’s library changes while a spell is being cast, the new top card won’t be revealed and can’t be looked at until the spell becomes cast (see rule 601.2i). ..."
- Question: An action that turns out to be illegal is reversed, but the library actions taken while attempting it stay. Does the engine keep them?
- Decision: No. The engine reverses an illegal action by restoring a copy of the whole game taken before it began (`Game::roll_back`), libraries, their order and the random generator included. Decided by the user, Oct 21. The user first accepted it as it is, a deviation, while no fully supported card was known to reach the difference (a first scan of every cost, keyword cost and mana ability of the 31,293 fully supported cards then found none), with the plan to fix it when a card that can reach it becomes supported. The scan test written after that found supported cards that do; told of the 16 it had found by then, the user chose that evening to follow 733.1 exactly: item `reversal-library-exceptions` (`docs/agents/items/reversal-library-exceptions.md`; recorded in `docs/agents/verification-plan.md`) will make the reversal keep the library actions 733.1 names, which covers the six the scan has found since too (Swans of Bryn Argoll, Lich's Mirror, Don't Blink, Library of Leng, Nephalia Academy; Stunning Reversal, newly supported on Oct 22). Until it lands, the engine still reverses them. The cards that reach the difference, by making something that happens in the middle of a payment a library action when the action then turns out illegal (22): a card a payment moves goes to a library instead (Blightsteel Colossus, Darksteel Colossus, Legacy Weapon and Progenitus, sacrificed or discarded; Nexus of Fate, discarded; Gravebane Zombie, Nissa's Chosen and Pulmonic Sliver, sacrificed; Wheel of Sun and Moon, the enchanted player's cards); damage dealt for mana (Shivan Reef's to its controller; the damage Overabundance's triggered mana ability deals to the player who tapped a land) becomes a mill or an exile from a library (Angel of Suffering, Crumbling Sanctuary, Bone Mask's shield; The Mindskinner only for Overabundance's damage to an opponent of its controller); life a mana ability gains (Pristine Talisman) becomes a draw (Lich, Nefarious Lich); life paid (a cost's "Pay N life", Mana Confluence's mana ability, Phyrexian mana) becomes exiling that many cards from the top of the payer's library (Ashiok, Wicked Manipulator, a rule implemented in code, `kw::pay_life_instead`); through what other replacement effects and rules make of a payment: damage dealt to Swans of Bryn Argoll (a player's damage that Pariah, Pariah's Shield, With Great Power . . . or Ancient Adamantoise deals to a permanent instead) becomes a draw for the source's controller (Swans of Bryn Argoll); a loss (Nefarious Lich's for damage its controller's graveyard can't pay for, or Enduring Angel's) becomes shuffling a hand, a graveyard and permanents into a library and drawing seven (Lich's Mirror), or drawing seven (Stunning Reversal); a creature an "until" exile returns in the middle of a payment (when Banisher Priest, say, is sacrificed for mana) is shuffled into its owner's library instead (Don't Blink); and a discard an effect makes in the middle of a payment made outside casting a spell and activating an ability (a cost paid as an ability resolves, an attack or block cost, a special action's cost) goes on top of a library instead (Library of Leng, Nephalia Academy): Heightened Awareness's "As ~ enters, discard your hand" (or Indominus Rex, Alpha's discard) for a card an "until" exile returns there; whether such a payment can return one before something in it fails is more than the scan follows, so these two are counted. (A cost's discard is never caused by an effect, wherever it's paid, as Library of Leng's ruling has it: `discard_rules::DISCARDED_BY_EFFECT`. Nephalia Academy would also apply to a cost's discard paid as an opponent's ability resolves, and no supported payment has one that something can fail after.) For example, casting Fling with Ashnod's Altar, Darksteel Colossus and a Mountain sacrifices the Colossus to the Altar for mana, which shuffles it into its owner's library; then no creature is left to sacrifice for Fling, and the engine reverses the shuffle with the cast, which 733.1 says stays. A test checks all this on every run, `tests/cr/r733_001_reversal_reachability.rs`: it scans the compiled abilities of every fully supported card (31,456 on Oct 21: playable cards legal in some format, sticker sheets aside, Arena-only cards and cards whose listed printing is an MTGO one included) and fails, naming the card and the ability, on any way to reach the difference not listed here, and on one listed here it no longer finds. What it checks: (a) A cost's library actions are paid where nothing can fail afterwards. The engine pays the mana first, checks that each other part can be paid, and pays the parts that move cards from a library to a public zone after the others (601.2h); nothing can fail after an activation cost or a cost paid as an ability resolves is paid, and the library parts are paid after every part that can fail (the parts the test says can't fail once checked are paid in a game with what they need gone). One of several costs, and a cost an effect has a player pay, are checked as payments of their own; an activation cost is checked again, in a game, with the parts each activation cost modifier adds after it (Drought, Brutal Suppression, Carth the Lion, Thran Portal) and with each alternative cost offered for it (the {0} equip, cycling and power-up costs of Forge Anew, Bruenor Battlehammer, Kíli the Resourceful, Gavi, Nest Warden, New Perspectives and Advancing the Spirit; Heart of Kiran's loyalty counter). The library-touching costs are all of those kinds: exiling the top cards of a library (Arc-Slogger, Royal Herbalist, Seasoned Tactician, Whirling Catapult, Thought Lash), milling (Rot Farm Skeleton, Sinister Concoction; and Millikin, Deranged Assistant, The Warring Triad, whose mill costs make their abilities not mana abilities, 605.1a), putting cards on a library (Penance, Leashling, Hidden Retreat from hand; Battlefield Scrounger from graveyard; Timestream Navigator itself), and costs paid as an ability resolves (Gurzigost, Anurid Scavenger, Deep Spawn, Tainted Specter; Decoy Gambit and Research // Development, which an opponent pays by having you draw a card; the cumulative upkeep of Thought Lash and Psychic Vortex). No spell's cost (a cost written in a rule implemented in code, such as casting a card from a graveyard by discarding, included), keyword cost, attack or block cost or special action's cost touches a library: a spell's cost is followed by the check that the spell still has the keywords that paid part of it, so one would be reachable. No cost has two parts that take cards from a library either: the engine checks each part against the whole library, not jointly, so with one card left "exile the top card of your library, mill a card" would be paid in part. (b) No mana ability (activated, triggered, or a delayed triggered mana ability an effect creates) and no special action that can be taken during a mana payment moves, shuffles or reveals library cards (the mana abilities of Rosheen, Roaring Prophet; Sacellum Godspeaker; and Metalworker reveal cards from a hand); the automatic payment uses only mana abilities whose costs are tapping, sacrificing, life and counters. Triggers on spending the mana or on casting the next spell (Gilanra, Caller of Wirewood draws; Lapis Orb of Dragonkind scries 2; Brass Infiniscope's delayed trigger draws) and Shaun & Rebecca's reflexive mill are ordinary triggered abilities, which 733.1 says don't trigger from an undone action; abilities that add mana and draw a card (Chromatic Sphere, the Eggs such as Skycloud Egg) or mill (Millikin, Deranged Assistant, Charmed Pendant) aren't mana abilities (605.1a), so they can't be activated while an action is attempted. (c) A cost part that searches or shuffles a library, or reveals library cards or moves them to a public zone, is paid after every other part (601.2h); no supported cost searches, shuffles or reveals a library. (d) No ability can be activated from a library (which would reveal the card, 602.2a); a keyword's cost is checked where the abilities it stands for pay it; a card cast from the top of a library goes to the stack, which may be reversed, and the next card isn't revealed while it's cast (401.5). (e) Which events can happen in the middle of a payment is computed over all the fully supported cards together: those every payment can cause (a permanent sacrificed, a card discarded, damage dealt to a player by a land tapped for mana, life paid, gained or lost, counters, tokens without abilities, mana), and those something supported causes there: a cost or a mana ability; a replacement effect that applies there, and what its action does (a redirection deals a player's damage to a permanent instead; Nefarious Lich and Enduring Angel make a player lose the game, and Enduring Angel transforms; Kill-Suit Cultist's shield destroys a creature dealt damage); the prevention and regeneration shields an effect creates, which are replacement effects too; and a rule the engine applies there: each mana ability's activation ends with `flush_events`, which begins with `until::check_untils`, so a card exiled "until [a permanent] leaves the battlefield" (Banisher Priest, Banishing Light and the other supported cards that exile until then) returns as soon as that permanent is sacrificed for mana (onto the battlefield; a card exiled from a library would go back into it, a library action, and no supported card does that). The events this adds today: a card or a token with abilities put onto the battlefield, a permanent destroyed, a permanent transformed, damage dealt to a permanent, a player losing the game, a card discarded because of an effect (`KNOWN_EVENTS` in the test, which fails when they change). Each replacement effect that does a library action and applies to an event that can happen there is listed above; what follows a library action isn't followed further (that library action is listed); a permanent that enters there wasn't cast, so "if it was kicked" doesn't hold for it (Monstrous War-Leech, Urborg Lhurgoyf). An event the test doesn't follow (a card cast, the game restarted, an unclassified keyword action) that a payment, a mana ability or such a replacement effect causes fails it and can't be listed. Every rule implemented in code that a supported card turns on by name, and every custom effect performed there, is classified in the test (`custom_rule`, `custom_effect_library`; the keywords' own rules in code were read by hand). Nothing else acts in the middle of a payment: triggered abilities wait (and are dropped with an undone action), and state-based actions aren't checked. Cards legal in no format are in no card pool (`docs/DEFERRED.md`) and outside the scan: Panglacial Shinobi (library ninjutsu: activated from a library, which reveals the card, 602.2a, with a cost that shuffles a creature into a library) and The Secret Lair aren't supported; Under-Construction Skyscraper is fully supported, and its LEVEL 8+ mana ability scries, which would reach the difference (the test checks that the scan reports it), but no card pool (random decks, the properties, the API's) deals it.
- Alternatives: Keep 733.1's exceptions: a reversal that knows which actions happened during the attempt and replays the library actions (moves, shuffles with the generator's state, reveals) onto the restored game. That's the fix the user chose on Oct 21 (item `reversal-library-exceptions`); with it, the cards above, and custom cards such as a triggered mana ability that draws or a mana ability that shuffles, keep their library actions.
- Why: Restoring the whole game is simple and exact for everything else 733.1 reverses, and with the cards known when the user first accepted the entry the result was the same. The plan was to fix it once a card could reach it; the scan found such cards that day, and the user chose to fix it (item `reversal-library-exceptions`). It stays a deviation until that lands; meanwhile the scan test keeps the list of cards that reach it exact.
- Affects: the 22 cards above (`KNOWN_REACHABLE` in the scan test), in a game where one applies in the middle of a payment of an action that then turns out illegal (Swans of Bryn Argoll with a redirection, Lich's Mirror or Stunning Reversal with Nefarious Lich or Enduring Angel, Don't Blink with an "until" exile, Library of Leng and Nephalia Academy with a card that discards as it enters returned by one); custom cards with a triggered mana ability that moves library cards, or a mana ability that shuffles or reveals one.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/game.rs` (`roll_back`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/casting.rs` (`cast_with_option`, `activate_ability`, `pay_total_cost_ordered`, `cost_part_pays_last`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/until.rs` (`check_untils`, which acts in the middle of a payment).
- Tests: `tests/cr/r733_001_library_exceptions.rs` (`library_costs_are_paid_after_every_other_cost`, `a_reversed_action_also_reverses_a_draw_made_while_attempting_it`); `tests/cr/r733_001_reversal_reachability.rs` (`every_fully_supported_card_is_scanned_for_ways_to_reach_the_exceptions`, `the_scan_reports_each_way_to_reach_the_exceptions`, `the_scan_reports_nested_payments_rules_in_code_and_cost_modifiers`, `the_scan_follows_what_replacement_effects_and_rules_cause_in_the_middle_of_a_payment`, `the_cost_parts_the_scan_says_cant_fail_dont_fail_in_a_game`, `a_reversed_cast_reverses_a_card_shuffled_into_a_library_by_a_replacement_effect`, `a_reversed_cast_reverses_a_mill_a_replacement_effect_made_of_a_lands_damage`, `a_reversed_cast_reverses_cards_exiled_instead_of_paying_life_for_mana`, `a_reversed_cast_reverses_a_draw_a_redirected_lands_damage_causes`, `a_reversed_cast_reverses_what_lichs_mirror_does_with_a_loss_in_the_middle_of_a_payment`, `a_reversed_cast_reverses_a_creature_shuffled_into_a_library_as_an_exile_ends`, `a_card_cast_from_the_top_of_a_library_goes_back_when_the_cast_is_reversed`).

## reversal-undoes-mana-abilities: Reversing an illegal action also reverses the mana abilities activated while attempting it
- Kind: policy
- Status: decided
- Rules: 733.1 "... Each player may also reverse any legal mana abilities that player activated while making the illegal play, unless mana from those abilities or from any triggered mana abilities they caused to trigger was spent on another mana ability that wasn’t reversed. ..."
- Question: Reversing a mana ability activated while attempting an illegal action is the player's choice. Does the engine reverse them?
- Decision: Yes, always: the whole game is restored to the moment before the action was proposed (`Game::roll_back`), so the mana abilities the engine activated to pay the cost (601.2g, `automatic-mana-payment`) are reversed with it: their sources untap, sacrificed sources return, and the mana they made is gone. Mana abilities a player activates with priority before proposing the action aren't part of it and stay.
- Alternatives: Ask the player which mana abilities to keep (the mana stays in the pool, and the costs stay paid).
- Why: The engine, not the player, chooses the mana abilities activated during a payment, so undoing its choices leaves the player where they decided to be; a player who wants the mana anyway can activate the abilities with priority first.
- Affects: every reversed cast or activation that paid mana by activating mana abilities, e.g. a sacrificed Lotus Petal or Treasure, which returns to the battlefield.
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/game.rs` (`roll_back`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/casting.rs` (`cast_with_option`, `activate_ability`).
- Tests: `tests/cr/r733_001_library_exceptions.rs` (`a_reversed_action_also_reverses_a_draw_made_while_attempting_it`, `a_reversed_cast_reverses_its_mana_abilities`).

## more-loyalty-activations-add-up: "You may activate ~'s loyalty ability two more times this turn" adds to the once of CR 606.3
- Kind: interpretation
- Status: decided
- Rules: 606.3 "A player may activate a loyalty ability of a permanent they control any time they have priority and the stack is empty during a main phase of their turn, but only if no player has previously activated a loyalty ability of that permanent that turn."
- Question: Comet, Stellar Pup's "you may activate Comet's loyalty ability two more times this turn" and Kaito-style "twice this turn rather than only once" both change the limit of 606.3. Do several such effects add up?
- Decision: "N more times" adds N activations of that permanent this turn, on top of the usual one and of other "more times" effects (like The Chain Veil's one more); "twice (N times) each turn rather than only once" sets the limit, and several such effects don't add up (the greatest applies), as before.
- Alternatives: Read "two more times" as "three times this turn rather than only once" (no stacking with other effects).
- Why: "More" counts from what the player may already do, as The Chain Veil's ruling reads its "once more"; "rather than only once" replaces the limit itself.
- Affects: Comet, Stellar Pup (and any "N more times" effect).
- Code: `crates/mtg-engine/src/kw/game_rules_4.rs` (`MORE_LOYALTY`, `extra_loyalty_activations`); `crates/mtg-engine/src/activation_costs.rs` (`loyalty_activations_per_turn`).
- Tests: `tests/cards/game_rules_4.rs` (`comet_rolls_a_six_and_activates_two_more_times`).
