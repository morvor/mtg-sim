# ruling-conflicts-7: "for each player, choose target permanent that player controls" when a player controls none

Run at effort max, with an independent investigator subagent and a separate adversarial skeptic subagent, as in ruling-conflicts-1. The user's standing rule: the CR and rulings should not contradict, and apparent conflicts are investigated before the user decides.

**Raised by:** rulings-P138 (landed b6fb0f3c). The Vaevictis ruling is left OPEN.

**The conflict.**
- Vaevictis Asmadi, the Dire (Oracle: "Whenever Vaevictis Asmadi attacks, for each player, choose target permanent that player controls. Those players sacrifice those permanents. ..."). Ruling (2018-07-13): "If any player controls no permanents, Vaevictis's ability won't have sufficient legal targets, so it's removed from the stack without any effect."
- The engine (src/per_player_targets.rs) gives a player with no legal choice no target and still puts the ability on the stack. That policy cites the Sylvan Primordial ruling (2014-02-01): "If an opponent has any legal permanents to target, you must target one of them."
- Mass Mutiny ("up to one target"), ruling (2012-06-01): "You can cast Mass Mutiny even if an opponent doesn't control any creatures. You simply won't choose a target for that opponent."
- CR 601.2c: "... In some cases, the number of targets will be defined by the spell's text. Once the number of targets the spell has is determined, that number doesn't change, even if the information used to determine the number of targets does." CR 603.3d: "... If a choice is required when the triggered ability goes on the stack but no legal choices can be made for it, or if a rule or a continuous effect otherwise makes the ability illegal, the ability is simply removed from the stack." Also check 115.1, 115.6, 601.2c in full, and 115.10-ish rules on "for each" targets.

**Survey.** Find every card with "for each [player/opponent], [choose] target ... that player controls" without "up to" (Sylvan Primordial, Blatant Thievery, Chaos Is My Plaything, Turf War, Guff Rewrites History, Frenzied Gorespawn, Vaevictis, and others). Collect every ruling on what happens when one player has no legal target: removed from the stack, fewer targets, or can't be cast. Group them by date and by spell versus triggered ability. For a spell, 601.2c's "can't be cast" may apply instead of 603.3d's removal.

**Outcome.**
- **The rulings and CR agree** on a rule (likely: a required target for each player means a spell can't be cast and a triggered ability is removed from the stack when any player has no legal target):
  - fix per_player_targets.rs generally;
  - cite the rulings;
  - add a register entry if a judgment remains;
  - report.
- **They contradict:** change nothing, and report both readings with every source quoted verbatim with dates for the user to decide.
- Either way, give the per-player required-target policy a register entry (the P138 reviewer found it has none).
- Follow worker.md's one-item-per-session rule and notify the coordinator when it lands.

## STATUS

Investigated and implemented (Nov 12) by an independent investigator; the adversarial skeptic's review follows separately. **Conclusion: A, reconciled.** The CR and every ruling on the question agree: a required target "for each player" (no "up to") is one target for each player the phrase names, the number of targets the text defines (CR 601.2c). If any of those players has no legal target, the spell can't be cast (CR 601.2) and a triggered ability is removed from the stack (CR 603.3d), as Vaevictis Asmadi, the Dire's ruling says. Sylvan Primordial's ruling only says a target must be chosen for an opponent who has one; it is silent about an opponent who has none, and its history shows what it was for. The engine's earlier reading, which skipped a player with no legal target, had no source. It is fixed generally, with tests; the provisional register entry is `per-player-required-targets` (docs/interpretations/stack-casting-costs.md).

**Finding.**
- **Reconciled:** Sylvan Primordial's "If an opponent has any legal permanents to target, you must target one of them." (2014-02-01) took the place of the bullet the Gatecrash FAQ (2013-01-21) had given it with the rest of its cycle, whose other four cards say "up to one": "You can choose a number of targets up to the number of opponents you have, one target per opponent." That bullet would have let its controller choose fewer targets than its text requires; the new ruling says they can't. It says nothing about an opponent with no legal permanent, so CR 603.3d decides that case, as Vaevictis's ruling (2018-07-13) does for "for each player". Blue Dragon's ruling (2021-07-23) uses the same words for a single required target ("If an opponent has at least one creature the ability could target, you must choose a target"), whose ability is removed from the stack under 603.3d when no opponent has one.
- **Supporting:** Mass Mutiny's errata (Planechase 2012 FAQ, 2012-05-24) added "up to one" to "For each opponent, gain control of target creature that player controls" and paired it with "You can cast Mass Mutiny even if an opponent doesn't control any creatures. You simply won't choose a target for that opponent.": the words "up to one" are what allow no target for an opponent.
- **Not outdated:** the rules involved predate every ruling: 603.3d's removal (410.4 in the archived CR file dated 1999-04-23, the archive's file dates being upload dates: "If no legal choice can be made (or if a rule or a continuous effect otherwise makes the ability illegal), the pseudospell is simply removed from the stack"; today's words in 410.4 from the file dated 2009-02-01, numbered 603.3d since the CR effective 2009-07-11) and 601.2's illegal casting ("unable to comply", in the 1999 files too). 601.2c's "In some cases, the number of targets will be defined by the spell’s text" was added in the CR effective 2015-09-26 and 115.6 (zero targets) in the CR effective 2019-01-25 (as 114.6); both clarify and change nothing here.
- **No contrary source:** no ruling found lets a required per-player target be skipped. Searched: every ruling with "sufficient/enough legal targets", "must target", "no legal (target|choice)", "one target per", "won't choose a target", "for each opponent/player ... target", and "an/any/each opponent/player controls no / doesn't control".

**Readings tried.**
- **Adopted:** the text defines one required target per named player (601.2c), so a player with no legal target makes the requirement impossible: 601.2 (spell), 602.2 (activated ability), 603.3d (triggered ability). Every ruling above fits it.
- **The engine's earlier reading:** a player with no legal target gets none and the rest goes ahead, reading Sylvan Primordial's "If an opponent has any legal permanents to target" as implying that an opponent without one is skipped. Rejected: it contradicts Vaevictis's ruling and 603.3d's words, it would have made Mass Mutiny's errata unnecessary for casting it, and the Sylvan Primordial ruling was written to forbid fewer targets, not to allow them. The same conditional words on Blue Dragon describe a single required target, where skipping isn't possible.
- **"For each opponent" meaning "for each opponent who controls one":** no words support it, and Mysterious Stranger shows how a card says it ("for each graveyard with an instant or sorcery card in it").

**Engine** (commit 335530b). The earlier behavior had no register entry of its own; a code comment in `src/per_player_targets.rs` cited Sylvan Primordial's ruling for it. One test relied on it: `tests/cards/cards6_per_player_results.rs` `guff_rewrites_history_each_controller_casts_the_card_they_exiled` cast Guff Rewrites History in a three-player game with no target for a player who controlled only a land, and entry `guff-players-who-controlled` named that case ("no target was chosen for them"). The test now asserts that this setup can't be cast and runs its controller and turn-order checks in a two-player game (the player who controlled none of those permanents is still covered by `guff_rewrites_history_a_player_whose_target_became_illegal_exiles_nothing`); the entry now says a target is required for every player.
- `src/per_player_targets.rs`: `possible` (each named player can get the targets the slot requires for them, all different, CR 115.3: a matching, `assign`); `choose` returns None when they can't, never lowers a player's requirement for a lack of candidates, and offers each player only targets that leave the later players theirs.
- `src/stack.rs`: `each_slot_possible` (castability, modes, activation, `default_x`, splice) uses `possible` for per-player slots; `choose_targets_for` propagates the failure, so casting and activating fail (733.1) and a trigger is removed (603.3d) by the existing paths.
- `src/oracle/patterns/per_player_targets.rs`: "for each graveyard with X in it" names only the players with a card the target's words describe in their graveyard (`PlayerFilter::History(Condition::Exists(..))`), since it no longer gets the skipping it relied on.
- `src/ability.rs`: the `TargetSpec::per_player` doc comment.
- mtg-api offers what `Game::legal_actions` lists and drops what can't be completed on a copy (`legal::can_take`), so such a spell or ability isn't offered to agents.

**Tests** (`tests/rulings/per_player_required_targets.rs`, 10 tests, one of them a custom card's activated ability; cited rulings: Vaevictis × 2, Sylvan Primordial, Mass Mutiny, Molten Primordial; `interp!("per-player-required-targets")` in each). With only the pattern file reverted, Mysterious Stranger's first case fails (its ability was removed).

- Mysterious Stranger still fails the round-trip check (as before); its new player filter renders through the history-filter renderer.

**For the user.** Accept conclusion A (the provisional entry `per-player-required-targets`): a required target "for each player" needs a legal target for every player the phrase names, or the spell can't be cast and the triggered ability is removed from the stack (Vaevictis's ruling); "up to one" allows none. Recommendation: **yes**. Consequences worth knowing: Sylvan Primordial's ability does nothing if any opponent controls no noncreature permanent it can target; Guff Rewrites History can't be cast unless its caster also controls a nonenchantment, nonland permanent; Turf War's ability needs a land of its controller's.
