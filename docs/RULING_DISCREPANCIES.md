# Ruling discrepancies

Wizards rulings (Scryfall's `data/rulings.jsonl.gz`) that the engine doesn't follow in full,
or tests on another card than the one the data attaches them to. This is where a human
checks each finding and reports it to Wizards of the Coast; nothing here changes how the
engine plays. The user's decision of Nov 5: "It looks like some rulings are applied as a
template, and are therefore occasionally incorrect. ... We should still keep a detailed
account of all of the instances though, so they can be checked by a human, and reported to
Wizards of the Coast if erroneous."

**Kinds.**
- **template error**: the ruling's words contradict the card's own Oracle text, and it is
  (most likely) a copy of a similar card's ruling. Policy `ruling-contradicting-oracle-gives-way`
  (`docs/interpretations/sources.md`, decided by the user Nov 5): the Oracle text wins
  (CR 108.1); the parts of the ruling that agree with it stay cited.
- **copying error** (a kind decided by the user Nov 5): the ruling's words contradict the
  card's own Oracle text, and the same ruling as Wizards first published it (in the set's
  release notes) has words that agree with the card: the data's text differs from its own
  source, as though miscopied. It falls under the same policy,
  `ruling-contradicting-oracle-gives-way`.
- **misattached**: the ruling describes another card. Policy (decided by the user Nov 5):
  it is tested on the card it describes and cited under the card it is attached to (the
  `ruling!` macro needs that), with a comment naming the card it describes; the attached
  card is set up in the test when it can take part. Being misattached is never a reason to
  exempt a ruling.
- **inconsistent**: the ruling contradicts other rulings on the same question.
- **outdated**: the ruling is older than a dated rules change or Oracle change, but is
  still published.
- **narrower than stated**: the ruling is right in the situation it was written for, but its
  words state a rule that is wrong elsewhere.
- **misleading** (a kind decided by the user Nov 19): the ruling is right for the case it
  clarifies, but its words read as a general rule a player could apply where it doesn't
  hold; it should be reported all the same.

**Statuses.** *open for review*: not yet checked by a human. *decided*: checked by the user,
who decided how the engine follows it and whether to report it (say when). *reported*:
reported to Wizards of the Coast (say when and where). *confirmed*: Wizards changed or
confirmed the ruling (say how).

**Entries.** Each entry gives the card the ruling is attached to and the ruling, verbatim
with its `published_at` date (`- Ruling, <card> (<date>): "<text>"`, one line each); the
source it conflicts with, quoted and dated; the evidence (the likely origin, release notes,
rules changes); what the engine does; and where it is recorded (exemption files, tests and
register ids, in backticks; test paths are relative to `crates/mtg-engine`). Oracle texts
and rulings are quoted from Scryfall's bulk data in `data/` (`oracle_cards.jsonl.gz` and
`rulings.jsonl.gz`, both updated 2026-09-24, per `data/manifest.json`); an Oracle text
quoted without a date is the text of that snapshot. Rules text is quoted from
`data/comprehensive-rules.txt` (effective 2026-09-25); earlier rules text from the archived
Comprehensive Rules at https://github.com/pit142857/mtg-cr (commit 8351b94f; the files are
named by upload date and state their own effective dates). Release notes and update
bulletins were outside these sessions' allowed network hosts (magic.wizards.com,
web.archive.org); where an entry cites one, it was read from a mirror of the Wizards article
archive or from search results, as the work item named in the entry says. The mirror is
ormos (https://github.com/maxmakesmagic/ormos, Markdown made from Wayback Machine captures,
each file naming its capture; commit bd11dee2), whose archive ends in 2022.

**Keeping it complete.** `cargo test -p mtg-data` (`ruling_discrepancies_log_is_complete`)
and `cargo run --release -q -p mtg-tools -- rulings-coverage --check` fail when a rulings
exemption whose reason calls the ruling outdated, superseded, overridden, inconsistent,
misattached, a template error or a copying error, or cites
`ruling-contradicting-oracle-gives-way`, has no
entry here quoting that ruling under that card; when a quoted ruling isn't word for word one
of that card's rulings, or has another date; and when a file, test or register id an entry
names doesn't exist (`crates/mtg-data/src/discrepancies.rs`). To add an instance, add an
entry under its kind with every field and run `cargo test -p mtg-data`. A contradiction with
no clear explanation goes to the coordinator as a conflict case first.

## Template errors

### Homicide Investigator: "triggers for each of them"
- Kind: template error
- Status: open for review
- Ruling, Homicide Investigator (2024-04-12): "If Homicide Investigator dies at the same time as one or more other nontoken creatures you control, Homicide Investigator's ability triggers for each of them."
- Conflicts with: the card's Oracle text, printed so in Murders at Karlov Manor (2024-02-09), its only set: "Whenever one or more nontoken creatures you control die, investigate. This ability triggers only once each turn." Creatures dying together are one event for a "one or more" trigger (CR 603.2c: "An ability triggers only once each time its trigger event occurs. However, it can trigger repeatedly if one event contains multiple occurrences."), and the second sentence forbids a second trigger that turn.
- Evidence: the ruling is Midnight Reaper's, word for word with the name swapped: "If Midnight Reaper dies at the same time as one or more other nontoken creatures you control, Midnight Reaper's ability triggers for each of them." (2018-10-05; Guilds of Ravnica release notes, 2018-09-20), right for Midnight Reaper's per-creature trigger ("Whenever a nontoken creature you control dies, ..."). Homicide Investigator's other 2024-04-12 ruling is Midnight Reaper's companion ruling, in the same order ("Midnight Reaper's ability triggers when it dies if it's not a token.", 2018-10-05); it agrees with the card and is cited. The two are the only Murders at Karlov Manor rulings dated after 2024-02-02. Rulings on the same template say the opposite: G'raha Tia ("Whenever one or more other creatures and/or artifacts you control die, draw a card. This ability triggers only once each turn.") "If G'raha Tia and one or more other creatures and/or artifacts you control die at the same time, its last ability will trigger once (as long as it hasn't already triggered this turn)." (2025-06-06). No rules or Oracle change explains it: 603.2c reads the same in the CR effective 2024-04-12 and today. Investigated in work item ruling-conflicts-2, case 2 (conclusion D).
- Engine: one Clue when nontoken creatures you control die together, the Investigator among them, and at most one each turn, as the Oracle text says.
- Recorded: exemption `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P005.tsv`; register `homicide-investigator-triggers-once`, `ruling-contradicting-oracle-gives-way`; test `tests/rulings/r_p005_dies.rs` (`homicide_investigator_investigates_once_for_creatures_dying_together`).

### Rimescale Dragon: "all permanents" for "Creatures"
- Kind: template error
- Status: open for review
- Ruling, Rimescale Dragon (2006-07-15): "Rimescale Dragon’s last ability affects all permanents with ice counters on them, whether or not it put the ice counters on them."
- Conflicts with: the card's Oracle text, printed so in Coldsnap (2006-07-21), its only set: "Creatures with ice counters on them don't untap during their controllers' untap steps." CR 109.2: "If a spell or ability uses a description of an object that includes a card type or subtype, but doesn’t refer to a specific zone or include the word “card,” “spell,” “source,” or “scheme,” it means a permanent of that card type or subtype on the battlefield."
- Evidence: the ruling is Rimefeather Owl's ruling of the same day, word for word with the name swapped: "Rimefeather Owl’s last ability affects all permanents with ice counters on them, whether or not it put the ice counters on them." (2006-07-15), right for the Owl ("Permanents with ice counters on them are snow."). The Dragon was printed "Creatures" and its Oracle text has always said so; its only Oracle change is the reminder text ("snow source", Kaldheim, 2021). No other ruling in the data widens the card types its card affects. Its "whether or not it put the ice counters on them" agrees with the card. Investigated in work item ruling-conflicts-2, case 4 (conclusion D in part).
- Engine: only creatures with ice counters on them don't untap, whoever put the counters there; a Forest with an ice counter untaps, and so does a land iced while it was animated, once it isn't a creature.
- Recorded: exemption `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P096.tsv` (its "all permanents"; the "whether or not" clause stays cited); register `rimescale-dragon-creatures-only`, `ruling-contradicting-oracle-gives-way`; tests `tests/rulings/r_p096_lockdown.rs` (`rimescale_dragon_freezes_creatures_with_ice_counters_from_any_source_but_not_other_permanents`, `rimescale_dragon_lets_a_land_iced_while_animated_untap_once_its_not_a_creature`).

### Combat Celebrant: "untaps all of your creatures" for "untap all other creatures you control"
- Kind: template error
- Status: open for review
- Ruling, Combat Celebrant (2017-04-18): "Combat Celebrant's ability untaps all of your creatures, not just the ones that are attacking."
- Conflicts with: the card's Oracle text: "If this creature hasn't been exerted this turn, you may exert it as it attacks. When you do, untap all other creatures you control and after this phase, there is an additional combat phase. (An exerted creature won't untap during your next untap step.)" "All of your creatures" includes Combat Celebrant; "other" leaves it tapped.
- Evidence: the ruling's point, "not just the ones that are attacking", agrees with the card; only its noun phrase drops "other". It is in the Amonkhet release notes directly under the card's text (2017-04-14, per work item ruling-conflicts-2; not re-read here). Likely origin (an inference): Lightning Runner's Aether Revolt ruling, "You untap all creatures you control, including ones that aren't attacking." (2017-02-09), written for the same design without "other" ("If you pay, untap all creatures you control, and after this phase, there is an additional combat phase."). Combat Celebrant's ruling about exerting several Celebrants in one combat ("... all of your creatures are untapped only during the current combat phase ...", 2017-04-18) is consistent with "other": each Celebrant's ability untaps the others.
- Engine: untaps every other creature you control, attacking or not; the exerted Combat Celebrant, tapped by attacking, stays tapped.
- Recorded: exemption `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P056.tsv` (its "untaps all of your creatures"; "not just the ones that are attacking" stays cited); register `ruling-contradicting-oracle-gives-way`; tests `tests/rulings/ruling_contradicting_oracle.rs` (`combat_celebrant_untaps_all_other_creatures_but_not_itself`), `tests/rulings/r_p056_extra_phases.rs` (`combat_celebrant_untaps_all_other_creatures_and_adds_a_combat_without_a_main_phase`).

### Citanul Druid: "all players" for "an opponent"
- Kind: template error
- Status: open for review
- Ruling, Citanul Druid (2004-10-04): "In multiplayer games it affects all players."
- Conflicts with: the card's Oracle text: "Whenever an opponent casts an artifact spell, put a +1/+1 counter on this creature." CR 102.2: "In a two-player game, a player’s opponent is the other player." CR 102.3: "In a multiplayer game between teams, a player’s teammates are the other players on their team, and the player’s opponents are all players not on their team." Read as written, "all players" includes the Druid's controller and teammates.
- Evidence: the ruling is the multiplayer note of Gatherer's first batch of rulings (2004-10-04), which says "opponents" or "opposing players" on every other card whose ability applies only to opponents: Malignant Growth "In multiplayer games it affects all opponents.", Havoc "In a multiplayer game, it affects all opponents.", Invoke Prejudice "In a multi-player game, it affects all opposing players.", Aura of Silence "Aura of Silence affects all opponents in a multiplayer game.", Lifetap, Lifeblood, Powerleech, Underworld Dreams, Grim Feast, Roots of Life and Misers' Cage (all 2004-10-04), Kismet and Frozen Aether (2007-02-01). It says "all players" only where the ability applies to every player: Thieves' Auction "In multi-player games, it affects all players and players make choices in turn order.", Sphere of Resistance and Spellshock "It affects all players, including you.", Dueling Grounds and Crumbling Sanctuary "The ability affects all players." (all 2004-10-04). Citanul Druid's note is Malignant Growth's sentence with "players" for "opponents". Its evident intent, that every opponent's artifact spells count, is what the card's text already says, and the ruling has no other content. No version of the card's text says "player": it was printed in Antiquities (1994-03-04) as "Druid gains a +1/+1 counter each time opponent casts an artifact." (two-player wording, as quoted by third-party card databases in search results on 2026-11-05; no scan was checked, since the sites that carry printed texts, such as mtgjson.com and Gatherer, were outside this session's allowed network hosts), which the Oracle text renders "an opponent". Decided under the policy by register-decisions-nov5 (Nov 5).
- Engine: every opponent's artifact spells put a counter on it, in free-for-all and team games; its controller's and teammates' don't.
- Recorded: exemption `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P178.tsv`; register `ruling-contradicting-oracle-gives-way`; test `tests/rulings/ruling_contradicting_oracle.rs` (`citanul_druid_grows_for_each_opponents_artifact_spells_but_not_its_controllers`).

### Acolyte Hybrid: "you" for "its controller"
- Kind: template error
- Status: open for review
- Ruling, Acolyte Hybrid (2022-10-07): "If the target artifact has indestructible, it isn't destroyed this way and you won't draw a card. If it is destroyed but put into a zone other than a graveyard, you'll draw a card."
- Conflicts with: the card's Oracle text: "Heavy Rock Cutter — Whenever this creature attacks, destroy up to one target artifact. If an artifact is destroyed this way, its controller draws a card." The destroyed artifact's controller draws, not Acolyte Hybrid's controller ("you"), unless they are the same player.
- Evidence: the Warhammer 40,000 Commander Release Notes (2022-09-19; ormos mirror, Wayback capture 2022-09-19) give the card's text as the Oracle text has it ("If an artifact is destroyed this way, its controller draws a card.") and the ruling word for word under it, so the data copies its source faithfully. The ruling is a template used for "destroy, then if it was destroyed this way" cards, filled in with "draw a card" but keeping the template's "you": Noxious Gearhulk's (2016-09-20) "If the target creature has indestructible, it isn't destroyed this way and you won't gain life. If it is destroyed but put into a zone other than a graveyard, you'll gain life.", right for a card whose controller gains the life ("If a creature is destroyed this way, you gain life equal to its toughness."), and Kaya's Wrath's (2019-01-25); where the card gives the result to the permanent's controller, the template says so: Rampage of the Clans (2019-01-25) "If a permanent has indestructible, it isn’t destroyed this way and its controller won’t create a Centaur token for it. ...". What the ruling says of when a card is drawn (an indestructible artifact isn't destroyed this way, and no card is drawn; one destroyed but put into another zone was destroyed, and a card is drawn) agrees with the card.
- Engine: the destroyed artifact's controller draws a card, also when Rest in Peace exiles the artifact instead; no card is drawn for an indestructible artifact; Acolyte Hybrid's controller draws nothing for an opponent's artifact.
- Recorded: exemption `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P060.tsv` (its "you"; the rest stays cited); register `ruling-contradicting-oracle-gives-way`; test `tests/rulings/r_p060_misc.rs` (`acolyte_hybrid_draws_only_if_the_artifact_was_destroyed`).

### Sakashima's Protege: "only a creature" for "any permanent"
- Kind: template error
- Status: open for review
- Ruling, Sakashima's Protege (2020-11-10): "If Sakashima's Protege somehow enters the battlefield at the same time as another creature, it can't become a copy of that creature. You may choose only a creature that's already on the battlefield and entered the battlefield this turn."
- Conflicts with: the card's Oracle text, "You may have this creature enter as a copy of any permanent that entered this turn." (printed in Commander Legends, 2020-11-20: "You may have Sakashima's Protege enter the battlefield as a copy of any permanent that entered the battlefield this turn."), and its own ruling of the same day: "If Sakashima's Protege enters the battlefield as a copy of a planeswalker, it will enter with a number of loyalty counters on it equal to the loyalty printed in the lower right corner of the planeswalker. It won't copy the number of counters currently on that planeswalker." (2020-11-10).
- Evidence: the ruling is the template of the Clone-like cards, which copy creatures: "If Progenitor Mimic somehow enters the battlefield at the same time as another creature, it can't become a copy of that creature. You may choose only a creature that's already on the battlefield." (2013-04-15), with 24 more cards worded so in the data (Mercurial Pretender 2014-07-18, Glasspool Mimic 2020-09-25, Sakashima of a Thousand Faces the same day as this one, 2020-11-10, ...), with "and entered the battlefield this turn" added. The Commander Legends release notes (2020-11-06; ormos mirror) print the same words under the card, so the data copied its source faithfully. Its first sentence agrees with the card: an object entering at the same time hasn't entered yet as the choice is made (CR 614.12, 707.5).
- Engine: Sakashima's Protege may copy any permanent that entered this turn and is already on the battlefield, a noncreature one too (Glorious Anthem in the test), but not one entering with it.
- Recorded: exemption `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P067.tsv` (its "You may choose only a creature" sentence; the first sentence stays cited); register `ruling-contradicting-oracle-gives-way`; test `tests/rulings/r_p067_misc.rs` (`sakashimas_protege_cant_copy_a_permanent_entering_with_it`).

### The Destined White Mage: "second ability" for its third
- Kind: template error
- Status: open for review
- Ruling, The Destined White Mage (2025-10-02): "If a creature you control is dealt lethal damage at the same time that you gain life, it can't receive a counter from The Destined White Mage's second ability in time to save it."
- Ruling, The Destined White Mage (2025-10-02): "The Destined White Mage's second ability triggers just once for each life-gaining event, whether it's 1 life from Al Bhed Salvagers or 3 life from Balamb T-Rexaur."
- Conflicts with: the card's Oracle text, whose second ability is an activated ability that neither triggers nor puts counters: "Lifelink / {W}, {T}: Another target creature you control gains lifelink until end of turn. / Whenever you gain life, put a +1/+1 counter on target creature you control. If you have a full party, put three +1/+1 counters on that creature instead." The ability both rulings describe is the third, and the card's other rulings of the same day number it so: "In a Two-Headed Giant game, life gained by your teammate won't cause The Destined White Mage's third ability to trigger, even though it caused your team's life total to increase.", "If you gain an amount of life "for each" of something or "equal to the number" of something, that life is gained as one event and The Destined White Mage's third ability triggers only once." and "Each creature with lifelink dealing combat damage causes a separate life-gaining event. For example, if two creatures you control with lifelink deal combat damage at the same time, The Destined White Mage's third ability will trigger twice. ..." (all 2025-10-02).
- Evidence: the second ruling is Aerith Gainsborough's, word for word with the name swapped: "Aerith Gainsborough's second ability triggers just once for each life-gaining event, whether it's 1 life from Al Bhed Salvagers or 3 life from Balamb T-Rexaur." (2025-06-06), right for Aerith ("Lifelink / Whenever you gain life, put a +1/+1 counter on Aerith Gainsborough. / ..."), whose life-gain trigger is its second ability; the same note on the set's other cards names the ability by its own place (Minwu, White Mage's "last ability", Excalibur II's "first ability", both 2025-06-06). The first ruling is the "in time to save it" note of the cards that put a counter on a creature you control when you gain life (Heliod, Sun-Crowned, 2020-01-24: "If a creature is dealt lethal damage at the same time that you gain life, it can't receive a counter from Heliod's ability in time to save it."; Archangel of Thune, 2020-08-07: "... it won't receive a counter from Archangel of Thune's ability in time to save it."), with the ordinal of Aerith's notes ("If Aerith Gainsborough is dealt lethal damage at the same time that you gain life, it will die before its second ability would resolve. ...", 2025-06-06). The set's release notes weren't read (magic.wizards.com is outside this session's allowed network hosts, and the ormos mirror ends in 2022). Both rulings agree with the card in everything but the ordinal. Found in the review of work item rulings-P158.
- Engine: as the card says: the third ability triggers once for each life-gaining event, however much life was gained, and a creature dealt lethal damage as you gain life dies to state-based actions before the trigger is put on the stack, so it can't be its target.
- Recorded: exemption `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P158.tsv` (the two rulings' "The Destined White Mage's second ability"; the rest of both stays cited); register `ruling-contradicting-oracle-gives-way`; tests `tests/rulings/r_p158_triggers.rs` (`white_mage_triggers_once_per_life_gain_event`, `white_mage_cant_save_a_creature_dealt_lethal_damage_as_the_life_is_gained`).

### Taeko, the Patient Avalanche, Slinza, the Spiked Stampede, Syndicate Heavy and Sorin of House Markov: a hybrid mana symbol written as two mana symbols
- Kind: template error
- Status: open for review
- Ruling, Taeko, the Patient Avalanche (2024-11-08): "You don't choose a target for Taeko's last ability at the time it triggers. Rather, a second "reflexive" ability triggers when you pay {U}{B} this way. You choose a target for that ability as it goes on the stack. Each player may respond to this triggered ability as normal."
- Ruling, Slinza, the Spiked Stampede (2024-11-08): "You don't choose a target for Slinza's last ability at the time it triggers. Rather, a second "reflexive" ability triggers when you pay {1}{R}{G} this way. You choose a target for that ability as it goes on the stack. Each player may respond to this triggered ability as normal."
- Ruling, Syndicate Heavy (2024-02-02): "You may pay {W}{B} a maximum of one time for each extort triggered ability. You decide whether to pay when the ability resolves."
- Ruling, Sorin of House Markov // Sorin, Ravenous Neonate (2024-06-07): "You may pay {W}{B} a maximum of one time for each extort triggered ability. You decide whether to pay when the ability resolves."
- Conflicts with: the cards' Oracle text, which has one hybrid mana symbol where each ruling has two mana symbols: Taeko "Whenever Taeko attacks, you may pay {U/B}. When you do, target attacking creature can't be blocked this turn."; Slinza "Whenever Slinza or another creature with power 4 or greater enters, you may pay {1}{R/G}. A hybrid symbol such as {W/U} can be paid with either white or blue mana, ...": {U/B} is one blue or one black mana, where {U}{B} is two mana, one of each.
- Evidence: each ruling is a template filled in with the card's name and cost, the cost's hybrid symbol written as its two halves. Taeko's and Slinza's are the reflexive-trigger template ("You don't choose a target for [card]'s last ability at the time it triggers. Rather, a second "reflexive" ability triggers when you pay [cost] this way. ..."), which on a third Foundations Jumpstart card the same day keeps the hybrid symbol whole: Hurska Sweet-Tooth (2024-11-08) "You don't choose a target for Hurska's last ability at the time it triggers. Rather, a second "reflexive" ability triggers when you pay {G/W} this way. ..." for "Whenever you gain life, you may pay {G/W}. When you do, ...". The extort ruling is word for word the one on the 14 other extort cards, there with "{W/B}": "You may pay {W/B} a maximum of one time for each extort triggered ability. You decide whether to pay when the ability resolves." (Basilica Guards, Crypt Ghast, Syndic of Tithes and 11 more, all 2024-01-12). The same rendering is in Kellan, the Kid's ruling (2024-04-12), whose "Cease's mana cost is {1}{B}{G}" is Cease's {1}{B/G}: there it doesn't contradict Kellan's own text and the ruling's conclusion holds, so it stays cited (noted under "Not yet investigated"). No dated rules or Oracle change explains the difference: each ruling is dated before its card's first release (Syndicate Heavy, Ravnica: Clue Edition, 2024-02-23; Sorin of House Markov, Modern Horizons 3, 2024-06-14; Taeko and Slinza, Foundations Jumpstart, 2024-11-15), as release-notes rulings are, and the data has no other version of the cards' Oracle text. The release notes themselves weren't read (magic.wizards.com is outside this session's allowed network hosts, and the ormos mirror ends in 2022). Taeko's was found in work item rulings-residue-reflexive-refund, the others in its review.
- Engine: as the cards say: one blue or one black mana pays Taeko's {U/B}, two mana ({1} and one red or green) pay Slinza's {1}{R/G}, and one white or one black mana pays extort's {W/B}; the reflexive abilities then target as their rulings say, and extort is paid at most once for each trigger, as it resolves.
- Recorded: exemptions `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P130.tsv` (Taeko's "when you pay {U}{B} this way" and Slinza's "when you pay {1}{R}{G} this way") and `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P214.tsv` (the extort ruling's "You may pay {W}{B}", for both cards); the rest of each ruling stays cited; register `ruling-contradicting-oracle-gives-way`; tests `tests/rulings/r_residue_reflexive_refund.rs` (`taeko_targets_an_attacking_creature_after_paying`, `slinza_pays_its_hybrid_cost_with_two_mana`), `tests/rulings/r_s07_extort.rs` (`extort_is_paid_at_most_once_per_trigger_as_it_resolves`).

### Shredder, Shadow Master: "his last ability" for his first
- Kind: template error
- Status: open for review
- Ruling, Shredder, Shadow Master (2026-01-27): "If Shredder leaves the battlefield before his last ability resolves, the tokens will still enter as a copy of Shredder, using Shredder's copiable values from when he was last on the battlefield."
- Ruling, Shredder, Shadow Master (2026-01-27): "In the unusual case where Shredder becomes a copy of something else while his last ability is on the stack but before it resolves, the tokens will enter as a copy of whatever Shredder is copying."
- Conflicts with: the card's Oracle text, whose last ability makes no tokens: "Whenever Shredder attacks a player, for each other opponent, create a token that's a copy of Shredder tapped and attacking that player, except it isn't legendary. Sacrifice those tokens at end of combat. / Whenever Shredder deals combat damage to a player, that player loses half their life, rounded up." The ability both rulings describe is the first, and the card's other ruling of the same day about it numbers it so: "Although the token copies created by Shredder's first ability enter attacking, they were never declared as attacking creatures. ### Chaotic Transformation: "that player first chooses" the order of their own exiled permanents' processes
- Kind: inconsistent
- Status: open for review
- Ruling, Chaotic Transformation (2022-09-09): "If a player controls multiple permanents that were exiled this way, that player first chooses which target they’re considering, then they reveal cards until they reveal a card that shares a type with that permanent as it last existed on the battlefield, then they put that card onto the battlefield and shuffle. They then repeat this process for the remaining targets they controlled."
- Conflicts with: CR 608.2f: "Some spells and abilities include actions taken on multiple players and/or objects. In most cases, each such action is processed simultaneously. If the action can’t be processed simultaneously, it’s instead processed considering each affected player or object individually. APNAP order is used to make the primary determination of the order of those actions. Secondarily, if the action is to be taken on both a player and an object they control or on multiple objects controlled by the same player, the player who controls the resolving spell or ability chooses the relative order of those actions." Its Soulfire Eruption example has the caster "first choose which target they are considering". The rulings that have the caster choose, for actions the caster performs: Soulfire Eruption (2020-11-10) and Kaboom! (2022-09-09), in the same words: "... You choose the relative order of targets within that group. ...". The card: "Exile up to one target artifact, up to one target creature, up to one target enchantment, up to one target planeswalker, and/or up to one target land. For each permanent exiled this way, its controller reveals cards from the top of their library until they reveal a card that shares a card type with it, puts that card onto the battlefield, then shuffles."
- Evidence: the rule is older than the ruling, so the ruling isn't outdated. 608.2f entered the CR effective 2020-11-20 (Commander Legends). The archived CR texts have no such rule in the CR effective 2020-09-25, and today's text from 2020-11-20 on; the only change since is "converted mana cost" to "mana value" in the example (CR effective 2021-04-22). The rule came two weeks after the Commander Legends Release Notes (Eli Shiffrin, 2020-11-06; ormos mirror, Wayback capture 2020-11-07) gave Soulfire Eruption's ruling, which it generalizes. No Wizards article explaining it was found: ormos has no rules-change article for that release. The ruling is in the Dominaria United Release Notes as the data has it (Jess Dunks, 2022-08-26; ormos mirror, Wayback capture 2022-08-26 15:02:32), 21 months after the rule. It borrows the example's words ("first chooses which target they're considering") with a different chooser, and Kaboom!'s ruling, published the same day, kept the caster for a process the caster performs. The two agree when the caster controlled the exiled permanents. For two or more of another player's permanents, the rule has the caster choose and the ruling that player; the order matters when that player's library top is known, or when a permanent the first process puts onto the battlefield changes how a later card can enter. One reading reconciles them. 608.2f's "Secondarily" sentence covers an action taken on a player and their objects, or on several objects of one player; Chaotic Transformation's second sentence acts on each controller's own library, not on the exiled permanents. So the rule leaves the order of one player's repetitions open, and the ruling decides it, as 101.4c lets a player order their own choices. The CR allocates the order the ruling's way for the two per-permanent processes it has each permanent's controller perform: connive, 701.50c "If multiple permanents are instructed to connive at the same time, the first player in APNAP order who controls (or, in the case of a permanent no longer on the battlefield, last controlled; see rule 701.50b) one or more of those permanents chooses one of them and it connives. Then this process is repeated for each remaining instruction to connive." (with the same chooser since the CR effective 2022-04-29, first as 701.47d, so in force when the ruling was written), and explore, 701.44d, in the same words (since the CR effective 2025-04-04). Neither calls itself an exception to 608.2f, but neither has differed from it in play: every card that has several permanents connive or explore at once has its controller's own do so (Change of Plans, Lethal Scheme, Hakbal of the Surging Soul). Read with the exiled permanents as the objects considered one at a time (the ruling's own words), they contradict. Investigated in work item ruling-conflicts-10 (conclusion D: the user decides; the recommendation is the reconciling reading; upheld by the adversarial skeptic, who added the connive and explore rules). The user's judgment (Nov 19): "I agree with the conclusion on chaotic transformation, but I would mark this as a legitimate inconsistency between the CR and the rulings. Based on the wording of the ruling, it seems clear the intention of the card is for the controllers to pick the order, but I see the CR as generally disagreeing with that." So this is a genuine inconsistency between CR 608.2f and the ruling, to report to Wizards, not one the reconciling reading resolves; the engine follows the ruling as the card's intent.
- Engine: follows the ruling (work item user-decisions-nov19). The players' groups go in APNAP order; within one player's group, each exiled permanent's controller chooses which of their permanents' processes comes next, before each one (by default the order of the target slots: artifact, creature, enchantment, planeswalker, land). For actions the caster performs or that are taken on the objects themselves (Kaboom!, Soulfire Eruption), the caster chooses, as 608.2f and their rulings say.

## Misleading rulings

### Grudge Keeper: "only if they voted for none of those choices" when the opponent has several votes too
- Kind: misleading
- Status: decided
- Ruling, Grudge Keeper (2014-05-29): "If you have additional votes because of Brago’s Representative, and you vote for more than one choice, an opponent will lose 2 life only if they voted for none of those choices."
- Conflicts with: Erestor of the Council's ruling on the same words ("each opponent who voted for a choice you voted for" / "opponents who voted for a choice you didn't vote for"): "If an opponent has multiple votes, it's possible for them to count as both an opponent who voted for a choice you voted for and an opponent who voted for a choice you didn't vote for." (2023-06-16). Grudge Keeper's Oracle text: "Whenever players finish voting, each opponent who voted for a choice you didn't vote for loses 2 life." When both players have several votes (you vote red and green, the opponent red and blue), the opponent "voted for a choice you didn't vote for" (blue), as the card asks and Erestor's ruling says, but didn't vote for "none of those choices" (red).
- Evidence: the ruling describes an opponent with one vote: its premise is that you have the additional votes (Brago's Representative, "While voting, you get an additional vote.", printed with Grudge Keeper in Conspiracy, 2014-06-06), and for a single vote "voted for a choice you didn't vote for" and "voted for none of those choices" are the same. Grudge Keeper's companion ruling of the same day covers an opponent with additional votes only when all of them are for choices you didn't vote for ("If one of your opponents has additional votes because of Brago’s Representative, and that player votes for more than one choice you didn’t vote for, that player will lose only 2 life."), so the two Grudge Keeper rulings never meet the case of an opponent whose votes partly match yours. Erestor of the Council (Tales of Middle-earth Commander, 2023-06-23), worded the same way, has the newer ruling for exactly that case. No rules change is involved: 701.38d ("If an effect gives a player multiple votes, those votes all happen at the same time the player would otherwise have voted.") says nothing about it. Found in work item rulings-P078. Decided by the user, Nov 19 (work item user-decisions-nov19c): the ruling is right for the case it clarifies, an opponent with one vote, but reads as a general rule, so it is misleading and should be reported: "I think the grudge keeper ruling is just clarifying the case Where you vote for A and B and an opponent votes for A. In this case, a player might believe an opponent loses life, since you voted for B and they didn't. The ruling corrects this, saying that the opponent would only lose life if they voted for a choice that wasn't A nor B. (for example, voting for C or for B and D). However, we should still probably report Grudge keeper as misleading at the very least."
- Engine: an opponent is one "who voted for a choice you didn't vote for" if any of their votes was for a choice none of your votes was for, also when another of their votes matched yours; with you voting red and green and the opponent red and blue, Grudge Keeper's opponent loses 2 life (and Erestor's opponent both creates a Treasure and counts toward X). An opponent with one vote, as in the ruling, loses life only if their vote is for none of your choices.
- Recorded: no exemption: the ruling stays cited for an opponent with one vote; register `opponent-with-several-votes-counts-both-ways`; tests `tests/rulings/r_p172_combat_votes.rs` (`grudge_keeper_with_additional_votes`), `tests/rulings/r_p078_group_hug.rs` (`erestor_counts_an_opponent_with_two_votes_both_ways`, `grudge_keeper_counts_an_opponent_whose_several_votes_partly_match_yours`).

### Hellkite Whelp: a battle's "controller is the defending player"
- Kind: outdated
- Status: open for review
- Ruling, Hellkite Whelp (2018-10-05): "If a creature attacks a planeswalker or battle, that permanent's controller is the defending player."

(its second sentence left without a battle), Mogg Flunkies (2018-03-16) "... other attacking creatures don't have to attack the same player, planeswalker, or battle. ...". The revision's "that permanent's controller" is right for planeswalkers and wrong for every battle in a format: all of them are Sieges (37 cards; the data's only other battle type, Control Point, is on 2 playtest cards the CR doesn't define), whose protector is always an opponent of their controller, so their controller is never the defending player (when a Siege's controller attacks it, the ruling would make the attacking player the defending player). - Engine: a creature attacking a battle has that battle's protector as its defending player (CR 310.9d, 508.5); Hellkite Whelp attacking a Siege that P2 controls and P1 protects can target only P1's creatures, and attacking a planeswalker, only its controller's.

### Clarion Ultimatum: "you chose an Illusion token and find the split card Illusion/Reality"
- Kind: outdated
- Status: open for review
- Ruling, Clarion Ultimatum (2008-10-01): "If you find a card that isn't a permanent card while searching (for example, you chose an Illusion token and find the split card Illusion/Reality), that card remains in your library."
- Conflicts with: CR 111.4: "A spell or ability that creates a token sets both its name and its subtype(s). If the spell or ability doesn’t specify the name of the token, its name is the same as its subtype(s) plus the word “Token.” Once a token is on the battlefield, changing its name doesn’t change its subtype(s), and vice versa." An Illusion token is named "Illusion Token", which isn't a name of Illusion // Reality, so Clarion Ultimatum can't find that card for it.
- Evidence: the ruling was written for Shards of Alara, when such a token was named after its creature types alone (the CR current as of 2008-10-01, 216.1a: "If the spell or ability doesn't specify the name of the creature token, its name is the same as its creature type(s)."). The archived CR texts date the change: 111.4 of the CR effective 2021-07-23 still reads "its name is the same as its subtype(s)", and the CR effective 2021-11-19 (Innistrad: Crimson Vow) adds "plus the word “Token.”" (the archive has no version between them). Wizards' "Comprehensive Rules Changes" for Innistrad: Crimson Vow (Jess Dunks, 2021-11-10; read from the ormos mirror) gives this very interaction as the reason: "Sometimes, however, the name of an unrelated card happens to overlap with the name of a token, such as the Illusion half of the split card Illusion // Reality. ... Battle for Bretagard's ruling in the data states the new rule under an earlier date (2021-02-05: "Unless the effect that creates a token specifies a different name, the name of a token is the same as its creature types plus the word "Token". ..."), but the Kaldheim release notes (2021-01-22, ormos mirror) have it without the word "Token" ("... the name of a token is the same as its creature types. ..."), so it was reworded after the change and kept its first date. Only the example is outdated; the rule it illustrates still holds. Found in work item rulings-P191; dated in its review.
- Engine: a search for a card with a permanent's name finds only permanent cards to put onto the battlefield; a nonpermanent card with that name stays in the library (tested with a test permanent named Lightning Bolt). Tokens without a specified name are named "[subtypes] Token" (Goblin Offensive's ruling, cited).

### Singing Tree: "Changes creature's current power to zero" after an earlier pump
- Kind: outdated
- Status: open for review
- Ruling, Singing Tree (2005-11-01): "Changes creature's current power to zero (as opposed to giving it -X/-0, like before) but does not prevent raising it after the Tree has been used on it."
- Conflicts with: CR 613.4b: "Layer 7b: Effects that set power and/or toughness to a specific number or value are applied. Effects that refer to the base power and/or toughness of a creature apply in this layer." CR 613.4c: "Layer 7c: Effects and counters that modify power and/or toughness (but don’t set power and/or toughness to a specific number or value) are applied." Singing Tree's Oracle text: "{T}: Target attacking creature has base power 0 until end of turn." A creature given +3/+3 before the ability resolves keeps the 3: its current power doesn't become 0.
- Evidence: the ruling stated the layer rules of its time. The CR current as of 2005-10-01, in force when it was published, applied power and toughness effects in sublayers (418.5a): "Inside layer 6, apply effects in a series of sublayers in the following order: (6a) effects from characteristic-setting abilities; (6b) all other effects not specifically applied in 6c, 6d, or 6e; (6c) changes from counters; (6d) effects from static abilities that modify power and/or toughness but don't set power and/or toughness to a specific number or value; and (6e) effects that switch a creature's power and toughness." An earlier +3/+3 from a spell and the Tree's setting effect were both in 6b, applied in timestamp order, so the Tree did set the pumped creature's current power to 0, and a later pump raised it again, as the ruling says. The same sublayers stood through the CR current as of 2009-05-01. The M10 CR ("effective as of July 11, 2009", file CR-2009-07-08.txt) split them: "612.3b Layer 7b: Effects that set power and/or toughness to a specific number or value are applied." and "612.3c Layer 7c: Effects that modify power and/or toughness (but don't set power and/or toughness to a specific number or value) are applied." (archived CR texts, https://github.com/pit142857/mtg-cr, commit 8351b94f). Since then an earlier pump applies after the Tree's effect, as the Oracle text's "base power" says. The ruling's other words still hold: the Tree sets the power to 0 (no longer -X/-0), and a later pump raises it. Found in the review of work item rulings-residue-hate-attack-block (Nov 19).
- Engine: the Tree's ability sets the creature's base power to 0 (layer 7b), and modifications apply after it whatever their timestamps (layer 7c): an attacking Hill Giant becomes 0/3, and 3/6 with a Giant Growth cast before or after the ability resolves.
- Recorded: exemption `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P079.tsv`; tests `tests/rulings/r_residue_hate_attack_block_attack.rs` (`singing_tree_sets_base_power_and_later_pumps_still_count`, `singing_tree_after_a_pump_sets_only_the_base_power`).

### Overabundance: "deals one damage even if the land produces no mana"
- Kind: outdated
- Status: open for review
- Ruling, Overabundance (2004-10-04): "The ability deals one damage even if the land produces no mana when it was tapped to use a mana ability. This can happen with lands that produce a variable amount of mana."
- Conflicts with: CR 106.12a: "An ability that triggers whenever a permanent “is tapped for mana” or is tapped for mana of a specified type triggers whenever such a mana ability resolves and produces mana or the specified type of mana." The card: "Whenever a player taps a land for mana, that player adds one mana of any type that land produced, and this enchantment deals 1 damage to the player." Forsaken Monument's ruling agrees with the rule: "You “tap a permanent for {C}” only if you activate a mana ability of that permanent that includes the {T} symbol in its cost, and only if it produces one or more {C} as it resolves."
- Evidence: the rule entered the CR effective 2015-07-17 (Magic Origins) as 106.10a, in today's words but for the quotation marks; the CR effective 2015-03-27 has only 106.10, the definition of tapping a permanent for mana (archived CR texts). It has been 106.11a and now 106.12a since. The ruling, eleven years older, reads "taps a land for mana" by that definition alone (a mana ability with {T} in its cost, whatever it produces), as the CR did then. Found by work item rulings-residue-misc-1 (pass B, Nov 20).
- Engine: "tapped for mana" triggers need the mana ability to have produced mana: Cabal Coffers tapped with no Swamps adds nothing, and Overabundance neither adds mana nor deals damage; with a Swamp, it adds {B} and deals 1 damage.
- Recorded: exemption `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P099.tsv`; test `tests/rulings/r_residue_misc_1_mana.rs` (`overabundance_doesnt_trigger_for_a_land_that_produced_no_mana`).

### Indestructibility: the planeswalker uniqueness rule
- Kind: outdated
- Status: open for review
- Ruling, Indestructibility (2013-07-01): "Lethal damage, damage from a source with deathtouch, and effects that say “destroy” won’t cause a permanent with indestructible to be put into the graveyard. However, a permanent with indestructible can be put into the graveyard for a number of reasons. The most likely reasons are if it’s sacrificed, if it’s legendary and another legendary permanent with the same name is controlled by the same player, if it’s a planeswalker and another planeswalker with the same subtype is controlled by the same player, if it’s a creature with toughness 0 or less, or if it’s an Aura that’s either unattached or attached to something illegal."
- Conflicts with: CR 306.4 (no rule puts a planeswalker into the graveyard for sharing a subtype with another): "Previously, planeswalkers were subject to a “planeswalker uniqueness rule” that stopped a player from controlling two planeswalkers of the same planeswalker type. This rule has been removed and planeswalker cards printed before this change have received errata in the Oracle card reference to have the legendary supertype."
- Evidence: the ruling was right when written: the CR effective 2017-08-25 still has 704.5j "If a player controls two or more planeswalkers that share a planeswalker type, that player chooses one of them, and the rest are put into their owners’ graveyards. This is called the “planeswalker uniqueness rule.”"; the CR effective 2017-09-29 (Ixalan) replaced it with the legend rule's 704.5j and added 306.4's note (archived CR texts). Its other reasons (sacrifice, the legend rule, toughness 0 or less, an Aura unattached or attached illegally) still hold. Found by work item rulings-residue-misc-1 (pass B, Nov 20).
- Engine: two planeswalkers with the same subtype both stay (Jace Beleren and Jace, the Mind Sculptor, both legendary since the errata, have different names).
- Recorded: exemption `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P073.tsv` (the planeswalker part); tests `tests/rulings/r_residue_misc_1_leaves.rs` (`two_planeswalkers_with_the_same_subtype_both_stay`, `indestructible_permanents_still_go_to_the_graveyard_other_ways`).

### Swift Reckoning: "you need two other instant and/or sorcery cards" to cast it from a graveyard as though it had flash
- Kind: outdated
- Status: open for review
- Ruling, Swift Reckoning (2018-12-07): "If an effect allows you to cast Swift Reckoning from your graveyard, you need two other instant and/or sorcery cards in your graveyard to cast it as though it had flash."
- Conflicts with: CR 601.6a: "Once a player has begun casting a spell that had flash because certain conditions were met or that could be cast as though it had flash because certain conditions were met (see 601.3d), they may continue to cast that spell as though it had flash even if those conditions stop being met." The card: "Spell mastery — If there are two or more instant and/or sorcery cards in your graveyard, you may cast this spell as though it had flash. (You may cast it any time you could cast an instant.)" As the casting begins, the card is in the graveyard and counts itself (CR 601.3: "A player can begin to cast a spell only if a rule or effect allows that player to cast it and no rule or effect prohibits that player from casting it."); moving it to the stack (601.2a) ends the condition, and 601.6a lets the player continue to cast it as though it had flash. Graveyard Shift's ruling on the same situation (2022-04-29): "If an effect allows you to cast Graveyard Shift from your graveyard, you may cast it any time you could cast an instant if you have five or more mana values among cards in your graveyard immediately before you begin to cast it, even if moving it to the stack would reduce that number to four."
- Evidence: the ruling is the card's note in the Ultimate Masters Release Notes (2018-11-29; ormos mirror, Wayback capture 2018-12-02), right under the CR of its time: 601.3 in the CRs effective 2016-07-22 to 2019-08-23 read "A player can’t begin to cast a spell unless a rule or effect allows that player to cast it. If that player is no longer allowed to cast that spell after completing its proposal, the casting of the spell is illegal and the game returns to the moment before the casting of that spell was proposed (see rule 721, “Handling Illegal Actions”)." (archived CR texts; rule 719, then 720, in the first ones), and once the proposal is complete the card is on the stack, where one other card no longer meets the condition. The CR effective 2019-10-04 (Throne of Eldraine) didn't remove that sentence, as the note on this ruling under "Not yet investigated" said until Nov 20: it moved it to a new 601.4, "If a player is no longer allowed to cast a spell after completing its proposal (see rules 601.2a–d), the casting of the spell is illegal and the game returns to the moment before the casting of that spell was proposed (see rule 722, “Handling Illegal Actions”). It doesn’t matter if a rule or effect would make the casting of the spell illegal while determining and paying that spell’s costs (see rules 601.2f-h) or any time after the spell has been cast." (Eli Shiffrin's Comprehensive Rules Changes, 2019-09-27: "The old 601.3 rule started off by talking about whether it was legal to begin casting, and then all of its subrules went into details. But then the main rule continued on talking about what if it became illegal! I broke that out into its own rule and added clarity that becoming illegal after the proposal is done doesn't matter."), 601.5 from the CR effective 2022-06-10 and 601.6 today. The change that outdates the ruling is the CR effective 2022-04-29 (Streets of New Capenna), which added 601.4a in today's 601.6a's words (601.5a from the CR effective 2022-06-10; the archive's last CR, effective 2026-04-17, still has it as 601.5a). Jess Dunks's Comprehensive Rules Changes for that set (2022-04-20): "This new rule explains what happens in the very rare case that a spell loses flash during the spell-casting process. If that happens, the player may finish casting the spell as though it had flash." Graveyard Shift's ruling, in the same set's release notes (2022-04-20), applies the rule to a card cast from a graveyard that counts itself. Swift Reckoning's other ruling, also dated 2018-12-07 and already in the Magic Origins Release Notes (2015-07-08), is consistent with today's rule but doesn't decide the question (the Ultimate Masters Release Notes print it right before the "two other" note): "The number of instant and/or sorcery cards in your graveyard matters only as you begin to cast Swift Reckoning. Once it's cast, that number doesn't matter and will have no effect on Swift Reckoning resolving."; so are the rulings on the other conditional flash permissions, whose conditions the move to the stack can't change, from Hungering Yeti's (2014-11-24, "Hungering Yeti checks if you control a green or blue permanent only as you begin to cast it. Once you begin to cast Hungering Yeti as though it had flash, it doesn't matter what happens to the green or blue permanents you control.") to Bard's Company's (2026-06-29, "If you use the first ability of Bard's Company to cast it as though it had flash, you must control a Human only as you begin the casting process and put Bard's Company on the stack. ..."). One reading would keep the ruling: 601.6a's "(see 601.3d)" taken to limit "could be cast as though it had flash because certain conditions were met" to spells that would have flash only if conditions are met (601.3d's kind, Graveyard Shift's "This spell has flash as long as"), leaving a permission to cast a spell as though it had flash, such as Swift Reckoning's, to 601.6 alone. Not taken: the rule's words are those of the permission; a "see" reference points to a related rule rather than narrowing the words before it (601.3d is the rule on "certain conditions", 601.3b's are "certain qualities"); and the change note's "a spell loses flash" is loose even for Graveyard Shift, the card the rule came with: cast from a graveyard, it never has flash (its "This spell has flash as long as ..." functions only on the stack, CR 113.6e, effective 2021-06-18, and there it has four mana values). Investigated in work item ruling-conflicts-13 (conclusion B: outdated, upheld by its review).
- Engine: Swift Reckoning cast at instant speed from a graveyard (with flashback from Snapcaster Mage or Lier, Disciple of the Drowned) counts itself as the casting begins: with one other instant or sorcery card there it is offered and can be cast, with none it is neither; its list of legal actions and its casting agree. Graveyard Shift is cast the same way with four other mana values there.
- Recorded: exemption `docs/rulings-exemptions/rulings-P011.tsv`; register `flash-permission-conditions-judged-as-casting-begins`; tests `tests/rulings/r_residue_misc_1_stack.rs` (`swift_reckoning_cast_from_the_graveyard_counts_itself_as_casting_begins`), `tests/rulings/r_p011_misc.rs` (`graveyard_shift_counts_itself_before_it_is_cast`).
