# Interpretation register

Generated by `cargo run --release -p mtg-tools -- interpretations --write` from `docs/interpretations/*.md`; don't edit by hand.

Every rules judgment call the engine makes has an entry: an **interpretation** (the rules are ambiguous or silent and we chose a reading), a **deviation** (the engine knowingly differs from the Comprehensive Rules) or a **policy** (the rules leave it to a player or to procedure and the engine decides). **Provisional** entries are calls a reasonable judge could make the other way; they await review. Tests that pin an entry cite it with `interp!("<id>")`. What the engine doesn't support at all is a scope decision, listed in `docs/DEFERRED.md` instead.

## Provisional entries

- **room-door-unlock-timestamp** (deviation, [characteristics-layers](interpretations/characteristics-layers.md)): A Room door unlocked after the permanent entered has its static abilities' effects timestamped from the unlocking
  - Question: Unlocking a door gives the permanent its half's rules text, but neither the permanent nor any effect gets a new timestamp under 613.7 (613.7d-k don't list unlocking, and no CR rule mentions timestamps for Rooms). Read literally, 613.7a gives the door's static abilities the Room's own timestamp from when it entered: the door's ability is "on" the Room, and the only effect involved, 709.5's gate, is a static ability of the Room itself (the Room's timestamp) that removes text rather than creating it; unlocking gives a designation (709.5e/f), not a continuous effect, so 613.7b doesn't apply either. Two rulings from the Rooms' own release say the door's effects date from the unlocking. In which order do that half's static effects apply relative to effects that began between the Room entering and the door being unlocked?
  - Decision: A knowing deviation from literal 613.7a in favour of the two rulings: the static abilities of a half unlocked after the permanent entered have the later of the permanent's timestamp and the moment that door was (last) unlocked, in layers and in rule-modifying effects alike. Only the unlocked door is re-timestamped, not the whole permanent. A door locked (709.5g) and unlocked again is timestamped from the new unlocking (no ruling covers this; consistent with the rulings). A half that was cast (and so entered unlocked, 709.5d) uses the permanent's timestamp, and if the permanent itself later gets a newer timestamp (613.7f), the newer one wins. Scope: this applies to Rooms only; Classes (716.2a) and levelers (711.2a) keep the permanent's timestamp for their gated abilities, as the leveler ruling says.
- **put-onto-battlefield-blocking-retriggers-becomes-blocked-by** (interpretation, [combat](interpretations/combat.md)): A creature put onto the battlefield blocking triggers "becomes blocked by a creature" again
  - Question: Deathgazer: "Whenever this creature blocks or becomes blocked by a nonblack creature, destroy that creature at end of combat." Aetherplasm (blue) blocks it, returns itself to hand and is put back onto the battlefield blocking Deathgazer. The old trigger's "that creature" is the old object (CR 400.7), but does the new Aetherplasm trigger Deathgazer again?
  - Decision: Yes. The engine follows CR 509.3d: Deathgazer becomes blocked by the new Aetherplasm, its ability triggers again, and the new Aetherplasm is destroyed at end of combat. Aetherplasm dodges only the abilities that already triggered for the old object, and abilities of the 509.3c form ("becomes blocked"), which don't trigger again for an attacker that is already blocked.
- **history-counts-players-who-left** (interpretation, [players-multiplayer](interpretations/players-multiplayer.md)): "For each opponent who [did something] this turn" counts opponents who have since left the game
  - Question: An effect counts the players for whom something happened this turn ("for each of your opponents who lost life this turn", "for each opponent who was dealt damage this turn"). Is a player who has since left the game counted? The CR doesn't decide it: 800.4i's second sentence covers only actions taken by a player who left (being dealt damage or losing life isn't one), its first sentence (last known information about a specific player) supports counting without compelling it, and 102.1 and 702.125b support a strict present-tense reading of "opponent". The rulings disagree: about 15 cards' rulings from 2007 to 2024 count them (Spinerock Knoll 2007-10-01; Notorious Throng 2008-04-01; Sygg, River Cutthroat 2008-05-01; Cryptborn Horror 2012-10-01; Thought Sponge 2019-08-23; Chandra's Incinerator 2020-06-23; Belbe, Corrupted Observer, Rakdos, Lord of Riots, Tymna the Weaver and Ludevic, Necro-Alchemist 2020-11-10; Dunerider Outlaw 2021-03-19; Teysa, Opulent Oligarch 2024-02-02; Moonshae Pixie 2024-04-12; Kaito, Bane of Nightmares 2024-09-20); Gnoll War Band's (2022-06-10) says "Opponents who have left the game will not be counted when determining how many opponents have been dealt damage this turn."
  - Decision: Counted. `Value::EventsThisTurn` and `Value::EventsDuring` with `Tally::Players` count every player the matching events were about, in the game or not. Counts of players now ("for each opponent you have") are unaffected: they only see players in the game. Likewise a value taken over players of what each did this turn (`Value::OverPlayers` of a `Value::EventsThisTurn` or `Value::EventsDuring` tally, or of a threshold on one: "the greatest number of cards an opponent has drawn this turn", "for each opponent who drew two or more cards this turn") considers every player, in the game or not; other values over players ("the highest life total among all players") only see players in the game. A count of players selected by what they did or what happened to them this turn (`Value::CountPlayers` with a `PlayerFilter::History` tally or `PlayerFilter::DealtDamageThisTurn`: Ludevic, Necro-Alchemist's "if a player other than you lost life this turn", Antagonism's "unless one of their opponents was dealt damage this turn", bloodthirst's "if an opponent was dealt damage this turn") also considers every player, in the game or not; whether a player who left is an opponent is judged as they left (team membership doesn't change).
- **cast-grant-have-after-cast** (deviation, [stack-casting-costs](interpretations/stack-casting-costs.md)): "Spells you cast have [ability]" stays on a spell after it's cast and doesn't reach spells already on the stack
  - Question: "[Spells] you cast have [ability]" (Wort, Imoti, Party Thrasher, Eirdu, Herigast, Henzie "Toolbox" Torre, ...) is a continuous effect of a static ability (604.2, 611.3a–b); "[spells] you cast gain [ability] as you cast them" (Zinnia, Valley's Voice; Ashling, the Limitless) is the one-shot effect of 610.5, which entered the CR on 2026-01-16 (Lorwyn Eclipsed; absent from the CR of 2025-11-14). The engine models both as a grant made as the spell is put on the stack (601.2a); a "have" grant is dropped if it stops applying before the spell becomes cast (601.2i), a "gain ... as you cast them" grant is not (`StaticAbility::as_cast`). What happens to a "have" grant after the spell is cast, and to a spell already on the stack when the source arrives?
  - Decision: After 601.2i a "have" grant stays on the spell until it leaves the stack, even if its source leaves the battlefield or stops describing the spell; a spell put on the stack before the source arrived (or before it described the spell) doesn't get it. A granted ability that functions on the battlefield is carried to the permanent the spell becomes (400.7b). For 611.3d grants (Henzie's blitz, an alternative cost) that carry-over is what the CR says: the battlefield half of blitz (haste and the "draw a card" trigger) lasts until the end of the game. One edge case: if Henzie is sacrificed while the blitzed spell is cast (to a mana ability), the 601.2i recheck drops the whole blitz grant, battlefield half included, which 611.3d arguably keeps; no ruling covers it.
- **random-reselection-same-target** (interpretation, [stack-casting-costs](interpretations/stack-casting-costs.md)): A target reselected at random as the original one is unchanged and doesn't become a target again
  - Question: Grip of Chaos ("reselect its target at random") and Chef's Kiss ("reselect the targets at random") choose among all the legal targets, the original one included. When the random choice is the original target, does it become the target of that spell or ability again (ward, "whenever this creature becomes the target of a spell or ability")?
  - Decision: No. Only a target that changed to another object or player becomes a target of the spell or ability; one reselected as itself stays the target it was, as a target left unchanged when new targets are chosen (115.7d).
- **when-that-permanent-enters-as-it-is-created** (interpretation, [triggers-durations](interpretations/triggers-durations.md)): "When that permanent enters" after the instruction that put it onto the battlefield triggers at once
  - Question: Jailbreak returns a card to the battlefield and then says "When that permanent enters, return up to one target permanent card with equal or lesser mana value ...". Read as a delayed trigger, it would be created after the permanent entered and never trigger (CR 603.7a). How does it work?
  - Decision: By CR 603.12, "When that permanent enters" after the instruction that put it onto the battlefield is a reflexive triggered ability: it is checked as it's created and triggers if the card entered the battlefield during the resolution; it's put on the stack the next time a player would receive priority, choosing its targets then, and "that permanent" is the new object on the battlefield. If the card didn't enter the battlefield (Grafdigger's Cage; a replacement such as Containment Priest's), nothing triggers, and the ability doesn't wait for a later entry.
- **ice-cauldron-last-exiled** (interpretation, [zones-objects](interpretations/zones-objects.md)): Ice Cauldron's "last card exiled with ~" is the last card its first ability exiled with a permission to cast it
  - Question: Which card is "the last card exiled with" the Cauldron, and is it still that card once it's cast from exile?
  - Decision: The card its first ability most recently exiled (and gave the permission to cast); the mana can be spent only to cast a spell that was that card in exile as it was cast (the spell is a new object, CR 400.7, whose previous object was the exiled card). If an activation exiled nothing, or a charge counter came from another source (Coretapper, Power Conduit), the card exiled before stays the last one.
- **cyclopean-tomb-land-choice** (policy, [zones-objects](interpretations/zones-objects.md)): Cyclopean Tomb's controller chooses which of its lands loses its mire counters, among those with mire counters
  - Question: Several lands qualify: which one? And does a qualifying land that no longer has mire counters (they were removed some other way) use up an upkeep?
  - Decision: The Tomb's controller chooses among the qualifying lands that still have mire counters (all qualifying lands if none has any). A land stops qualifying once mire counters have been removed from it this way; removing none (it had none) doesn't count as removing one "with ~".
- **mdfc-back-face-up-enters-transformed** (interpretation, [zones-objects](interpretations/zones-objects.md)): A modal double-faced card cast or played with its back face up "enters transformed"
  - Question: The CR uses "enters transformed" only as an outcome (702.145b, 712.13a, 712.2) and never defines it. Corruption of Towashi: "Whenever a permanent you control transforms or a permanent you control enters transformed, you may draw a card." Does a modal double-faced card cast as its back face (712.11b, 712.13) or played as its back-face land (712.12; a Pathway, Hagra Broodpit) enter transformed? A modal double-faced card put onto the battlefield transformed does (the 2025-09-19 ruling), and is a transformed permanent once on the battlefield either way (701.27g, which needs no interpretation).
  - Decision: Yes. "Enters transformed" means "is a transformed permanent (701.27g) as it enters": a double-faced permanent entering with its back face up, whatever put that face up (an instruction to put it onto the battlefield transformed, casting it transformed or as its back face, playing its back face). Melded and merged permanents, face-down permanents and a card that isn't double-faced don't enter transformed.
- **self-shuffle-gone-library-still-shuffled** (interpretation, [zones-objects](interpretations/zones-objects.md)): "Its owner shuffles it into their library" shuffles that library even when it has left
  - Question: Blitz Hellion ("At the beginning of the end step, this creature's owner shuffles it into their library.") has left the battlefield when its ability resolves. It stays where it is; is its owner's library shuffled?
  - Decision: Yes. The engine follows CR 701.24c, which has an example of the same shape (Guile exiled in response to "shuffle it into its owner's library"): the object stays where it is and the library is shuffled. Shuffle triggers trigger.
