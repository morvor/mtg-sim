Tests that pin an entry cite it with `interp!("<id>")`. What the engine doesn't support at all is a scope decision, listed in `docs/DEFERRED.md` instead.

## Provisional entries

- **kept-for-counters-none-able-all-sacrificed** (interpretation, [decisions-policies](interpretations/decisions-policies.md)): A player who can't put Promise of Loyalty's vow counter on any of their creatures keeps none and sacrifices them all
  - Question: Under `counter-choice-excludes-what-cant-have-them` (decided by the user, Nov 20), a creature that can't have a vow counter can't be chosen for Promise of Loyalty's counter. What happens to a player whose only creatures can't have one (Melira's Keepers, creatures under Solemnity): do they keep a creature without a counter, or none?
  - Decision: None: the player puts no vow counter (they aren't able to), so no creature is the one they put it on, and "the rest", every creature they control, are sacrificed. The ruling's "They can't choose to opt out and sacrifice all of their creatures" forbids declining a counter the player is able to put; it doesn't keep a creature for a player who isn't able to.
- **that-opponent-fixed-as-it-triggers** (interpretation, [triggers-durations](interpretations/triggers-durations.md)): "That player" / "that opponent" of a trigger condition's "an opponent" is fixed as the ability triggers; an object-based "its controller" is read as it resolves
  - Question: Norn's Decree: "Whenever one or more creatures an opponent controls deal combat damage to you, that opponent gets a poison counter." Coveted Jewel: "Whenever one or more creatures an opponent controls attack you and aren't blocked, that player draws three cards and gains control of this artifact. Untap it." Before the ability resolves, the creatures change control (the Jewel's controller gains control of an unblocked attacker in response; another opponent does; only some of them do) or leave the battlefield. Is "that opponent" ("that player") the opponent whose creatures they were as the ability triggered, or their controller as it resolves? The same question for every "[object] an opponent controls [event], that player" trigger (Blood Seeker, Michiko Konda, Truth Seeker, ward), and, the other way round, for the object-based "its controller" / "that spell's controller" wordings.
  - Decision: Provisional (the user, Nov 19: "I am really not sure here"; research item ruling-research-2 found no source that decides it). A player-referring "that player" / "that opponent" / "they" whose antecedent is the trigger condition's "an opponent" (or "a player", or "enchanted player") is the player the condition named as the ability triggered: for Norn's Decree and Coveted Jewel the opponent whose creatures they were (each trigger is for one such opponent, `one-or-more-an-opponent-controls-per-opponent`, recorded as the event's player); for "[a permanent] an opponent controls [enters, is dealt damage, becomes tapped, ...]", "a land enters under an opponent's control", "a creature enchanted player controls enters" (Trespasser's Curse) and "a source an opponent controls deals damage to you", the controller the object had as the ability triggered; for "a spell or ability an opponent controls" (ward), the spell's controller as it targeted. A later control change (of all or only some of the objects, to you or to another opponent) or an object leaving the battlefield doesn't change who it is. An object-based "its controller" / "that creature's controller" / "that source's controller" / "that spell's controller" / "the controller of those creatures" is the object's controller as the ability resolves, or as it last existed (CR 608.2h), as the rulings on those wordings say: a spell stolen in response makes its new controller the one who may pay Frost Titan's {2} or is dealt Bonecrusher Giant's 2 damage, and a creature stolen after it dealt combat damage to the monarch makes its new controller the monarch (CR 725.2). The initiative's "the controller of those creatures takes the initiative" (CR 726.2), which the designation's own text words "that player takes the initiative" and the release notes "the first player takes the initiative", keeps the player the trigger was created for (a question for the user).
- **next-turns-upkeep-is-the-next-turns-first** (interpretation, [triggers-durations](interpretations/triggers-durations.md)): "At the beginning of the next turn's upkeep" is the first upkeep of the next turn; with none, it never triggers
  - Question: Two delay phrases name an upkeep: "at the beginning of the next upkeep" (Archangel Avacyn, The Eagles Are Coming!, Firion) and "at the beginning of the next turn's upkeep" (Heal, Mishra's Bauble and about 55 other cards). Which upkeep step does each mean when the turn has additional upkeep steps, and what if the next turn has no upkeep step?
  - Decision: "The next upkeep" is the next upkeep step to begin, whoever's turn it is, an additional upkeep step later in the same turn included (Paradox Haze: created during the first, it triggers at the second). "The next turn's upkeep" is the first upkeep step of the turn after the one the delayed ability was created in (turn N+1 of the game, whoever takes it): not an additional upkeep later in the creating turn, nor a second upkeep of that next turn. A skipped turn doesn't count: it's as though it didn't exist (500.11), and what's scheduled for the "next" turn waits for the first turn that isn't skipped (614.10a), so with the next turn skipped (Chronatog's "You skip your next turn.") it's the first upkeep of the turn after it. If the next turn has no upkeep step (Eon Hub's "Players skip their upkeep steps."), it never triggers: it doesn't wait for a later turn's upkeep (614.10a, "Anything scheduled for a skipped step ... won't happen").
