# Interpretation register

Generated by `cargo run --release -p mtg-tools -- interpretations --write` from `docs/interpretations/*.md`; don't edit by hand.

Every rules judgment call the engine makes has an entry: an **interpretation** (the rules are ambiguous or silent and we chose a reading), a **deviation** (the engine knowingly differs from the Comprehensive Rules) or a **policy** (the rules leave it to a player or to procedure and the engine decides). **Provisional** entries are calls a reasonable judge could make the other way; they await review. Tests that pin an entry cite it with `interp!("<id>")`. What the engine doesn't support at all is a scope decision, listed in `docs/DEFERRED.md` instead.

## Provisional entries

- **block-count-triggers-at-the-nth-block** (interpretation, [combat](interpretations/combat.md)): "Blocks N or more" and "becomes blocked by N or more" trigger when a block brings the number to N
  - Question: 509.3e says that effects adding or removing blockers "can also cause such abilities to trigger", but not when, or how often. "Whenever this creature blocks or becomes blocked by one or more black creatures" (Serra Inquisitors), "Whenever Rashka blocks one or more black creatures", "Whenever this creature blocks two or more creatures" (Lairwatch Giant), "Whenever equipped creature becomes blocked by one or more colorless creatures" (Hedron Blade): does an added block trigger them when the creature already blocks or is blocked by other creatures, and how many times when one effect adds several blocks, or when blockers were removed and others added (Sorrow's Path removes two blockers from combat and has each block what the other was blocking)?
  - Decision: As blockers are declared, the ability triggers once for each creature that blocks, or is blocked by, at least N matching creatures. After that, it triggers when a block added by an effect (or, for the "becomes blocked by" side only, a creature put onto the battlefield blocking) brings the number of matching creatures from below N to N, counted as of that block: an effect adding several blocks at once triggers it once (Sorrow's Path giving Lairwatch Giant two attackers; Mirror Match with Doubling Season putting two colorless tokens onto the battlefield blocking a Hedron Blade creature), more blocks beyond N don't trigger it again, and an attacker already blocked by other creatures triggers "becomes blocked by one or more black creatures" when a black one is added (unlike "becomes blocked", 509.3c). If the number fell below N because blockers or attackers were removed from combat, a later block that brings it back to N triggers it again: Sorrow's Path swapping Serra Inquisitors' black blocker for another black creature triggers it a second time. Removing blockers never triggers an "N or more" ability, since it can't raise the number. The "blocks" side never triggers for a creature put onto the battlefield blocking (509.4). "Blocks or becomes blocked by" is one ability with two trigger events; a creature can't be both blocking and attacking, so one block never triggers it twice.
- **copy-for-each-one-copy-event** (interpretation, [copy](interpretations/copy.md)): One instruction's copies of a spell are one copy event, also when it copies the spell "for each" of several objects or players
  - Question: An instruction that copies a spell once for each of several objects or players ("for each of those creatures, copy that spell. The copy targets that creature.", Feather; "for each other opponent, ..., copy that spell", Zevlor) copies it N times. Is that one event of copying the spell N times, which Twinning Staff makes N + 1 copies, or N events of copying it once, each made 2 (2N)? "Copy it twice", "copy it X times" and "copy it for each ..." (storm, replicate, CR 707.10d) are one instruction copying the spell several times, and no ruling or rule speaks to the "for each ..., copy" form.
  - Decision: One event. The copies one instruction makes of one spell for one player are one copy event, however it's worded, and Twinning Staff adds one copy to it (one for each Staff). Feather with two creatures chosen makes three copies with one Staff, four with two; Zevlor with two other opponents makes three with one Staff. Copies that different players make are their own events (Hive Mind, demonstrate, tempting offer: each player is a different "you"), as are copies of different spells and copies made by separate instructions or abilities (two storm triggers).
- **cast-if-able-mana-abilities-default** (policy, [decisions-policies](interpretations/decisions-policies.md)): A spell cast "if able" whose mana the pool doesn't cover is paid for with mana abilities by default
  - Question: A spell an effect makes a player cast "if able" during its resolution (Wild Evocation's "casts it without paying its mana cost if able") has a total cost with a mana payment (an additional cost such as Lash of the Balrog's "sacrifice a creature or pay {4}" with no creature to sacrifice, a kicker cost the player announced, a cost increase) that the mana in their pool doesn't cover. The ruling lets them decline to activate mana abilities, so the card stays uncast. What does a player who gives no answer choose, and what does declining do when the card could be cast without that mana?
  - Decision: The engine asks the player (a yes/no decision, "Activate mana abilities to pay for this spell?") only in that situation: not when the pool's mana pays the cost (it must be spent), nor when no mana ability could pay it (the card stays uncast). A default answer activates them and casts the spell. Declining reverses the casting (733.1), and the player casts the card again, as they must if able, with no mana ability (nor special action that adds mana) available: only costs the pool's mana pays can be chosen, so a player who announced a kicker cost and then won't produce the mana for it casts the spell unkicked (733.2); if a mandatory cost needs more mana than the pool has, the card stays uncast.
- **choices-for-each-player-made-in-turn** (interpretation, [players-multiplayer](interpretations/players-multiplayer.md)): "For each opponent, you [act on an object you choose]" is performed for each opponent in turn
  - Question: An instruction performed for each of several players or objects whose choices one player makes for all of them, not each player for themselves ("for each opponent, sacrifice a creature of your choice", "for each opponent, return a creature card from your graveyard to your hand", "for each opponent, ~ deals 2 damage to a creature of your choice"): are all the choices made first and then the actions performed together (101.4, 608.2f "in most cases"), or is the instruction performed for each player in turn, each choice made after the earlier actions?
  - Decision: In turn, in APNAP order of the players the instruction is for (objects: by their controllers): the chooser makes the choice for the first one, that action happens, then the choice for the next one (`simultaneous.rs`, module doc; `plannable`, `chosen_by`). So an object acted on for one opponent isn't offered for the next (it has moved; an answer naming it is invalid and falls back to the default), a choice made for an opponent isn't recorded as that opponent's for the later choosers (101.4b), and "the damage dealt this way" is each part's own damage. An instruction whose choices are made by the player it's for is planned: every player chooses in APNAP order, then the actions happen together. Planned whoever makes the choice: "choose one", a condition and what "[that player]" does, settled for every player or object before any of them acts (an option, a condition or a "may" can't be used up; an action that can't be planned is then still performed in turn); token copies (copying one object twice is possible); and searches for each of several objects (Krenko's Buzzcrusher, From the Ashes, Wave of Vitriol): a player who searches the same library again in the instruction continues the same search, one search for all those cards (701.23h), so a card found for one object isn't offered for the next, the cards found move together, and that library is shuffled once.
- **mixed-each-player-instruction** (interpretation, [players-multiplayer](interpretations/players-multiplayer.md)): In an "each player" instruction where one player's part is a process of several steps, the other players' single actions are still one simultaneous event
  - Question: An instruction performed for each player can give the players different parts ("each player: if it's your turn, mill a card, then draw a card; otherwise, lose 1 life"). When one player's part is a process of several steps, which 608.2f processes individually, are the other players' single actions (the life losses) still processed simultaneously, one event, or is the whole instruction "the action" that can't be processed simultaneously, so every part is processed individually?
  - Decision: Each process part is performed on its own (its events are checked for triggers before the next process begins, `perform_instruction`'s `sequential_step`), and the other players' single actions stay together: one event, checked once ("one or more" triggers trigger once for them, CR 603.2c).
- **opponent-with-several-votes-counts-both-ways** (interpretation, [players-multiplayer](interpretations/players-multiplayer.md)): An opponent with several votes can have voted both for a choice you voted for and for one you didn't
  - Question: "Each opponent who voted for a choice you voted for" / "each opponent who voted for a choice you didn't vote for" (Erestor of the Council, Grudge Keeper; Model of Unity's "you and each opponent who voted for a choice you voted for"): is an opponent who cast several votes, some for a choice you voted for and some for one you didn't, in one group, the other, or both? Erestor's ruling says both. Grudge Keeper's ruling about your additional votes, read on its own, says an opponent loses life "only if they voted for none of" your choices; it is written for an opponent with one vote (you have the additional votes), and the two rulings only meet when both of you have several votes.
  - Decision: Both, for every card with this wording: each group holds every opponent who voted at least once for a choice of that kind (`PlayerFilter::VotedLikeYou`). An opponent with one vote is in exactly one group, so Grudge Keeper's rulings hold as written for the situations they describe; an opponent with two votes, one for your choice and one for another, loses 2 life to Grudge Keeper (by analogy with Erestor's ruling), also when you have several votes too (you red and green, they red and blue).
- **damage-counters-put-by-different-players-are-separate-events** (interpretation, [replacement-prevention](interpretations/replacement-prevention.md)): Counters that damage dealt at the same time causes, put by different players, are separate events
  - Question: When the creatures dealing wither or infect damage to a creature at once are controlled by different players (Two-Headed Giant: a creature blocked by both defending players' creatures), are their counters one event, as the Vizier ruling's words read alone say, or one event per player putting them?
  - Decision: One event per player putting them: each source's controller puts its counters (CR 120.3d), and effects that care who puts counters (Vorinclex, Monstrous Raider; Nest of Scarabs; Hapatra, Vizier of Poisons) apply to each player's counters. Vizier of Remedies then puts one counter fewer for each player's counters.
- **infect-and-toxic-poison-counters-are-separate-events** (interpretation, [replacement-prevention](interpretations/replacement-prevention.md)): A creature with infect and toxic gives the poison counters of its damage and of toxic as two events
  - Question: Combat damage to a player from a creature with infect and toxic gives that player poison counters twice over: that many for the damage (120.3b) and its total toxic value (120.3g), both given by its controller at the same time. One counter-putting event, or two? It matters to "that many plus one" (Winding Constrictor), "half that many" (Vorinclex, Monstrous Raider) and "whenever ... one or more counters" triggers.
  - Decision: Two events: the damage's poison counters are put with the other counters the damage causes (`simultaneous-damage-counters-one-event`), then toxic's, one event for each player and controller (the ruling's single event for several creatures with toxic). The engine already did this before the entry was written; nothing in the CR or the rulings says either way.
- **compleated-is-ordered-with-other-replacements** (interpretation, [replacement-prevention](interpretations/replacement-prevention.md)): Compleated and another effect on the loyalty counters a planeswalker enters with are applied in the order its controller chooses
  - Question: With compleated and another effect on those counters (Doubling Season: "If an effect would put one or more counters on a permanent you control, it puts twice that many of those counters on that permanent instead."), which applies first?
  - Decision: Neither by rule: compleated is a static ability's replacement effect, not a self-replacement effect (614.15 is about a resolving spell's or ability's own effect), so the controller chooses the order (616.1e). Jace, the Perfected Mind with {U/P} paid with life enters with 6 loyalty counters (5 − 2, doubled) or 8 (5 doubled, − 2).
- **unless-any-player-pays-offers-everyone** (interpretation, [stack-casting-costs](interpretations/stack-casting-costs.md)): "[Effect] unless any player pays [cost]" offers the cost to every player, even after one has paid
  - Question: With several players able to pay ("unless any player pays {1}"), is a player still offered the cost after another has paid? A later payment stops nothing more; only the payment itself happens. The rulings of the "unless any player pays" cards give each player the option (Aether Rift's checks whether anyone paid only after every player was given it) but none speaks of a player after a payment, and no rule does.
  - Decision: Yes, as for "any player may [cost]. If a player does" (`any-player-may-after-a-payment`), which 118.12a makes the same template: each player in turn order, starting with the active player, is offered the cost; the effect is stopped once, by the first payment.
- **foretell-reduced-cost-of-the-half-cast** (interpretation, [stack-casting-costs](interpretations/stack-casting-costs.md)): A foretold split card's or adventurer card's foretell cost "equal to its mana cost reduced by {2}" is that of the half or Adventure cast
  - Question: Dream Devourer ("Each nonland card in your hand without foretell has foretell. Its foretell cost is equal to its mana cost reduced by {2}.") and Ethereal Valkyrie ("... exile a card from your hand face down. It becomes foretold. Its foretell cost is its mana cost reduced by {2}.") give a card a foretell cost from "its mana cost". In the hand and in exile, a split card's mana cost is its halves' combined (709.4b) and an adventurer card's is its creature's (715.4). When the foretold card is cast as one half of a split card, or as its Adventure, is the foretell cost that half's or Adventure's mana cost reduced by {2}, or the card's mana cost as it is in the hand and in exile? No rule or ruling speaks to split cards or Adventures foretold this way (found in rulings-P167).
  - Decision: The mana cost of the half or Adventure cast, reduced by {2}, determined as the spell is cast: Alive // Well's Alive ({3}{G}) for {1}{G} and its Well ({W}) for {W}; Beanstalk Giant // Fertile Footsteps's Fertile Footsteps ({2}{G}) for {G} and its Beanstalk Giant ({6}{G}) for {4}{G}. The card becomes foretold with the reduction, not with a cost fixed in the hand.
- **thaumaturge-counts-each-targeted-creature-once** (interpretation, [stack-casting-costs](interpretations/stack-casting-costs.md)): "Costs {1} less to cast for each creature it targets" counts each creature the spell targets once
  - Question: A spell that targets the same creature with two instances of "target" (Seeds of Strength, Epic Fight with both modes, a fused Turn // Burn): does "for each creature it targets" count that creature once (the creatures it targets) or twice (its targets that are creatures)? Battlefield Thaumaturge's ruling can be read either way: "{1} per target creature" counting each creature that is a target, which stays so "even if the spell targets a creature more than once", or counting each "target creature", which holds even when two of them are the same creature.
  - Decision: Once: the reduction is {1} for each creature that is a target of the spell, however many instances of "target" chose it (found in rulings-P088; the engine counted each instance until the review of that batch).
- **that-opponent-fixed-as-it-triggers** (interpretation, [triggers-durations](interpretations/triggers-durations.md)): "That opponent" of a "one or more creatures an opponent controls ..." trigger is the opponent whose creatures they were as it triggered
  - Question: Control of the creatures can change between the trigger event and the ability's resolution (the Jewel's controller gains control of an unblocked attacker in response). Is "that opponent" ("that player") the opponent whose creatures they were as the ability triggered, or the creatures' controller as it resolves?
  - Decision: The opponent whose creatures they were as the ability triggered: the trigger is one of a batch grouped by that controller (`one-or-more-an-opponent-controls-per-opponent`), it records that player as its event's player, and "that opponent"/"that player" reads it. A creature that has since changed control, or left the battlefield, doesn't change who it is.
- **control-lost-before-a-for-as-long-as-you-control-effect-begins** (interpretation, [triggers-durations](interpretations/triggers-durations.md)): Control of the source lost while the ability is on the stack ends "for as long as you control ~", even if it's regained before the ability resolves
  - Question: The rulings say losing control of the source before its ability resolves means the "for as long as you control ~" effect never begins (and, for Willow Satyr, so does the source untapping, "even if it becomes tapped again right away"). What if control is lost and regained before the ability resolves?
  - Decision: The effect does nothing, as for untapping and tapping again: the "for as long as you control ~" duration ended while the ability was on the stack, and it doesn't begin again during the resolution (CR 611.2b). As an effect of an ability of the source begins (a control change, a modification or a rule-modifying effect such as "doesn't untap"), the engine looks for the source changing control away from the ability's controller after the ability was put on the stack, as it looks for the source untapping for "for as long as ~ remains tapped". Effects of spells, and those whose duration is about another object, aren't affected.
- **first-turn-skips-one-draw-step** (interpretation, [turn-structure](interpretations/turn-structure.md)): The starting player skips only the first draw step of their first turn, not the draw step of an additional beginning phase
  - Question: An effect gives the starting player an additional beginning phase in their first turn (Shadow of the Second Sun, Sphinx of the Second Sun, Temple of Atropos), or a lone additional draw step (Untap, Upkeep, Draw). 103.8a has them skip "the draw step" of that turn, written for a turn with one draw step. Is the additional draw step skipped too?
  - Decision: No. 103.8a (and 103.8b for the team that plays first) skips one draw step: the first one that would begin in the starting player's first turn, the normal beginning phase's. A draw step added later in that turn happens, and the player draws. A "skip your next draw step" effect waits past the one 103.8a skips (614.10a), so it applies to the added one.
