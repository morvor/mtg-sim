# Interpretation register

Generated by `cargo run --release -p mtg-tools -- interpretations --write` from `docs/interpretations/*.md`; don't edit by hand.

Every rules judgment call the engine makes has an entry: an **interpretation** (the rules are ambiguous or silent and we chose a reading), a **deviation** (the engine knowingly differs from the Comprehensive Rules) or a **policy** (the rules leave it to a player or to procedure and the engine decides). **Provisional** entries are calls a reasonable judge could make the other way; they await review. Tests that pin an entry cite it with `interp!("<id>")`. What the engine doesn't support at all is a scope decision, listed in `docs/DEFERRED.md` instead.

## Provisional entries

- **combat-tax-scaled-to-nothing** (deviation, [combat](interpretations/combat.md)): A scaled tax to attack or block that comes to nothing isn't a cost
  - Question: A tax scaled by a count ("pays {X} for each creature they control that's attacking you, where X is the number of basic land types among lands you control", "{1} for each counter on it") can come to nothing. Is a creature under such a tax one that "can't attack unless a player pays a cost" (so requirements don't force it), or is there no cost?
  - Decision: No cost. When every applicable tax is a scaled amount that comes to nothing, the creature has no cost to attack or block, so "attacks each combat if able" and similar requirements force it.
- **growing-loops-run-to-the-limit** (deviation, [game-end](interpretations/game-end.md)): A loop that grows the game state isn't recognized and ends only at the action limit
  - Question: With two Charismatic Conquerors on each side ("Whenever an artifact or creature an opponent controls enters untapped, they may tap that permanent. If they don't, you create a 1/1 white Vampire creature token with flying."), each entering token triggers both of the opponent's Conquerors and only one of them can tap it, so every token makes another one for the other side. The optional action (tapping) can't stop the chain. Is that a draw, and when?
  - Decision: The engine recognizes loops only when the game state repeats, and here it never does (there are more tokens each time around). The game goes on until it reaches the configured action limit (`simulation-limits`) and ends in a draw there.
- **conspiracy-draft-needs-three-players** (interpretation, [players-multiplayer](interpretations/players-multiplayer.md)): A Conspiracy Draft game (like every multiplayer variant) needs more than two players
  - Question: Is a Conspiracy Draft game between exactly two players valid? 905.3 calls the game multiplayer and 100.1b defines multiplayer as more than two players, but no rule forbids a two-player Conspiracy Draft game outright.
  - Decision: Invalid. `GameConfig::validate` rejects a Conspiracy Draft game (a Free-for-All configuration with `limited`) for two players with `SetupError::NotMultiplayer`, as it does every multiplayer variant (Free-for-All, Grand Melee, Two-Headed Giant, Emperor, Team vs. Team, Alternating Teams).
- **attacking-player-is-an-active-player** (deviation, [players-multiplayer](interpretations/players-multiplayer.md)): "The attacking player" is the active player; with shared team turns its controller chooses one
  - Question: Card text "the attacking player" compiles to the active player. That is exact in games without shared team turns (506.2). With shared team turns (Two-Headed Giant, 805.10), which attacking player does it mean?
  - Decision: The engine resolves "the attacking player" like "the active player" (805.9): the ability's controller chooses one of the active team's players. That matches 805.10c's last sentence for abilities that don't involve a blocker (Contested Game Ball), but not its blocker rule: for an ability of a blocking creature (Souls of the Faultless) 805.10c fixes the attacking player as the controller of the creature it blocks, with no choice.
- **end-of-combat-on-your-next-turn-without-combat** (interpretation, [triggers-durations](interpretations/triggers-durations.md)): "Until end of combat on your next turn" ends with a turn that has no combat
  - Question: Brazen Cannonade's "Until end of combat on your next turn, you may play that card" names the end of the combat phase of your next turn. If that turn has no combat phase (all combat phases skipped), when does the permission end?
  - Decision: It ends as that turn ends (in its cleanup step, with "until end of turn" effects). With a combat phase, it ends as the first combat phase of that turn ends.
