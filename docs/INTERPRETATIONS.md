# Interpretation register

Generated by `cargo run --release -p mtg-tools -- interpretations --write` from `docs/interpretations/*.md`; don't edit by hand.

Every rules judgment call the engine makes has an entry: an **interpretation** (the rules are ambiguous or silent and we chose a reading), a **deviation** (the engine knowingly differs from the Comprehensive Rules) or a **policy** (the rules leave it to a player or to procedure and the engine decides). **Provisional** entries are calls a reasonable judge could make the other way; they await review. Tests that pin an entry cite it with `interp!("<id>")`. What the engine doesn't support at all is a scope decision, listed in `docs/DEFERRED.md` instead.

## Provisional entries

- **attack-total-cost-contains-x** (interpretation, [combat](interpretations/combat.md)): The total cost to attack or block contains {X} if any tax in it does
  - Question: Mana that may be spent only on costs that contain {X} may pay any part of such a cost. When creatures attack under several taxes at once (Sphere of Safety's "{X} for each of those creatures" and Ghostly Prison's "{2} for each creature"), the active player pays one total cost to attack (508.1h). Is that whole total a cost that contains {X}, or only the part one tax adds?
  - Decision: The whole total cost to attack (or each player's total cost to block) contains {X} when any tax in it does, so the restricted mana may pay any part of it, Ghostly Prison's {2} included; without a tax with {X} it pays none of it.
- **own-permission-first** (policy, [decisions-policies](interpretations/decisions-policies.md)): A card's own permission is the one used when an effect's permission would allow the same way; the player is asked only among effects' permissions
  - Question: When a card's own permission (a rule: casting from the hand; or its own ability: mayhem, flashback, retrace, an adventurer card on an adventure) and an effect's permission (Muldrotha, Crucible of Worlds, Omniscience) both let the player play it the same way, which is used, and is the player asked?
  - Decision: The card's own, without asking. A land in the hand, or one a keyword's rule lets the player play (mayhem without a cost, an adventurer land on an adventure), is played with no effect's permission. A way of casting the card that its own permissions allow is offered with an effect's permission only when that permission changes how it's cast (flash, a cost it requires, a cost reduction, a cost increase per card, the face, how mana may be spent), and never when the way's cost comes with the card's own permission (flashback's alternative cost; retrace's additional cost outside the hand; in the hand an own way's additional cost, such as Rout's "as though it had flash if you pay {2} more", comes with a timing permission, CR 601.3c, so an effect's permission is offered with it). Among effects' permissions the player is asked (by source, or by name), by default the first whose costs could be paid, permissions usable any number of times before once-each-turn ones.
- **entry-effects-of-a-leaving-source** (interpretation, [replacement-prevention](interpretations/replacement-prevention.md)): The effects modifying how a permanent enters that applied just before their source left the battlefield still apply
  - Question: While a permanent's entry is being modified, one of the effects applied (an "as this enters, return a nonland permanent you control" choice, Rescuer Sphinx; devour's sacrifice) makes a permanent that generated another of the effects leave the battlefield. Rhythm of the Wild's ruling says such an effect still applies, but which of that permanent's effects: those determined as the event was proposed, or those that applied to the event just before the permanent left, with the effects applied so far? They differ when an effect applied before it left changed what applies: a copy, control, back-face or face-down effect (Clone entering as a copy of Rescuer Sphinx), or one that changed what the permanent would be (Master Biomancer's "as a Mutant", after which Metallic Mimic naming Mutant applies, 616.2).
  - Decision: Those that applied just before it left. The engine determines the effects that apply at each step of the entry's replacement until a permanent leaves (`EntrySnapshot`), from the permanent as it would exist with the effects applied so far (614.12), and once one has left it adds back those not yet applied, as long as the permanent still enters the same way (no copy, control, back-face or face-down effect applied since). A Clone entering as a copy of Rescuer Sphinx that returns Grumgully, the Generous gets Grumgully's counter as Rescuer Sphinx itself does (2 counters); Rescuer Sphinx entering under Master Biomancer and Metallic Mimic naming Mutant, with Biomancer's effect applied first and then the Sphinx's returning the Mimic, gets the Mimic's counter too (4 counters).
- **that-opponent-fixed-as-it-triggers** (interpretation, [triggers-durations](interpretations/triggers-durations.md)): "That player" / "that opponent" of a trigger condition's "an opponent" is fixed as the ability triggers; an object-based "its controller" is read as it resolves
  - Question: Norn's Decree: "Whenever one or more creatures an opponent controls deal combat damage to you, that opponent gets a poison counter." Coveted Jewel: "Whenever one or more creatures an opponent controls attack you and aren't blocked, that player draws three cards and gains control of this artifact. Untap it." Before the ability resolves, the creatures change control (the Jewel's controller gains control of an unblocked attacker in response; another opponent does; only some of them do) or leave the battlefield. Is "that opponent" ("that player") the opponent whose creatures they were as the ability triggered, or their controller as it resolves? The same question for every "[object] an opponent controls [event], that player" trigger (Blood Seeker, Michiko Konda, Truth Seeker, ward), and, the other way round, for the object-based "its controller" / "that spell's controller" wordings.
  - Decision: Provisional (the user, Nov 19: "I am really not sure here"; research item ruling-research-2 found no source that decides it). A player-referring "that player" / "that opponent" / "they" whose antecedent is the trigger condition's "an opponent" (or "a player", or "enchanted player") is the player the condition named as the ability triggered: for Norn's Decree and Coveted Jewel the opponent whose creatures they were (each trigger is for one such opponent, `one-or-more-an-opponent-controls-per-opponent`, recorded as the event's player); for "[a permanent] an opponent controls [enters, is dealt damage, becomes tapped, ...]", "a land enters under an opponent's control", "a creature enchanted player controls enters" (Trespasser's Curse) and "a source an opponent controls deals damage to you", the controller the object had as the ability triggered; for "a spell or ability an opponent controls" (ward), the spell's controller as it targeted. A later control change (of all or only some of the objects, to you or to another opponent) or an object leaving the battlefield doesn't change who it is. An object-based "its controller" / "that creature's controller" / "that source's controller" / "that spell's controller" / "the controller of those creatures" is the object's controller as the ability resolves, or as it last existed (CR 608.2h), as the rulings on those wordings say: a spell stolen in response makes its new controller the one who may pay Frost Titan's {2} or is dealt Bonecrusher Giant's 2 damage, and a creature stolen after it dealt combat damage to the monarch makes its new controller the monarch (CR 725.2). The initiative's "the controller of those creatures takes the initiative" (CR 726.2), which the designation's own text words "that player takes the initiative" and the release notes "the first player takes the initiative", keeps the player the trigger was created for (a question for the user).
- **copied-ability-duration-starts-as-the-copy-is-created** (interpretation, [triggers-durations](interpretations/triggers-durations.md)): The "for as long as" durations of a copy of an ability start as the copy is put onto the stack, not with the original's
  - Question: The period of an ability's "for as long as" duration starts as the ability becomes activated or triggers. A copy of an ability (Strionic Resonator, Lithoform Engine, Rings of Brighthearth) is neither activated nor triggered. Does the period of the copy's durations start with the original's, or as the copy is put onto the stack?
  - Decision: As the copy is put onto the stack. From then on it's checked as the original's is: the source changing control, becoming untapped (or tapped), or phasing out or in while the copy waits ends it; a condition that doesn't hold as the copy is created never starts. What happened to the source between the original's activation or triggering and the copy's creation doesn't matter to the copy. (The engine first checks the conditions of duration-about-another-object-ends-while-the-ability-waits as the copy is created, with the original's targets when new ones are chosen for the copy afterwards: a condition about a new target that doesn't hold then has ended rather than not started yet, which differs only if it holds by the time the copy resolves.)
- **phasing-out-ends-a-waiting-for-as-long-as-duration** (interpretation, [triggers-durations](interpretations/triggers-durations.md)): The source phasing out while its ability waits ends a "for as long as" duration about it, even if it phases in before the ability resolves; phasing doesn't end "until ~ leaves the battlefield"
  - Question: Phasing out doesn't change a permanent's control or zone, and "effects that check a phased-in permanent's history" don't treat it as having left its controller's control (702.26d). If an ability's source phases out and back in while the ability waits on the stack, has its "for as long as you control ~" ("~ remains tapped", "~ remains on the battlefield") duration ended? And an effect lasting "until ~ leaves the battlefield"?
  - Decision: Yes: it ended as the source phased out (702.26f), and phasing in doesn't begin it again; the effect does nothing (what for-as-long-as-checked-continually-from-activation says of a duration that ended while the ability waited). "Until ~ leaves the battlefield" (Gaea's Liege, Graceful Antelope, Secret Invasion) isn't a "for as long as" duration: phasing out isn't leaving the battlefield (702.26d), so the source phasing out ends neither an effect that exists nor one its ability, waiting on the stack, is about to create; only leaving the battlefield (a new object, CR 400.7) does.
- **duration-about-another-object-ends-while-the-ability-waits** (interpretation, [triggers-durations](interpretations/triggers-durations.md)): A "for as long as" condition about another object or player that held while the ability waited and then didn't has ended, even if it holds again as the ability resolves; one that didn't hold at first never started unless it begins during the resolution
  - Question: A duration's condition is about another object or a player ("for as long as ~ remains tapped and that creature's power remains less than or equal to ~'s power", "for as long as they're the monarch", "for as long as that Aura is attached to it", "for as long as that creature is enchanted", "for as long as ~ remains attached to it"). If it stops holding while the ability waits on the stack and holds again by the time the ability resolves, does the effect begin? And if it doesn't hold as the ability is put on the stack and holds by the time the ability resolves?
  - Decision: No to both, unless it begins during the resolution. While an ability waits, these conditions of its effects' durations are checked each time characteristics are computed (as the engine checks the durations of existing effects), from the first check after the ability is all on the stack (targets chosen, costs paid); one that held then and later doesn't has ended. One that doesn't hold at that first check never started: if it holds at a later check while the ability waits, it didn't begin during the resolution, and the effect does nothing (Rootwater Matriarch's target, unenchanted as the ability is activated and enchanted in response); one that still doesn't hold as the ability begins to resolve is checked as the effect begins, since it can begin during the resolution, which may set it up ("you may have an opponent gain control of it. If you do, it's goaded for as long as they control it", Vislor Turlough; an earlier instruction of the same ability). One that has ended isn't checked again for beginning during the resolution (no printed card's ability restores one of these conditions as it resolves). Conditions of the kinds an ability's own instructions set up, about the affected objects being tapped or having counters: duration-the-resolution-sets-up-is-checked-as-the-effect-begins.
- **duration-the-resolution-sets-up-is-checked-as-the-effect-begins** (interpretation, [triggers-durations](interpretations/triggers-durations.md)): A "for as long as" condition an ability's own instructions set up (a counter it puts on the object, a tap) is checked as the effect begins, whatever happened while the ability waited
  - Question: Xolatoyac ("put a flood counter on target land. That land is an Island in addition to its other types for as long as it has a flood counter on it"), Braided Net and Zygon Infiltrator (they tap the target; "for as long as it remains tapped") set up their duration's condition as they resolve. If the target already met the condition as the ability was put on the stack, stopped meeting it while the ability waited, and meets it again before the ability resolves (another counter, tapped again by another effect), or only through the resolution, does the effect begin?
  - Decision: Yes, in both cases: conditions about the affected objects being tapped or having counters are checked only as the effect begins, after the instructions before it in the same ability; what happened while the ability waited doesn't matter.
