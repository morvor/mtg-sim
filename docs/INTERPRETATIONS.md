# Interpretation register

Generated by `cargo run --release -p mtg-tools -- interpretations --write` from `docs/interpretations/*.md`; don't edit by hand.

Every rules judgment call the engine makes has an entry: an **interpretation** (the rules are ambiguous or silent and we chose a reading), a **deviation** (the engine knowingly differs from the Comprehensive Rules) or a **policy** (the rules leave it to a player or to procedure and the engine decides). **Provisional** entries are calls a reasonable judge could make the other way; they await review. Tests that pin an entry cite it with `interp!("<id>")`. What the engine doesn't support at all is a scope decision, listed in `docs/DEFERRED.md` instead.

## Provisional entries

- **full-text-undefined-mana-cost** (interpretation, [characteristics-layers](interpretations/characteristics-layers.md)): An object given the "full text" of a card with no mana cost keeps its own mana cost, and its color comes from that mana cost and the card's color indicator
  - Question: Volrath's Shapeshifter ({1}{U}{U}) with a creature card that has no mana cost on top of the graveyard (Dryad Arbor, green by its color indicator): the first ruling keeps the Shapeshifter's own mana cost, but what color is it?
  - Decision: It keeps {1}{U}{U} (mana value 3) and takes the card's color indicator, so it's the colors of both: blue (its mana cost, 202.2) and green (the indicator, 202.2e).
- **attack-requirements-once-a-cost-is-paid** (interpretation, [combat](interpretations/combat.md)): A declaration that pays costs to attack obeys as many requirements as it could paying only those costs
  - Question: 508.1d doesn't require paying a cost to obey a requirement, and says nothing of a player who pays costs to attack anyway. Once a creature attacks paying a cost, may it ignore its requirements (attack another player than the one it must attack)? May a requirement obeyed by paying make up for another one that could be obeyed for free? And which costs count as costs "to attack at all"?
  - Decision: A declaration that has creatures attacking for a cost must also obey as many requirements as could be obeyed with each of those creatures free to make its paid attack, or to attack whatever needs no cost beyond its costs to attack at all (the restrictions taxing everything it can attack), and every other creature attacking only for free. So a creature with a cost to attack at all (Brainwash, "can't attack unless its controller pays {3}"; War Tax) may not attack, or attack as its requirements say; it may not attack another player instead. A cost only to attack some players or planeswalkers (Ghostly Prison, Propaganda, Baird) binds nothing else: a creature paying it may attack whatever else it can, paying that one's cost if any, even when every player it can attack has such a tax (with Propaganda on every opponent, a goaded creature may attack the goading player, paying that player's cost). A requirement obeyed by paying (attacking the Ghostly Prison player it must attack) doesn't make up for another creature's requirement that could be obeyed for free. Provisional, where the ruling's sentences pull apart: when obeying the requirement needs a cost beyond the cost to attack at all (Brainwash plus Ghostly Prison on the required player), the creature may attack another player paying only the cost to attack at all, since its controller isn't required to pay the other cost; and a single tax on everything the creature can attack (Baird in a two-player game, taxing the player and their planeswalkers) is a cost to attack at all, so paying it the creature attacks the player, not their planeswalker.
- **copy-target-legality-original-or-copy** (interpretation, [copy](interpretations/copy.md)): Copies made for each object a spell "could target" are decided from the original spell's point of view; a copy given a specified target is created only if the copy itself can target it
  - Question: 707.10d and 707.10e end with the same words: if "that player or object isn’t a legal target for each instance of the word “target,”" the copy isn't created. A legal target for which spell: the original (cast by its controller, with its characteristics as they are on the stack), or the copy (controlled by whoever copies it, with only the original's copiable values, 707.2)? The two differ when the copy has another controller (Ink-Treader Nephilim copies an opponent's spell) or when an effect changed the original on the stack (Blind Seer made it another color).
  - Decision: A copy made "for each [object or player] that spell could target" (707.10d) is decided from the original spell's point of view: one copy for each object or player the original could target, even one the copy itself can't target; that copy is put onto the stack targeting it and, if its target is illegal as it resolves, it doesn't resolve (608.2b). A copy whose new target an effect specifies (707.10e: "Copy that spell. The copy targets the chosen creature.") is created only if that object or player is a legal target both for the original spell (the choices offered; Ivy's and Zevlor's "a legal target for the spell") and for the copy itself as it's put onto the stack (Beamsplitter Mage's ruling). Otherwise the copy isn't created: it never was on the stack, so nothing sees it being copied or becoming a target ("whenever you copy a spell" doesn't trigger).
- **number-guess-range** (interpretation, [decisions-policies](interpretations/decisions-policies.md)): A player guessing "which number you chose" names one of the numbers of the choice's range
  - Question: "An opponent guesses which number you chose, ... If they guessed wrong, they lose life equal to the number they guessed": which numbers may the opponent guess?
  - Decision: One of the numbers of the range the card names (1 to 5), including numbers chosen for it on earlier turns, which the opponent knows (they were revealed) can't be the answer: such a guess is wrong, and the guesser loses that much life.
- **next-time-damage-batch** (interpretation, [replacement-prevention](interpretations/replacement-prevention.md)): A one-use "the next time ... would deal damage / be dealt damage" effect applies to all the damage dealt at the same time
  - Question: Damage dealt at the same time is several damage events to the engine (one per source and recipient). A limited-use replacement or prevention effect ("the next time damage would be dealt to this creature and/or you this turn", "the next time target creature would deal damage this turn") is used up by the first of them it applies to. Does it then apply to the others dealt at the same time?
  - Decision: Yes. While damage dealt at the same time is being replaced, a limited-use effect used up by one of its events still applies to the others (as one entering-the-battlefield batch already did for "the next time one or more creatures enter"). So Glarecaster redirects both chunks, and Awe Strike prevents both the damage a trampler deals to its blocker and the damage it deals to the player. Effects that prevent a number of damage ("the next 3 damage") are unaffected: they're shields with an amount, not one-use effects. Damage of the batch that can't be prevented doesn't use up a prevention effect (615.12), so when part of the batch can't be prevented, the effect prevents the rest and is used up by it.
- **x-in-a-cost-equal-to-a-cards-mana-cost-is-0** (interpretation, [stack-casting-costs](interpretations/stack-casting-costs.md)): A granted ability's cost "equal to its mana cost" has X as 0 when the card isn't a spell
  - Question: An effect gives a card in a graveyard an activated keyword ability whose cost "is equal to its mana cost" (Araumi of the Dead Tide's encore: "Exile the creature card and pay its mana cost"; the scavenge of Varolz, Young Deathclaws and The Cave of Skulls; the unearth of Grixis and Highway Reaver; the embalm of Cursecloth Wrappings), and the card's mana cost has {X}. Does the player announce X and pay it (107.3a), or is X 0?
  - Decision: X is 0: the cost is the card's mana cost with X as 0, and no X is announced; tokens copying the card have X 0 too. Provisional (work item ruling-conflicts-3, case 6, conclusion D): Araumi's ruling has the player choose any X; the user decides whether its "you can choose any value for X" is set aside as inconsistent with 107.3h and the scavenge rulings (recommended), as Gnoll War Band's ruling was.
- **target-player-reference-rechecked** (interpretation, [stack-casting-costs](interpretations/stack-casting-costs.md)): A target restriction naming "that player" (another target's controller) is checked against that target's controller as the spell resolves
  - Question: Mutiny ("Target creature an opponent controls deals damage equal to its power to another target creature that player controls") is cast in a multiplayer game; before it resolves, a third player (also an opponent) gains control of the first target. The first target is still a creature an opponent controls. Is the second target, still controlled by the original player, a creature "that player" controls?
  - Decision: No. "That player" is the first target's controller as the targets are checked on resolution, so the second target is illegal and, per the ruling, no damage is dealt.
- **hybrid-half-for-a-reduction** (deviation, [stack-casting-costs](interpretations/stack-casting-costs.md)): A cost reduction that can reduce a hybrid symbol of the cost reduces it, as though the player had announced the half it reduces
  - Question: The halves of a cost's hybrid symbols are announced before the total cost is determined, so whether a reduction reduces a hybrid symbol depends on the half announced. The engine asks how hybrid symbols will be paid only once the total cost is known (`choose_payment_ways_for`, after the reductions). Which half does a reduction see?
  - Decision: The half the reduction can reduce, chosen for the player: a reduction by colored mana ("cost {B}{R} less", Ragemonger, Edgewalker, Morophon) that finds no plain symbol of its color in the cost reduces a hybrid symbol with that color ({R/W}, {2/R}, ...), as though that half had been announced; a reduction by an amount of generic mana ("cost {1} less") that the cost's generic mana can't absorb reduces a monocolored hybrid symbol as though its generic half had been announced ({2/W} reduced by {1} becomes {1}). The player announces the halves of the other hybrid symbols as they pay. The player can't announce the other half of a symbol a reduction would reduce, to keep the reduction from applying.
- **limited-free-basics-have-a-basic-land-type** (policy, [zones-objects](interpretations/zones-objects.md)): Limited decks add only the five non-snow basic lands beyond the card pool
  - Question: 100.2b lets a limited deck use "basic land cards" beyond the product; which basic land cards may be added that way?
  - Decision: Only basic lands with a basic land type (Plains, Island, Swamp, Mountain, Forest) that aren't snow; Wastes, Snow-Covered Wastes and the basic snow lands count against the card pool like any other card.
